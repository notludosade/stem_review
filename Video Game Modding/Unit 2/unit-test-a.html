1. What is the main engineering reason studios bundle assets into one large archive file?

Performance — fast sequential reads of one large file — is the primary engineering reason for archive formats.

2. Fill in the blank: a community-built tool that reads a proprietary archive and pulls out its individual files is called an ___.

Extractors exist for most popular archive formats thanks to community reverse-engineering.

3. Why is DDS such a common texture format in games specifically?

DDS is specifically designed for fast, low-overhead reading directly by graphics hardware.

4. Fill in the blank: the raw 3D geometry of an object — points, edges, and surfaces defining its shape — is called its ___.

A mesh is the raw shape; a separate rig or skeleton describes how it moves.

5. Why can't audio in an FMOD- or Wwise-based game always be replaced just by dropping in a new sound file?

A bank system's reference matters as much as the raw file — replacing audio usually means editing what the bank already points to.

6. An unfamiliar archive format is often described as light obfuscation, not real security. Answer both parts:

(a) Why isn't an unfamiliar format the same as encryption?

(b) Where do reverse-engineered format specifications for popular engines usually come from?

Archive formats are an inconvenience solved by community reverse-engineering, not a cryptographic barrier.

7. A model file bundles more than one kind of data. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the mesh define?

(b) What does a rig (or skeleton) add on top of the mesh?

The mesh is static geometry; the rig is what lets that geometry move realistically.

8. Config files are plain, human-readable text. Answer both parts:

(a) What makes a format "human-readable" rather than binary?

(b) Why does data-driven design make config edits the safest modding entry point?

Human-readable, data-driven config values are editable with nothing but a text editor and no risk to binary assets.

9. Some asset formats are proprietary, others are open. Answer both parts:

(a) What defines a proprietary format?

(b) Why do proprietary formats make community-built converters necessary?

Open formats like PNG or OBJ need no conversion tools; proprietary ones do, precisely because they aren't openly documented.

10. An unpacked archive contains .dds, .fbx, and .ini files. Answer both parts:

(a) Which of these is most likely a texture?

(b) Which of these is most likely a plain-text config file?

.dds is a texture format, .fbx is a model interchange format, and .ini is a common human-readable config format.
