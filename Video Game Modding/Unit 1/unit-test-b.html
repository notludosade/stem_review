1. Adding a single new weapon to a game, built in the game's existing model format and dropped in alongside the originals, is an example of which category?

An asset addition introduces genuinely new content built in the game's existing format, without replacing everything the way a total conversion does.

2. Fill in the blank: a mod that replaces nearly all of a game's assets, rules, and story while keeping the original engine is called a ___ ___.

Dota began this way, as a total-conversion mod of Warcraft III's map editor.

3. What does a game's EULA actually govern?

A EULA is the license agreement setting terms for using the developer's copyrighted work, separate from technical details like file formats.

4. Fill in the blank: courts evaluate fair use ___ ___, weighing factors like how much of the original was used and whether the new work harms its market.

Fair use is not automatic or absolute — it is weighed case by case against specific factors.

5. Which of these is the clearest example of a developer taking an actively hostile stance toward modding?

Cease-and-desist letters are active enforcement, distinguishing a hostile stance from a merely neutral one.

6. A modder wants to sell a mod built entirely around another studio's copyrighted characters and story, without permission. Answer both parts:

(a) Is this generally safe under most EULAs and copyright law?

(b) What would make a similar mod meaningfully safer?

Selling a mod built on someone else's IP without permission is a clear risk; non-commercial, base-game-dependent mods are generally much safer ground.

7. Developer stances toward modding exist on a spectrum. Answer both parts:

(a) What generally characterizes a "neutral or tolerated" stance?

(b) Which of this course's units mostly assumes this kind of stance?

Most everyday modding techniques (unpacking, asset replacement) operate comfortably in the "neutral or tolerated" middle ground.

8. Anti-cheat systems and modding tools often overlap technically. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the shared technical territory that causes this overlap?

(b) Is this tension a reason developer stances toward modding are always permanent and unchanging?

The overlap in low-level access is real, but a developer's overall stance can still shift as the game's priorities change over time.

9. A once-commercial game hasn't been sold or updated by its studio in over a decade. Answer both parts:

(a) What term describes this practical situation?

(b) Has the game's underlying copyright actually expired just because it's abandonware?

Abandonware is a practical, not legal, description — the underlying copyright remains in force even when enforcement has quietly stopped.

10. Where a mod sits on the modding spectrum predicts roughly how much effort it takes. Answer both parts:

(a) What tool alone is typically enough for a config tweak?

(b) What does a scripted mod additionally require, beyond what a config tweak needs?

Config tweaks need only a text editor; scripted mods step up to requiring real programming in the game's scripting language.
