1. What distinguishes a CPU's design from a GPU's, at a high level?

Few powerful cores versus many simple cores is the core architectural contrast behind CPU/GPU performance differences here.

2. Fill in the blank: processing operations one at a time, in sequence, is called ___ computation.

Total time for this style of computation scales directly with the number of operations.

3. Why do early CNN layers transfer well across very different visual tasks?

Generic low-level visual features are broadly reusable, unlike deeper, more task-specific combinations of them.

4. Fill in the blank: unfreezing some pretrained layers, typically with a small learning rate, in addition to training a new head, is called ___.

A small learning rate on these unfrozen layers helps avoid catastrophic forgetting.

5. Of all the hyperparameters this course has covered, which one has this unit repeatedly stressed as the most consistently impactful?

Lesson 3, Lesson 5, and Lesson 16's widgets all demonstrated learning rate's outsized sensitivity.

6. Independent versus dependent operations. Answer both parts:

(a) A single layer's weighted sums, computed independently of each other, are well suited to...

(b) Successive training iterations, where each needs the previous one's updated weights, are inherently...

Independence enables parallelism; a genuine dependency between steps cannot be parallelized away.

7. What throughput measures. Answer both parts:

(a) Throughput is best described as...

(b) A GPU can have high throughput even though...

Many simple cores working in parallel can out-throughput a few fast cores, without any single core winning a speed contest.

8. Feature extraction versus fine-tuning. Answer both parts:

(a) Feature extraction trains...

(b) Fine-tuning, by contrast, also trains...

Feature extraction keeps pretrained weights fixed; fine-tuning lets some of them keep adjusting, carefully.

9. Why a small learning rate specifically. Answer both parts:

(a) A large learning rate applied to unfrozen pretrained layers risks...

(b) Fill in the blank: this specific failure mode is called ___.

Catastrophic forgetting is exactly the risk a small learning rate on pretrained layers is meant to guard against.

10. Why random search's coverage differs from grid search's. Answer both parts:

(a) Grid search's total trial count grows...

(b) Random search's advantage comes from...

Grid search's combinatorial explosion wastes budget on unimportant hyperparameters; random search doesn't need to know which one matters in advance.
