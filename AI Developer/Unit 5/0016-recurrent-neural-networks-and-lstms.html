Recurrent Neural Networks and LSTMs

A CNN's filter has no sense of order. Text, audio, and time series all depend on order completely — that calls for a different architecture.

Lesson 17 → Contents Glossary →

A recurrent neural network (RNN) processes a sequence one element at a time, carrying a hidden state forward at each step — a running summary of everything seen so far, updated with each new element rather than discarded between them.

Python — PyTorch 
import torch.nn as nn

rnn = nn.RNN(input_size=10, hidden_size=20, batch_first=True)
output, hidden = rnn(sequence)   # hidden carries context from earlier steps forward

lstm = nn.LSTM(input_size=10, hidden_size=20, batch_first=True)
output, (hidden, cell) = lstm(sequence)   # gates decide what to keep, forget, and output

A plain RNN's hidden state lets it use context from earlier in a sequence — predicting the next word in a sentence depends on the words already read. But over a long sequence, backpropagation (Lesson 14) has to flow the gradient through every single step, and that gradient tends to shrink toward zero the further back it travels — a vanishing gradient. In practice, the network effectively forgets context from many steps ago. An LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) fixes this with explicit gates — small learned mechanisms that decide what to keep, what to forget, and what to output from its state at each step — letting important information survive across much longer stretches of a sequence than a plain RNN can manage.

Definition A recurrent neural network (RNN) processes a sequence step by step, carrying a hidden state forward. A vanishing gradient is a gradient that shrinks toward zero over many steps, weakening long-range learning. An LSTM uses learned gates to control what its state keeps, forgets, or outputs.
Why it matters here Vanishing gradients are the exact "historically important problem" flagged back in Lesson 14 without explanation — this is it, in its most visible form. Lesson 17's Transformers exist largely because even LSTM gates only partly solve this: processing a whole sequence at once, instead of one step at a time, sidesteps the problem in a completely different way.

Check your recall

1. What does an RNN's hidden state carry from one step to the next?

The hidden state is updated at every step, folding in new context rather than discarding what came before.

2. What problem do long sequences cause for a plain RNN?

The gradient has to flow back through every step during training, and tends to shrink the further it travels.

3. What do an LSTM's gates control?

Gates are learned mechanisms deciding exactly this at every single step of the sequence.
