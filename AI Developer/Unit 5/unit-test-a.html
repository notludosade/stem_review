1. A CNN slides a 3×3 filter across a grayscale image. What operation does the filter perform at each position?

The filter's weights are reused at every position it slides to — that reuse is exactly what a convolution is.

2. After a convolution layer produces a feature map, a max-pooling layer with a 2×2 window is applied. What does this pooling step do?

Pooling shrinks a feature map by keeping only the strongest signal in each small region.

3. An RNN processes a sentence one word at a time. What does its hidden state carry from one word to the next?

The hidden state is updated at every step, folding new context into what came before rather than discarding it.

4. Fill in the blank: an LSTM uses learned ___ to decide what information its state keeps, forgets, or outputs at each step of a sequence.

Gates are the small learned mechanisms that let an LSTM control what its state carries forward.

5. Fill in the blank: in self-attention, the vector that represents what a token is looking for — as opposed to what it offers or what it contributes — is called its ___.

Every token produces a query (what it's looking for), a key (what it offers), and a value (what it contributes).

6. A CNN filter's weights are reused at every position across an image, rather than each position getting its own independent set of weights. Answer both parts:

(a) What is this reuse of the same filter weights across every position called?

(b) Why does weight sharing make a CNN need far fewer weights than a fully-connected layer over raw pixels?

(a) Reusing one filter's weights at every position is weight sharing. (b) That reuse is exactly why a CNN is dramatically smaller than a fully-connected layer over raw pixels.

7. A plain RNN is trained on very long sequences, and backpropagation must flow the gradient back through every single step. Answer both parts:

(a) What tends to happen to the gradient the further back it travels through many steps?

(b) Fill in the blank: as a practical consequence, the network effectively ___ context from many steps ago.

(a) The gradient tends to shrink toward zero the further back it has to travel. (b) In practice this means the network forgets context from far earlier in the sequence.

8. Attention lets a Transformer relate token 1 and token 20 in a 20-token sequence directly. Answer both parts:

(a) How does attention relate these two distant tokens, compared to how an RNN would?

(b) Fill in the blank: attention that relates every position in a sequence to every other position within that same sequence is called ___-attention.

(a) Attention relates any two positions directly, with no sequential dependency. (b) When every position attends to every other position in the same sequence, that's self-attention.

9. Unlike an RNN, a Transformer's self-attention computation is a small number of matrix multiplications over an entire sequence at once. Answer both parts:

(a) What does this enable, compared to an RNN's one-step-at-a-time processing?

(b) Fill in the blank: Q @ K.T followed by softmax and weights @ V is the entire attention computation for the whole sequence at once, which is why it runs in ___ rather than step by step.

(a) Because attention is matrix multiplication over the whole sequence, it runs in parallel, unlike an RNN's sequential loop. (b) That same reasoning is exactly why attention runs in parallel.

10. CNNs, RNNs/LSTMs, and Transformers each reshape what a network's layers look like for a specific kind of data, while keeping the same underlying training loop. Answer both parts:

(a) Which architecture is built specifically for image-shaped data, using filters shared across positions?

(b) Which architecture removes the sequential, one-step-at-a-time bottleneck entirely by relating any two positions directly?

(a) A CNN's filters, shared across positions, are built for image-shaped data. (b) A Transformer's self-attention removes the step-by-step bottleneck that limits RNNs and LSTMs.
