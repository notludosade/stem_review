Decision Trees

The same spam-or-not problem Lesson 8 solved with probabilities and a sigmoid — solved again with nothing but a sequence of yes/no questions.

Lesson 10 → Contents Glossary →

A decision tree classifies an example by asking a sequence of simple questions about its features, branching one way or the other at each question, until it reaches a final answer. No probabilities, no sigmoid — just a chain of comparisons.

Python — scikit-learn 
from sklearn.tree import DecisionTreeClassifier

model = DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=3)
model.fit(X_train, y_train)   # X: num_links, num_exclamations; y: spam or not

predictions = model.predict(X_val)

# What the tree actually learned looks roughly like:
#   if num_links > 2:
#       if num_exclamations > 5:  predict spam
#       else:                     predict not spam
#   else:                         predict not spam

Each internal question — num_links > 2, num_exclamations > 5 — is a node, and the specific feature-and-threshold it tests is a split. fit() chooses both which feature to test at each node and where to put the threshold, picking whichever split best separates spam from non-spam at that point. max_depth limits how many questions deep the tree is allowed to go — left unlimited, a tree can keep splitting until every single training example has its own tiny branch, which is a direct route to Lesson 6's overfitting: perfect on training data, unreliable on anything new.

Definition A decision tree classifies by branching through a sequence of feature-based questions to a final answer. A node is one question in the tree; a split is the specific feature and threshold it tests. max_depth limits how many questions deep the tree can grow.
Why it matters here max_depth is a direct, concrete example of controlling overfitting from Lesson 6 by limiting model complexity rather than by touching the data at all — the same idea reappears constantly in Unit 4's neural networks, just under different names. Comparing a tree's predictions against logistic regression's from Lesson 8, on the exact same problem, is exactly what Lesson 10's scoring tools are for.
