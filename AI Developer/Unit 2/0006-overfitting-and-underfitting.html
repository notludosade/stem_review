Overfitting and Underfitting

Lesson 5's split exists almost entirely to catch these two failure modes — and they look completely different once you know what to compare.

Lesson 7 → Contents Glossary →

Comparing a model's score on its training set against its score on its validation set reveals which of two opposite failure modes is happening — if either is.

Python — scikit-learn 
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression

model = LinearRegression()
model.fit(X_train, y_train)

train_score = model.score(X_train, y_train)   # how well it fits data it trained on
val_score = model.score(X_val, y_val)         # how well it fits data it never trained on

print(train_score, val_score)

# Overfitting:   0.98, 0.45   — memorized the training data, fails on anything new
# Underfitting:  0.40, 0.38   — never learned the real pattern anywhere, even in training
# Good fit:      0.85, 0.82   — learned the real pattern, and it holds up on new data

Overfitting means the model latched onto the training data too closely — including its noise and quirks — so it performs great on data it's already seen and noticeably worse on anything new. Underfitting is the opposite problem: the model is too simple, or wasn't trained enough, to capture the real pattern at all, so it performs poorly everywhere, training data included. The goal — generalization — sits in between: a model that performs well on new, unseen data because it learned the actual underlying pattern rather than memorizing specific examples.

Definition Overfitting is a large gap between a much better training score and a worse validation score, from memorizing training data instead of the real pattern. Underfitting is a poor score on both, from a model too simple to capture the pattern at all. Generalization is performing well on new, unseen data — the actual goal.
Why it matters here This closes Unit 2. Every unit from here forward assumes this exact check as a habit: after training any model — the classical models in Unit 3, the neural networks in Unit 4 — comparing its training score against its validation score is the very first thing to do, before trusting any other number about it.
