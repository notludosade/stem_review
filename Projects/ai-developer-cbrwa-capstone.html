AI Developer: CB/RWA Capstone

An open-ended build, not a quiz — this project exists to see whether you've actually proved yourself across all seven AI Developer: CB/RWA courses: Computer Programming 1, Computer Programming 2, Data Handling: CB, AI Developer, Applied Machine/Deep Learning, Cloud Computing A, and Cloud Computing B / DevOps.

The Brief

Take a real dataset from raw file to a trained model you built and tuned by hand — not just a pretrained API call — then design the cloud architecture and CI/CD pipeline that would actually serve it and keep it healthy in production. This is the regular AI & Data capstone's job plus two more: proving you can build the model yourself, and proving you can ship and watch it once it's live.

Deliverables

  • A cleaned, documented dataset pipeline (even a single CSV to a validated training set counts) with at least one honest visualization of the data's shape or limitations
  • A neural network you built and trained yourself — architecture, loss function, and optimizer named and justified, not just imported and run
  • A training write-up: what the loss curve looked like, one overfitting or underfitting symptom you saw, and what you changed in response
  • An evaluation beyond accuracy: at least one other metric (precision/recall/F1, a confusion matrix, or equivalent) and what it reveals that accuracy alone would hide
  • A deployment sketch: where the model would run in the cloud, what CI/CD stage would retrain or redeploy it, and one metric that would tell you the model has drifted
  • A short "responsible AI" pass: one limitation, bias, or failure mode of your model, and what you'd tell a user relying on it

Reflection

Answer in your own words. There's no grading here — just save your answers below.

1. Data Handling: CB taught you to distrust data before trusting a model. What's one thing you found or fixed in your dataset that would have quietly wrecked your results if you'd skipped it?

2. AI Developer introduced neural networks at a conceptual level. Where did Applied Machine/Deep Learning's deeper math (backprop, a specific architecture choice, a regularization technique) change something you actually did here?

3. Describe one real training run that didn't go the way you expected. What did the loss curve or metrics tell you, and what did you change?

4. Why did you pick the evaluation metric you used beyond accuracy? Give a concrete case where accuracy alone would have told you the model was fine when it wasn't.

5. Walk through your deployment sketch using Cloud Computing A and B ideas by name — where does the model actually run, and what part of your CI/CD pipeline would catch a bad retrain before it reached users?

6. What's the one failure mode or bias you found in your model, and what would you actually tell a user who was about to rely on it?

7. Looking back across all seven courses, what's the biggest gap between "a model that works in a notebook" and "a model I'd actually trust in production" — and which course closed that gap the most for you?
