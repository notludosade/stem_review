Engineering & Physics Capstone

An open-ended design, not a quiz — this project exists to see whether you've actually proved yourself on Precalculus, AP Physics 1, Engineering 1, AP Physics 2, and Career Applied Engineering.

← Engineering & Physics Pathway All Projects →

The Brief

Design a physical system or device under a real constraint you choose — a budget, a weight limit, a required safety margin, a load it has to carry. Back every major decision with an actual physics calculation, and package the result the way a working engineer would present it: a design brief with reasoning shown, not just a final answer.

Deliverables

  • A one-paragraph problem statement: what the system does, and the constraint that makes it non-trivial
  • At least 2 physics-backed calculations that directly drove a design decision, shown with units
  • A materials or statics decision justified using Engineering 1 concepts — why this material, this cross-section, this joint
  • A stated safety margin or failure mode, and what you did about it
  • A one-page "professional summary" of the design, written for a non-engineer stakeholder or a portfolio, the way Career Applied Engineering taught

Reflection

Answer in your own words. There's no grading here — just save your answers below.

1. Which AP Physics 1 concept (forces, energy, momentum, rotation) directly sized or shaped part of your design? Show the calculation and say what would break if you'd ignored it.

2. What material or structural choice did you make, drawing on Engineering 1's materials and statics units? What's the failure mode you're most worried about, and roughly what's your safety margin?

3. Where does thermodynamics, electricity, or optics from AP Physics 2 show up in your system, even indirectly (heat dissipation, a sensor, a power budget)? How did that change a decision?

4. What's one constraint — safety, a standard, an ethical trade-off — from Engineering 1's safety & ethics unit that you had to design around rather than just optimize past?

5. If you had to defend this design in a 5-minute technical interview, the way Career Applied Engineering prepared you for, what's the question you're least prepared for — and what's your honest answer to it right now?

6. What's the single number in your design (a dimension, a load, a margin) you're least confident in, and what would you measure or test first to check it?
