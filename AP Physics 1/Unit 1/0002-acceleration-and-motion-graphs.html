Acceleration and Motion Graphs

Position, velocity, and acceleration are three views of the same motion — and each graph's slope is the next one's height.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

Velocity describes how position changes. Acceleration describes how velocity changes — the rate at which speed, direction, or both are shifting:

average acceleration = Δv / Δt
Acceleration doesn't mean "speeding up" Everyday speech treats acceleration as speeding up and braking as something else. Physics doesn't: any change in velocity is acceleration, including slowing down (a is opposite in sign to v) and even turning at constant speed (Unit 2 covers this — direction change alone is acceleration). A car braking to a stop has acceleration just as real as a car speeding up.

Reading motion graphs

Because velocity is the rate of change of position, and acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, three graphs of the same motion are linked by slope:

The slope relationship Slope of the position-time graph at an instant = velocity at that instant. Slope of the velocity-time graph at an instant = acceleration at that instant. Flip it around: the area under a velocity-time graph over some interval equals the displacement during that interval, and the area under an acceleration-time graph equals the change in velocity.
Example: reading a velocity-time graph Suppose velocity is a constant +4 m/s for 5 s, then drops linearly to 0 over the next 2 s. The area under the first stretch is a rectangle: 4 × 5 = 20 m of displacement. The area under the second stretch is a triangle: ½ × 2 × 4 = 4 m. Total displacement over the 7 s: 24 m — read entirely off the graph's shape, no equation needed.

Sign conventions on graphs

On a velocity-time graph, area above the t-axis counts as positive displacement and area below counts as negative — they don't just add, they can cancel. An object can travel a large distance while its net displacement (net signed area) comes out small, or even zero, if it spends time moving in the negative direction too.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. On a position-time graph, a horizontal (flat) segment means the object is:

Slope of the position-time graph is velocity. A flat segment has zero slope, so velocity is zero there.

2. A car brakes from 20 m/s to 0 m/s while still moving forward. Its acceleration is:

Any change in velocity is acceleration. Slowing down means acceleration points opposite to the direction of motion.

3. The area under a velocity-time graph over some time interval equals:

Area under v-t is displacement — this is the graphical mirror of "slope of x-t is velocity."

4. Fill in the blank: the slope of a velocity-time graph at an instant equals the ___ at that instant.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, so it's exactly the v-t graph's slope.
