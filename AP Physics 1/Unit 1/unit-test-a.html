1. Fill in the blank: A dog runs 20 m to fetch a ball, then runs 20 m back to its owner. The dog's ___ for this round trip is 40 m, even though its displacement is 0 m.

Distance is the total path length covered and only accumulates — it doesn't care that the dog ended up back where it started. Displacement, by contrast, only compares final position to initial position.

2. A position-time graph shows a straight line with a constant positive slope for an entire 10 s interval. During this interval, the object is moving with:

Slope of a position-time graph is velocity. A straight line has constant (unchanging) slope, so velocity is constant and nonzero — and a constant velocity means zero acceleration, not a large one.

3. A train's initial speed, final speed, and elapsed time are all known, but its acceleration is not given and is not needed to find its displacement. Which kinematic equation applies?

Of the four kinematic equations, Δx = ½(v₀ + v)t is the only one that doesn't contain a — exactly the variable missing from this problem.

4. Which of the following is a vector quantity, not a scalar?

Displacement carries direction as part of its meaning, which is what makes it a vector. Distance, time, and mass are all magnitudes only, with no direction attached.

5. A ball is launched at 30° above horizontal, and an identical ball is launched at 60° above horizontal, both at the same speed and both landing at the same height they launched from. How do their ranges compare?

Range is proportional to sin(2θ). Here 2θ is 60° for one ball and 120° for the other, and sin(120°) = sin(180° − 120°) = sin(60°) — so any two launch angles that add to 90° give equal ranges, even though their flight times and max heights differ.

6. A runner starts at x = 0 m, reaches x = 60 m at t = 10 s, then jogs back to x = 20 m at t = 16 s. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the runner's average velocity for the entire 16 s?

(b) Fill in the blank: the runner's average speed for the entire 16 s is ___ m/s.

(a) Average velocity = Δx/Δt = (20 − 0)/16 = 1.25 m/s. (b) Total distance covered is 60 m (out) + 40 m (back) = 100 m, so average speed = 100/16 = 6.25 m/s — a different number describing the same 16 seconds.

7. An object's velocity is a constant +6 m/s for 4 s, then decreases linearly to 0 m/s over the following 3 s. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the object's total displacement over the full 7 s, found from the area under its velocity-time graph?

(b) During the final 3 s, while velocity decreases from 6 m/s to 0 m/s, the object's acceleration is:

(a) The first 4 s is a rectangle: 6 × 4 = 24 m. The next 3 s is a triangle: ½ × 3 × 6 = 9 m. Total: 24 + 9 = 33 m. (b) a = Δv/Δt = (0 − 6)/3 = −2 m/s² — opposite in sign to the (positive) velocity, exactly what slowing down looks like.

8. A car starts at rest and accelerates uniformly at 2.5 m/s² for 6 s. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the car's velocity at the end of the 6 s?

(b) Fill in the blank: the car's displacement during these 6 s is ___ m.

(a) v = v₀ + at = 0 + 2.5(6) = 15 m/s. (b) Δx = v₀t + ½at² = 0 + ½(2.5)(6²) = ½(2.5)(36) = 45 m.

9. A ball is thrown straight up at 19.6 m/s from ground level. Using g = 9.8 m/s² and taking up as positive, answer both parts:

(a) How long does it take the ball to reach its highest point?

(b) Fill in the blank: the ball's maximum height above the ground is ___ m.

(a) At the peak v = 0, so from v = v₀ + at: 0 = 19.6 + (−9.8)t → t = 2 s. (b) H = v₀²/(2g) = 19.6²/19.6 = 19.6 m — acceleration is g downward the entire time, including at the peak itself.

10. A projectile is launched at 29.4 m/s at an angle of 30° above horizontal, landing at the same height it launched from. Using g = 9.8 m/s², sin 30° = 0.5, and cos 30° ≈ 0.87, answer both parts:

(a) What is the initial vertical component of velocity, v0y?

(b) Fill in the blank: the projectile's total time of flight is ___ s.

(a) v₀y = v₀ sin θ = 29.4(0.5) = 14.7 m/s; the 25.5 m/s distractor is actually v₀x = 29.4(0.87). (b) T = 2v₀y/g = 2(14.7)/9.8 = 3 s, from the symmetry of time up and time down.
