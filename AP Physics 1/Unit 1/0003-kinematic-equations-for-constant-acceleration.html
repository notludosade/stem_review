Kinematic Equations for Constant Acceleration

Four equations, five variables each — pick the one missing exactly the variable you don't have.

← Lesson 2 Lesson 4 → Contents Glossary →

When acceleration is constant (not changing over time), the graph relationships from Lesson 2 collapse into four algebraic equations. Each one relates five quantities — initial velocity v0, final velocity v, displacement Δx, acceleration a, and time t — using only four of the five at once:

v = v0 + at (missing Δx)
Δx = v0t + ½at² (missing v)
v² = v0² + 2aΔx (missing t)
Δx = ½(v0 + v)t (missing a)
Why this works: constant acceleration only Every equation above assumes a is constant over the interval in question — if acceleration changes mid-problem (a car that brakes, then speeds up again), you must split the motion into separate constant-acceleration stretches and apply these equations to each stretch separately.

The problem-solving pattern

Every kinematics problem reduces to the same three steps: list the quantities you're given (and the one you're asked to find), identify which of v0, v, Δx, a, t is missing entirely, and pick the one equation above that doesn't need it.

Example A car starts at rest (v0 = 0) and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 s. Find its displacement. Given: v0, a, t. Missing: v. Use Δx = v0t + ½at² = 0(4) + ½(3)(4)² = 24 m.
Example A cyclist traveling at 8 m/s brakes at a constant 2 m/s² and comes to rest. How far does she travel while braking? Given: v0 = 8, v = 0, a = −2 (opposing motion). Missing: t. Use v² = v0² + 2aΔx → 0 = 64 + 2(−2)Δx → Δx = 16 m.

Sign convention discipline

Every one of these equations is a signed vector equation collapsed onto one axis. Pick a positive direction before starting, and stay consistent: if "up" or "forward" is positive, an acceleration or velocity pointing the other way must be entered as negative, not just described in words as "the opposite direction." Sign errors are the single most common mistake in kinematics — not a misunderstanding of the physics.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. You're given v0, a, and Δx, and asked to find t. Which equation should you use?

The equation that doesn't require v (the one quantity missing from the given list) is Δx = v₀t + ½at² — the only one of the four with no v in it.

2. Which equation is the right choice when time t is not known and not needed?

v² = v₀² + 2aΔx is the only one of the four with no t in it at all.

3. A ball's acceleration changes partway through its motion (constant, then a different constant). What must you do to apply these equations?

Each equation assumes one constant a throughout. A change in acceleration mid-problem requires treating it as two separate constant-acceleration problems in sequence.

4. Fill in the blank: the most common source of error in kinematics problems isn't misunderstanding the physics — it's inconsistent ___ conventions.

Every kinematics equation is a signed one-axis equation — a velocity or acceleration opposing your chosen positive direction must be entered as negative.
