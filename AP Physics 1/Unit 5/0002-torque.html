Torque

The same push does nothing or everything, depending entirely on where and at what angle you apply it.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

Torque, defined Torque is the rotational equivalent of force — a measure of how effectively a force causes rotation about a pivot: τ = rF sin θ, where r is the distance from the pivot to the point where the force is applied, F is the force's magnitude, and θ is the angle between the force vector and the line from the pivot to the point of application. Torque is measured in N·m and, by convention, is positive for counterclockwise rotation and negative for clockwise.
Why r and sin θ both matter A force applied far from the pivot (large r) or perpendicular to the line to the pivot (θ = 90°, sin θ = 1) produces the most torque for a given force magnitude. This is exactly why door handles are placed far from the hinge — maximizing r — and why pushing a door straight at the hinge (θ = 0°, sin θ = 0) produces zero torque no matter how hard you push, since the force has no rotational leverage at all.
τ = rF⊥ = r(F sin θ)

Torque can equivalently be computed using only the component of force perpendicular to the line from the pivot (F⊥ = F sin θ) — the parallel component (F cos θ) contributes no torque, since it points directly toward or away from the pivot and produces no rotational tendency.

Example: a wrench turning a bolt A 0.25 m wrench has a 60 N force applied perpendicular to it at the very end. τ = rF sin θ = (0.25)(60)(sin 90°) = 15 N·m.
Example: pushing at an angle The same wrench instead receives a 60 N push at 40° to the handle. τ = (0.25)(60)(sin 40°) = 15(0.643) ≈ 9.65 N·m — noticeably less torque for the identical force, purely because of the less favorable angle.
Net torque and multiple forces When several forces act on an object, each contributes its own torque about a chosen pivot, and the net torque τnet is their sum, with counterclockwise contributions positive and clockwise negative — exactly parallel to how Unit 2 summed force components with a sign convention for direction.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Why does pushing a door directly toward its hinge produce zero torque, no matter how large the force?

τ = rF sin θ; when the force points straight at the pivot, θ = 0° and sin 0° = 0, eliminating torque regardless of force magnitude.

2. A 40 N force is applied perpendicular to a 0.4 m wrench handle. What torque does it produce?

τ = rF sin θ = (0.4)(40)(sin 90°) = 16 N·m.

3. Why are door handles placed far from the hinge rather than close to it?

Since τ = rF sin θ, maximizing r (placing the handle far from the hinge) maximizes torque for a given applied force, making rotation easier.

4. Fill in the blank: the rotational equivalent of force, given by τ = rF sin θ, is called ___.

Torque measures a force's effectiveness at producing rotation about a pivot, depending on both distance and angle.
