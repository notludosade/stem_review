1. A wheel starts at rest and accelerates at a constant α = 4 rad/s² for 3 s. What is its angular velocity at t = 3 s?

ω = ω_0 + αt = 0 + (4)(3) = 12 rad/s. (18 rad/s comes from mistakenly using the angle formula θ = ½αt² = ½(4)(9) = 18 instead.)

2. A carousel horse sits at r = 3 m from the center; another horse sits at r = 1.5 m. The carousel spins at ω = 2 rad/s. Answer both parts:

(a) How do the angular velocities of the two horses compare?

(b) Fill in the blank: the tangential speed of the outer horse (r = 3 m) is ___ m/s.

(a) A rigid body's every point completes the same angle in the same time, so ω (and α) are identical everywhere on it — only v_t = rω and a_t = rα depend on r. (b) v_t = rω = (3)(2) = 6 m/s.

3. A 0.5 m long wrench has an 80 N force applied perpendicular to the handle at its very end. What torque does this produce?

τ = rF sin θ = (0.5)(80)(sin 90°) = 40 N·m.

4. A mechanic applies a 50 N force to a 0.3 m breaker bar. Answer both parts:

(a) If the mechanic instead pushes at 25° to the bar (rather than perpendicular, 90°), what happens to the torque produced, compared to pushing perpendicular with the same force?

(b) Fill in the blank: if instead the mechanic pushes exactly along the bar's length (θ = 0°), the resulting torque is ___ N·m.

(a) τ = rF sin θ; since sin 25° is less than sin 90° = 1, the same force at 25° produces less torque than at 90°. (b) At θ = 0°, sin 0° = 0, so τ = (0.3)(50)(0) = 0 N·m — a force pointed straight along the bar toward or away from the pivot has no rotational leverage.

5. A solid disk has mass 6 kg and radius 0.4 m and rotates about its center. What is its moment of inertia?

I = ½MR² = ½(6)(0.4²) = ½(6)(0.16) = 0.48 kg·m². (0.96 kg·m² comes from using the hoop formula MR² instead; 1.2 kg·m² comes from forgetting to square R.)

6. A hoop and a solid sphere have the same mass M and radius R. Answer both parts:

(a) Which has the larger moment of inertia about its central axis, and why?

(b) Fill in the blank: writing I = cMR² with c = 1 for the hoop and c = ⅖ for the solid sphere, if M = 2 kg and R = 0.5 m, the sphere's moment of inertia is ___ kg·m².

(a) Since I depends on r², concentrating mass at the largest radius (the hoop) maximizes I compared to spreading mass throughout the interior (the sphere). (b) I = (⅖)(2)(0.5²) = (0.4)(2)(0.25) = 0.2 kg·m².

7. A net torque of 6 N·m is applied to a wheel with moment of inertia 3 kg·m². Answer both parts:

(a) What angular acceleration results?

(b) Fill in the blank: if this torque instead comes from a string wrapped around this wheel (acting as a pulley of radius 0.2 m) turning as a mass hangs from the string, the linear acceleration of the hanging mass is a = Rα. Using α from part (a), a = ___ m/s².

(a) α = τ_net/I = 6/3 = 2 rad/s². (b) At the point of contact, the string doesn't slip, so a = Rα = (0.2)(2) = 0.4 m/s².

8. A ceiling fan's blades are spinning up from rest under a constant net torque, while the fan's housing stays bolted motionlessly to the ceiling. Which of the following is true?

Full static equilibrium requires both ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0. A spinning-up object can satisfy one without the other — here the housing has ΣF = 0 but the blades have Στ ≠ 0 as long as they're speeding up.

9. A 5 m long seesaw pivots at its exact center. A 25 kg child sits 2 m from the pivot on the left side. (Use g = 9.8 m/s².) Answer both parts:

(a) Where must a 20 kg child sit on the right side to balance the seesaw?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the 20 kg child instead sits only 1 m from the pivot (not the balancing distance from part (a)), the magnitude of the net torque about the pivot is ___ N·m.

(a) Setting net torque to zero: (25)(9.8)(2) = (20)(9.8)(d) ⟹ 490 = 196d ⟹ d = 2.5 m (the g's cancel, so 25(2) = 20(d) ⟹ d = 2.5 m works directly too). (b) Left torque = (25)(9.8)(2) = 490 N·m. Right torque = (20)(9.8)(1) = 196 N·m. Net = 490 − 196 = 294 N·m, unbalanced toward the left child.

10. A wheel of radius 0.25 m rolls without slipping at v_cm = 10 m/s. What is its angular velocity?

ω = v_cm/R = 10/0.25 = 40 rad/s. (10 rad/s forgets R entirely; 2.5 rad/s multiplies R×v instead of dividing; 0.025 rad/s inverts the division as R/v.)
