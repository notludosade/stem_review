Rotational Kinematics

Every kinematic equation from Unit 1 has a rotational twin — just swap linear quantities for their angular counterparts.

← Unit 4, Lesson 5 Lesson 2 → Contents Glossary →

Angular Position, Velocity, and Acceleration Angular position θ (measured in radians) plays the role of position. Angular velocity ω = Δθ/Δt (rad/s) plays the role of velocity. Angular acceleration α = Δω/Δt (rad/s²) plays the role of acceleration. Every quantity describes rotation exactly as its linear counterpart describes straight-line motion.
Why radians, specifically A radian is defined so that arc length s = rθ — a direct, unit-free proportionality between angle and distance along the circle's edge. This clean relationship (no extra conversion factor) is exactly why radians, not degrees, are the natural unit for rotational kinematics: every formula linking linear and angular quantities (s = rθ, v = rω, atangential = rα) works only when θ, ω, and α are expressed in radians.
ω = ω0 + αt  θ = θ0 + ω0t + ½αt²  ω² = ω0² + 2αΔθ

These are Unit 1's three constant-acceleration kinematic equations with x → θ, v → ω, and a → α substituted directly — for constant angular acceleration, they hold in exactly the same form and are used exactly the same way.

Connecting Angular and Linear (Tangential) Quantities For a point at distance r from the rotation axis: vt = rω (tangential speed) and at = rα (tangential acceleration). Points farther from the axis move faster and accelerate more even though every point on a rigid rotating object shares the exact same ω and α.
Why every point shares ω but not v A rigid body rotates as one unit, so every point completes the same angle in the same time — ω and α are identical everywhere on the object. But vt = rω depends on r, so a point twice as far from the axis moves twice as fast even though both points share the same angular velocity. This is why a merry-go-round horse near the edge feels a much faster ride than one near the center, despite completing the same rotation in the same time.
Example: a spinning wheel A wheel starts at rest and speeds up to ω = 12 rad/s over 4 s at constant angular acceleration. α = Δω/Δt = 12/4 = 3 rad/s². Angle swept: θ = ω0t + ½αt² = 0 + ½(3)(16) = 24 rad (about 3.8 revolutions, since 1 revolution = 2π rad).
Example: tangential speed at the rim A 0.3 m radius wheel spins at ω = 20 rad/s. A point on its rim moves at vt = rω = (0.3)(20) = 6 m/s, while a point halfway to the center (r = 0.15 m) moves at only 3 m/s — half the speed, even though both points share ω = 20 rad/s.

1. On a rigid, rotating disk, which quantity is the same for every point on the disk, regardless of distance from the center?

Since the disk is rigid, every point completes the same angle in the same time, giving identical ω (and α) everywhere — but v_t = rω and a_t = rα both depend on r, differing point to point.

2. Why must angles be in radians when using v_t = rω?

The clean proportionality s = rθ (and its derivatives v_t = rω, a_t = rα) only holds when θ is measured in radians, since that's how the radian is defined in the first place.

3. A wheel of radius 0.5 m has an angular velocity of 8 rad/s. What is the tangential speed of a point on its rim?

v_t = rω = (0.5)(8) = 4 m/s.

4. Fill in the blank: the natural unit for angular position, in which arc length equals radius times angle with no conversion factor, is the ___.

The radian is defined by s = rθ, making it the unit that keeps every angular-linear relationship free of extra conversion constants.
