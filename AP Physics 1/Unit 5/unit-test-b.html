1. A merry-go-round starts at rest and accelerates at a constant α = 2 rad/s² for 5 s. What is its angular velocity at t = 5 s?

ω = ω_0 + αt = 0 + (2)(5) = 10 rad/s. (25 rad/s comes from mistakenly using the angle formula θ = ½αt² = ½(2)(25) = 25 instead.)

2. A spinning bicycle wheel has a point on its rim at r = 0.4 m and a point on a spoke one-quarter of the way out, at r = 0.1 m. The wheel spins at ω = 15 rad/s and is speeding up. Answer both parts:

(a) How do the angular accelerations of the two points compare?

(b) Fill in the blank: the tangential speed of the rim point (r = 0.4 m) is ___ m/s.

(a) A rigid body's every point completes the same angle in the same time, so ω and α are identical everywhere on it — only v_t = rω and a_t = rα depend on r. (b) v_t = rω = (0.4)(15) = 6 m/s.

3. A 0.6 m long lever has a 45 N force applied perpendicular to it at the far end. What torque does this produce?

τ = rF sin θ = (0.6)(45)(sin 90°) = 27 N·m.

4. A cyclist applies a 60 N force to a 0.17 m bicycle pedal crank arm. Answer both parts:

(a) If the cyclist pushes at 60° to the crank arm instead of perpendicular (90°), what happens to the torque produced, compared to pushing perpendicular with the same force?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the cyclist instead pushes exactly perpendicular (θ = 90°) with the same 60 N force, the resulting torque is ___ N·m (to one decimal place).

(a) τ = rF sin θ; since sin 60° is less than sin 90° = 1, the same force at 60° produces less torque than at 90°. (b) τ = (0.17)(60)(sin 90°) = (0.17)(60)(1) = 10.2 N·m.

5. A thin hoop has mass 3 kg and radius 0.5 m and rotates about its central axis. What is its moment of inertia?

I = MR² = (3)(0.5²) = (3)(0.25) = 0.75 kg·m². (0.375 kg·m² comes from mistakenly using the disk formula ½MR²; 1.5 kg·m² comes from forgetting to square R.)

6. A thin rod of mass M and length L can be rotated either about its center or about one end. Answer both parts:

(a) Which configuration has the larger moment of inertia, and why?

(b) Fill in the blank: using I_end = (1/3)ML², if M = 3 kg and L = 2 m, the moment of inertia about one end is ___ kg·m².

(a) I_center = (1/12)ML² while I_end = (1/3)ML² — four times larger, since about the end, essentially all of the rod's mass lies farther from the axis than it did about the center. (b) I_end = (1/3)(3)(2²) = (1/3)(3)(4) = 4 kg·m².

7. A net torque of 10 N·m is applied to a wheel with moment of inertia 5 kg·m². Answer both parts:

(a) What angular acceleration results?

(b) Fill in the blank: if this torque instead comes from a string wrapped around this wheel (acting as a pulley of radius 0.25 m) turning as a mass hangs from the string, the linear acceleration of the hanging mass is a = Rα. Using α from part (a), a = ___ m/s².

(a) α = τ_net/I = 10/5 = 2 rad/s². (b) At the point of contact, the string doesn't slip, so a = Rα = (0.25)(2) = 0.5 m/s².

8. A helicopter's main rotor blades are spinning up to takeoff speed while the helicopter's body sits stationary on the ground. Which of the following is true of the rotor blades during spin-up?

Full static equilibrium requires both ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0. A spinning-up object can satisfy one without the other — here the helicopter's body has ΣF = 0 but the blades have Στ ≠ 0 as long as they're speeding up.

9. A 6 m long seesaw pivots at its exact center. A 40 kg child sits 1.5 m from the pivot on the left side. (Use g = 9.8 m/s².) Answer both parts:

(a) Where must a 30 kg child sit on the right side to balance the seesaw?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the 30 kg child instead sits 2.5 m from the pivot (not the balancing distance from part (a)), the magnitude of the net torque about the pivot is ___ N·m.

(a) Setting net torque to zero: (40)(1.5) = (30)(d) ⟹ 60 = 30d ⟹ d = 2 m (the g's cancel out of both sides). (b) Left torque = (40)(9.8)(1.5) = 588 N·m. Right torque = (30)(9.8)(2.5) = 735 N·m. Net = 735 − 588 = 147 N·m, unbalanced toward the right child.

10. A wheel of radius 0.2 m rolls without slipping at v_cm = 10 m/s. What is its angular velocity?

ω = v_cm/R = 10/0.2 = 50 rad/s. (10 rad/s forgets R entirely; 2 rad/s multiplies R×v instead of dividing; 0.02 rad/s inverts the division as R/v.)
