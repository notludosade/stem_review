1. A hockey puck slides across a frictionless ice surface at constant velocity. According to Newton's first law, the net force acting on the puck is:

Constant velocity means zero acceleration, which by Newton's first law means zero net force — gravity and the ice's support force still act, but they cancel vertically, and nothing acts horizontally.

2. A swimmer pushes backward against the water with her hands (the action). Which force is the true Newton's third-law reaction?

A genuine action-reaction pair acts on two different objects, is the same type of force, and occurs simultaneously. The water pushing forward on the swimmer's hands is exactly this pair with the swimmer pushing backward on the water — buoyancy and drag are separate forces entirely.

3. A book rests without sliding on a table tilted at a slight angle (static friction holds it in place). Compared to the book's weight, the normal force from the table is:

Tilting the axes to match the incline splits gravity into mg sin θ along the surface and mg cos θ into the surface. The normal force only needs to balance the perpendicular component, mg cos θ — friction, not the normal force, is what handles the along-the-surface component.

4. A ball moves in a horizontal circle at constant speed on the end of a string. Which statement correctly describes its acceleration?

Velocity is a vector, and its direction is constantly changing even at fixed speed — that changing direction is itself an acceleration, pointing toward the circle's center. There is no real outward (centrifugal) acceleration.

5. Fill in the blank: If the distance between two masses is doubled, the gravitational force between them decreases to ___ of its original value (enter as a fraction like 1/4, or as a decimal).

Gravitational force follows an inverse-square law, F ∝ 1/r². Doubling r divides the force by 2² = 4, leaving one-fourth of the original value.

6. An 8 kg crate is pulled to the right with 40 N of force while a resistive force of 16 N acts to the left. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the net force on the crate?

(b) Fill in the blank: the crate's acceleration is ___ m/s².

(a) Net force = 40 − 16 = 24 N, to the right (the direction of the larger force). (b) a = ΣF/m = 24/8 = 3 m/s².

7. A 10 kg box sits on a floor with μs = 0.5 and μk = 0.3. Using g = 9.8 m/s², answer both parts:

(a) If a horizontal push of 40 N is applied, does the box begin to slide?

(b) Fill in the blank: the kinetic friction force that would act on the box if it were sliding is ___ N.

(a) Normal force N = mg = 10(9.8) = 98 N, so maximum static friction is μ_sN = 0.5(98) = 49 N. Since 40 N < 49 N, static friction simply matches the push and the box stays put. (b) Kinetic friction, if sliding, would be μ_kN = 0.3(98) = 29.4 N.

8. A 4 kg block rests on a frictionless incline at 30°, held in place by a rope parallel to the incline surface. Using g = 9.8 m/s², sin 30° = 0.5, and cos 30° ≈ 0.87, answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the tension in the rope is ___ N.

(b) What is the normal force on the block from the incline surface?

(a) Along the slope: T = mg sin θ = 4(9.8)(0.5) = 19.6 N. (b) Perpendicular to the slope: N = mg cos θ = 4(9.8)(0.87) ≈ 34.1 N — tilting the axes puts tension and normal force each entirely on one axis.

9. A 0.3 kg ball moves in a horizontal circle of radius 0.9 m at a constant speed of 3 m/s, held by a string. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the ball's centripetal acceleration?

(b) What is the tension in the string providing this centripetal force?

(a) a_c = v²/r = 3²/0.9 = 9/0.9 = 10 m/s². (b) The string is the only horizontal force, so it must supply the entire centripetal force: T = ma_c = 0.3(10) = 3 N (equivalently, mv²/r = 0.3(9)/0.9 = 3 N).

10. Two masses, 3 × 10⁵ kg and 2 × 10⁴ kg, are separated by 2 m. Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the gravitational force between them is approximately ___ N.

(b) If the distance between them were instead doubled to 4 m, the new gravitational force would be approximately:

(a) F = Gm₁m₂/r² = (6.67×10⁻¹¹)(3×10⁵)(2×10⁴)/2² ≈ 0.1 N. (b) Doubling r from 2 m to 4 m divides the force by 2² = 4, giving 0.1/4 = 0.025 N.
