Unit 1: Kinematics
Lesson 1

Position, Displacement, Velocity, and Speed

Four words physics uses much more precisely than everyday speech does — and the difference matters the moment an object doubles back on itself.

 Lesson 2

Acceleration and Motion Graphs

Position, velocity, and acceleration are three views of the same motion — and each graph's slope is the next one's height.

 Lesson 3

Kinematic Equations for Constant Acceleration

Four equations, five variables each — pick the one missing exactly the variable you don't have.

 Lesson 4

Free Fall

Vectors and Two-Dimensional Motion

Unit 2: Force and Translational Dynamics
Lesson 1

Newton's First Law and Inertia

Unit 1 described motion without asking why. Unit 2 opens with the most counterintuitive fact in mechanics: moving objects don't need a push to keep moving.

 Lesson 2

Newton's Second Law

The single most-used equation in this entire course: net force causes acceleration, in direct proportion, inversely scaled by mass.

 Lesson 3

Newton's Third Law and Force Pairs

Every force is one half of a pair — and the two halves never act on the same object, which is exactly why they don't cancel each other out.

 Lesson 4

Free-Body Diagrams and Equilibrium

The single most useful habit in all of dynamics: isolate one object, draw every force acting on it, and nothing else.

 Lesson 5

Friction

Every "frictionless" example so far was an idealization. Friction is the real-world force that opposes sliding — and it comes in two distinct flavors.

 Lesson 6

Uniform Circular Motion and Centripetal Force

Constant speed, constantly changing velocity: moving in a circle is accelerating, even when the speedometer never budges.

 Lesson 7

Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

The same force that drops an apple holds the Moon in orbit — one equation, one constant, for every pair of masses in the universe.

Unit 3: Work, Energy, and Power
Lesson 1

Work Done by a Force

Physics gives "work" a stricter meaning than everyday speech: no displacement, no work — no matter how hard you push or how long you hold something up.

 Lesson 2

The Work-Energy Theorem and Kinetic Energy

A second route to every problem Newton's second law solves — sometimes far shorter, especially when acceleration isn't constant.

 Lesson 3

Gravitational and Elastic Potential Energy

Energy doesn't only live in motion — it can be stored in position, waiting to be released.

 Lesson 4

Conservation of Mechanical Energy

When only conservative forces act, the total of kinetic and potential energy never changes — one of the most powerful shortcuts in all of physics.

 Lesson 5

Nonconservative Forces and Energy Dissipation

Mechanical energy isn't destroyed when friction acts — it's converted into a form this course doesn't track: heat.

 Lesson 6

Power

Work tells you how much energy is transferred. Power tells you how fast.

Unit 4: Linear Momentum
Lesson 1

Linear Momentum and Impulse

A second Newton's-second-law-flavored quantity — but one built for problems where force changes suddenly, like a collision.

 Lesson 2

Conservation of Momentum

In any isolated system, total momentum never changes — no matter how violent the collision inside it.

 Lesson 3

Elastic and Inelastic Collisions

Momentum is conserved in every collision. Kinetic energy is only conserved in the special ones.

 Lesson 4

Collisions in Two Dimensions

Momentum conservation still holds — it just has to hold separately in each direction.

 Lesson 5

Center of Mass

Every system of particles, no matter how complicated, has one special point that moves as if all the mass and all the external force were concentrated there.

Unit 5: Torque and Rotational Dynamics
Lesson 1

Rotational Kinematics

Every kinematic equation from Unit 1 has a rotational twin — just swap linear quantities for their angular counterparts.

 Lesson 2

Torque

The same push does nothing or everything, depending entirely on where and at what angle you apply it.

 Lesson 3

Moment of Inertia

Rotational inertia isn't just about how much mass an object has — it's about how far that mass sits from the axis.

 Lesson 4

Newton's Second Law for Rotation

τ = Iα is F = ma's rotational twin, and it's used exactly the same way.

 Lesson 5

Rotational Equilibrium

A sign hanging perfectly still needs more than balanced forces — it needs balanced torques too.

 Lesson 6

Rolling Motion

Rolling without slipping links an object's translation and its rotation with a single constraint equation.

Unit 6: Energy and Momentum of Rotating Systems
Lesson 1

Rotational Kinetic Energy

A spinning object has kinetic energy even if its center of mass isn't going anywhere at all.

 Lesson 2

Rolling Objects and Energy Conservation

Why a solid ball always beats a hoop down the same ramp — proven directly from energy conservation, no race required.

 Lesson 3

Angular Momentum

Momentum's rotational twin — a quantity that torque changes, exactly as force changes linear momentum.

 Lesson 4

Conservation of Angular Momentum

Why a spinning figure skater pulls her arms in to spin faster — no torque required, just the shape of L = Iω.

 Lesson 5

Torque, Angular Momentum, and Applications

Closing the loop between torque and angular momentum — and putting conservation to work on real spinning systems.

Unit 7: Oscillations
Lesson 1

Simple Harmonic Motion

A whole new kind of motion — not constant velocity, not constant acceleration, but a restoring force that always pulls back toward equilibrium.

 Lesson 2

The Spring-Mass System

A mass on a spring is the textbook example of SHM — and its period reveals a surprising independence from amplitude.

 Lesson 3

The Pendulum

A swinging pendulum is SHM in disguise — but only if you don't swing it too far.

 Lesson 4

Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion

Total energy stays fixed throughout an oscillation — only its split between kinetic and potential ever changes.

 Lesson 5

Damping and Resonance

Real oscillators lose energy to friction — and can gain it back dramatically if pushed at just the right rhythm.

Unit 8: Fluids
Lesson 1

Density and Pressure

Two quantities that let us describe fluids without tracking every individual molecule.

 Lesson 2

Pascal's Principle

 Lesson 3

Buoyancy and Archimedes' Principle

 Lesson 4

Fluid Dynamics and Continuity

 Lesson 5

Bernoulli's Equation

Energy conservation applied to flowing fluid — explaining why fast-moving air exerts less pressure, and how wings generate lift.
