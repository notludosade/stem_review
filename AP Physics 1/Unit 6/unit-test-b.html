1. A grinding wheel has moment of inertia I = 0.4 kg·m² and spins at ω = 20 rad/s. What is its rotational kinetic energy?

KE_rot = ½Iω² = ½(0.4)(400) = 80 J. (160 J comes from forgetting the ½; 8 J comes from computing L = Iω = 8 instead; 4 J comes from omitting the ω² and using ½Iω = ½(0.4)(20) = 4.)

2. A hoop (I = MR²) of mass 2 kg and radius 0.3 m rolls without slipping at v_cm = 6 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) Which expression correctly gives this hoop's total kinetic energy as it rolls?

(b) Fill in the blank: using ω = v_cm/R = 20 rad/s and I = MR² = 0.18 kg·m², this hoop's total kinetic energy is ___ J.

(a) A rolling object's total kinetic energy is the sum of translational KE of the center of mass and rotational KE about the center of mass — neither term alone captures the whole. (b) KE_trans = ½(2)(6²) = 36 J; KE_rot = ½(0.18)(20²) = 36 J; total = 36 + 36 = 72 J — for a hoop (c = 1), rotational and translational KE always come out equal.

3. A hoop (c = 1, rolling without slipping) and a frictionless block (c = 0, sliding without any rotation) are released from rest at the same height on identical ramps. Which reaches the bottom with greater speed?

v_cm = √[2gh/(1+c)] is largest when c is smallest; the sliding block's c = 0 gives v = √(2gh), the fastest possible case, since none of its potential energy is diverted into spinning up rotational motion the way the hoop's is.

4. A solid sphere (I = ⅖MR², so c = ⅖) is released from rest at height h = 2.5 m on a ramp and rolls without slipping. (Use g = 9.8 m/s².) Answer both parts:

(a) Which of the following correctly explains why the sphere's final speed doesn't depend on its mass or radius?

(b) Fill in the blank: using v_cm = √[2gh/(1+c)], the sphere's speed at the bottom is ___ m/s (to two decimal places).

(a) mgh = ½mv_cm²(1+c) once I = cMR² and ω = v_cm/R are substituted — mass cancels from both sides, leaving v_cm dependent only on g, h, and the shape constant c. (b) v_cm = √[2(9.8)(2.5)/1.4] = √(49/1.4) = √35 ≈ 5.92 m/s.

5. Fill in the blank: a wheel with I = 0.25 kg·m² spins at ω = 8 rad/s. Its angular momentum is ___ kg·m²/s.

L = Iω = (0.25)(8) = 2 kg·m²/s.

6. A planet, treated as a point mass of 3 kg for this problem, orbits at radius 2 m with tangential speed 6 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) Even though this planet isn't physically spinning about the point it orbits, why can its angular momentum still be written as L = mrv?

(b) Fill in the blank: this planet's angular momentum about the point it orbits is ___ kg·m²/s.

(a) Substituting I = mr² and ω = v/r into L = Iω gives L = mr²(v/r) = mrv exactly, so no separate calculation of I and ω is needed for a point mass in circular motion. (b) L = mrv = (3)(2)(6) = 36 kg·m²/s.

7. A diver leaves the board with arms and legs extended, giving I_i = 8 kg·m² at ω_i = 2 rad/s. Tucking into a ball reduces this to I_f = 2 kg·m² (once airborne, no external torque acts about the center of mass). Answer both parts:

(a) Why does the diver's angular velocity increase upon tucking, given that no net external torque acts once airborne?

(b) Fill in the blank: using L_i = L_f, the diver's angular velocity while tucked is ___ rad/s.

(a) With zero net external torque, L = Iω must remain fixed, so shrinking I mandates a proportionally larger ω. (b) I_iω_i = I_fω_f ⟹ (8)(2) = (2)ω_f ⟹ ω_f = 8 rad/s.

8. A person pushes on a merry-go-round to spin it up, then lets go and steps away entirely. During which phase is the merry-go-round's total angular momentum conserved?

While the person is pushing, they exert an external torque on the merry-go-round, so L is not conserved during that phase; once they let go, the net external torque drops to (approximately) zero, and L is conserved from that point forward.

9. Net torque equals the rate of change of angular momentum, τ_net = ΔL/Δt. Which linear-motion relationship shares this exact same structure (net cause equals the rate of change of a momentum-type quantity)?

F_net = Δp/Δt is the linear statement that net force equals the rate of change of linear momentum — τ_net = ΔL/Δt is exactly this idea transplanted to rotation, with force and momentum replaced by torque and angular momentum.

10. A planet orbits the Sun in an elliptical path. At perihelion (closest approach) it is at r_p = 2.0×10¹⁰ m moving at v_p = 5.0×10⁴ m/s, purely tangential to its position vector. At aphelion (farthest point) it is at r_a = 5.0×10¹⁰ m, also moving purely tangentially. Answer both parts:

(a) The planet moves faster at perihelion than at aphelion. Why?

(b) Fill in the blank: using conservation of angular momentum, the planet's speed at aphelion is ___ m/s.

(a) With zero torque about the Sun, L = mrv stays fixed throughout the orbit, so a smaller r (perihelion) forces a larger v to compensate, and a larger r (aphelion) allows a smaller v. (b) m r_p v_p = m r_a v_a ⟹ v_a = r_p v_p / r_a = (2.0×10¹⁰)(5.0×10⁴)/(5.0×10¹⁰) = 2.0×10⁴ = 20000 m/s.
