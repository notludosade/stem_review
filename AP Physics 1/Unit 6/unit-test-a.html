1. A flywheel has moment of inertia I = 2 kg·m² and spins at ω = 8 rad/s. What is its rotational kinetic energy?

KE_rot = ½Iω² = ½(2)(8²) = ½(2)(64) = 64 J. (128 J comes from forgetting the ½; 16 J comes from computing L = Iω = 16 instead; 8 J comes from omitting the ω² and using ½Iω = ½(2)(8) = 8.)

2. A solid sphere (I = ⅖MR²) of mass 3 kg and radius 0.2 m rolls without slipping at v_cm = 5 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) Which expression correctly gives this sphere's total kinetic energy as it rolls?

(b) Fill in the blank: using ω = v_cm/R = 25 rad/s and I = ⅖MR² = 0.048 kg·m², this sphere's total kinetic energy is ___ J.

(a) A rolling object's total kinetic energy is the sum of translational KE of the center of mass and rotational KE about the center of mass — neither term alone captures the whole. (b) KE_trans = ½(3)(5²) = 37.5 J; KE_rot = ½(0.048)(25²) = 15 J; total = 37.5 + 15 = 52.5 J.

3. A solid sphere (c = ⅖) and a solid disk (c = ½) of different masses and radii are released from rest at the same height on identical ramps, both rolling without slipping. Which reaches the bottom with greater speed?

v_cm = √[2gh/(1+c)] depends only on the shape constant c — the sphere's smaller c (⅖ vs ½) means a larger share of its potential energy converts to translational KE, giving it the higher final speed regardless of mass or radius.

4. A solid disk (I = ½MR², so c = ½) is released from rest at height h = 2 m on a ramp and rolls without slipping. (Use g = 9.8 m/s².) Answer both parts:

(a) Which of the following correctly explains why the disk's final speed doesn't depend on its mass or radius?

(b) Fill in the blank: using v_cm = √[2gh/(1+c)], the disk's speed at the bottom is ___ m/s (to two decimal places).

(a) mgh = ½mv_cm²(1+c) once I = cMR² and ω = v_cm/R are substituted — mass cancels from both sides, leaving v_cm dependent only on g, h, and the shape constant c. (b) v_cm = √[2(9.8)(2)/1.5] = √(39.2/1.5) = √26.13 ≈ 5.11 m/s.

5. Fill in the blank: a disk with I = 0.6 kg·m² spins at ω = 4 rad/s. Its angular momentum is ___ kg·m²/s.

L = Iω = (0.6)(4) = 2.4 kg·m²/s.

6. A 2 kg point mass moves in a circle of radius 0.5 m at a constant speed of 4 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) Treating this object as a point mass, why does its angular momentum reduce to the simple form L = mrv rather than requiring a separate calculation of I and ω?

(b) Fill in the blank: this point mass's angular momentum about the center of the circle is ___ kg·m²/s.

(a) Substituting I = mr² and ω = v/r into L = Iω gives L = mr²(v/r) = mrv exactly, so no separate calculation of I and ω is needed for a point mass. (b) L = mrv = (2)(0.5)(4) = 4 kg·m²/s.

7. A skater spins with arms extended at I_i = 5 kg·m², ω_i = 3 rad/s, then pulls her arms in to I_f = 2 kg·m² (the ice exerts negligible external torque). Answer both parts:

(a) Why does her angular velocity increase as she pulls her arms in, given that no net external torque acts on her?

(b) Fill in the blank: using L_i = L_f, her new angular velocity is ___ rad/s.

(a) With zero net external torque, L = Iω must remain fixed, so shrinking I mandates a proportionally larger ω. (b) I_iω_i = I_fω_f ⟹ (5)(3) = (2)ω_f ⟹ ω_f = 7.5 rad/s.

8. Which of the following is the precise condition under which a system's total angular momentum remains constant over time?

From τ_net·Δt = ΔL, zero net external torque directly implies ΔL = 0 — the system's total angular momentum is conserved even if I and ω individually change internally, as when a skater pulls in her arms.

9. Which of the following linear relationships is τ_net = ΔL/Δt the direct rotational analog of?

Net torque equals the rate of change of angular momentum, exactly as net force equals the rate of change of linear momentum (F_net = Δp/Δt) — mass and velocity are replaced by moment of inertia and angular velocity throughout.

10. A planet orbits the Sun in an elliptical path. At perihelion (closest approach) it is at r_p = 1.0×10¹¹ m moving at v_p = 3.0×10⁴ m/s, purely tangential to its position vector. At aphelion (farthest point) it is at r_a = 2.0×10¹¹ m, also moving purely tangentially. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is the planet's angular momentum about the Sun conserved throughout its orbit?

(b) Fill in the blank: using conservation of angular momentum (L = mrv, since velocity is purely tangential at both points), the planet's speed at aphelion is ___ m/s.

(a) Torque requires a force component perpendicular to the position vector; gravity here points straight at the pivot (θ = 0°), so τ = rF sin θ = 0, and L is conserved. (b) m r_p v_p = m r_a v_a ⟹ v_a = r_p v_p / r_a = (1.0×10¹¹)(3.0×10⁴)/(2.0×10¹¹) = 1.5×10⁴ = 15000 m/s — slower at the larger radius, exactly Kepler's second law.
