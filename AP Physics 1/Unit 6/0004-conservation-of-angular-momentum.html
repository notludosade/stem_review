Conservation of Angular Momentum

Why a spinning figure skater pulls her arms in to spin faster — no torque required, just the shape of L = Iω.

← Lesson 3 Lesson 5 → Contents Glossary →

Conservation of Angular Momentum If τnet,ext = 0 on a system, its total angular momentum is conserved: Li = Lf, or Iiωi = Ifωf. This follows directly from the angular impulse-momentum theorem (Lesson 3): if τnetΔt = ΔL and τnet = 0, then ΔL = 0.
Why this is the direct rotational parallel to Unit 4 This mirrors linear momentum conservation exactly (Unit 4, Lesson 2): there, zero net external force meant p was conserved; here, zero net external torque means L is conserved. Both are consequences of Newton's laws — one linear, one rotational — and both apply to isolated systems where no outside agent exerts the relevant influence (force or torque).
Example: the spinning skater A skater spinning with arms extended has Ii = 4 kg·m² at ωi = 2 rad/s. Pulling her arms in reduces her moment of inertia to If = 1 kg·m². Since no external torque acts (ignoring the small friction of skate on ice), L is conserved: Iiωi = Ifωf ⟹ (4)(2) = (1)ωf ⟹ ωf = 8 rad/s. Pulling her mass closer to the rotation axis quadruples her spin rate.
Why pulling arms in speeds up the spin Moment of inertia depends on how far mass sits from the axis (I = Σmr² — Unit 5, Lesson 3), so pulling the arms inward directly shrinks I. Since L = Iω must stay fixed with no external torque, a smaller I forces a larger ω to compensate — the skater isn't doing anything to "add" rotation, she's just redistributing her own mass closer to the axis and letting conservation do the rest.
Example: a diver's tuck A diver leaving the board with arms and legs extended has a larger I and spins slowly; tucking into a ball drastically reduces I, spinning up the somersault rate, exactly as in the skater example — then extending again before entry increases I back and slows the spin for a clean, controlled entry.

1. Angular momentum is conserved for a system whenever:

From τ_net·Δt = ΔL, zero net external torque directly implies ΔL = 0, regardless of whether I or ω individually change.

2. A skater spinning at 3 rad/s with I = 6 kg·m² pulls her arms in to reach I = 2 kg·m². What is her new angular velocity?

I_iω_i = I_fω_f ⟹ (6)(3) = (2)ω_f ⟹ ω_f = 9 rad/s.

3. Why does pulling the arms inward increase a spinning skater's angular velocity, given that no external torque acts?

With L fixed by conservation, I and ω are inversely related — shrinking I (via I = Σmr² and smaller r) mandates a larger ω.

4. Fill in the blank: angular momentum conservation, like linear momentum conservation, applies to an ___ system with no net external torque (or force).

Just as isolated systems conserve linear momentum (Unit 4, Lesson 2), they conserve angular momentum when free of external torque.
