1. A crane holds a steel beam stationary in midair for 30 seconds before setting it down. While the beam is held stationary, the work done by the crane's cable on the beam is:

W = Fd cos θ requires a displacement. With d = 0 while the beam is held stationary, the work done is exactly zero, no matter how large the cable's tension force is or how long it's held.

2. An object's speed is cut in half, while its mass stays the same. Its kinetic energy becomes:

KE = ½mv² depends on speed squared, so halving v multiplies KE by (½)² = ¼.

3. Why does the formula for elastic potential energy include a factor of ½, while gravitational potential energy (mgh) does not?

Work equals the area under a force-vs-distance graph. Gravity's constant force gives a rectangular area (mgh); a spring's linearly increasing force (F = kx) gives a triangular area, ½ × x × kx = ½kx².

4. A roller coaster car (assume no friction) starts from rest at the top of a hill of height h. Regardless of the hill's exact shape — a steep drop or a gradual curve — the car's speed at the bottom depends on:

Since gravity is conservative and there's no friction, mechanical energy conservation gives v = √(2gh) — the shape and length of the path never enter the calculation, only the net height dropped.

5. Fill in the blank: Motor P completes a task in 5 s, and Motor Q completes the identical task (the same total work) in 15 s. Motor P is ___ times as powerful as Motor Q.

Power is work divided by time. For the same work, Motor P's time is one-third of Motor Q's, so Motor P's power is 3 times as large: 15/5 = 3.

6. A sled is pulled by a rope at 45° above horizontal with 100 N of tension, moving the sled 10 m horizontally across snow. Using cos 45° ≈ 0.71, answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the work done by the tension is ___ J.

(b) If the same rope instead pulled at 0° (perfectly horizontal) over the same 10 m displacement, the work done would be:

(a) W = Fd cos θ = 100(10)(0.71) = 710 J. (b) At 0°, cos 0° = 1, the maximum possible value — pulling exactly along the direction of motion gives the largest work for a given force and displacement: 100(10)(1) = 1000 J.

7. A 3 kg cart starts at 4 m/s. A net force does 30 J of work on it as it moves. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the cart's initial kinetic energy?

(b) Fill in the blank: the cart's final speed is ___ m/s.

(a) KE_i = ½(3)(4²) = 24 J. (b) By the work-energy theorem, KE_f = KE_i + W_net = 24 + 30 = 54 J. Solving ½(3)v² = 54 gives v² = 36, so v = 6 m/s.

8. Answer both parts, which use two different scenarios:

(a) A 5 kg object is lifted 3 m straight up. Fill in the blank: the increase in its gravitational potential energy is ___ J.

(b) A spring with spring constant k = 600 N/m is stretched 0.05 m from its natural length. The elastic potential energy stored is:

(a) ΔPE = mgh = 5(9.8)(3) = 147 J. (b) PE_elastic = ½kx² = ½(600)(0.05²) = ½(600)(0.0025) = 0.75 J.

9. A 3 kg block starts from rest at the top of a 1.8 m high ramp. Using g = 10 m/s² for clean numbers, answer both parts:

(a) If the ramp is frictionless, the block's speed at the bottom is:

(b) In reality, the ramp has friction, and the block actually reaches the bottom at 4 m/s instead. How much energy was dissipated by friction?

(a) v = √(2gh) = √(2·10·1.8) = √36 = 6 m/s. (b) ME_i = mgh = 3(10)(1.8) = 54 J. ME_f = ½(3)(4²) = 24 J. Energy dissipated: 54 − 24 = 30 J.

10. A motor does 8000 J of work lifting a load over 4 seconds, with the lifting force acting parallel to the load's motion, at a constant final speed of 4 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the motor's average power?

(b) Fill in the blank: using P = Fv at that constant speed of 4 m/s, the force being applied is ___ N.

(a) P = W/t = 8000/4 = 2000 W. (b) From P = Fv: F = P/v = 2000/4 = 500 N.
