Power

Work tells you how much energy is transferred. Power tells you how fast.

← Lesson 5 Unit 4, Lesson 1 → Contents Glossary →

Power, defined Power is the rate at which work is done, or equivalently the rate at which energy is transferred: P = W/t. Power is measured in watts (W), where 1 watt = 1 joule/second.
Why two engines can do the same work but not the same job Two cars can accelerate the same mass to the same final speed, doing the exact same amount of work — but one that does it in 3 seconds is far more powerful than one that takes 10 seconds. Work only measures the total energy transferred; power captures the practically important difference of how quickly.

Power in terms of force and velocity

Since W = Fd cos θ (Lesson 1), and velocity is displacement over time, dividing both sides by time gives an equivalent, often more directly useful form:

P = Fv cos θ

where v is the object's speed and θ is the angle between the force and the velocity. For a force acting parallel to motion (the most common case in this course), this simplifies to P = Fv.

Why this form is often more practical Many real problems (a car engine, a motor lifting a load at constant speed) specify an instantaneous speed rather than a total time and distance. P = Fv gives the instantaneous power directly from the force and the speed at that moment, without needing to know how long the motion lasts or how far it travels.
Example: average power from work and time A motor does 6000 J of work lifting a load over 4 seconds. Average power: P = W/t = 6000/4 = 1500 W = 1.5 kW.
Example: power from force and speed A car engine delivers a forward force of 2000 N while cruising at a constant 25 m/s. Power output: P = Fv = 2000(25) = 50,000 W = 50 kW.
Example: power to lift against gravity A 60 kg hiker climbs a hill, gaining 100 m of height in 200 seconds at constant speed. The work done against gravity is W = mgh = 60(9.8)(100) = 58,800 J, so the average power output is P = 58800/200 = 294 W — for comparison, a healthy adult can sustain roughly this much power output over many minutes.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Two motors lift identical loads to the same height, doing the same amount of work. Motor A takes 5 s; Motor B takes 20 s. Which statement is correct?

Power is work divided by time; with equal work, less time means greater power, so Motor A is four times as powerful as Motor B.

2. A car engine exerts a constant 1500 N forward force while traveling at a constant 20 m/s. Its power output is:

P = Fv = 1500(20) = 30,000 W.

3. Which formula for power is generally more convenient when you know an object's instantaneous speed but not the total time or distance traveled?

P = Fv cos θ uses only force and instantaneous speed (and the angle between them), avoiding the need for total elapsed time or distance.

4. Fill in the blank: power is measured in units called ___, equal to one joule per second.

The watt (W) is the SI unit of power, defined as 1 J/s.
