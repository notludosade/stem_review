1. A person carries a suitcase at constant height across a level airport terminal, walking at constant velocity. How much work does the person's upward supporting force do on the suitcase?

W = Fd cos θ. The supporting force is vertical while the displacement is horizontal, so θ = 90° and cos θ = 0 — zero work, no matter how tiring the walk feels.

2. If an object's speed is tripled while its mass stays the same, its kinetic energy becomes:

KE = ½mv² depends on speed squared, so tripling v multiplies KE by 3² = 9.

3. A student sets the reference height (h = 0) at the ceiling of a room instead of the floor. Compared to using the floor as reference, the gravitational PE calculated for a book on a table:

The reference level can be placed anywhere — it shifts every PE value by a constant amount, but any physically meaningful result depends only on ΔPE, which is unaffected by this choice.

4. Two frictionless slides begin at the same height but have different shapes — one straight, one curved. A ball released from rest at the top of each slide reaches the bottom with:

Gravity is a conservative force, so with no friction, mechanical energy conservation gives v = √(2gh) — mass and path shape both cancel out, leaving only the height drop to matter.

5. Fill in the blank: Two motors lift identical crates to the same height, doing equal amounts of work. Motor X takes 4 s and Motor Y takes 12 s. Motor X is ___ times as powerful as Motor Y.

Power is work divided by time. For the same work, Motor X's time is one-third of Motor Y's, so Motor X's power is 3 times as large: 12/4 = 3.

6. A crate is pulled by a rope at 60° above horizontal with 80 N of tension, moving the crate 15 m horizontally. Using cos 60° = 0.5, answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the work done by the tension is ___ J.

(b) If instead the same 80 N tension were applied exactly perpendicular (90°) to the crate's 15 m horizontal displacement, the work done by that tension would be:

(a) W = Fd cos θ = 80(15)(0.5) = 600 J. (b) At 90°, cos 90° = 0, so the work done is exactly zero — only the component of force along the direction of motion ever contributes.

7. A 4 kg cart starts at 2 m/s. A net force does 64 J of work on it as it moves. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the cart's initial kinetic energy?

(b) Fill in the blank: the cart's final speed is ___ m/s.

(a) KE_i = ½(4)(2²) = 8 J. (b) By the work-energy theorem, KE_f = KE_i + W_net = 8 + 64 = 72 J. Solving ½(4)v² = 72 gives v² = 36, so v = 6 m/s.

8. Answer both parts, which use two different scenarios:

(a) A 3 kg object is lifted 2.5 m straight up. Fill in the blank: the increase in its gravitational potential energy is ___ J.

(b) A spring with spring constant k = 400 N/m is compressed 0.1 m from its natural length. The elastic potential energy stored is:

(a) ΔPE = mgh = 3(9.8)(2.5) = 73.5 J. (b) PE_elastic = ½kx² = ½(400)(0.1²) = ½(400)(0.01) = 2 J.

9. A 2 kg block starts from rest at the top of a 5 m high ramp. Using g = 10 m/s² for clean numbers, answer both parts:

(a) If the ramp is frictionless, the block's speed at the bottom is:

(b) In reality, the ramp has friction, and the block actually reaches the bottom at 8 m/s instead. How much energy was dissipated by friction?

(a) v = √(2gh) = √(2·10·5) = √100 = 10 m/s. (b) ME_i = mgh = 2(10)(5) = 100 J. ME_f = ½(2)(8²) = 64 J. Energy dissipated: 100 − 64 = 36 J.

10. A motor does 4500 J of work lifting a load over 3 seconds, with the lifting force acting straight up, parallel to the load's motion, at a constant final speed of 5 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the motor's average power?

(b) Fill in the blank: using P = Fv at that constant speed of 5 m/s, the force being applied is ___ N.

(a) P = W/t = 4500/3 = 1500 W. (b) From P = Fv: F = P/v = 1500/5 = 300 N.
