# AP Physics 1 Resources

## Knowledge

- [Book: _University Physics Volume 1_ — OpenStax](https://openstax.org/details/books/university-physics-volume-1)
  Free, legally-hosted, calculus-based text, but its worked examples and conceptual explanations for mechanics (Units 1-7 here) are excellent and algebra-friendly if the calculus is skipped. Use for: the primary worked narrative and figures behind most mechanics lessons.
- [College Board: AP Physics 1 Course and Exam Description](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-physics-1)
  The official framework this course's unit structure and scope follow directly (2025-26 revision: 8 units, Fluids moved in as Unit 8). Use for: confirming exactly what's in and out of scope for a given unit.
- [The Physics Classroom](https://www.physicsclassroom.com/)
  Free, algebra-based conceptual explanations written specifically for an introductory audience, with excellent plain-language framing for topics like circular motion and rotational analogues. Use for: a second explanation when OpenStax's calculus-adjacent framing isn't landing.
- [Khan Academy: AP Physics 1](https://www.khanacademy.org/science/ap-physics-1)
  Free video walkthroughs aligned to the same College Board framework, with practice problems. Use for: an alternate spoken explanation of any topic, and extra practice.
- [HyperPhysics](http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/hframe.html)
  Georgia State's concept-map reference site — extremely fast lookup for a single formula or relationship once a topic is already partly understood. Use for: quick reference, not a first explanation.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/AP_Physics](https://reddit.com/r/AP_Physics)
  Active, exam-focused community for AP Physics 1/2/C students trading problems and FRQ strategy.
- [r/HomeworkHelp](https://reddit.com/r/HomeworkHelp) (physics-tagged posts)
  Broader but high-traffic backup for a specific stuck problem when r/AP_Physics is quiet.
