1. A 0.3 kg hockey puck moving at 10 m/s is stopped by a goalie's glove in 0.05 s. What average force does the glove exert on the puck?

Δp = m(v_f − v_i) = 0.3(0 − 10) = −3 kg·m/s. F = Δp/Δt = −3/0.05 = −60 N, magnitude 60 N.

2. A 6 kg cart moving at 4 m/s collides with a stationary 3 kg cart. After the collision, the 6 kg cart moves at 1 m/s in the same direction. What is the 3 kg cart's velocity after the collision?

(6)(4) + (3)(0) = (6)(1) + (3)v_2f ⟹ 24 = 6 + 3v_2f ⟹ v_2f = 6 m/s.

3. A 2000 kg truck moving at 10 m/s rear-ends a stationary 3000 kg truck, and the two lock together. What is their common velocity after the collision?

(2000)(10) + (3000)(0) = (2000+3000)v_f ⟹ 20000 = 5000v_f ⟹ v_f = 4 m/s.

4. Fill in the blank: even in two dimensions, momentum conservation for a colliding pair of objects follows from Newton's ___ law, since the collision forces are always equal and opposite along every axis.

Newton's third law guarantees the collision forces (and their x- and y-components) are equal and opposite, so the impulses — and hence momentum components — cancel in every direction, not just as a combined vector.

5. A 4 kg mass sits at x = 2 m, and a 2 kg mass sits at x = 8 m. What is the system's center of mass?

x_cm = [(4)(2) + (2)(8)] / (4+2) = (8 + 16)/6 = 24/6 = 4 m — closer to the heavier mass, as expected from a mass-weighted average.

6. A stuntman falls and lands on a soft airbag mat instead of concrete. Answer both parts:

(a) Given the same change in momentum upon landing, why does the airbag mat reduce injury risk?

(b) Fill in the blank: An 80 kg stuntman reaches 6 m/s just before impact and comes to rest on the airbag over 0.5 s. The average force exerted by the airbag is ___ N.

(a) F = Δp/Δt; with Δp fixed by the fall, a longer stopping time directly reduces the average force. (b) Δp = 80(0 − 6) = −480 kg·m/s. F = Δp/Δt = −480/0.5 = −960 N, magnitude 960 N.

7. A 50 kg person, standing at rest on a frictionless skateboard, throws a 5 kg medicine ball forward at 6 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) What must be true about the person's velocity after the throw, according to conservation of momentum?

(b) Fill in the blank: the person's recoil speed is ___ m/s (magnitude, to one decimal place).

(a) Starting from zero total momentum, the two final momenta must be equal in magnitude and opposite in direction — but since the masses differ, the velocities are not equal in magnitude. (b) 0 = (5)(6) + 50v ⟹ v = −30/50 = −0.6 m/s, so recoil speed is 0.6 m/s.

8. A 5 kg cart moving at 6 m/s collides with a stationary 5 kg cart. After the collision, the first cart is at rest and the second moves at 6 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) Is this collision elastic or inelastic?

(b) Fill in the blank: the amount of kinetic energy dissipated in this collision is ___ J.

(a)/(b) KE_i = ½(5)(6²) = 90 J. KE_f = ½(5)(0²) + ½(5)(6²) = 0 + 90 = 90 J. Since kinetic energy is fully conserved, this equal-mass "velocity swap" collision is elastic and exactly 0 J was dissipated.

9. A 3 kg puck moving at 6 m/s in the +x direction strikes a stationary 3 kg puck on a frictionless surface. After the collision, puck 1 moves off at 5 m/s at 37° above the x-axis (sin 37° ≈ 0.6). Answer both parts:

(a) Which statement about this collision's kinetic energy is correct in general?

(b) Fill in the blank: using conservation of y-momentum (initially zero), puck 2's y-velocity component after the collision is ___ m/s (include the sign).

(a) Momentum's independence by component is a hard requirement, but kinetic energy conservation is a separate, stronger condition that only holds for elastic collisions and contributes one equation (it's a scalar). (b) Puck 1's y-momentum is (3)(5)(sin 37°) = (3)(5)(0.6) = 9 kg·m/s. Since initial y-momentum was zero, puck 2's y-momentum must be −9 kg·m/s, so v_2y = −9/3 = −3 m/s.

10. An ice skater is spinning while holding a ball; she suddenly throws the ball outward, an internal force pair between her and the ball. Answer both parts:

(a) What happens to the path of the center of mass of the skater-plus-ball system at the instant of the throw?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the skater-plus-ball system has a total mass of 60 kg and a total momentum of 30 kg·m/s just before the throw, the center of mass's velocity at that instant is ___ m/s.

(a) Internal forces (the throw) cancel out of the center-of-mass equation entirely — only external forces determine its motion. (b) Since p_total = m_total·v_cm, v_cm = 30/60 = 0.5 m/s.
