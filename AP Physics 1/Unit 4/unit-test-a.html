1. A 0.5 kg ball moving at 12 m/s is stopped by a glove in 0.02 s. What average force does the glove exert on the ball?

Δp = m(v_f − v_i) = 0.5(0 − 12) = −6 kg·m/s. F = Δp/Δt = −6/0.02 = −300 N, magnitude 300 N.

2. A 5 kg cart moving at 8 m/s collides with a stationary 10 kg cart. After the collision, the 5 kg cart moves at 2 m/s in the same direction. What is the 10 kg cart's velocity after the collision?

(5)(8) + (10)(0) = (5)(2) + (10)v_2f ⟹ 40 = 10 + 10v_2f ⟹ v_2f = 3 m/s.

3. A 1200 kg car moving at 20 m/s rear-ends a stationary 1800 kg car, and the two lock together. What is their common velocity after the collision?

(1200)(20) + (1800)(0) = (1200+1800)v_f ⟹ 24000 = 3000v_f ⟹ v_f = 8 m/s.

4. Fill in the blank: in a two-dimensional collision, momentum conservation must be applied separately to each ___ of the momentum vector (for example, x and y).

Momentum is a vector, so its x- and y-components are each conserved independently, giving two separate one-dimensional equations instead of one combined one.

5. A 3 kg mass sits at x = 1 m, and a 5 kg mass sits at x = 5 m. What is the system's center of mass?

x_cm = [(3)(1) + (5)(5)] / (3+5) = (3 + 25)/8 = 28/8 = 3.5 m — closer to the heavier mass, as expected from a mass-weighted average.

6. A boxer "rides the punch" by pulling their head back as it lands, extending the contact time. Answer both parts:

(a) Given the same change in momentum, what effect does the longer contact time have on the average force experienced?

(b) Fill in the blank: A 0.4 kg fist is stopped from 8 m/s to 0 m/s. Riding the punch extends the contact time from 0.01 s to 0.04 s. The average force decreases by a factor of ___.

(a) F = Δp/Δt; with Δp fixed, a longer Δt directly reduces the average force. (b) Δp = 0.4(8) = 3.2 kg·m/s either way. F_1 = 3.2/0.01 = 320 N; F_2 = 3.2/0.04 = 80 N. The time increased by a factor of 4, so the force dropped by that same factor: 320/80 = 4.

7. A 75 kg astronaut, initially at rest in deep space, throws a 3 kg tool away at 10 m/s to propel themselves toward their ship. Answer both parts:

(a) What does conservation of momentum say about the resulting motion of the astronaut and the tool?

(b) Fill in the blank: the astronaut's recoil speed is ___ m/s (magnitude, to one decimal place).

(a) Starting from zero total momentum, the two final momenta must sum to zero, so they're equal in magnitude and opposite in direction — but since masses differ, velocities are not equal in magnitude. (b) 0 = (3)(10) + 75v ⟹ v = −30/75 = −0.4 m/s, so recoil speed is 0.4 m/s.

8. A 4 kg cart moving at 5 m/s collides with a stationary 2 kg cart. After the collision, the 4 kg cart moves at 3 m/s and the 2 kg cart moves at 4 m/s (momentum checks out: 20 = 12 + 8). Answer both parts:

(a) Is this collision elastic or inelastic?

(b) Fill in the blank: the amount of kinetic energy dissipated in this collision is ___ J.

(a)/(b) KE_i = ½(4)(5²) = 50 J. KE_f = ½(4)(3²) + ½(2)(4²) = 18 + 16 = 34 J. Momentum conservation alone never guarantees kinetic energy conservation — it must always be checked separately. 50 − 34 = 16 J was dissipated, so the collision is inelastic.

9. A 2 kg puck moving at 5 m/s in the +x direction strikes a stationary 2 kg puck on a frictionless surface. After the collision, puck 1 moves off at 4 m/s at 30° above the x-axis. Answer both parts:

(a) In this 2D elastic collision, how many independent equations does momentum conservation contribute, and how many does kinetic energy conservation contribute?

(b) Fill in the blank: using conservation of y-momentum (initially zero), puck 2's y-velocity component after the collision is ___ m/s (include the sign).

(a) Momentum's x- and y-components are each conserved independently (two equations), while kinetic energy is a single scalar quantity (one equation). (b) Puck 1's y-momentum is (2)(4)(sin 30°) = 4 kg·m/s. Since initial y-momentum was zero, puck 2's y-momentum must be −4 kg·m/s, so v_2y = −4/2 = −2 m/s.

10. A 2 kg toy rocket coasts through the air (only gravity acting externally) with total momentum 10 kg·m/s directed horizontally, when an internal charge splits it into three fragments. Answer both parts:

(a) What happens to the center of mass's trajectory at the instant of the explosion?

(b) Fill in the blank: immediately before (and after) the explosion, the center of mass's horizontal velocity is ___ m/s.

(a) Internal forces (the explosion) cancel out of the center-of-mass equation entirely; only gravity, acting before and after, determines the parabolic path. (b) Since p_total = m_total·v_cm, v_cm = 10/2 = 5 m/s.
