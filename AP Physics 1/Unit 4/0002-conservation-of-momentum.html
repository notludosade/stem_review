Conservation of Momentum

In any isolated system, total momentum never changes — no matter how violent the collision inside it.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

Conservation of Momentum For an isolated system (no net external force acting on it), total momentum is conserved: ptotal, i = ptotal, f. In a two-object collision, this means m1v1i + m2v2i = m1v1f + m2v2f.
Why this follows directly from Newton's third law During a collision between objects 1 and 2, Newton's third law (Unit 2, Lesson 3) guarantees the force object 1 exerts on object 2 is equal and opposite to the force object 2 exerts on object 1, at every instant. Since both forces act over the exact same time interval Δt, the impulses are equal and opposite: Δp1 = −Δp2. Adding these, Δp1 + Δp2 = 0 — the total momentum of the two-object system doesn't change. Momentum conservation isn't a new, separate law; it's Newton's third law applied to a pair of colliding objects.
Why "isolated system" matters Momentum conservation applies to the total momentum of a system only when no external net force acts on it. Internal forces (the collision forces between the two objects) always cancel by Newton's third law, but an external force (like friction from the ground, or gravity if the motion isn't horizontal) would add extra impulse to the system and break the conservation — this is why most introductory momentum problems specify a frictionless surface or a purely horizontal collision.
Example: a two-cart collision A 3 kg cart moving at 4 m/s collides with a stationary 5 kg cart. After the collision, the 3 kg cart moves at 0.5 m/s in the same direction. Conservation of momentum: (3)(4) + (5)(0) = (3)(0.5) + (5)v2f ⟹ 12 = 1.5 + 5v2f ⟹ v2f = 2.1 m/s.
Example: recoil A 60 kg person standing at rest on frictionless ice throws a 2 kg ball forward at 8 m/s. Since the system starts at rest, total momentum stays zero: 0 = (2)(8) + (60)vperson ⟹ vperson = −16/60 ≈ −0.27 m/s — the person recoils backward, exactly the same principle behind rocket propulsion (Unit 2, Lesson 3).

1. Conservation of momentum in a two-object collision follows most directly from:

The equal-and-opposite forces of a Newton's third law pair act over the same time interval, producing equal-and-opposite impulses that sum to zero for the system.

2. A 60 kg person at rest on frictionless ice throws a 3 kg object forward at 10 m/s. What is the person's recoil speed?

0 = (3)(10) + 60v ⟹ v = −30/60 = −0.5 m/s, so recoil speed is 0.5 m/s backward.

3. Momentum conservation for a system requires that:

Internal forces always cancel by Newton's third law; conservation of total momentum requires the absence of a net external force, regardless of whether kinetic energy is conserved.

4. Fill in the blank: momentum is conserved for a system with no net external force acting on it, called an ___ system.

An isolated system has no net external force, so internal forces (which always cancel in pairs by Newton's third law) are the only ones present, keeping total momentum constant.
