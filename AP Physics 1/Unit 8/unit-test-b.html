1. A metal sample has mass 356 g and volume 40 cm³. What is its density in kg/m³?

Converting to base units: ρ = m/V = 0.356 kg / (40 × 10⁻⁶ m³) = 0.356/0.00004 = 8900 kg/m³.

2. A tank of oil (ρ = 900 kg/m³) is open to the atmosphere (P₀ = 101,325 Pa) and is 4 m deep. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the gauge pressure due to the oil alone (not counting atmospheric pressure) at the bottom is ___ Pa.

(b) What is the total (absolute) pressure at the bottom?

(a) The oil column's own contribution is ρgh = (900)(9.8)(4) = 35,280 Pa. (b) Total pressure is P₀ + ρgh = 101,325 + 35,280 = 136,605 ≈ 136,600 Pa.

3. Which everyday device relies directly on Pascal's principle — pressure transmitted undiminished throughout an enclosed fluid?

Hydraulic brakes, like hydraulic lifts, work because a pressure change applied at the pedal's small piston transmits undiminished to the larger pistons at each wheel.

4. A hydraulic press has a small piston of area 0.002 m² and a large piston of area 0.6 m². Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the ratio A₂/A₁ between the large and small piston areas is ___.

(b) A 40 N force on the small piston produces what force on the large piston?

(a) A₂/A₁ = 0.6/0.002 = 300. (b) F₂ = F₁(A₂/A₁) = 40(300) = 12,000 N.

5. A block of volume 0.008 m³ is fully submerged in seawater (ρ = 1025 kg/m³). What is the buoyant force on it (to the nearest 10 N)?

F_b = ρ_fluid·V·g = (1025)(0.008)(9.8) ≈ 80.4 N — depending only on the displaced seawater's weight, not the block's own material.

6. A raft has mass 45 kg and volume 0.06 m³, placed in water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³). Answer both parts:

(a) Does the raft float or sink?

(b) Fill in the blank: to float in equilibrium, the raft must displace ___ m³ of water.

(a) Average density = 45/0.06 = 750 kg/m³, less than water's 1000 kg/m³, so the raft floats. (b) Floating equilibrium requires the buoyant force to equal the raft's weight, so it displaces V = m/ρ_water = 45/1000 = 0.045 m³ of water.

7. Fill in the blank: squeezing a garden hose's opening with your thumb makes the water shoot out faster, since the volume flow rate must stay the same — a direct consequence of the ___ equation, A₁v₁ = A₂v₂.

This is the continuity equation: reducing the exit area without changing the supplied volume flow rate forces the exit speed to increase to compensate.

8. An artery of cross-sectional area 0.0004 m² carries blood at 0.25 m/s. It narrows to area 0.0001 m² at a point of stenosis. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the volume flow rate of blood through the artery is ___ m³/s.

(b) What is the blood speed at the narrowed section?

(a) Q = A₁v₁ = 0.0004 × 0.25 = 0.0001 m³/s. (b) v₂ = Q/A₂ = 0.0001/0.0001 = 1 m/s — a fourfold speed increase matching the fourfold area decrease.

9. In Bernoulli's equation, P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant, if a fluid flows horizontally (h constant) and slows down, its pressure:

With height constant, P + ½ρv² = constant, so a decrease in speed must be balanced by an increase in pressure — the opposite of the speeding-up case.

10. Water flows horizontally through a wide pipe at 1.5 m/s under pressure 180,000 Pa, then enters a narrow section where its speed increases to 6 m/s (height unchanged, water density ρ = 1000 kg/m³). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: using Bernoulli's equation, the pressure in the narrow section is ___ Pa (to the nearest 1,000 Pa).

(b) This pressure drop as the water speeds up through the narrow section is an example of:

(a) 180,000 + ½(1000)(1.5)² = P₂ + ½(1000)(6)² ⟹ 180,000 + 1,125 = P₂ + 18,000 ⟹ P₂ = 163,125 ≈ 163,000 Pa. (b) With height constant, Bernoulli's equation trades pressure for kinetic energy as speed increases — energy per volume is conserved, not violated.
