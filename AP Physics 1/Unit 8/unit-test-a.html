1. A block has mass 900 g and volume 300 cm³. What is its density in kg/m³?

Converting to base units: 0.9 kg / (300 × 10⁻⁶ m³) = 0.9/0.0003 = 3000 kg/m³.

2. A lake is 5 m deep, with water density ρ = 1000 kg/m³ and atmospheric pressure P₀ = 101,325 Pa at the surface. Answer both parts:

(a) Why does pressure increase with depth in the lake?

(b) Fill in the blank: the pressure at the bottom of the lake is ___ Pa (to the nearest 1,000 Pa).

(a) A column of fluid's weight must be supported by the pressure difference across it, which is exactly where P = P₀ + ρgh comes from. (b) P = 101,325 + (1000)(9.8)(5) = 101,325 + 49,000 = 150,325 ≈ 150,000 Pa.

3. Fill in the blank: the principle stating that a pressure change applied anywhere in an enclosed, incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished to every other point in the fluid and its container walls is called ___.

This is Pascal's principle — the basis for hydraulic lifts, hydraulic brakes, and every other enclosed-fluid pressure system.

4. A hydraulic lift has a small piston of area 0.005 m² and a large piston of area 0.4 m². Answer both parts:

(a) A 150 N force is applied to the small piston. What force appears on the large piston?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the large piston needs to rise 2 cm, the small piston must move ___ cm.

(a) F₂ = F₁(A₂/A₁) = 150(0.4/0.005) = 150(80) = 12,000 N. (b) Incompressibility requires A₁d₁ = A₂d₂, so d₁ = d₂(A₂/A₁) = 2(80) = 160 cm — the small piston travels 80 times farther, matching the area ratio.

5. A block of volume 0.03 m³ is fully submerged in water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³). What is the buoyant force on it?

F_b = ρ_fluid·V·g = (1000)(0.03)(9.8) = 294 N, regardless of what the block itself is made of.

6. A block has mass 8 kg and volume 0.01 m³, placed in water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the block's average density is ___ kg/m³.

(b) Does the block float or sink, and why?

(a) ρ = m/V = 8/0.01 = 800 kg/m³. (b) An object floats when its average density is less than the fluid's — 800 kg/m³ is less than water's 1000 kg/m³, so the block floats.

7. The continuity equation A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ for an incompressible fluid in a pipe follows directly from:

For an incompressible fluid in steady flow, the volume passing any cross-section per second (Av) must be the same everywhere, since fluid can neither pile up nor vanish along the pipe.

8. Water flows through a pipe of area 0.006 m² at 3 m/s, entering a narrower section of area 0.0015 m². Answer both parts:

(a) What is the flow speed in the narrower section?

(b) Fill in the blank: the volume flow rate through the pipe is ___ m³/s.

(a) By continuity, v₂ = A₁v₁/A₂ = (0.006 × 3)/0.0015 = 0.018/0.0015 = 12 m/s. (b) Volume flow rate is Av = 0.006 × 3 = 0.018 m³/s, the same at every point along the pipe.

9. Bernoulli's equation, P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant along a streamline, is best understood as:

Each term in Bernoulli's equation has units of energy per volume, and their sum staying constant mirrors ME = KE + PE = constant, just reframed per unit volume for a fluid.

10. A large open tank has a small hole at depth h = 1.25 m below the surface, with g = 9.8 m/s². Answer both parts:

(a) Using Torricelli's law, what is the speed of water exiting the hole?

(b) Fill in the blank: this result, v = √(2gh), matches the speed of an object that has ___ from the same height h.

(a) v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 1.25) = √24.5 ≈ 4.95 m/s. (b) Bernoulli's equation at the hole reduces to ρgh = ½ρv², the same energy-conservation statement as mgh = ½mv² for an object that has fallen freely from height h.
