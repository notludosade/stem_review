1. A boat heads directly across a river at 4 m/s (relative to the water) while the current flows downstream at 3 m/s. What is the boat's resultant speed relative to the shore?

The components are perpendicular (4 and 3), so the resultant is √(4²+3²) = √25 = 5 m/s — a 3-4-5 right triangle.

2. A car accelerates from rest at 4 m/s² for 3 s, then travels at the resulting constant velocity for 5 more seconds. What is the car's total displacement over the full 8 s?

Stage 1: Δx₁ = ½(4)(3²) = 18 m, reaching v = 12 m/s. Stage 2: Δx₂ = 12(5) = 60 m. Total = 18 + 60 = 78 m.

3. Two blocks, 3 kg and 2 kg, are connected by a string and pulled across a frictionless floor by a horizontal 20 N force applied to the 3 kg block. What is the acceleration of the system?

Total mass = 5 kg, a = ΣF/m = 20/5 = 4 m/s² (treat both blocks as one system since they move together).

4. A ball on a string moves in a vertical circle. At the very top of the loop, which equation correctly relates the forces to the centripetal requirement (taking toward the center, i.e. downward, as positive)?

At the top, both tension and gravity point toward the center (downward), so they add together to supply the centripetal force: T + mg = mv²/r.

5. A spring-loaded toy dart gun does 2.4 J of work on a 0.05 kg dart, starting from rest. What is the dart's launch speed?

W = ΔKE = ½mv² → 2.4 = ½(0.05)v² → v² = 96 → v ≈ 9.8 m/s (energy methods handle a spring's non-constant force easily, unlike Newton's laws directly).

6. Fill in the blank: a 1 kg block slides from rest down a frictionless 2 m high ramp onto a rough horizontal floor (μk = 0.2), then slides to a stop. Using g = 10 m/s², it slides ___ m on the rough floor before stopping.

KE at the bottom of the ramp = mgh = 1(10)(2) = 20 J. Friction force = μkmg = 0.2(1)(10) = 2 N. Distance = KE/friction = 20/2 = 10 m.

7. Two ice skaters, mass 60 kg and 40 kg, push off from rest against each other. If the 60 kg skater moves backward at 2 m/s, what is the 40 kg skater's speed?

Total momentum starts at zero (both at rest). 60(2) = 120 = 40v ⟹ v = 3 m/s, in the opposite direction from the 60 kg skater.

8. Fill in the blank: a 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary 1 kg cart. Their combined final velocity is ___ m/s.

Momentum is conserved: 2(3) = 6 = (2+1)v ⟹ v = 2 m/s.

9. A 3 m long uniform beam of weight 100 N is supported at its left end and by a rope at its right end, held horizontal. What is the tension in the rope?

A uniform beam's weight acts at its center (1.5 m from the left support). Sum of torques about the left support = 0: T(3) = 100(1.5) ⟹ T = 50 N.

10. Fill in the blank: a net torque of 12 N·m acts on a wheel with moment of inertia 4 kg·m². The wheel's angular acceleration is ___ rad/s².

τ = Iα ⟹ α = τ/I = 12/4 = 3 rad/s².

11. A 40 kg ice skater spinning at ω_i = 4 rad/s with I_i = 3 kg·m² catches a ball, increasing her effective moment of inertia to I_f = 3.5 kg·m². Why is the skater-plus-ball system's angular momentum conserved during the catch?

Any forces during the catch are internal to the skater-ball system, so they exert no net external torque, and L is conserved through the catch.

12. Fill in the blank: a net torque of 3 N·m acts on a wheel for 5 s, starting from rest. The wheel's angular momentum at the end of this interval is ___ kg·m²/s.

τΔt = ΔL, so ΔL = (3)(5) = 15 kg·m²/s; since the wheel starts at rest, its angular momentum after 5 s is 15 kg·m²/s.

13. Both a mass on a spring and a small-angle pendulum exhibit simple harmonic motion because, in both cases:

Despite completely different physical origins (Hooke's law vs. gravity's tangential pull), both reduce to a = −ω²x — the defining equation of SHM.

14. Fill in the blank: a 0.25 kg mass oscillates on a spring with spring constant k = 16 N/m and amplitude 0.12 m. The maximum speed v_max of the mass is ___ m/s (to 2 decimal places).

v_max = A√(k/m) = 0.12√(16/0.25) = 0.12√64 = 0.12(8) = 0.96 m/s.

15. Water flows through a horizontal pipe that narrows from area 0.02 m² to 0.005 m². The wide section carries water at 1 m/s. What is the speed in the narrow section?

By continuity, v₂ = v₁(A₁/A₂) = 1(0.02/0.005) = 4 m/s.

16. A cube of side length 0.2 m and mass 6 kg floats in water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³). This submerged fraction of the cube's volume equals:

At equilibrium, buoyant force equals weight: ρ_water·V_sub·g = mg, so dividing by V_total shows the submerged fraction always equals ρ_avg/ρ_water — here 750/1000 = 0.75.
