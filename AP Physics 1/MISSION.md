# Mission: AP Physics 1

## Why
The first real physics course: how forces, energy, and motion actually work, following the College Board's AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based course framework (revised for the 2025-26 exam, which added Fluids as Unit 8). Builds the mechanics foundation every later physics course — AP Physics 2, and any calculus-based physics beyond this site — assumes already exists.

## Success looks like
- Can describe motion using position, velocity, and acceleration graphs, and solve constant-acceleration and projectile-motion problems
- Can draw a free-body diagram for any situation and apply Newton's three laws, including friction, circular motion, and gravitation
- Can apply conservation of energy and the work-energy theorem to solve problems without needing forces directly
- Can apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explain when kinetic energy is and isn't conserved
- Can extend every translational concept (kinematics, Newton's second law, energy, momentum) to rotational motion, and connect the two via rolling-without-slipping
- Can describe simple harmonic motion quantitatively for springs and pendulums, and apply the basic laws of fluid statics and dynamics

## Constraints
- Algebra/trigonometry-based, not calculus-based — matches the real AP Physics 1 course, which explicitly avoids calculus notation even though this site's own Precalculus and AP Calculus BC courses exist
- Assumes Precalculus (vectors, trigonometry, function graphs) — does not assume AP Calculus BC
- Follows the College Board's 2025-26 unit structure and ordering (8 units, Fluids last) rather than an older or reorganized sequence, so a student cross-checking against real AP materials sees the same shape
- Diagrams are static, labeled SVG figures (free-body diagrams, motion graphs, ray-analogue force diagrams) in the style already established by Precalculus and Real Analysis A; a small number of lessons reuse the existing `assets/interactive.js` primitives (`mountParametricCurve` for projectile trajectories, `mountPointOnCurve`-style dragging for oscillation graphs) where a draggable exploration is worth the cost, not as a rule for every lesson

## Out of scope
- Calculus-based mechanics (AP Physics C: Mechanics) — no derivatives, integrals, or differential equations
- Electricity, magnetism, thermodynamics, optics, and modern physics — deferred to AP Physics 2
- Lab work and hands-on experimental design (AP Physics 1 is partly a lab course in a physical classroom) — this course covers the conceptual and quantitative content, not lab technique
