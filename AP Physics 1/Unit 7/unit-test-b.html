1. Which of the following best describes the restoring force required for simple harmonic motion?

SHM requires a restoring force directly proportional to displacement and opposite in sign to it, F = −kx — this is the defining condition, not merely a force that happens to point toward equilibrium.

2. Fill in the blank: a mass in simple harmonic motion has frequency f = 5 Hz. Its period T is ___ s.

Period is the reciprocal of frequency: T = 1/f = 1/5 = 0.2 s.

3. Two identical mass-spring systems are released from rest, one displaced 3 cm and the other displaced 9 cm. Which statement is true?

T = 2π√(m/k) contains no amplitude term at all — a larger displacement means proportionally more restoring force and speed at every point, and the two effects exactly cancel, leaving the period unchanged.

4. The formula T = 2π√(L/g) for a pendulum's period relies on the approximation:

The true restoring force involves sin θ, not θ; only for small angles does sin θ ≈ θ, making the motion approximately SHM and the period formula accurate.

5. A door closer lets a door swing shut, overshoot slightly, swing back, overshoot less, and gradually settle at rest over several oscillations. This is an example of:

A system that still oscillates back and forth while its amplitude shrinks over time is underdamped, distinct from critical damping (no oscillation, fastest return) and overdamping (no oscillation, slower return).

6. A 0.8 kg mass on a spring completes 5 oscillations in 4 seconds. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the period T of one oscillation is ___ s.

(b) What is the spring constant k?

(a) T = total time / number of oscillations = 4/5 = 0.8 s. (b) Solving T = 2π√(m/k) for k: k = m(2π/T)² = 0.8(2π/0.8)² ≈ 49.3 N/m.

7. A pendulum clock design uses a pendulum of length L = 2.45 m at a location where g = 9.8 m/s². Answer both parts:

(a) What is its period (to 2 decimal places)?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the pendulum length were increased to 4 times its original length (mass unchanged), the new period would be approximately ___ times the original.

(a) T = 2π√(L/g) = 2π√(2.45/9.8) = 2π√0.25 = 2π(0.5) = π ≈ 3.14 s. (b) Since T ∝ √L, quadrupling L multiplies T by √4 = 2.

8. A 0.4 kg mass oscillates on a spring with k = 32 N/m and amplitude A = 0.15 m. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the total mechanical energy E of the system is ___ J.

(b) At the position x = A (maximum displacement), the kinetic energy of the mass is:

(a) E = ½kA² = ½(32)(0.15)² = 0.36 J. (b) At x = A the mass is momentarily at rest, so kinetic energy is zero and all of E = ½kA² is potential.

9. A 1.0 kg mass oscillates on a spring with k = 40 N/m and amplitude A = 0.25 m. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the maximum speed vmax is ___ m/s (to 2 decimal places).

(b) What is the mass's speed at x = 0.20 m?

(a) vmax = A√(k/m) = 0.25√(40/1) = 0.25√40 ≈ 1.58 m/s. (b) v = √[(k/m)(A² − x²)] = √[40(0.0625 − 0.04)] = √0.9 ≈ 0.95 m/s.

10. A bridge has a natural oscillation period of 3 s. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: wind gusts arriving in rhythm with the bridge's own natural period can build dangerously large amplitude — a phenomenon called ___.

(b) Which historical event is most often cited as a real-world example of this phenomenon?

(a) This buildup from matching driving and natural frequency is called resonance. (b) Wind gusts near the Tacoma Narrows Bridge's natural frequency drove increasingly large oscillations, leading to its collapse in 1940.
