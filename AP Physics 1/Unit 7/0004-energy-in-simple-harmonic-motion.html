Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion

Total energy stays fixed throughout an oscillation — only its split between kinetic and potential ever changes.

← Lesson 3 Lesson 5 → Contents Glossary →

Total Mechanical Energy in SHM For a spring-mass system, total mechanical energy is E = ½kA², constant throughout the motion (assuming no friction — Unit 3, Lesson 4's conservation of mechanical energy). At any position x, E = ½kx² + ½mv², so the speed at any point can be found from v = ±√[(k/m)(A² − x²)].
Where E = ½kA² comes from At maximum displacement (x = A), the mass is momentarily at rest (v = 0), so all energy is elastic potential energy (Unit 3, Lesson 3): E = ½kA² + 0 = ½kA². Since total mechanical energy is conserved throughout the motion (no nonconservative forces acting), this same value of E holds at every other point in the cycle too — it's simply evaluated at the one point (maximum displacement) where the calculation is easiest.
Why speed is maximum at equilibrium and zero at the extremes At x = 0 (equilibrium), all energy is kinetic: ½mvmax² = ½kA² ⟹ vmax = A√(k/m) — the fastest point in the motion. At x = ±A (the turning points), all energy is potential and v = 0 — the object momentarily stops before reversing direction, exactly as a ball thrown upward momentarily stops at its peak (Unit 1). Between these extremes, energy continuously trades between the two forms while their sum stays fixed.
Example: finding speed at a given position A 0.5 kg mass oscillates with A = 0.1 m on a spring with k = 20 N/m. Total energy: E = ½(20)(0.1)² = 0.1 J. At x = 0.05 m: ½(20)(0.05)² + ½(0.5)v² = 0.1 ⟹ 0.025 + 0.25v² = 0.1 ⟹ v² = 0.3 ⟹ v ≈ 0.548 m/s.
Example: maximum speed Using the same system, vmax = A√(k/m) = 0.1√(20/0.5) = 0.1√40 ≈ 0.632 m/s at x = 0 — faster than at any other point, as expected since all the system's energy is kinetic there.

1. In SHM with no friction, total mechanical energy:

With no nonconservative forces, mechanical energy is conserved throughout SHM, only exchanging between kinetic and potential forms.

2. At which point in a spring-mass system's oscillation is its speed greatest?

At equilibrium, all energy is kinetic (zero potential energy since x = 0), making this the point of maximum speed.

3. Why is a mass on a spring momentarily at rest exactly at its maximum displacement, x = A?

E = ½kA² + ½mv² with E = ½kA² fixed means v = 0 exactly when x = A, since all energy is potential at that instant.

4. Fill in the blank: a mass oscillating on a spring reaches its maximum speed at ___, where all its energy is kinetic.

At equilibrium (x = 0), potential energy is zero, so all the system's fixed total energy is kinetic, giving maximum speed.
