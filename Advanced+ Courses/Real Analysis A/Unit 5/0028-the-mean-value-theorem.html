The Mean Value Theorem

Tilt Rolle's Theorem's picture, and the "flat spot" becomes "a tangent parallel to the secant line" — one algebraic trick away.

← Lesson 27 Lesson 29 → Contents Glossary →

AP Calc BC used the Mean Value Theorem constantly — to justify that average and instantaneous rates of change must coincide somewhere. This lesson proves it, by reducing it directly to Rolle's Theorem from Lesson 27.

Theorem — Mean Value Theorem Let f be continuous on [a, b] and differentiable on (a, b). Then there exists c ∈ (a, b) with f′(c) = (f(b) − f(a)) / (b − a).

Proof, by reduction to Rolle's Theorem

Proof Define g(x) = f(x) − f(a) − [(f(b) − f(a))/(b − a)]·(x − a) — f(x) with the secant line from (a, f(a)) to (b, f(b)) subtracted off. g is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b) (a difference of such functions, Unit 4 Lesson 21 and Unit 5 Lesson 26). Check the endpoints: g(a) = f(a) − f(a) − 0 = 0, and g(b) = f(b) − f(a) − (f(b) − f(a)) = 0. So g(a) = g(b), and Rolle's Theorem applies: there is c ∈ (a,b) with g′(c) = 0. Since g′(x) = f′(x) − (f(b)−f(a))/(b−a), this gives f′(c) = (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a). ∎
Why subtracting the secant line is exactly the right move g is built specifically so that its "tilt" cancels out the secant line's slope — g(a) = g(b) = 0 by construction, turning MVT into a direct instance of Rolle's Theorem. This is the standard analysis move of reducing a new theorem to one already proved, by transforming the problem until the old theorem's hypotheses are met exactly.

The tangent line at c is parallel to the secant line joining the endpoints.

1. What does the Mean Value Theorem conclude?

MVT guarantees the instantaneous rate matches the average rate somewhere in the interior — the generalization of Rolle's flat-spot conclusion.

2. What is the purpose of the auxiliary function g(x) = f(x) − f(a) − [(f(b)−f(a))/(b−a)](x−a) in the proof?

g is engineered precisely so its endpoint values match, satisfying Rolle's Theorem's hypothesis exactly.

3. Why is the Mean Value Theorem considered a generalization of Rolle's Theorem, rather than an unrelated result?

When f(a) = f(b), the average rate of change is 0, and MVT's conclusion collapses exactly to Rolle's f′(c) = 0.

4. Fill in the blank: the auxiliary function in the proof is built by subtracting the ___ line joining (a,f(a)) and (b,f(b)) from f.

Secant is the line whose slope equals the average rate of change — exactly what's subtracted to make Rolle's Theorem apply.

5. Fill in the blank: the Mean Value Theorem is proved by direct reduction to ___, applied to a carefully constructed auxiliary function.

Rolle's Theorem, from Lesson 27, is exactly the special case MVT reduces to via the secant-subtracted auxiliary function.
