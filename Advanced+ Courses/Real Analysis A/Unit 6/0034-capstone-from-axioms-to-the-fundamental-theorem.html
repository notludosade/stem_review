Capstone — From Axioms to the Fundamental Theorem

The whole course, traced as one dependency chain: how eleven field and order axioms end up proving that differentiation undoes integration.

← Lesson 33 Contents Glossary →

This closing lesson adds no new theorems. Instead, it retraces the entire logical chain built across 34 lessons — showing that every rigorous fact used in Lesson 33's proof of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus ultimately traces back to nothing but the field axioms, the order axioms, and the single extra axiom that makes ℝ different from ℚ: completeness.

The dependency chain

Unit 1 — Foundations The field and order axioms (Lesson 2) define arithmetic and ordering on ℝ. The Completeness Axiom (Lesson 5) — every nonempty bounded-above set has a least upper bound — is the one axiom ℚ lacks and ℝ has. Every later existence theorem in this course (a limit exists, a supremum exists, a maximum is attained) ultimately cashes out to an invocation of completeness.
Unit 2 — Sequences and Limits The ε-N definition of a sequence limit (Lesson 7) replaces "gets close to" with a checkable inequality. The Monotone Convergence Theorem (Lesson 10) is completeness applied directly: an increasing bounded sequence converges to its sup. The Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem (Lesson 12) — every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence — is proved by repeated bisection, itself powered by completeness, and becomes the engine behind the Cauchy Criterion (Lesson 13) and, much later, the Extreme Value Theorem.
Unit 3 — Topology of the Real Line Open and closed sets (Lesson 14) and compactness (Lesson 16) give vocabulary for "well-behaved" subsets of ℝ. The Heine-Borel Theorem (Lesson 17) shows compact means exactly closed-and-bounded, tying the sequential definition to Bolzano-Weierstrass. Connectedness (Lesson 18) — intervals are exactly the connected subsets of ℝ — is the fact the Intermediate Value Theorem will need.
Unit 4 — Limits and Continuity of Functions The ε-δ functional limit (Lesson 19) extends Unit 2's sequence limit to functions. Continuity (Lesson 20) is the case where the limit matches the function's own value. The Intermediate Value Theorem (Lesson 22) cashes out Unit 3's connectedness; the Extreme Value Theorem (Lesson 23) cashes out Unit 3's compactness via Bolzano-Weierstrass; Uniform Continuity (Lesson 24) — one δ working across an entire compact domain — becomes essential machinery for Unit 6.
Unit 5 — Differentiation The derivative (Lesson 25) is Unit 4's functional limit, applied to the difference quotient — no new machinery at all. Rolle's Theorem (Lesson 27) cashes out the Extreme Value Theorem. The Mean Value Theorem (Lesson 28) reduces to Rolle's Theorem via an auxiliary function. Its corollary — zero derivative implies constant (Lesson 29) — sits quietly until Lesson 33, where it becomes the single fact that makes FTC Part 2 work.
Unit 6 — Riemann Integration Upper and lower sums (Lesson 30) use completeness again, this time to guarantee sup and inf on every subinterval. The Riemann integral (Lesson 31) is defined as the point where the sup of lower sums meets the inf of upper sums — and continuous-implies-integrable leans on Unit 4's uniform continuity plus the Extreme Value Theorem. Properties of the integral (Lesson 32) all reduce to Cauchy-Criterion-style estimates. And the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Lesson 33) closes the loop: Part 1 uses continuity directly; Part 2 needs Unit 5's zero-derivative-implies-constant corollary to finish the job.
Why this chain is the actual point of the course Every theorem in AP Calc BC — that a positive derivative means increasing, that continuous functions attain a max, that the integral and the antiderivative are the same computation — was, in that course, either asserted or justified by a picture. Nothing in this course was asserted without a proof, and every one of those proofs, traced far enough back, uses only the eleven axioms of Lesson 2 and the one completeness axiom of Lesson 5. That is what it means for a body of mathematics to be built on rigorous foundations rather than intuition.
