1. Which of the following sets is NOT open?

At x = 1, every interval (1 − ε, 1 + ε) contains points less than 1, which lie outside [1, 3) no matter how small ε is — so [1, 3) is not open, while all three other sets are.

2. Fill in the blank: a set O ⊆ ℝ is open exactly when its complement ℝ \ O is ___.

Since a set F is closed exactly when ℝ \ F is open, taking F = ℝ \ O shows the same relation runs the other way: O is open exactly when its complement is closed.

3. What are the limit points of A = (2, 4)?

Every point of the closed interval [2, 4], including the endpoints 2 and 4 which are not themselves in A, is a limit point of the open interval (2, 4).

4. Why is ℝ itself not compact?

ℝ is closed but unbounded — every subsequence of n² is itself unbounded and hence divergent, so no convergent subsequence exists at all.

5. Fill in the blank: a set E is disconnected if it can be written as E = A ∪ B for two nonempty ___ sets A and B.

Separated sets — where neither set's closure meets the other — are exactly what a connected set cannot be split into.

6. Let A = [1, 4). Answer both parts:

(a) What is the closure Ā of A = [1, 4)?

(b) Fill in the blank: since 4 is a limit point of A = [1,4) not contained in A, adding it to A produces the closure — the smallest ___ set containing A.

(a) 1 is already in A, but 4 is a limit point missing from A, so the closure is [1,4]. (b) The closure is always the smallest closed set containing the original set.

7. Consider the open sets (1, 3), (2, 4), and (3, 5). Answer both parts:

(a) What is (1, 3) ∪ (2, 4) ∪ (3, 5)?

(b) Fill in the blank: since a ___ of open sets is always open, and (1, 5) is indeed an open interval, this matches the theorem that an arbitrary collection of open sets combines to stay open.

(a) The three intervals overlap in a chain — (1,3) and (2,4) overlap on (2,3), and (2,4) and (3,5) overlap on (3,4) — so their union is the single interval (1,5). (b) A union (of any size) of open sets is always open.

8. In the proof that the finite-subcover property forces a set K to be closed, suppose x were a limit point of K not in K. Answer both parts:

(a) According to the Heine-Borel Theorem, a set K ⊆ ℝ is compact if and only if:

(b) Fill in the blank: the sets O_n = {y : |y − x| > 1/n} would form an open cover of K with no finite subcover, because points of K get arbitrarily close to x, closer than any fixed 1/___.

(a) The finite-subcover property is the Heine-Borel Theorem's defining characterization of compactness. (b) Since x is a limit point of K, no fixed 1/n can keep every point of K farther than 1/n from x — so no finite subcollection of the O_n suffices.

9. Let K = (0, 1]. Answer both parts:

(a) Is K = (0, 1] compact, using the sequential definition?

(b) Fill in the blank: K = (0, 1] fails to be compact because it is bounded but not ___ — it is missing the limit point 0.

(a) The sequence 1/n stays inside (0,1] for every n but its limit, 0, escapes the set — exactly what compactness forbids. (b) (0,1] is bounded but not closed, since it's missing its limit point 0.

10. Let E = [1, 2] ∪ [4, 6]. Answer both parts:

(a) Is E connected?

(b) Fill in the blank: E is not an interval, since it contains 1 and 4 but excludes points like 3, which lie strictly between them — matching the characterization theorem that connected subsets of ℝ (with ≥2 points) are exactly the ___s.

(a) A = [1,2] and B = [4,6] are already closed, so their closures equal themselves and don't touch the other piece — a valid separation. (b) Connected subsets of ℝ with at least two points are exactly the intervals.
