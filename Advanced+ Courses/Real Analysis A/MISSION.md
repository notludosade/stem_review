# Mission: Real Analysis A

## Why
AP Calculus BC teaches how to use limits, continuity, derivatives, and integrals — not why any of it is actually true. Real Analysis A supplies the missing rigor: the precise definitions and proofs that justify the calculus already learned, and the mathematical maturity (reading and writing real proofs) that every more advanced math course afterward assumes.

## Success looks like
- Can state the field, order, and completeness axioms for the real numbers, and explain why completeness is what distinguishes ℝ from ℚ
- Can write a direct epsilon-N proof that a specific sequence converges to a specific limit
- Can state and apply the Monotone Convergence Theorem, Bolzano-Weierstrass, and the Cauchy criterion
- Can write a direct epsilon-delta proof of a functional limit, and explain why continuity, the Intermediate Value Theorem, and the Extreme Value Theorem all ultimately rest on completeness
- Can state and prove Rolle's Theorem and the Mean Value Theorem, and explain why differentiability implies continuity but not the reverse
- Can construct the Riemann integral from upper and lower sums and explain, at the level of definitions rather than mechanics, why the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus is true

## Constraints
- Assumes AP Calculus BC (or equivalent) — comfortable using limits, derivatives, and integrals computationally, but not assumed to have written a real proof before
- This is the first course in the "Advanced+ Courses" category and stands alone — no other course is a prerequisite besides the calculus background above
- Proof-writing is new territory: favor fully worked proofs with the logical structure made explicit (what is assumed, what is being shown, where each step comes from) over terse textbook-style proofs that skip steps
- Static, worked figures (in the style of Precalculus's vertical-line-test diagram) are enough where a visual helps — this course does not need AP Calculus BC's draggable interactive widgets, since the content here is about logical structure, not about exploring function behavior

## Out of scope
- Measure theory and Lebesgue integration — real analysis at the "B" level, not "A"
- General metric space and abstract topology — topology here is scoped strictly to the real line
- Multivariable and complex analysis
- Series of functions, uniform convergence of function sequences, and power series (these build directly on this course's sequence content but are left for a possible future course)
