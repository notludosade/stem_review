1. What does Lesson 1 identify as the actual structural difference between ℚ and ℝ?

This gap-free property is completeness — the defining structural difference between the rationals and the reals for everything this course builds.

2. In the proof that a · 0 = 0 for any a in an ordered field, what step immediately follows applying distributivity to a·(0+0)?

Adding −(a·0) to both sides is what isolates and proves a·0 = 0, without ever assuming the result or dividing by a.

3. Fill in the blank: |a − b| gives a precise measure of ___ between two real numbers on the number line, which is what every epsilon-based proof later in this course depends on.

Distance is the concept absolute value formalizes, and it's the backbone of every limit definition later in the course.

4. What is sup A for A = {2 − 1/n : n ∈ ℕ} = {1, 3/2, 5/3, 7/4, …}?

2 − 1/n never reaches 2 but gets arbitrarily close, since 1/n → 0. And 2 is an upper bound since 1/n > 0 always. So sup A = 2, even though it is never attained — note 1 is the set's minimum, not its supremum.

5. Fill in the blank: the Greatest Lower Bound Property (every nonempty, bounded-below subset of ℝ has an infimum in ℝ) is proved as a ___ of the completeness axiom, not stated as a second separate axiom.

Rather than a second axiom, the Greatest Lower Bound Property is proved by negating the set and reusing the completeness axiom already established for suprema.

6. Consider applying the Archimedean Property to prove lim (1/n) = 0 (Lesson 8). Answer both parts:

(a) What role does the Archimedean Property play in this proof?

(b) Fill in the blank: since the natural numbers are unbounded above by the Archimedean Property, for every ε > 0 a natural number N > 1/ε exists, so n > N forces 1/n < ___.

(a) Without the Archimedean Property, there is no guarantee a suitable N even exists. (b) n > N > 1/ε forces 1/n < ε directly.

7. Answer both parts about why density of ℚ in ℝ matters:

(a) What does density of ℚ in ℝ mean, precisely?

(b) Fill in the blank: combined with Lesson 1's observation that irrationals fill genuine gaps between rationals, density shows ℚ is dense but ___, while ℝ is both dense in itself and complete.

(a) Density means a rational always fits strictly between any two distinct reals, no matter how close together they are. (b) ℚ is packed densely throughout ℝ yet still incomplete — it has gaps that only ℝ fills.

8. Let A = {n/(n+1) : n ∈ ℕ} = {1/2, 2/3, 3/4, …}. Answer both parts:

(a) What is sup A?

(b) Fill in the blank: by the epsilon characterization, for every ε > 0 some term n/(n+1) satisfies n/(n+1) > 1 − ε; since n/(n+1) = 1 − 1/(n+1), this requires 1/(n+1) < ε, which the ___ Property guarantees is achievable for large enough n.

(a) n/(n+1) increases toward 1 but never reaches it, so sup A = 1. (b) Making 1/(n+1) arbitrarily small is exactly what the Archimedean Property guarantees.

9. Suppose |x − 5| < 0.1. Answer both parts about bounding |x² − 25|:

(a) Which bound on |x + 5| follows from the triangle inequality, given |x − 5| < 0.1?

(b) Fill in the blank: combining |x−5| < 0.1 and |x+5| < 10.1, the bound on |x² − 25| = |x−5|·|x+5| is less than 0.1 × 10.1 = ___.

(a) Writing x + 5 = (x − 5) + 10, the triangle inequality gives |x+5| ≤ |x−5| + 10 < 0.1 + 10 = 10.1. (b) Multiplying: 0.1 × 10.1 = 1.01.

10. Consider again A = {q ∈ ℚ : q² < 2}, viewed within ℝ rather than ℚ. Answer both parts:

(a) Within ℝ, what is sup A?

(b) Fill in the blank: A is nonempty (0 ∈ A) and bounded above (by 2, for instance) — exactly the two hypotheses the ___ axiom requires before it guarantees a real supremum exists.

(a) Within ℝ, √2 is exactly the least upper bound of A. (b) Nonempty and bounded above are exactly completeness's two hypotheses, and within ℝ they guarantee sup A exists as a real number.
