1. According to Lesson 1, what goes wrong with the increasing, bounded-above sequence 1, 1.4, 1.41, 1.414, … if only rational numbers are assumed to exist?

The sequence itself is perfectly fine — increasing and bounded above by 2. The gap is that its limit, √2, isn't rational, so within ℚ alone that limit doesn't exist anywhere to converge to.

2. Which statement correctly distinguishes the roles of the field/order axioms versus the completeness axiom?

Both ℚ and ℝ satisfy every field and order axiom. The real distinguishing property, completeness, is a separate axiom not yet introduced at that point.

3. Fill in the blank: for any a, b ∈ ℝ, |a + b| ≤ |a| + |b| is called the ___ inequality, proved using −|a| ≤ a ≤ |a| and the same fact for b.

This is the triangle inequality — adding the two bracketing inequalities for a and b, then recognizing the −c ≤ x ≤ c pattern, is the entire proof.

4. Let A = {1/n : n ∈ ℕ} = {1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, …}. What is inf A?

0 is a lower bound of A since every term is positive. No positive number can be a lower bound, since some 1/n eventually falls below it — so 0 is the greatest lower bound, even though 0 ∉ A.

5. Fill in the blank: the completeness axiom states that every nonempty, bounded-above subset of ℝ has a ___ that is itself a real number.

Supremum — the least upper bound — is exactly what the completeness axiom guarantees exists, and exists as a real number.

6. The Archimedean Property states that for every real x, some natural number exceeds it. Answer both parts about its proof:

(a) The proof assumes, toward contradiction, that ℕ is bounded above by some real x. What does completeness then supply?

(b) Fill in the blank: since s = sup ℕ is the least upper bound, s − 1 is not an upper bound, so some natural number m satisfies m > s − 1, giving m + 1 > s — but m + 1 is a natural number exceeding s, contradicting that s is a(n) ___ of ℕ.

(a) Assuming ℕ is bounded above lets the completeness axiom immediately supply a supremum, s. (b) Since m + 1 is a natural number exceeding s, this directly contradicts s being an upper bound of ℕ at all — so no bound x can exist.

7. For real numbers a < b, the density theorem produces a rational q with a < q < b. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the first step of the proof?

(b) Fill in the blank: after finding the least integer m with m > na, the proof shows na < m ≤ na + 1, then divides by n to get a < m/n ≤ a + 1/n < b, so q = m/___ is the rational number strictly between a and b.

(a) Making 1/n smaller than the gap (b − a) is exactly what guarantees a multiple of 1/n can be squeezed strictly inside that gap. (b) Dividing na < m ≤ na + 1 by n produces q = m/n strictly between a and b.

8. Let A = (0, 2]. Answer both parts:

(a) For A = (0, 2] with sup A = 2, which statement correctly applies the epsilon characterization of supremum?

(b) Fill in the blank: since 2 itself lies in A = (0, 2], choosing a = 2 satisfies a > 2 − ε for every ε > 0 — so the supremum is achieved and equals the ___ of A.

(a) The epsilon characterization requires "for every ε, some element of A" gets within ε of the supremum — not a single fixed ε and not every element. (b) When the supremum belongs to the set itself, it's also the set's maximum, achieved exactly rather than merely approached.

9. Suppose |x − 4| < 0.2. Answer both parts about bounding |x² − 16|:

(a) Which bound on |x + 4| follows from the triangle inequality, given |x − 4| < 0.2?

(b) Fill in the blank: combining |x−4| < 0.2 and |x+4| < 8.2, the product bound gives |x² − 16| = |x−4|·|x+4| < 0.2 × 8.2 = ___.

(a) Writing x + 4 = (x − 4) + 8, the triangle inequality gives |x+4| ≤ |x−4| + 8 < 0.2 + 8 = 8.2. (b) Multiplying the two bounds: 0.2 × 8.2 = 1.64.

10. Let A = {q ∈ ℚ : q² < 2}. Answer both parts:

(a) Why does A fail to have a supremum within ℚ, even though it's nonempty and bounded above?

(b) Fill in the blank: this failure is exactly why completeness must be stated as an ___, not derived as a theorem from the field and order axioms alone, since ℚ satisfies those axioms yet still lacks this property.

(a) Within ℝ, sup A = √2 exactly — but since √2 ∉ ℚ, no rational number can serve as A's supremum. (b) Because ℚ is a counterexample satisfying the field and order axioms yet lacking this property, completeness must be assumed as a genuinely separate axiom.
