1. In proving limx→2 (3x − 1) = 5 directly from the epsilon-delta definition, algebra shows |f(x) − 5| = 3|x − 2|. Which choice of δ (in terms of ε) makes the proof work?

Since |f(x) − 5| = 3|x − 2|, forcing this below ε requires |x − 2| < ε/3 — so δ = ε/3 suffices, mirroring Lesson 19's worked example exactly.

2. Let g(x) = (x² − 4)/(x − 2) for x ≠ 2, and define g(2) = 5. Answer both parts:

(a) What is limx→2 g(x)?

(b) Fill in the blank: since the limit equals ___ but g(2) = 5, g is discontinuous at x = 2.

(a) For x ≠ 2, (x²−4)/(x−2) = x+2, so the limit as x→2 is 4. (b) Continuity requires the limit to match the function's value there; since 4 ≠ 5, g fails to be continuous at x = 2 despite the limit existing.

3. Fill in the blank: to prove a function is not continuous at a, it suffices to exhibit a single sequence xₙ → a with f(xₙ) not converging to f(a) — this is the ___ characterization of continuity.

The sequential characterization's "if and only if" means one failing sequence is a complete disproof of continuity, without needing to negate the epsilon-delta definition directly.

4. Let h(x) = (x + 1)/(x² − 9). Answer both parts:

(a) Which rule guarantees h is continuous at every point where its denominator is nonzero?

(b) Fill in the blank: h fails to be continuous only at x = ±___.

(a) x+1 and x²−9 are each continuous everywhere (sums/products of continuous power functions), so their quotient is continuous wherever the denominator is nonzero. (b) x² − 9 = 0 exactly when x = 3 or x = −3, the only two excluded points.

5. Suppose f is continuous at a, and g is continuous at f(a). Which function is guaranteed continuous at a?

The composition theorem (Lesson 21) requires exactly this: f continuous at a and g continuous at f(a), which together guarantee g∘f is continuous at a — nothing is claimed about f∘g, which need not even be defined.

6. Let p(x) = x³ − x − 3, with p(1) = −3 and p(2) = 3. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the Intermediate Value Theorem guarantee here?

(b) Fill in the blank: IVT applies here because p is ___ on [1,2] (the only extra hypothesis needed, beyond the sign change).

(a) Since p is continuous and 0 lies between p(1) = −3 and p(2) = 3, IVT guarantees some c ∈ (1,2) with p(c) = 0 — a root, not an exact location or monotonicity. (b) Polynomials are continuous everywhere, which is exactly the hypothesis IVT needs alongside the sign change.

7. Which hypothesis is essential for the Extreme Value Theorem to guarantee f attains a maximum?

Compactness is the missing ingredient boundedness alone can't supply — f(x) = x on (0,1) is continuous and bounded but never attains its supremum 1, since (0,1) isn't compact.

8. Let f(x) = −x² + 4x on [0, 3]. Answer both parts:

(a) Since f is continuous on the compact set [0,3], what does the Extreme Value Theorem guarantee?

(b) Fill in the blank: the maximum value of f on [0,3] is ___.

(a) EVT guarantees both extremes are actually attained, not merely approached. (b) Checking f(0)=0, f(3)=3, and the vertex at x=2 gives f(2) = −4+8 = 4 — the largest of these, and indeed the maximum on [0,3].

9. Fill in the blank: unlike ordinary continuity, ___ continuity requires a single δ (given ε) to work for every pair of points in the domain simultaneously, not chosen separately at each point.

Ordinary continuity lets δ depend on the specific point; uniform continuity demands one δ serve the whole set at once.

10. Consider f(x) = 1/x. Answer both parts:

(a) Is f uniformly continuous on (0, 1)?

(b) Fill in the blank: however, f is uniformly continuous on [1, 2], because [1,2] is ___.

(a) Points 1/n and 1/(n+1) get arbitrarily close together while their images stay a fixed distance apart, so no single δ works near 0. (b) [1,2] is compact, and continuity on a compact set always upgrades to uniform continuity (Lesson 24).
