1. Which property must every metric d on a set X satisfy?

The triangle inequality is one of the three metric axioms, alongside positivity (d(x,y) ≥ 0, = 0 iff x=y) and symmetry (d(x,y) = d(y,x)).

2. What does the theorem "Every Open Ball Is Open" rely on in its proof?

The triangle inequality bounds d(x,z) using d(x,y) and d(y,z), producing the shrunk radius r′ that fits a ball around any y ∈ B(x,r) inside B(x,r).

3. Fill in the blank: ℚ with the usual absolute-value metric is not complete because some Cauchy sequences in ℚ (e.g. decimal truncations of √2) have no limit within ___.

The truncation sequence is Cauchy in ℚ but its limit, √2, is irrational, so no rational number in ℚ serves as its limit.

4. According to the Sequential Characterization of Continuity in metric spaces, f is continuous at x₀ if and only if:

This is the metric-space restatement of Real Analysis A's sequential characterization, transferred verbatim with d in place of |·|.

5. What is a "sequentially compact" set K ⊆ X?

Sequential compactness is defined via subsequences converging within the set, proved equivalent to (but distinct in definition from) the open-cover form of compactness.

6. Consider (C[a,b], d) with d(f,g) = supx∈[a,b]|f(x) − g(x)|. Answer both parts.

(a) Why does this d satisfy the triangle inequality?

(b) Fill in the blank: d(fₙ, f) → 0 in this metric is exactly the definition of fₙ → f ___ from Unit 1.

(a) The pointwise triangle inequality holds at every x, and taking a supremum over x preserves the inequality. (b) d(fₙ,f) = sup|fₙ−f| shrinking to 0 is precisely Unit 1's definition of uniform convergence.

7. In the discrete metric (Lesson 11), B(x, 1/2) = {x} for every x. Answer both parts.

(a) What does this imply about every subset U of a discrete metric space?

(b) Fill in the blank: since every subset is open, every subset's complement is also open — so every subset of a discrete metric space is both open and ___ ("clopen").

(a) Since the singleton ball around any x already sits inside any set containing x, every subset is open. (b) Because every subset and every complement is open, every subset is also closed — clopen.

8. In the proof that (C[a,b], dsup) is complete, once N is chosen so d(fₙ,fₘ) < ε/2 for m,n ≥ N, the argument lets m → ∞ in |fₙ(x) − fₘ(x)| < ε/2. Answer both parts.

(a) What does this step establish?

(b) Fill in the blank: since this bound holds for every x simultaneously, it shows fₙ → f ___, which is exactly the convergence needed in the metric dsup.

(a) Because N was chosen independently of x, the bound ε/2 applies to every x ∈ [a,b] at once. (b) A bound holding uniformly in x is exactly the definition of uniform convergence, which is what dsup(fₙ,f) → 0 means.

9. In the proof of the Topological Characterization (⇒ direction), f is continuous and V ⊆ Y is open. Answer both parts.

(a) Given x₀ ∈ f⁻¹(V), what does openness of V supply first?

(b) Fill in the blank: continuity at x₀ then gives δ > 0 with f(B(x₀,δ)) ⊆ B(f(x₀),ε) ⊆ V, showing B(x₀,δ) ⊆ f⁻¹(V) — so f⁻¹(V) is ___.

(a) Since V is open and f(x₀) ∈ V, some ball around f(x₀) fits inside V. (b) Every point of f⁻¹(V) has a ball around it contained in f⁻¹(V), so f⁻¹(V) is open.

10. In the proof that continuous images of connected sets are connected, U = f⁻¹(A) and V = f⁻¹(B) for a hypothetical separation f(X) = A ∪ B. Answer both parts.

(a) What does the proof show about Ū ∩ V, using continuity of f?

(b) Fill in the blank: since U and V turn out separated and nonempty with U ∪ V = X, this contradicts the assumption that X is ___, proving f(X) must be connected.

(a) Continuity turns the ball around f(x) missing A into a ball around x missing U, showing x is not a limit point of U. (b) The resulting separation of X contradicts X's assumed connectedness, so the original supposition that f(X) is disconnected must be false.
