1. Which of the following functions fails to be a metric on ℝ?

(x−y)² is non-negative and symmetric, but the specific numbers 0, 1, 2 show the triangle inequality genuinely fails.

2. What does it mean for a set U ⊆ X to be open in a metric space?

Openness only requires, for each point of U, some (possibly tiny) ball around it to fit inside U — not every radius, and not phrased via closed sets.

3. Fill in the blank: a metric space where every Cauchy sequence converges to a point of the space is called ___.

Completeness is exactly the property that Cauchy sequences never "fall through a gap" in the space.

4. Let f: X → Y between metric spaces (X, dX) and (Y, dY). Which is the correct ε–δ definition of continuity at x₀?

This is exactly Real Analysis A's ε–δ definition with d in place of |·|: closeness in the domain (δ) must force closeness in the range (ε).

5. Which is the correct open-cover definition of compactness for K ⊆ X?

Compactness (open-cover form) requires that any collection of open sets covering K can be trimmed down to a finite subcollection that still covers K.

6. Consider the discrete metric on an arbitrary set X: d(x,y) = 0 if x = y, d(x,y) = 1 otherwise. Answer both parts.

(a) Why does the discrete metric satisfy the triangle inequality?

(b) Fill in the blank: the discrete metric shows a metric can be defined on any set at all, with no algebraic or order ___ required.

(a) The only nontrivial case is x ≠ z, and since y cannot equal both, at least one of the two right-hand distances is already 1. (b) No algebraic or order structure on X is ever used — only the three metric axioms.

7. Recall: x is a limit point of E if every ball around x contains a point of E other than x. Answer both parts.

(a) According to this unit's theorem, when is a set E closed?

(b) Fill in the blank: E together with all of its limit points is called the ___ of E, and is the smallest closed set containing E.

(a) Closedness is equivalent to containing all limit points. (b) The closure Ē = E plus its limit points is the minimal closed set containing E.

8. The proof that (C[a,b], dsup) is complete starts with a Cauchy sequence (fₙ) in this metric. Answer both parts.

(a) What does fixing x ∈ [a,b] and looking at (fₙ(x)) give?

(b) Fill in the blank: once fₙ → f is shown to be uniform, Unit 1's theorem on the Uniform Limit of Continuous Functions guarantees f is ___, so f ∈ C[a,b].

(a) Since ℝ is complete, this pointwise Cauchy sequence of numbers converges, defining f(x) pointwise. (b) Once the convergence is shown uniform, Unit 1 guarantees the limit f is continuous.

9. The Topological Characterization states f: X → Y is continuous iff the preimage of every open V ⊆ Y is open in X. Answer both parts.

(a) Why is every function out of a discrete metric space automatically continuous?

(b) Fill in the blank: this characterization uses preimages, not images — the condition is about f⁻¹(V) for open V, ___ f(U) for open U.

(a) Since every subset of X is open when X is discrete, the preimage condition is satisfied vacuously for any f. (b) The characterization is strictly about preimages of open sets, not images.

10. The Clopen Characterization states X is disconnected iff X has a nonempty proper clopen subset. Answer both parts.

(a) Why is every discrete metric space with at least two points disconnected?

(b) Fill in the blank: the Intermediate Value Theorem is recovered as the special case of "continuous images of ___ sets are ___" applied to an interval (same word both blanks).

(a) Every subset of a discrete space is clopen (Lesson 12), so a single point is always a valid nonempty proper clopen witness. (b) Connected is the property preserved by continuous images; intervals are exactly the connected subsets of ℝ.
