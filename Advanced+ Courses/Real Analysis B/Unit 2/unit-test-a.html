1. For Σ n!·xⁿ, what is the radius of convergence, and why?

The Ratio Test ratio |(n+1)!xⁿ⁺¹|/|n!xⁿ| = (n+1)|x| → ∞ for any fixed x ≠ 0, so the series diverges everywhere except at the center — radius of convergence 0.

2. Let Σaₙ(x−c)ⁿ have radius of convergence R = 5. On which set does the theorem guarantee uniform convergence?

The theorem guarantees uniform convergence on any closed subinterval strictly inside the radius, not necessarily on the whole open interval itself.

3. Let f(x) = Σxⁿ (radius of convergence R = 1). Fill in the blank: the term-by-term derivative series Σn·xⁿ⁻¹ has radius of convergence ___.

The differentiation lemma guarantees the differentiated series has exactly the same radius of convergence as the original — here, 1.

4. If f is infinitely differentiable at c, which statement about its Taylor series is correct?

Being C^∞ at c is enough to write down the Taylor series, but says nothing yet about whether it converges, or converges to f — both can genuinely fail.

5. What does it mean for f to be analytic at c, as distinct from merely being C^∞ at c?

Analyticity is strictly stronger than C^∞: it additionally requires the Taylor series to actually converge back to f itself near c.

6. Σ xⁿ/n has radius of convergence R = 1. Answer both parts about its endpoint behavior.

(a) What happens at x = 1?

(b) Fill in the blank: at x = −1, the series becomes Σ(−1)ⁿ/n, which converges ___ by the alternating series test, giving interval of convergence [−1, 1).

(a) At x=1 this is the harmonic series, which diverges. (b) At x=−1 it's the alternating harmonic series, convergent conditionally (not absolutely, since Σ1/n diverges).

7. Σxⁿ has sum s(x) = 1/(1−x) and R = 1, with partial sums sₙ(x) = (1−xⁿ⁺¹)/(1−x). Answer both parts.

(a) What happens to supx∈(−1,1)|sₙ(x) − s(x)| for fixed n, over the whole open interval (−1,1)?

(b) Fill in the blank: however, on the closed subinterval [−1/2, 1/2], the same series does converge ___, exactly as the theorem for 0 < r < R guarantees.

(a) For any fixed n, the gap grows without bound as x approaches 1 within the interval, so uniform convergence fails on the full open interval. (b) On any closed [−r,r] with r < 1, the M-test-based theorem guarantees uniform convergence.

8. Repeated term-by-term differentiation gives f(k)(x) = Σn(n−1)⋯(n−k+1)aₙ(x−c)ⁿ⁻ᵏ. Answer both parts about evaluating this at x = c.

(a) What happens to every term with n ≠ k when this is evaluated at x = c?

(b) Fill in the blank: the surviving term is n = k, giving f(k)(c) = k!·aₖ, so aₖ = f(k)(c)/___.

(a) Lower powers are already killed by differentiation; higher powers still have a positive power of (x−c), which vanishes at x=c. (b) Only n=k survives, giving f(k)(c) = k!aₖ, so aₖ = f(k)(c)/k!.

9. Taylor's Theorem states Rₙ(x) = f(x) − Pₙ(x) = f(n+1)(ξ)/(n+1)!·(x−c)ⁿ⁺¹ for some ξ between c and x. Answer both parts about the proof.

(a) Which classical theorem from Real Analysis A supplies the point ξ?

(b) Fill in the blank: f is analytic at c exactly when this remainder term Rₙ(x) → ___ as n → ∞.

(a) Rolle's Theorem, applied to a carefully constructed g with g(c)=g(x)=0, supplies the intermediate point ξ. (b) Rₙ(x) → 0 is exactly the statement that the Taylor polynomials converge to f(x).

10. Let f(x) = e−1/x² for x ≠ 0, and f(0) = 0. Answer both parts.

(a) What is f(k)(0) for every k?

(b) Fill in the blank: since every derivative at 0 vanishes, f's Taylor series at 0 is identically 0, yet f(x) ≠ 0 for x ≠ 0 — so f is C^∞ at 0 but not ___ at 0.

(a) The Exponential Decay Beats Any Power lemma forces every derivative at 0 to equal 0. (b) The Taylor series is trivially 0 everywhere, but f itself is nonzero away from 0 — the standard example separating C^∞ from analytic.
