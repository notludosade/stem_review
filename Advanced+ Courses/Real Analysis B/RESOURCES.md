# Real Analysis B Resources

## Knowledge

- [Book: _Understanding Analysis_ — Stephen Abbott](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4939-2712-8)
  Continues directly from Real Analysis A: Chapter 6 (sequences and series of functions — uniform convergence, power series) and Chapter 8.1 (metric spaces) sit squarely in this course's opening units. Use for: the primary worked narrative behind Units 1–3.
- [Book: _Principles of Mathematical Analysis_ — Walter Rudin](https://archive.org/details/PrinciplesOfMathematicalAnalysis)
  Chapter 2 (basic topology, stated for general metric spaces from the start), Chapter 7 (sequences and series of functions), and Chapter 9 (functions of several variables — the total derivative as a linear map, done rigorously) are the canonical compressed references for Units 1, 3, and 4.
  Note: Rudin also has a short Chapter 11 introducing Lebesgue theory, but Axler (below) is the primary source for Units 5–6 — it's a full, free, modern treatment rather than Rudin's compressed capstone chapter.
- [Book: _Introduction to Real Analysis_ — William F. Trench](https://digitalcommons.trinity.edu/mono/7/)
  Free, legally-hosted full textbook. Chapters 5–6 (real- and vector-valued functions of several variables) give a more computational, calculus-adjacent entry into Unit 4 alongside Rudin's more abstract treatment.
- [Book: _Measure, Integration & Real Analysis_ — Sheldon Axler](https://measure.axler.net/MIRA.pdf)
  Free, open-access (Springer Open) textbook written specifically to be an accessible first course in measure theory — starts from exactly this course's prerequisite level rather than assuming graduate background. Also on [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-030-33143-6) and the [book's homepage](https://measure.axler.net/). Use for: the primary worked narrative behind Units 5–6 (Lebesgue measure and the Lebesgue integral).
