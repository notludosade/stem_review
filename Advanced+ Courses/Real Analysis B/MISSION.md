# Mission: Real Analysis B

## Why
Real Analysis A built the fundamentals — sequences, continuity, differentiation, and the Riemann integral, all on the real line. But mathematics built on top of analysis — functional analysis, differential geometry, probability theory, PDEs — needs tools Real Analysis A explicitly left out: convergence of whole sequences of functions (not just numbers), spaces more general than ℝ, calculus in more than one variable, and an integral that doesn't break under the limits real work requires. Real Analysis B supplies exactly the four extensions Real Analysis A flagged as out of scope, completing the rigorous foundation a graduate-level treatment (or any subsequent applied field) assumes.

## Success looks like
- Can distinguish pointwise from uniform convergence of a sequence of functions, and explain why only uniform convergence guarantees the limit function inherits continuity, and permits term-by-term integration
- Can determine the radius of convergence of a power series, and explain why a power series can be differentiated and integrated term by term within its radius of convergence
- Can verify whether a given distance function is a valid metric, and state the definitions of open/closed sets, completeness, compactness, and connectedness in a general metric space
- Can compute the total (Fréchet) derivative and Jacobian of a multivariable function, and explain why the mere existence of partial derivatives is weaker than differentiability
- Can construct Lebesgue outer measure and explain, at the level of definitions, why not every set of reals is Lebesgue measurable
- Can state the Monotone and Dominated Convergence Theorems and explain why they succeed where naive term-by-term limit interchange with the Riemann integral fails

## Constraints
- Assumes Real Analysis A in full — comfortable with epsilon-N and epsilon-delta proofs, sequences, completeness of ℝ, continuity, differentiation, and the Riemann integral
- Second course in "Advanced+ Courses," continuing directly from Real Analysis A — no other prerequisite
- Same proof-writing standard as Real Analysis A: fully worked proofs with explicit logical structure, not terse textbook compressions
- Static, worked figures only where a visual genuinely clarifies a definition (e.g., a nested-rectangle picture for outer measure) — no draggable interactive widgets, matching Real Analysis A's precedent that this content is about logical structure, not explorable behavior

## Out of scope
- Complex analysis — a distinct subject in its own right (holomorphic functions, contour integration), not a natural extension of real-variable metric-space/measure content; left for a possible dedicated future course
- General topology beyond metric spaces (topological spaces without a metric, separation axioms) — scoped strictly to metric spaces here
- Functional analysis (Banach/Hilbert spaces, operator theory) — builds directly on this course's metric-space content but is its own subsequent course
- Fubini's theorem, integration on product spaces, and Lᵖ spaces — left past the single-variable Lebesgue integral built here
