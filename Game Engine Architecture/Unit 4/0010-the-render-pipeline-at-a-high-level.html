The Render Pipeline at a High Level

Unit 3's render() call was a black box: "draw the current state." This unit opens it up. First stop: the fixed sequence of stages every triangle passes through on its way from 3D data to a lit pixel on your screen.

← Unit 3, Lesson 9 Lesson 11 → Contents Glossary →

Every 3D shape a GPU knows how to draw is ultimately made of triangles — a sphere, a character, a mountain, all triangles, just a lot of them. The render pipeline is the fixed sequence of steps that turns a list of triangle corners in 3D space into colored pixels in a 2D image.

From vertices to pixels, in order

Definition The render pipeline (or graphics pipeline) is the sequence of stages a GPU runs, in order, for every triangle it draws: vertex processing (transform each triangle corner from 3D model space into its final 2D screen position), rasterization (figure out exactly which screen pixels fall inside that triangle), fragment/pixel processing (compute the actual color of each of those pixels — lighting, texture lookups, and so on), and finally writing the result into the framebuffer, the block of memory that becomes the image shown on screen.

Worked example A character's shoulder is one vertex among thousands making up their mesh. Vertex processing moves that one point from "3 units up, 1 unit right, relative to the character's own origin" all the way to "this exact pixel column and row on your monitor," accounting for the character's position in the world, the camera's position and angle, and the screen's resolution — all in one stage, for every vertex, every single frame.
Why this pipeline is fixed, not something an engine invents Modern GPUs are built as specialized hardware around exactly this sequence — vertex stage, rasterization, fragment stage — because that division is what let chip designers build massively parallel hardware that processes millions of vertices and pixels independently and simultaneously. A game engine doesn't reinvent this pipeline; it writes small custom programs (shaders, which Unit 4 touches again in Lesson 12) that plug into the vertex and fragment stages, while rasterization itself stays fixed, dedicated hardware the engine never has to implement.
