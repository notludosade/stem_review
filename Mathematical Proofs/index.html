Unit 1 — Logical Foundations
Lesson 1

Statements and Logical Connectives

A proof is an argument built entirely out of statements. Before you can build one, you need to know exactly what a statement is, and exactly what happens when you glue two of them together.

 Lesson 2

Quantifiers: For All and There Exists

"Every even number greater than 2 is a sum of two primes" and "some even number greater than 2 is a sum of two primes" are wildly different claims. Quantifiers are what tell them apart.

 Lesson 3

Reading and Writing Mathematical Definitions

A definition isn't background reading — it's an exact specification of what you're allowed to assume and what you're required to produce. Misreading one is the single most common reason a proof attempt goes nowhere.

Unit 2 — Direct Proof
Lesson 4

The Structure of a Direct Proof

The simplest, most common proof technique there is: assume the hypothesis, reason forward one legal step at a time, and land on the conclusion. Every other technique in this course is a workaround for when this one doesn't apply cleanly.

 Lesson 5

Proving Statements About Even/Odd Integers and Divisibility

The most common testing ground for direct proof: a small, closed world of integers, parity, and divisibility, where every claim reduces to substituting a definition and doing algebra.

 Lesson 6

Proving Statements About Sets

Closing out Unit 2 by pointing direct proof at sets instead of integers — same structure, one new trick: picking an arbitrary element and arguing about it.

Unit 3 — Contradiction & Contrapositive
Lesson 7

Proof by Contrapositive

Some conditionals are painful to prove head-on and easy to prove sideways. Contrapositive proof is the same direct-proof machinery from Unit 2, just aimed at a logically equivalent statement that happens to be easier to work with.

 Lesson 8

Proof by Contradiction

Assume the claim is false, reason forward anyway, and watch the assumption collapse under its own weight. A genuinely different logical structure from contrapositive, not just another name for it.

 Lesson 9

Classic Contradiction Proofs

Two of the oldest proofs in mathematics, both over two thousand years old, both still the cleanest possible demonstration of what contradiction is for.

Unit 4 — Mathematical Induction
Lesson 10

The Principle of Mathematical Induction

A technique for proving a claim about every natural number at once, without ever checking more than two cases by hand: the first one, and a generic "next" one.

 Lesson 11

Induction Proofs Involving Sums and Inequalities

The same base case / inductive step skeleton from Lesson 10, run on a longer sum formula and then on an inequality — where the base case doesn't even have to be n = 1.

 Lesson 12

Strong Induction

Some inductive steps need more than just the case immediately before them — they need every earlier case, on call, all at once. Same overall shape as Lesson 10, one enlarged assumption.

Unit 5 — Proof Strategies in Practice
Lesson 13

Proof by Cases

When a single argument can't cover every possibility at once, split the domain into pieces small enough to handle separately — as long as no possibility is left out.

 Lesson 14

Existence and Uniqueness Proofs

"There is a solution" and "there is exactly one solution" are different claims, proved by different arguments — and mixing them up is one of the easiest mistakes to make without noticing.

 Lesson 15

Choosing the Right Strategy (Capstone)

Every technique in this course, reduced to a handful of questions you can ask about any claim before writing a single line of proof.
