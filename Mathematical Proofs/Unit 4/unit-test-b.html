1. Which pair of ingredients correctly makes up a (weak) mathematical induction proof?

A confirmed starting case plus a step that always propagates truth forward together cover every relevant n.

2. What role does the inductive hypothesis P(k) play inside the inductive step?

The inductive hypothesis is an assumption used only to establish the conditional P(k) → P(k+1) — the base case is what supplies an actual confirmed instance.

3. Fill in the blank: for the claim 2 + 4 + 6 + ⋯ + 2n = n(n+1), the base case n = 1 checks that the left side equals ___.

At n = 1 the sum has just one term, 2, which matches 1(2) = 2.

4. In general, why can an induction proof's base case sometimes need to start well above n = 1?

Induction can only prove what's actually true — if a claim fails for small n, the base case must be set at the first n where it holds.

5. Fill in the blank: the technique whose inductive hypothesis assumes every case from the base case through k, not just P(k) alone, is called ___.

Strong induction — needed whenever P(k+1) can depend on an arbitrary earlier case, not necessarily the immediately preceding one.

6. Consider proving 2 + 4 + 6 + ⋯ + 2n = n(n+1) by induction. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the base case n = 1 verify?

(b) Fill in the blank: in the inductive step, the (k+1)-th even number added to the sum is 2k + ___.

(a) 2 = 1(2) confirms the base case. (b) The (k+1)-th even number is 2(k+1) = 2k + 2, and k(k+1) + (2k+2) = (k+1)(k+2), completing the inductive step.

7. Consider proving n! > 4ⁿ by induction. Answer both parts:

(a) Which is the correct base case?

(b) Fill in the blank: in the inductive step, since k ≥ 9, k + 1 ≥ ___.

(a) The inequality first becomes true at n = 9, while it fails at n = 8. (b) k ≥ 9 gives k + 1 ≥ 10, which is what forces (k+1)·4ᵏ > 4·4ᵏ = 4ᵏ⁺¹.

8. Consider proving 33 is a product of primes using strong induction, having already established P(2) through P(32). Answer both parts:

(a) Why is P(32) alone (weak induction's hypothesis) not useful here?

(b) Fill in the blank: using strong induction, 33 = 3 × ___, and since both factors are prime, 33 is a product of primes.

(a) 33's relevant factors (3 and 11) are unrelated to 32, so P(32) alone is useless. (b) 33 = 3 × 11, both prime, applying the strong inductive hypothesis to each.

9. Answer both parts distinguishing weak from strong induction:

(a) Which statement correctly describes the difference?

(b) Fill in the blank: weak induction's inductive step may assume only P(___).

(a) The sole structural difference is the size of the inductive hypothesis. (b) Weak induction's hypothesis is the single case P(k), unlike strong induction's full range.

10. Consider the claim n² > 4n + 5, for integers n. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the smallest integer base case for which this claim is true?

(b) Fill in the blank: at n = 6, the left side is 36 and the right side is 4(6) + 5 = ___.

(a) n = 5 gives 25 = 25 (not strictly greater); n = 6 gives 36 > 29, the first true case. (b) 4(6) + 5 = 29.
