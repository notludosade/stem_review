1. What are the two ingredients of a (weak) mathematical induction proof?

A confirmed starting case plus a step that always propagates truth forward together cover every relevant n.

2. In the inductive step of a weak induction proof, what exactly is being proved?

The inductive step proves a conditional, using P(k) only as an assumption (the inductive hypothesis), not as an independently established fact.

3. Fill in the blank: for the claim 1 + 3 + 5 + ⋯ + (2n − 1) = n², the base case n = 1 checks that the left side equals ___.

At n = 1 the sum has just one term, 1, which matches 1² = 1.

4. Why must the base case for "n! > 2ⁿ" be set at n = 4 rather than n = 1?

Induction can only prove what's actually true — since the inequality fails at n = 1, 2, 3, the base case must start where it first holds, n = 4.

5. Fill in the blank: the technique whose inductive hypothesis assumes every case from the base case through k, not just P(k) alone, is called ___.

Strong induction — needed whenever P(k+1) can depend on an arbitrary earlier case, not necessarily the immediately preceding one.

6. Consider proving 1 + 3 + 5 + ⋯ + (2n − 1) = n² by induction. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the base case n = 1 verify?

(b) Fill in the blank: in the inductive step, the (k+1)-th odd number added to the sum is 2k + ___.

(a) 1 = 1² confirms the base case. (b) The (k+1)-th odd number is 2(k+1) − 1 = 2k + 1, and k² + (2k+1) = (k+1)², completing the inductive step.

7. Consider proving n! > 3ⁿ by induction. Answer both parts:

(a) Which is the correct base case?

(b) Fill in the blank: in the inductive step, since k ≥ 7, k + 1 ≥ ___.

(a) The inequality first becomes true at n = 7 (5040 > 2187), while it fails at n = 6 (720 < 729). (b) k ≥ 7 gives k + 1 ≥ 8, which is what forces (k+1)·3ᵏ > 3·3ᵏ = 3ᵏ⁺¹.

8. Consider proving 21 is a product of primes using strong induction, having already established P(2) through P(20). Answer both parts:

(a) Why is P(20) alone (weak induction's hypothesis) not useful here?

(b) Fill in the blank: using strong induction, 21 = 3 × ___, and since both factors are prime, 21 is a product of primes.

(a) 21's relevant factors (3 and 7) are unrelated to 20, so P(20) alone is useless. (b) 21 = 3 × 7, both prime, applying the strong inductive hypothesis to each.

9. Answer both parts distinguishing weak from strong induction:

(a) Which statement correctly describes the difference?

(b) Fill in the blank: strong induction's inductive step may assume P(n₀) through P(___).

(a) The sole structural difference is the size of the inductive hypothesis. (b) Strong induction assumes the full range P(n₀), …, P(k) before proving P(k+1).

10. Consider the claim n² > 3n + 4, for integers n. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the smallest integer base case for which this claim is true?

(b) Fill in the blank: at n = 5, the left side is 25 and the right side is 3(5) + 4 = ___.

(a) n = 4 gives 16 = 16 (not strictly greater); n = 5 gives 25 > 19, the first true case. (b) 3(5) + 4 = 19.
