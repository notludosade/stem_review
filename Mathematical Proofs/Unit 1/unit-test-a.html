1. What is the truth value of "If 3 is even, then 5 is prime," and why?

P → Q is false only when P is true and Q is false. Here P ("3 is even") is false, so the conditional is vacuously true regardless of Q.

2. Let P be true and Q be false. What is the truth value of P ∨ ¬Q?

¬Q is true (since Q is false), and P is already true, so P ∨ ¬Q is true either way — both disjuncts hold here.

3. Fill in the blank: the connective that is true exactly when both of its parts are true is called ___.

Conjunction (P ∧ Q) requires both sides true — unlike disjunction, which only needs one.

4. What is the negation of ∃x ∈ ℝ, x² = −1?

Negating ∃ flips it to ∀ and negates the predicate: ¬(x² = −1) is x² ≠ −1.

5. Fill in the blank: proving ∃x ∈ D, P(x) is false requires showing P(x) fails for ___ x in D.

¬(∃x, P(x)) ≡ ∀x, ¬P(x) — disproving an existence claim requires ruling out the entire domain, not just one case.

6. Consider two statements about real numbers: (I) ∀x ∃y, x < y. (II) ∃y ∀x, x < y. Answer both parts:

(a) Which statement, if either, is true?

(b) Fill in the blank: statement (II) fails because no single y can exceed ___ real number simultaneously.

(a) In (I), y is chosen after and depends on x, so y = x + 1 always works. (b) In (II), one fixed y would have to beat every real number at once, which is impossible.

7. The definition of even integer reads: "n is even if there exists an integer k with n = 2k." Answer both parts:

(a) By the definitional-if convention, this definition also implicitly asserts:

(b) Fill in the blank: because evenness is defined existentially, proving a specific number is even means producing a ___.

(a) Definitions are read as biconditionals by convention. (b) An existential definitional claim is proved by exhibiting a witness — here, the specific k.

8. Consider the claim 6 ∣ 54. Answer both parts:

(a) Which integer k correctly witnesses this claim (54 = 6k)?

(b) Fill in the blank: since 54 = 6 × 9, k = ___.

6 × 9 = 54, so k = 9 is the required witness — the entire content of this divisibility proof.

9. Let P = "the moon is made of cheese" (false) and Q = "2 + 2 = 4" (true). Answer both parts:

(a) What is the truth value of P → Q?

(b) Fill in the blank: the truth value of Q → P is ___.

(a) P false makes P → Q vacuously true. (b) Q → P has a true hypothesis and false conclusion — the one case that makes a conditional false.

10. Consider: "Every integer greater than 1 has a prime factor," symbolically ∀n ∈ ℤ with n > 1, ∃p prime, p ∣ n. Answer both parts:

(a) Which is the correct negation?

(b) Fill in the blank: the negation says some integer greater than 1 has ___ prime factor at all.

(a) Both quantifiers flip and the innermost predicate negates: ∀→∃, ∃→∀, p∣n → p∤n. (b) That correctly reads as "some n>1 has no prime factor" — which is in fact false, but forming the negation correctly is the skill being tested.
