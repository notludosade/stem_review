1. What core problem does cloud computing's elastic, pay-as-you-go model solve, compared to on-premises infrastructure?

Cloud computing shifts the hard capacity-planning guess onto a provider with pooled demand across many customers.

2. Fill in the blank: the framework splitting security and reliability tasks into "the provider's job" and "the customer's job," with the exact split depending on the service model, is called the ___.

The shared responsibility model defines exactly this division, sliding based on IaaS, PaaS, or SaaS.

3. Why might a hospital network use hybrid cloud instead of committing fully to public or private cloud?

Hybrid cloud matches each workload to the deployment model that actually fits its constraints.

4. What role does a hypervisor play in cloud computing?

The hypervisor is the isolation and allocation layer between physical hardware and every VM running on it.

5. Fill in the blank: containers on the same host share the operating system ___ instead of each running their own separate copy, which is why they start much faster than VMs.

Sharing the kernel avoids the overhead of booting a full guest OS for every workload.

6. Why is serverless computing often cheaper than an always-on VM for a spiky, mostly-idle workload?

Serverless's cost advantage is specific to idle, spiky traffic patterns, not a universal rule over VMs.

7. A production database needs frequent, small, low-latency random reads and writes. Which storage model fits, and why?

Block storage's low-latency small I/O is exactly what a database's data files need.

8. Fill in the blank: the percentage of time a service is actually reachable and responding correctly is called ___, a separate guarantee from durability.

Availability measures reachability, distinct from durability's data-survival guarantee.

9. What does moving data to a "cold" or "archive" storage tier trade away, in exchange for a much lower cost per gigabyte?

Cold and archive tiers trade slower retrieval for significantly reduced storage cost, without changing the underlying storage model.

10. Why does a database typically sit in a private subnet rather than the same public subnet as a web server?

Removing the internet route entirely blocks outside access at the network level, before any credential is even checked.

11. Fill in the blank: the load balancer routing algorithm that always sends the next request to whichever server currently has the fewest active connections is called ___.

Least connections adapts to real-time server load, unlike round robin's fixed rotation.

12. What is the main benefit a CDN provides beyond faster requests for distant users?

Edge caching absorbs most requests, reducing the load that ever reaches the origin server.

13. What does a managed database service take over from the customer, compared to a self-managed database on a VM?

Operational upkeep moves to the provider; schema design and queries remain the customer's responsibility.

14. Fill in the blank: a message queue lets a producer and consumer operate independently in time and pace, a property called ___.

Decoupling means neither side needs to know about, or run in sync with, the other.

15. Why does the principle of least privilege limit the damage a stolen credential can cause?

Least privilege doesn't stop theft — it caps what a compromised credential can actually reach afterward.

16. Why does an auto scaling group's maximum setting matter as much as its minimum?

The maximum is both a spending limit and a safeguard against unbounded scaling; the minimum guarantees baseline capacity.

17. Fill in the blank: data leaving a provider's network — to the internet or between regions — is called ___, and it's typically billed while inbound data usually isn't.

Egress pricing is the asymmetry that can produce a surprisingly large bill from a chatty cross-region architecture.

18. An application's servers are deployed across three availability zones behind a load balancer. What happens if one entire AZ loses power?

Health checks combined with multi-AZ redundancy let the system survive a whole zone failing automatically.

19. Why is copying a large streaming company's public architecture diagram risky for a small startup's internal tool?

An architecture should fit the actual workload, not be borrowed wholesale from a different scale and problem.

20. Fill in the blank: the difficulty and cost of switching away from a provider due to dependence on its proprietary services is called ___.

Vendor lock-in exists on a spectrum, and accepting some of it can be a rational tradeoff for a managed service's real benefits.
