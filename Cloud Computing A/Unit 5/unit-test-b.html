Managed Services. 10 questions covering every lesson in this unit. Score 90% or higher to clear this unit.

1. What remains the customer's job even with a fully managed database service?

Patching, backups, and failover move to the provider; schema and query design remain the customer's job.

2. Fill in the blank: a managed database is usually more expensive per hour than an equivalent self-managed VM, since some of that extra ___ pays for the reduced operational burden.

Managed databases carry a cost premium in exchange for taking operational upkeep off the customer's plate.

3. A team switches from a self-managed database on a VM to a fully managed database service. What happens to their share of operational responsibility?

Managed databases shift operational responsibility toward the provider, the same logic as moving from IaaS toward PaaS.

4. What does "decoupling" mean for a producer and consumer connected through a message queue?

Decoupling is exactly this independence in time, pace, and mutual awareness between producer and consumer.

5. Why does a message queue help absorb a sudden burst of incoming orders, tying into Unit 6's later coverage of elasticity?

The queue buffers the mismatch between arrival rate and processing rate, protecting the producer from a traffic spike.

6. Comparing a plain queue to publish/subscribe. Answer both parts:

(a) How many consumers process a given message in a plain queue?

(b) How many subscribers receive a copy of a given message in pub/sub?

A plain queue delivers to one consumer per message; pub/sub broadcasts a copy to every subscriber on the topic.

7. Fill in the blank: IAM defines who or what — a human user or a non-human application — is called an ___, and what actions it may take.

An identity can be a human user or a non-human service, each governed by IAM's permission rules.

8. Why do administrators typically assign identities to roles instead of granting permissions one at a time to each individual user?

Roles make permission management scalable by bundling and reusing sensible permission sets by job function.

9. Fill in the blank: least privilege limits the ___ — how much damage a single stolen credential can cause — by ensuring that credential was never granted more access than its job required.

"Blast radius" describes the scope of damage a compromised credential can cause, which least privilege deliberately shrinks.

10. A Read-Only Analyst role has no delete, deploy, or billing permissions. If that analyst's credential is phished, what is the practical effect of least privilege here?

Least privilege doesn't stop the phishing attempt — it caps what the compromised credential can actually do afterward.
