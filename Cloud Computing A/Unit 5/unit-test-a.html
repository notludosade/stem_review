1. What does a managed database service take over from the customer?

Operational upkeep of the engine moves to the provider; schema and query design remain the customer's job.

2. Fill in the blank: a genuine tradeoff of choosing a managed database is reduced low-level ___, such as not being able to SSH into the underlying machine.

Managed databases trade some low-level control for reduced operational burden, usually at a higher hourly cost.

3. How does a managed database relate to the shared responsibility spectrum from Unit 1?

Managed databases apply the same responsibility-shifting logic from Lesson 2, scoped to one specific service.

4. What is the core function of a message queue?

A queue decouples producer and consumer by holding messages between them.

5. In the order-processing example, why does using a queue make the system more resilient to a worker-fleet crash?

The queue absorbs the outage, letting the producer keep succeeding while messages wait for consumers to recover.

6. A new-order event must independently trigger three separate systems: inventory, email, and analytics. Answer both parts:

(a) Which messaging pattern fits this need?

(b) Why doesn't a plain queue fit this need as well?

Pub/sub broadcasts each message to every subscriber, which is exactly what's needed when multiple independent systems must all react to the same event.

7. Fill in the blank: the system for defining identities and permissions, and enforcing them on every resource request, is called ___.

IAM checks identity, action, and resource together on every single request.

8. What is the relationship between a role and a policy in IAM?

Roles bundle policies so permissions can be assigned by job function rather than one at a time.

9. Fill in the blank: granting every identity only the minimum permissions it actually needs, and nothing more, is called the principle of ___.

Least privilege limits how much damage a mistake or stolen credential can cause.

10. Why does an overly broad IAM policy (like a marketing tool with permission to delete production databases) matter, even if it's never intentionally misused?

The risk isn't intentional misuse — it's what a leaked or stolen credential could do with unnecessary permissions attached.
