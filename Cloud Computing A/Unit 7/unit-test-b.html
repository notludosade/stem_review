Unit 7 Test — Version B

Putting It Together. 10 questions covering every lesson in this unit. Score 90% or higher to clear this unit.

← Course Contents ← Version A

1. Why does this unit recommend working through compute, storage, networking, managed services, and reliability questions in order, rather than picking services at random?

Working through the checklist in order grounds each decision in the workload's real characteristics instead of guesswork.

2. Fill in the blank: a photo-sharing app serving global public users with static, cacheable images benefits from a ___ in front of its storage, unlike a purely internal API.

A CDN earns its keep on public, cacheable, globally distributed content, unlike private internal-only traffic.

3. A photo-sharing app stores millions of uploaded images, mostly written once and read often. Which storage approach fits, combined with what cost-saving technique from Unit 3?

Object storage suits whole-file, write-once/read-often content, and lifecycle policies control cost as access frequency drops over time.

4. What should be used instead of copying a well-known company's architecture diagram wholesale?

The checklist grounded in the actual workload is what should drive the decision, not an imported template.

5. Is vendor lock-in an all-or-nothing state, or a spectrum?

Lock-in varies by service — some choices are far more portable than others within the same architecture.

6. Comparing what actually has to change during each type of migration. Answer both parts:

(a) Moving standard VMs and an open-source database to a new provider mainly requires changing...

(b) Moving away from a proprietary serverless event system mainly requires changing...

Standard technology mainly requires reconfiguring deployment; proprietary services are woven into the application code itself, making migration far more invasive.

7. Fill in the blank: the pattern of "more managed convenience often means more lock-in" echoes Unit 1's ___ spectrum, where more provider management also meant less customer control.

Both spectrums trade customer control (or portability) for provider-managed convenience.

8. What is a real cost of adopting a multi-cloud strategy to reduce lock-in?

Multi-cloud trades away simplicity for reduced dependence on any single provider — a real cost, not a free option.

9. What is "the mistake," according to this lesson, when it comes to lock-in?

The lesson frames the real mistake as an unexamined tradeoff, not the act of accepting lock-in itself.

10. Fill in the blank: running many containers together in production, called ___, was mentioned as existing but deliberately left out of this course's depth.

Container orchestration is explicitly flagged as out of scope, reserved for a follow-on DevOps course.
