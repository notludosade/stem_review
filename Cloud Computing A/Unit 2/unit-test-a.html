1. What is a virtual machine?

A VM emulates a whole computer in software while sharing physical hardware with other VMs, isolated by the hypervisor.

2. Fill in the blank: the software that runs on physical hardware and creates, manages, and isolates multiple virtual machines is called the ___.

The hypervisor is the isolation and allocation layer between physical hardware and each VM.

3. Why do cloud providers sell VMs in predefined "instance" sizes rather than negotiating a custom carve-up for every customer?

Predefined instance sizes make picking an appropriately sized slice fast and standardized.

4. What is the defining architectural trait of a container?

Sharing the host OS kernel, rather than duplicating it, is what makes a container lighter-weight than a VM.

5. Why does a container typically start much faster than a VM?

Booting a full OS (VM) is inherently slower than starting one more process on an already-running OS (container).

6. A sensitive multi-tenant workload sometimes runs inside a VM instead of a bare container. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is a VM's isolation considered stronger?

(b) What does a container's isolation depend on that a VM's does not?

A VM's isolation is enforced at the hardware level by the hypervisor; a container's isolation depends on the shared kernel underneath being secure for every tenant using it.

7. Fill in the blank: uploading a single function's worth of code without provisioning any server, VM, or container yourself, and letting the provider run it only when triggered, is called ___.

Function as a Service (FaaS), the concrete form of serverless computing, matches this description exactly.

8. What does "scale-to-zero" mean for a serverless function with no incoming traffic?

Scale-to-zero means genuinely zero capacity and zero cost while idle, unlike a VM's always-on baseline.

9. Fill in the blank: the extra delay a serverless function experiences the first time it runs after sitting idle, while a fresh execution environment spins up, is called a ___.

A cold start is skipped only when the function was invoked recently enough to still have a warm environment.

10. A workload runs constantly at very high, predictable volume around the clock. Why might a provisioned VM be cheaper for it than a serverless function?

At constant high utilization, a VM you're already paying for around the clock avoids the per-invocation premium serverless charges for its on-demand convenience.
