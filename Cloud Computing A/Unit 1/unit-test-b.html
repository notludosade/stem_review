1. A company keeps its servers in a closet down the hall, and its own staff installs and maintains every piece of hardware. What is this arrangement called?

Owning and physically operating your own hardware, wherever it's located, is on-premises infrastructure.

2. Fill in the blank: guessing future demand months ahead of time in order to decide how much hardware to buy — where guessing too low risks an outage and guessing too high wastes money — is the problem of ___.

This forecasting problem is capacity planning, and it's much harder on-prem than in the cloud because of hardware's long lead time.

3. A startup pays only for the exact number of hours its servers actually run, adjusting capacity up or down within minutes as user traffic changes. This arrangement is best described as:

Metered, elastic renting of infrastructure from a provider is exactly the definition of cloud computing.

4. Fill in the blank: a recurring cost that scales with actual usage, such as a monthly cloud bill, is classified in accounting as ___.

OpEx scales with usage and recurs regularly, unlike the one-time upfront nature of CapEx.

5. A customer configures a database with no password and it gets breached. Under the shared responsibility model, whose failure is this?

Configuration of the resources a customer deploys is squarely their responsibility, not the provider's.

6. A provider's network backbone suffers a hardware failure, and separately, a customer's engineer accidentally grants an intern full administrator access to production. Answer both parts:

(a) The network hardware failure falls under security or reliability...

(b) The overly broad access grant falls under security...

Provider hardware failures are "of the cloud"; customer-side access configuration mistakes are "in the cloud."

7. Fill in the blank: a fully complete, ready-to-use application like an email service, where the customer only manages their own data and account settings, is an example of ___.

SaaS hands the customer a finished application, leaving only data and account configuration on their side.

8. Why does this course assume a public cloud context by default?

Public cloud maximizes the demand-pooling benefit that makes elasticity and metered pricing possible in the first place.

9. What is the defining trait of private cloud, compared to public cloud?

Dedicated (not shared) hardware is the key trait — private cloud can still be provider-hosted and still uses cloud-style tooling.

10. Fill in the blank: a hospital keeps patient records on a private cloud for regulatory reasons but runs its public appointment-booking website on public cloud, with the two connected. This deployment strategy is called ___ cloud.

Deliberately combining private/on-prem and public infrastructure for different workloads is the definition of hybrid cloud.
