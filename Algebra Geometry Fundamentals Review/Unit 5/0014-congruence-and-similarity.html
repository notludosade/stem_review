Congruence and Similarity

"Exactly the same shape and size" versus "exactly the same shape, scaled" — a distinction that unlocks a huge amount of geometry once you can set up the right ratio.

← Lesson 13 Lesson 15 → Contents Glossary →

Two figures can match in shape without matching in size, or match in both. Geometry has a separate word for each case, and similarity in particular turns into pure algebra once you set it up as a proportion.

Congruent vs. similar

Definitions Congruent figures are identical in both shape and size — every corresponding side and angle matches exactly, symbol ≅. Similar figures have the same shape but not necessarily the same size — every corresponding angle is equal, and every corresponding side is scaled by the same scale factor, symbol ~. Every pair of congruent figures is also similar (with scale factor 1), but similar figures aren't congruent unless that scale factor happens to be exactly 1.

Why area scales by k², not k Both legs of the right triangle above are 80 units at k = 1. Scale by k = 2: each side doubles, but the area — half of base times height — has both the base and the height doubled, so the area quadruples (2² = 4), not just doubles. Every 2D measurement (area) scales by k²; every 1D measurement (side length, perimeter) scales by k¹; volume (Lesson 15) scales by k³.

Solving for an unknown side using a proportion

Worked example Triangle ABC ~ Triangle DEF. Side AB = 6, side DE = 9 (corresponding sides), and side BC = 8. Find side EF.
Set up the proportion: AB/DE = BC/EF, so 6/9 = 8/EF.
Cross-multiply: 6 · EF = 9 · 8 = 72, so EF = 12.
Reading correspondence from the name order Writing "Triangle ABC ~ Triangle DEF" isn't just labeling — it declares the correspondence: A matches D, B matches E, C matches F, in that exact order. Side AB corresponds to side DE (first two letters to first two letters), not to some other side, purely because of how the similarity statement was written.

1. What's the key difference between congruent and similar figures?

Congruent means identical in shape AND size; similar only requires the same shape, scaled by some factor.

2. A triangle is scaled by a factor of 3. If its original area was 5 square units, what is the new area?

Area scales by k² for a scale factor k: 5 × 3² = 5 × 9 = 45.

3. Triangle PQR ~ Triangle XYZ, with PQ = 4, XY = 10, and QR = 6. What is YZ?

PQ/XY = QR/YZ, so 4/10 = 6/YZ. Cross-multiplying: 4 · YZ = 60, so YZ = 15.

4. Fill in the blank: the single number that every corresponding side length in a similar figure is multiplied by is called the ___.

The scale factor is the single ratio applied uniformly to every side length between similar figures.

5. Fill in the blank: congruent figures are similar figures with a scale factor of exactly ___.

A scale factor of 1 means no resizing at all — exactly what congruence requires on top of matching shape.
