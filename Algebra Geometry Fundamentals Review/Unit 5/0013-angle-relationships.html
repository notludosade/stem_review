Complementary and supplementary angles

Definitions Complementary angles add up to 90°. Supplementary angles add up to 180°. Neither definition requires the angles to be next to each other — only that their measures add to the right total.

Angles formed by intersecting lines and a transversal

Definitions Vertical angles — the pair of opposite angles formed when two lines cross — are always equal. When a third line (a transversal) crosses two parallel lines, it creates several named pairs: corresponding angles (same position at each intersection) are equal, and alternate interior angles (opposite sides of the transversal, between the two parallel lines) are also equal.
Turning a relationship into an equation Two angles are supplementary, and one measures 3x + 10 while the other measures 2x. Since supplementary means they sum to 180: (3x + 10) + 2x = 180 → 5x + 10 = 180 → 5x = 170 → x = 34. The first angle is 3(34) + 10 = 112°, and the second is 2(34) = 68°; check: 112 + 68 = 180 ✓.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Two angles are complementary. One measures 35°. What does the other measure?

Complementary angles sum to 90°: 90 − 35 = 55°.

2. Two angles are supplementary. One measures 112°. What does the other measure?

Supplementary angles sum to 180°: 180 − 112 = 68°.

3. Two lines cross, forming vertical angles. If one angle measures 47°, what does its vertical angle measure?

Vertical angles — the opposite pair formed by two crossing lines — are always equal.

4. Fill in the blank: a line that crosses two parallel lines, creating corresponding and alternate interior angle pairs, is called a ___.

A transversal cutting across two parallel lines is what creates all of the named equal-angle pairs in this lesson.

5. Fill in the blank: two supplementary angles measure (3x + 10)° and 2x°. Solving 5x + 10 = 180 gives x = ___.

5x + 10 = 180 → 5x = 170 → x = 34.
