The Quadratic Formula

Factoring only works when the numbers cooperate. This formula solves any quadratic equation, every time — and one piece of it tells you how many real solutions to expect before you even finish.

← Lesson 5 Lesson 7 → Contents Glossary →

Some quadratics — like x² + 4x + 1 = 0 — don't factor over the integers no matter how long you search. The quadratic formula solves any quadratic equation directly from its a, b, and c coefficients, no factoring required.

The formula

Formula For any quadratic ax² + bx + c = 0 (a ≠ 0), the solutions are:

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a)

The ± means there are generally two solutions — one from adding the square root, one from subtracting it.

The discriminant: how many solutions, before you finish

Definition The expression under the square root, b² − 4ac, is called the discriminant. Its sign alone tells you how many real solutions the equation has: positive → two distinct real solutions; zero → exactly one real solution (a repeated root); negative → no real solutions (the square root of a negative number isn't a real number).

Adjust a, b, and c and watch the discriminant, the number of real roots, and the parabola all update together.

Why the discriminant's sign controls the count The roots come from adding or subtracting √(discriminant) inside the formula. A positive discriminant has two different square roots (positive and negative), giving two different x-values. A discriminant of exactly 0 means adding or subtracting 0 changes nothing — both branches of ± collapse to the same value. A negative discriminant has no real square root at all, so the formula never produces a real number — matching the "no real solutions" case where the parabola never touches the x-axis.

Worked example

Solve 2x² + 3x − 5 = 0 Here a = 2, b = 3, c = −5.
Discriminant: b² − 4ac = 9 − 4(2)(−5) = 9 + 40 = 49.
x = (−3 ± √49) / (2·2) = (−3 ± 7) / 4.
x = 4/4 = 1, or x = −10/4 = −2.5.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. In the quadratic formula, what expression sits under the square root?

The discriminant is b² − 4ac, the exact expression that appears under the radical in x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a).

2. A quadratic has discriminant −12. How many real solutions does it have?

A negative discriminant means the square root of a negative number, which has no real value — so no real solutions exist.

3. Solve x² − 6x + 9 = 0 using the quadratic formula. What do you find?

Discriminant = 36 − 36 = 0, so x = (6 ± 0) / 2 = 3 — a single repeated solution.

4. Fill in the blank: the expression b² − 4ac, whose sign tells you how many real solutions a quadratic has, is called the ___.

The discriminant's sign — positive, zero, or negative — determines whether there are two, one, or zero real solutions.

5. Fill in the blank: for x² − 4 = 0, a = 1, b = 0, c = −4, so the discriminant is 16 and the equation has ___ real solution(s).

A positive discriminant (16 > 0) always means two distinct real solutions — here, x = 2 and x = −2.
