What Makes an Equation Quadratic

One term — the x² — changes everything about how an equation behaves and how many solutions it can have.

← Lesson 3 Lesson 5 → Contents Glossary →

Every equation in Unit 1 was linear — x appeared only to the first power, and its graph was a straight line with at most one solution. A quadratic equation introduces x², and that single change means its graph curves, and it can have up to two solutions instead of one.

The standard form

Definition A quadratic equation in standard form is ax² + bx + c = 0, where a, b, and c are constants and a ≠ 0. That last condition is not a technicality — if a = 0, the x² term disappears and the equation is just linear again. a, b, and c also directly control the shape of the equation's graph, a curve called a parabola.

Why a = 0 breaks it Drag the a slider to exactly 0: the curve straightens into a line, because the equation is no longer quadratic at all — it's just y = bx + c. This is why the definition insists a ≠ 0: without it, "quadratic" would just mean "linear in disguise."

What a, b, and c each control

Reading the graph a controls whether the parabola opens upward (a > 0) or downward (a < 0), and how narrow or wide it is. c is the y-intercept — where the graph crosses the y-axis, since plugging in x = 0 leaves y = c. b shifts the parabola sideways and combines with a and c to set the location of its highest or lowest point, called the vertex.
Not every quadratic-looking equation needs solving the same way x² − 9 = 0 (b = 0), 2x² + 5x = 0 (c = 0), and 3x² − 4x + 1 = 0 (all three terms present) are all quadratic equations. The number of nonzero terms doesn't change what makes them quadratic — a ≠ 0 is the only requirement — but it does change which solving method is fastest, which the next two lessons cover.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Which condition is required for ax² + bx + c = 0 to actually be a quadratic equation?

If a were 0, the x² term would vanish and the equation would just be linear — a ≠ 0 is what makes it quadratic.

2. In y = ax² + bx + c, what does c represent on the graph?

Plugging x = 0 into ax² + bx + c leaves just c, so c is exactly where the graph crosses the y-axis.

3. A parabola opens downward and is very narrow. What does that tell you about a?

Downward-opening means a < 0; narrow means |a| is large — together, a large negative number.

4. Fill in the blank: the curved graph of any quadratic equation is called a ___.

Every quadratic ax² + bx + c graphs as a parabola, opening up or down depending on the sign of a.

5. Fill in the blank: the highest or lowest point on a parabola is called its ___.

The vertex is the parabola's turning point — its minimum if it opens upward, its maximum if it opens downward.
