Laws of Exponents

A handful of short rules for combining powers — the toolkit every polynomial operation in this unit leans on.

← Lesson 6 Lesson 8 → Contents Glossary →

An exponent tells you how many times to multiply a base by itself: x⁴ means x·x·x·x. Every law in this lesson is really just that definition, applied carefully — none of it is arbitrary.

The core laws

Laws (a, b ≠ 0) Product of powers: xᵐ · xⁿ = xᵐ⁺ⁿ
Quotient of powers: xᵐ / xⁿ = xᵐ⁻ⁿ
Power of a power: (xᵐ)ⁿ = xᵐⁿ
Power of a product: (xy)ⁿ = xⁿyⁿ
Zero exponent: x⁰ = 1
Negative exponent: x⁻ⁿ = 1/xⁿ

Pick two exponents and an operation below and watch which law applies and why it produces that result.

Try It — Combine Two Powers of x
×

x³ × x² = x⁵

Why the exponents add instead of multiply x³ means x·x·x. x² means x·x. Multiplying them strings all five factors of x together: x·x·x·x·x = x⁵. The rule "add the exponents" isn't a separate fact to memorize — it's just counting how many x's end up multiplied together once you write both out fully.

The two rules that feel backwards at first

Zero and negative exponents x⁰ = 1 falls straight out of the quotient rule: xⁿ / xⁿ = xⁿ⁻ⁿ = x⁰, but anything divided by itself is also just 1 — so x⁰ must equal 1. x⁻ⁿ = 1/xⁿ falls out the same way: x⁰/xⁿ = x⁰⁻ⁿ = x⁻ⁿ, and 1/xⁿ is also exactly what x⁰/xⁿ equals. A negative exponent never means a negative number — it means "reciprocal."
Worked examples 2³ · 2⁴ = 2⁷ = 128 (add exponents, same base) · x⁸ / x³ = x⁵ (subtract exponents) · (x³)² = x⁶ (multiply exponents) · (2x)³ = 2³x³ = 8x³ (distribute the outer exponent) · 5⁻² = 1/5² = 1/25.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Simplify: x⁵ · x³

Multiplying powers with the same base adds the exponents: 5 + 3 = 8.

2. Simplify: x⁹ / x⁴

Dividing powers with the same base subtracts the exponents: 9 − 4 = 5.

3. Simplify: (x⁴)³

Raising a power to a power multiplies the exponents: 4 × 3 = 12.

4. Fill in the blank: for any nonzero x, x⁰ = ___.

Any nonzero number raised to the zero power equals 1 — it falls directly out of the quotient rule applied to xⁿ/xⁿ.

5. Fill in the blank: 7⁻² = ___ (write it as a fraction, e.g. 1/9).

A negative exponent means "take the reciprocal": 7⁻² = 1/7² = 1/49.
