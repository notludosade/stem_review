Factoring Trinomials

For x² + bx + c, factoring comes down to a single search: find two numbers with the right product and the right sum.

← Lesson 10 Lesson 12 → Contents Glossary →

Once a GCF is pulled out (or there isn't one), a trinomial like x² + bx + c can often be factored into two binomials — reversing the FOIL multiplication from Lesson 8. The whole method reduces to one search.

The number-pair search

Method (leading coefficient 1) To factor x² + bx + c: find two numbers that multiply to c and add to b. Call them p and q — the factored form is (x + p)(x + q). This works because FOILing (x + p)(x + q) gives x² + (p + q)x + pq, matching x² + bx + c term for term exactly when p + q = b and pq = c.

For x² + 7x + 12, try each candidate pair of factors of 12 below and see which one also adds to 7.

Why you check every pair Every pair of factors listed above has the right product (they all multiply to 12) — but only one pair also has the right sum. Both conditions have to hold at once; a pair that satisfies only one of them isn't the answer.
Worked example: factor x² + 7x + 12 Factors of 12: (1,12), (2,6), (3,4). Check sums: 1+12=13, 2+6=8, 3+4=7 ✓.
Factored form: (x + 3)(x + 4).
Check by FOILing back: x² + 4x + 3x + 12 = x² + 7x + 12 ✓
Watch the signs x² − 7x + 12 needs a pair that multiplies to positive 12 but adds to negative 7 — both numbers must be negative: −3 and −4. x² + x − 12 needs a pair multiplying to negative 12 (one positive, one negative) that adds to positive 1: 4 and −3. Getting the sign of c right first narrows the search immediately — same signs when c is positive, opposite signs when c is negative.

1. Factor: x² + 9x + 20

4 and 5 multiply to 20 and add to 9 — the only pair satisfying both conditions.

2. Factor: x² − 3x − 10

c = −10 is negative, so the numbers have opposite signs: −5 and 2 multiply to −10 and add to −3.

3. Before searching for a number pair on x² − 5x + 6, what does the positive c (= 6) tell you about the signs of the two numbers?

A positive c means the two numbers share a sign; whether that sign is positive or negative then depends on the sign of b — here negative, since b = −5.

4. Fill in the blank: x² + 8x + 12 factors as ___.

2 and 6 multiply to 12 and add to 8, giving (x + 2)(x + 6).

5. Fill in the blank: to factor x² + bx + c, you need two numbers whose product is c and whose ___ is b.

Both conditions — product equals c, sum equals b — must hold at once; a pair matching only one of them isn't the answer.
