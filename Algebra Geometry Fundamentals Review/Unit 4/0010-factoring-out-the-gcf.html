Factoring Out the GCF

Factoring is multiplication in reverse — and pulling out the greatest common factor first is the check you should run before any other factoring method.

← Lesson 9 Lesson 11 → Contents Glossary →

Factoring rewrites a polynomial as a product of simpler polynomials — the reverse of the multiplying you did in Unit 3. The very first thing to check on any factoring problem, before anything fancier, is whether every term shares a common factor that can be pulled out front.

Greatest common factor

Definition The greatest common factor (GCF) of a polynomial's terms is the largest expression that divides every term evenly — combining the largest shared numeric factor with the lowest power of any variable common to all terms. Factoring it out uses the distributive property in reverse: ab + ac = a(b + c).

Select a candidate GCF below and see whether it actually divides every term of 12x³ + 18x².

Try It — Find the GCF of 12x³ + 18x²

Why "greatest" matters, not just "a" common factor 3 divides both terms, and so does 6x — but neither is the greatest one. Stopping early leaves a polynomial that still has a common factor hiding inside the parentheses, meaning the factoring isn't finished. 12x² over-reaches — it doesn't divide 18x² evenly at all. Only 6x² is both large enough and small enough to be correct.
Worked example: factor 12x³ + 18x² GCF = 6x². Divide each term by it: 12x³/6x² = 2x, and 18x²/6x² = 3.
Factored form: 6x²(2x + 3).
Check by multiplying back: 6x²(2x + 3) = 12x³ + 18x² ✓
A three-term example Factor 8x³ − 12x² + 20x. GCF = 4x (numeric GCF of 8, 12, 20 is 4; lowest shared power of x is x¹). Dividing each term: 2x² − 3x + 5. Factored form: 4x(2x² − 3x + 5).

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. What is the GCF of 15x⁴ and 25x²?

The numeric GCF of 15 and 25 is 5; the lowest shared power of x between x⁴ and x² is x² — combined, 5x².

2. Factor: 9x² + 21x

The GCF of 9x² and 21x is 3x. Dividing each term by 3x gives 3x + 7, so the factored form is 3x(3x + 7) — using the greatest common factor, not just any common factor.

3. Why is 4(2x² + 6x) not a fully factored form of 8x² + 24x?

The true GCF of 8x² and 24x is 8x, not 4 — stopping at 4 leaves a factor of 2x still shared by every term inside the parentheses.

4. Fill in the blank: factoring 8x³ − 12x² + 20x completely gives ___.

GCF = 4x. Dividing each term: 8x³/4x = 2x², −12x²/4x = −3x, 20x/4x = 5, giving 4x(2x² − 3x + 5).

5. Fill in the blank: factoring out a GCF, ab + ac = a(b + c), is the ___ property used in reverse.

The distributive property normally expands a(b + c) into ab + ac; factoring out a GCF runs that exact process backwards.
