The Pythagorean Theorem

One equation connects all three sides of any right triangle — and it holds no matter how the triangle is stretched or shrunk.

← Lesson 15 Lesson 17 → Contents Glossary →

A right triangle has one 90° angle. Its two shorter sides — the legs — meet at that right angle; the longest side, opposite the right angle, is the hypotenuse. The Pythagorean Theorem is the exact relationship between all three.

The theorem

Theorem For a right triangle with legs a and b and hypotenuse c: a² + b² = c². This only applies to right triangles — the 90° angle is a required condition, not a detail.

Why it's squares, not the sides themselves The theorem is literally about area: build a square on each of the three sides, and the areas of the two smaller squares (a² and b²) add up exactly to the area of the largest square (c²) — for every right triangle, regardless of its size or proportions. That area relationship is the actual content of the theorem; "a + b = c" would be false for a right triangle (the two legs are always shorter, combined, than making a straight path would need — the hypotenuse is a shortcut, not a sum).

Solving for a missing side

Find the hypotenuse Legs 5 and 12. c² = 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169, so c = √169 = 13.
Find a missing leg Hypotenuse 17, one leg 8. Rearrange: b² = c² − a² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225, so b = √225 = 15. Solving for a leg subtracts instead of adds — the hypotenuse is always the largest term in the equation, never one of the two being added.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. What is the hypotenuse?

c² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100, so c = √100 = 10.

2. A right triangle has hypotenuse 13 and one leg 5. What is the other leg?

b² = 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144, so b = √144 = 12.

3. Why does a² + b² = c² only apply to right triangles?

The 90° angle is exactly what makes the sum of the two smaller squares equal the largest square — change that angle and the equality breaks.

4. Fill in the blank: in a right triangle, the longest side, opposite the right angle, is called the ___.

The hypotenuse is always the side across from the 90° angle, and always the longest of the three sides.

5. Fill in the blank: a right triangle has legs 12 and 16. Its hypotenuse is ___.

c² = 12² + 16² = 144 + 256 = 400, so c = √400 = 20.
