The Converse and Pythagorean Triples

Run the theorem backwards and it becomes a test: three side lengths alone can prove — or disprove — that a triangle has a right angle, with no protractor required.

← Lesson 16 Lesson 18 → Contents Glossary →

Lesson 16 started with "this is a right triangle" and computed a side. This lesson starts with just three numbers and asks the reverse question: do they even form a right triangle?

The converse

Theorem (the converse) Given three side lengths a, b, c (with c the longest), if a² + b² = c², the triangle is a right triangle. This isn't automatic just because the original theorem is true — a converse can be false even when the original statement is true — but for the Pythagorean Theorem specifically, the converse also holds, and it's provably true.

Type three side lengths and the checker will tell you whether they form a right triangle — and, if not, whether the triangle is acute or obtuse.

Why a² + b² vs. c² also tells you acute or obtuse If a² + b² > c² (the two shorter squares outweigh the longest), the angle opposite c is less than 90° — the triangle is acute. If a² + b² < c², that angle is more than 90° — the triangle is obtuse. The right-triangle case sits exactly on the boundary between these two, which is exactly why equality is the special, exact condition.

Pythagorean triples

Definition A Pythagorean triple is a set of three positive whole numbers (a, b, c) satisfying a² + b² = c². The most common ones — (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (8, 15, 17), (7, 24, 25) — are worth recognizing on sight, since they let you skip straight to an answer without a calculator.
Scaling a triple still works (3, 4, 5) is a triple, so is (6, 8, 10) — every term doubled — and (9, 12, 15) — every term tripled. Multiplying every side of a Pythagorean triple by the same positive number always produces another valid triple, because a² + b² = c² scales consistently: (ka)² + (kb)² = k²a² + k²b² = k²(a² + b²) = k²c² = (kc)².

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. A triangle has sides 9, 12, and 15. Is it a right triangle?

9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225, and 15² = 225 — they're equal, so this is a right triangle (it's (3,4,5) scaled by 3).

2. A triangle has sides 5, 6, and 9. Since 5² + 6² = 61 and 9² = 81, what kind of triangle is it?

Since a² + b² (61) < c² (81), the angle opposite the longest side exceeds 90° — the triangle is obtuse.

3. If (5, 12, 13) is a Pythagorean triple, which of these is also guaranteed to be one?

(10, 24, 26) is (5, 12, 13) with every term scaled by 2 — a scaled Pythagorean triple is always still a Pythagorean triple.

4. Fill in the blank: if a² + b² > c² for a triangle's sides (c the longest), the triangle is ___.

When the two smaller squares outweigh the largest, the angle opposite the longest side is less than 90° — the triangle is acute.

5. Fill in the blank: a set of three positive whole numbers satisfying a² + b² = c², such as (3, 4, 5), is called a ___.

A Pythagorean triple is exactly a whole-number solution to the Pythagorean relationship, and every scaled multiple of one is also a valid triple.
