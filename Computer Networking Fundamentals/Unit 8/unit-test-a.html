1. What does basic packet-filtering examine to decide whether to allow or block a packet?

Packet filtering works on header fields, reusing addressing and port concepts from earlier units.

2. Fill in the blank: the more restrictive firewall approach, which blocks all traffic except what's explicitly permitted, is called a(n) ___ approach.

An allowlist (default-deny) blocks anything not explicitly permitted, including unanticipated threats.

3. What does IP spoofing exploit?

Routers forward based on destination address and generally don't verify source address honesty, which spoofing exploits.

4. Why does loading a site over HTTPS instead of plain HTTP protect against a packet sniffer on the same network?

Encryption doesn't stop capture — it makes what's captured unreadable, unlike plain-text HTTP.

5. Fill in the blank: an attack where an attacker secretly positions themselves between two communicating parties, intercepting traffic while both believe they're talking directly, is called a ___ attack.

TLS certificate verification (Lesson 19) is the specific countermeasure against this attack.

6. What does a successful ping to a destination confirm, and what protocol does it use? Answer both parts:

(a) What does it confirm?

(b) What protocol does ping rely on?

Ping uses ICMP and only confirms Internet-layer reachability, not DNS, ports, or the application itself.

7. How does traceroute reveal each router along a path?

Traceroute exploits TTL expiration (Lesson 11) to reveal one hop at a time.

8. Fill in the blank: when troubleshooting bottom-up through the layers, the very first thing to check — before even attempting a ping — is whether the ___ layer is connected at all (Wi-Fi on, cable plugged in).

Starting at the Link layer and working upward rules out entire categories of causes before moving to the next layer.

9. Pinging a domain name fails to resolve, but pinging a known-good IP address (like 8.8.8.8) directly succeeds. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the successful ping to 8.8.8.8 rule out?

(b) What does the failed domain-name ping isolate the problem to?

Testing a known-good IP first isolates whether the Internet layer works, narrowing a failed domain-name ping specifically to DNS.

10. Fill in the blank: working through ping, DNS, port connectivity, and application response in order rules out an entire ___'s worth of possible causes at each step, instead of guessing randomly.

The layered model doubles as a systematic troubleshooting checklist, not just a way to organize protocols.
