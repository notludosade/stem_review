1. Why isn't a raw stream of bits enough for two computers to communicate?

Meaning comes from a shared rulebook (a protocol) both sides agree to in advance.

2. Fill in the blank: the delay before a single piece of data arrives, dominated by distance and hop count rather than pipe width, is called ___.

Latency is independent of bandwidth — a wider pipe doesn't shorten physical distance or hop count.

3. Why does packet switching fit bursty computer traffic better than circuit switching?

No exclusive reservation means idle periods are immediately available to other traffic.

4. What does encapsulation refer to?

Encapsulation is the wrapping process on the way out; decapsulation is the reverse.

5. Fill in the blank: a 48-bit identifier, permanently assigned by the manufacturer, meaningful only for the immediate next hop, is called a ___.

A MAC address is replaced at every hop, unlike the IP address, which stays the same for the whole journey.

6. A subnet has 4 host bits. How many usable host addresses does it have?

Usable hosts = 2^h − 2, subtracting the reserved network and broadcast addresses.

7. Why did IPv6 use 128-bit addresses instead of simply extending IPv4's address size slightly?

Since a protocol change was unavoidable regardless of the new size, going big enough to avoid repeating the overhaul made sense.

8. Why can a chain of routers deliver a packet without any single router knowing the full route in advance?

Local, next-hop-only decisions repeated at each hop are sufficient, like following highway signs.

9. Fill in the blank: the technique that rewrites a private device's address to a router's shared public address for outgoing traffic is called ___.

NAT lets an entire private network share a single public IP address transparently.

10. Why is DNS organized hierarchically (root, TLD, authoritative) instead of one giant lookup table?

Hierarchical delegation keeps each server's responsibility manageable, mirroring routing's local-knowledge principle.

11. What does a port number identify, on top of what an IP address already identifies?

An IP address identifies the device; a port identifies the program on that device.

12. Fill in the blank: the first message a client sends to begin TCP's three-way handshake is a ___.

The handshake proceeds SYN, then SYN-ACK, then ACK.

13. A live video call favors dropping a late packet over waiting for it to be retransmitted. Which protocol fits this, and why?

UDP's willingness to drop data instead of pausing for retransmission suits real-time applications.

14. What four parts make up every HTTP message, in order?

Start line, headers, blank line, and optional body make up every HTTP message.

15. Fill in the blank: the property of HTTP where the server does not automatically remember previous requests is called being ___.

Cookies are a workaround built on top of stateless HTTP, not a violation of it.

16. Why does TLS verify a server's identity with a certificate, rather than only encrypting the data?

A certificate protects against connecting to an impersonator, distinct from simply encrypting data in transit.

17. What happens, in terms of earlier lessons, when client code calls connect() on a TCP socket?

connect() is the client-side trigger for TCP's full handshake, hidden behind one function call.

18. Fill in the blank: in a peer-to-peer network, a device that can act as both client and server simultaneously is called a ___.

Peers play both roles at once, unlike the fixed client/server split.

19. Why is a default-deny (allowlist) firewall approach generally stronger than default-allow (denylist)?

Default-deny doesn't require anticipating every specific threat in advance.

20. Pinging a domain name fails, but pinging a known-good IP address (like 8.8.8.8) directly succeeds. What does this isolate the problem to?

A successful ping to a known-good IP rules out the Internet layer, isolating a failed domain-name ping to DNS.
