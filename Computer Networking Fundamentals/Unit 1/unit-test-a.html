1. What are the two required components of any network, according to this unit's definition?

Even two devices connected by a single link already satisfy the definition of a network.

2. Fill in the blank: an agreed-upon set of rules that lets two independently built pieces of software understand each other's data is called a ___.

A protocol is the shared rulebook that gives a raw stream of bits actual meaning.

3. Why isn't a raw stream of bits enough for two computers to communicate?

Meaning comes from a shared rulebook both sides agree to in advance, not from the raw signal itself.

4. Fill in the blank: a network confined to a small physical area, like a single home or office building, is called a ___.

A LAN is local in scope; a WAN spans a much larger geographic area, typically across infrastructure the owner doesn't fully control.

5. Why is "the internet is one giant, centrally-owned network" an inaccurate description?

Ownership and management are split across countless independent networks that simply agree to cooperate.

6. A home network connects to the wider internet. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the company providing that connection called?

(b) The home network itself, confined to one house, is best classified as which kind of network?

(a) An ISP is the on-ramp connecting a smaller local network to the wider internet. (b) A single home's network, confined to one physical location, is a LAN.

7. What does bandwidth measure?

Bandwidth is a ceiling on capacity, not a guarantee of what's actually achieved — that's throughput.

8. Fill in the blank: the delay before a single piece of data arrives at its destination, dominated by distance and hop count, is called ___.

Latency is the delay itself, distinct from bandwidth (capacity) and throughput (actual achieved rate).

9. A user's video call is laggy (noticeable delay between speaking and being heard) even after upgrading to a much higher-bandwidth plan. What does this most likely indicate, and why doesn't the upgrade help? Answer both parts:

(a) What kind of problem is this most likely?

(b) Why doesn't more bandwidth fix it?

Bandwidth and latency are independent properties — more capacity doesn't reduce the delay caused by distance or hop count.

10. Fill in the blank: the rate of data actually, successfully transferred in real-world conditions — always at or below the bandwidth ceiling because of overhead — is called ___.

Throughput is what you actually got in practice, after accounting for headers, congestion, and retransmission.
