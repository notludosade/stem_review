Unit 1 Test — Version B

What Networks Actually Are. 10 questions covering every lesson in this unit. Score 90% or higher to clear this unit.

← Course Contents ← Version A

1. Two laptops are connected by a single cable and nothing else. According to this unit's definition, do they form a network?

Nodes plus a link is the whole definition — no router, internet connection, or minimum device count is required.

2. Fill in the blank: the flashlight-signaling analogy's "agree in advance whether a short flash means a dot" represents a real network concept called a ___.

A protocol is exactly this kind of pre-agreed rulebook, just applied to bits instead of light flashes.

3. Two computers exchange a stream of 1s and 0s with no agreed protocol in place. What happens?

The bits themselves carry no built-in meaning; a shared protocol is what makes them interpretable.

4. Fill in the blank: a network that spans a large geographic area, connecting LANs in different cities or countries, is called a ___.

A WAN spans distance, typically relying on infrastructure — like leased fiber or another company's cables — the owner doesn't fully control.

5. Why does the internet have no single "off switch" that could take the whole thing down at once?

Decentralized ownership is exactly what removes any single point of total control.

6. Data travels from a home LAN, through an ISP, across a backbone network, to a destination server's LAN in another country. Answer both parts:

(a) How many independently owned networks does this path realistically cross?

(b) Does any single one of those networks own or control the entire path end to end?

A single web request typically crosses many independently owned and managed networks, none of which controls the full path.

7. In the garden hose analogy, what does the width (diameter) of the hose represent?

Hose width maps to bandwidth (capacity); hose length maps to latency (delay).

8. Fill in the blank: even at the speed of light, sending a signal from New York to Tokyo takes real, measurable time — this delay is called ___.

Latency is dominated by physical distance and the number of intermediate hops, not by bandwidth.

9. A very large file transfer and a tiny single request-response exchange happen over the same connection. Answer both parts:

(a) For the large file transfer, which factor dominates total time?

(b) For the tiny request-response, which factor tends to dominate?

Latency is roughly a fixed cost paid once (or twice, round trip); it swamps a tiny transfer but is negligible next to a large one where bandwidth dominates.

10. Fill in the blank: bandwidth is the posted speed limit; ___ is the speed you actually drove, after accounting for traffic, red lights, and merging.

Throughput is the real-world achieved rate, always at or below the bandwidth ceiling.
