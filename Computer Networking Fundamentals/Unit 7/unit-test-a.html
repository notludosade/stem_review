1. What is a socket?

A socket is a software handle for network I/O, conceptually similar to a file handle.

2. Fill in the blank: the socket operation a client calls to initiate a connection to a specific address and port, triggering TCP's handshake, is ___.

Connect() is the client's call to initiate a connection, distinct from a server's bind/listen/accept sequence.

3. Why does a UDP socket skip connect(), listen(), and accept()?

UDP's connectionless design means there's no handshake step for those calls to perform.

4. What's the defining asymmetry of the client-server model?

The server waits and listens; the client initiates — a fixed, asymmetric relationship.

5. Fill in the blank: in a peer-to-peer network, a device that can act as both client and server simultaneously is called a ___.

Peers play both roles at once, unlike the fixed client/server split.

6. Comparing client-server and peer-to-peer file distribution. Answer both parts:

(a) What's a practical advantage of client-server?

(b) What's a practical advantage of peer-to-peer for distributing the same file to many people?

Client-server centralizes control and consistency; P2P distributes load and resilience across many participants.

7. In the end-to-end traced exchange, in what order are headers added to the outgoing message?

Encapsulation proceeds Transport, then Internet, then Link, matching Lesson 6's order.

8. Fill in the blank: when a message arrives at a server, the destination ___ number is what determines which specific listening program receives it.

Demultiplexing by destination port delivers arriving data to the correct program, as covered in Lesson 14.

9. Why does the traced exchange for a tiny custom message apply just as well to loading a full website? Answer both parts:

(a) What's the underlying reason?

(b) What might additionally be layered in for a real HTTPS website that wasn't in the toy example?

Layering keeps the lower layers indifferent to payload content; TLS is the one addition an HTTPS site layers in before Application data flows.

10. Fill in the blank: as the message's packet passes through each router on its way to the server, its ___ field is decremented by 1 at every hop.

TTL is decremented at every router hop, exactly as covered in Lesson 11.
