Parametrized Surfaces and Surface Integrals

A surface is a two-parameter map; its cross product supplies both local area and orientation.

← Lesson 18 Lesson 20 → Contents Glossary →

From parameters to area

DefinitionA parametrized surface is r(u,v). Its tangent vectors are ru and rv; the area element is dS = |ru × rv| du dv. For a vector field F, oriented flux is ∬S F · (ru × rv) du dv.
Worked ExampleFor r(u,v)=⟨u,v,u+v⟩, ru=⟨1,0,1⟩ and rv=⟨0,1,1⟩, so ru×rv=⟨−1,−1,1⟩ and dS=√3 du dv.
Why This MattersSurface integrals measure curved area, mass on a shell, and fluid flux through a boundary.
Try It — Plane z = au + bv

Check your recall

1. Which vector is normal to a parametrized surface?

The cross product is perpendicular to both tangent directions.

2. For r(u,v)=⟨u,v,u+v⟩, |ru×rv| = ___.

|⟨−1,−1,1⟩|=√3.

3. Reversing a surface's orientation does what to flux?

Reversing orientation negates the chosen normal.
