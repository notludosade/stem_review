The Definite Integral

Riemann sums were an approximation. Taking the limit as the rectangles get infinitely thin turns that approximation into an exact number — with its own name and notation.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

1. Definition and notation

Definition — Definite Integral The definite integral of f from a to b is the limit of a Riemann sum as the number of rectangles n → ∞: ∫ab f(x) dx = lim (n→∞) Σ f(xᵢ) Δx. The ∫ symbol is an elongated S for "sum," dx marks the variable being integrated, and a and b are the limits of integration. Left, right, and midpoint sums (Lesson 1) all converge to this same limit for a continuous f.

2. Signed area

Fact — Net Signed Area A rectangle's height is negative wherever f(x) < 0, so a definite integral measures net signed area, not plain area: regions above the x-axis add positively, regions below subtract. ∫02π sin(x) dx = 0, for instance, because the positive hump on [0, π] exactly cancels the negative dip on [π, 2π] — even though the plain (unsigned) area enclosed is not zero at all.

3. Proving a limit exactly, with summation formulas

Lesson 1 estimated ∫04 x² dx by rectangles and landed near 21.33. Closed-form summation formulas make it possible to take that limit exactly, with no approximation left over.

Worked Example Right sum for f(x) = x² on [0, 4] with n rectangles: Δx = 4/n, sample points xᵢ = 4i/n. Sum = Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ (4i/n)² · (4/n) = (64/n³) Σ i² = (64/n³) · n(n+1)(2n+1)/6, using the summation formula Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ i² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6. Expanding and taking n → ∞, every lower-order term vanishes, leaving exactly 64/3 — matching Lesson 1's rectangles, which were only ever approximating this same limit.

4. Watching the exact limit emerge

Slide to larger n and watch the right Riemann sum for x² on [0, 4] close in on 64/3 ≈ 21.33333 — the value Lesson 3's Fundamental Theorem will make trivial to find directly.

Check your recall

1. A definite integral ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx is defined as the limit of a Riemann sum as:

More, thinner rectangles come from letting n grow without bound — which simultaneously forces Δx = (b−a)/n toward zero, not infinity.

2. When a curve dips below the x-axis, the definite integral over that region is ___.

Rectangle heights below the x-axis are negative numbers, so their contribution to the sum — and to the limiting definite integral — subtracts rather than adds.

3. The exact value of ∫₀⁴ x² dx is:

Taking the limit of the right Riemann sum (64/n³)·n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 as n → ∞ leaves exactly 64/3, matching the value the rectangles from Lesson 1 were closing in on.
