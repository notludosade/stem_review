Radius and Interval of Convergence

A power series is a different animal for every x plugged in — sometimes a convergent number, sometimes a divergent mess. The radius of convergence draws the boundary.

← Lesson 7 Lesson 9 → Contents Glossary →

1. Where a power series behaves

Definition — Power Series, Radius of Convergence A power series centered at a has the form Σ (from n=0 to ∞) cₙ(x − a)ⁿ. There is always a radius of convergence R (possibly 0 or ∞) such that the series converges absolutely for |x − a| < R and diverges for |x − a| > R. R is found by applying the ratio test to the series and solving the resulting inequality for |x − a|.
Method — Finding the Interval of Convergence R only settles the open interval (a − R, a + R). The two endpoints x = a − R and x = a + R must be checked separately, plugging each in and testing the resulting numeric series with the tools from Lessons 2–6 — the ratio test is always inconclusive exactly at the endpoints (L = 1), so a different test is required there.
Worked Example For Σ xⁿ/n: ratio test gives limₙ→∞ |xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1)| · |n/xⁿ| = |x| · limₙ→∞ n/(n+1) = |x|. Converges when |x| < 1, so R = 1. Check endpoints: at x = 1, Σ1/n is the harmonic series — diverges. At x = −1, Σ(−1)ⁿ/n converges by the alternating series test. Interval of convergence: [−1, 1) — closed on the left, open on the right.

Check your recall

1. The radius of convergence R is typically found using:

The ratio test naturally produces an inequality in |x − a| after taking the limit of consecutive-term ratios, which is exactly the boundary condition that defines R.

2. The ratio test is always inconclusive (L = 1) exactly at the interval's ___

By construction, R is exactly the boundary where the ratio test's limit equals 1 — so the two x-values at distance R from the center always require a separate, different test.

3. Σ n!·xⁿ has ratio limₙ→∞ |x|·(n+1) = ∞ for any x ≠ 0. This series:

An infinite ratio for every nonzero x means the series diverges everywhere except its center — the factorial coefficients grow too fast for any nonzero x to tame, giving the smallest possible radius, R = 0.
