The Candidates Test

A local max is only a peak among its neighbors. Finding the single tallest point on the whole interval takes one more step — checking every candidate at once.

← Lesson 2 Lesson 4 → Contents Glossary →

Lesson 2's First Derivative Test finds every local extremum — but a closed interval [a, b] can also have its true maximum or minimum sitting right at an endpoint, where no sign change ever happens. The Candidates Test checks every possible location at once, so nothing gets missed.

1. The test

Test — Candidates Test To find the absolute maximum and minimum of a continuous f on [a, b]: (1) find every critical point of f inside (a, b); (2) evaluate f at each critical point and at both endpoints a and b; (3) the largest value found is the absolute maximum, the smallest is the absolute minimum. The Extreme Value Theorem (Lesson 2) is what guarantees this search can't come up empty.
Worked Example f(x) = x³ − 3x on [−2, 2] — the same function as Lesson 2. Critical points inside (−2, 2): x = −1 and x = 1. Candidates: x = −2, −1, 1, 2. Evaluating: f(−2) = −2, f(−1) = 2, f(1) = −2, f(2) = 2. The absolute maximum, 2, is hit twice — once at a critical point (x=−1), once at an endpoint (x=2). The absolute minimum, −2, ties the same way.

This tie is exactly why every candidate has to be checked: an endpoint can match, or even beat, every local extremum inside the interval, and there's no way to know which without evaluating all of them.

Check your recall

1. To find absolute extrema on [a,b], evaluate f at:

Skipping either critical points or endpoints can miss the true absolute extremum — the Candidates Test requires checking both groups together, not just one.

2. The Candidates Test says the absolute maximum is the ___ value among all candidates.

After evaluating f at every critical point and endpoint, the absolute maximum is simply whichever candidate value is largest — no further test is needed once the list is complete.

3. For f(x) = x³ − 3x on [−2, 2], the absolute maximum value is:

f(−1) = 2 and f(2) = 2 — the largest value among all four candidates, so 2 is the absolute maximum (reached at two different x-values).
