Behavior of Implicit Relations

Every test this unit built — direction, extrema, concavity — still works when a curve is never solved for y. Only the way dy/dx gets found has to change.

← Lesson 6 Contents Glossary →

Every function this unit has analyzed was already solved for y — f(x) = something. Not every curve cooperates: x² + y² = 25 defines a perfectly good circle, but solving for y forces an awkward split into two separate square-root functions. Implicit differentiation sidesteps the split entirely.

1. Implicit differentiation

Method — Differentiating implicitly Implicit differentiation is needed when a relation cannot easily be solved for y. Differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to x, treating y as a function of x — any term with y needs the Chain Rule, contributing a factor of dy/dx. Then collect every dy/dx term on one side and solve for it algebraically.
Worked Example x² + y² = 25 (a circle of radius 5). Differentiating both sides: 2x + 2y(dy/dx) = 0. Solving: dy/dx = −x/y. At (3, 4): dy/dx = −3/4. At (3, −4): dy/dx = 3/4. No square root was ever needed — the slope came directly from the original equation, valid on both halves of the circle at once.

2. Reading the curve's shape from dy/dx

Once dy/dx is known, every tool from Lessons 2 through 5 still applies exactly as before — the sign of dy/dx still says increasing or decreasing, and dy/dx = 0 (or undefined) still marks a critical point.

Worked Example On the upper half of x² + y² = 25 (where y > 0), dy/dx = −x/y is positive for x < 0 (increasing, left side climbing toward the top) and negative for x > 0 (decreasing, right side falling away). At x = 0, dy/dx = 0 — the horizontal tangent at the very top of the circle, (0, 5), which the First Derivative Test would call a local maximum of that upper branch.

3. Reading it live

Drag the point along the upper half of the circle. The readout computes dy/dx from the implicit formula −x/y (never from a square-root derivative) and classifies the curve's direction there.

Check your recall

1. Implicit differentiation is needed when a relation:

A relation like x² + y² = 25 is perfectly differentiable — it just isn't already isolated as y = f(x), and solving for y would require splitting into two separate square-root pieces. Implicit differentiation avoids that split.

2. Differentiating x² + y² = 25 implicitly and solving for dy/dx gives dy/dx = ___.

2x + 2y(dy/dx) = 0 rearranges to 2y(dy/dx) = −2x, and dividing both sides by 2y leaves dy/dx = −x/y.

3. On the circle x²+y²=25, the tangent is horizontal at the point:

dy/dx = −x/y equals 0 only when x = 0 (and y ≠ 0), which happens at (0, 5) on the upper half. At (5, 0), dy/dx is undefined instead — a vertical, not horizontal, tangent.
