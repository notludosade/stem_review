Unit 4: Contextual Applications of Differentiation

Unit 5: Analytical Applications of Differentiation

Lesson 1

The Mean Value Theorem

Somewhere between two points on a smooth curve, the instantaneous rate of change must match the average rate of change — no gap allowed.

 Lesson 2

Extrema and the First Derivative Test

The sign of f′ is a map of the whole function's terrain — where it climbs, where it descends, and exactly where a peak or valley must be hiding.

 Lesson 3

The Candidates Test

A local max is only a peak among its neighbors. Finding the single tallest point on the whole interval takes one more step — checking every candidate at once.

 Lesson 4

Concavity and the Second Derivative Test

f′ told you which way the curve is heading. f″ tells you how it's bending — and gives a shortcut for classifying extrema without a sign chart.

 Lesson 5

Sketching Graphs from f, f′, and f″

Every tool from this unit — direction, extrema, and concavity — combines into one skill: reading three related graphs as a single connected story.

 Lesson 6

Optimization Problems

The Candidates Test was never just an abstract exercise — it's the exact tool that turns a real design constraint into a single best answer.

 Lesson 7

Behavior of Implicit Relations

Every test this unit built — direction, extrema, concavity — still works when a curve is never solved for y. Only the way dy/dx gets found has to change.

Unit 6: Integration and Accumulation of Change

Lesson 1

Riemann Sums and Approximating Area

Units 1–5 built the derivative. Unit 6 turns around and asks the opposite question: given a rate, how much has actually accumulated? It starts with rectangles.

 Lesson 2

The Definite Integral

Riemann sums were an approximation. Taking the limit as the rectangles get infinitely thin turns that approximation into an exact number — with its own name and notation.

 Lesson 3

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 1, and Accumulation Functions

A definite integral with a variable upper bound is itself a function of that bound — and its derivative turns out to need no new work at all.

 Lesson 4

Properties of Definite Integrals

Zero-width intervals, reversed bounds, constant multiples, sums, and splitting an interval in two — the algebra that makes integrals easy to manipulate.

 Lesson 5

FTC Part 2 and Net Change

An antiderivative turns a definite integral into simple subtraction — and reveals the difference between net displacement and total distance traveled.

 Lesson 6

Antiderivatives and Indefinite Integrals

FTC Part 2 needs an antiderivative to evaluate a definite integral. This lesson builds a toolkit for finding one — by running differentiation rules in reverse.

 Lesson 7

Integration by u-Substitution

The table only matches an integrand exactly. u-substitution is the Chain Rule run backward — it turns a composed, messier integral into one the table already knows.

 Lesson 8

Long Division, Completing the Square, and Partial Fractions

Some rational integrands need algebra before any integration rule applies at all — reshaping them into pieces the earlier lessons already know how to handle.

 Lesson 9

Integration by Parts, Improper Integrals, and Selecting Techniques

One more technique for products that don't substitute cleanly, one new kind of integral whose bounds stretch to infinity, and a closing guide for picking the right tool.

Unit 7: Differential Equations

Lesson 1

Modeling and Verifying Differential Equations

Unit 6 built a toolkit for antidifferentiation. Unit 7 puts it to work solving equations that describe a function through its own rate of change.

 Lesson 2

Slope Fields

A differential equation gives a slope at every point in the plane, before any solving happens at all. Plotting all those slopes at once is a slope field.

 Lesson 3

Euler's Method

Following a slope field by eye gives a rough sketch. Euler's Method turns "follow the arrows" into an exact numerical procedure.

 Lesson 4

Separation of Variables

Euler's Method approximates. This lesson solves — a technique that finds an exact formula for every solution of certain differential equations at once.

 Lesson 5

Exponential Models with Differential Equations

"Rate of change proportional to the current amount" is the single most common phrase in applied differential equations.

 Lesson 6

Logistic Models with Differential Equations

Exponential growth never slows down — real populations do, as resources run out. The logistic model builds a limit into the differential equation itself.

Unit 8: Applications of Integration

Lesson 1

Average Value of a Function

Unit 7 solved equations for functions. Unit 8 turns back to the definite integral itself, using it to compute averages, areas, volumes, and lengths.

 Lesson 2

Position, Velocity, and Applied Accumulation

The Net Change Theorem turns motion problems and rate-in-context problems into the same pattern: integrate a rate to find a total.

 Lesson 3

Area Between Curves

A single integral found the area under one curve. Subtracting one function from another before integrating finds the area trapped between two.

 Lesson 4

Area Between Curves — More Than Two Intersections

When two curves cross more than twice, "top minus bottom" isn't fixed for the whole region — it flips at every crossing, so the integral has to be split.

 Lesson 5

Cross Sections: Squares and Semicircles

A 2D base region can be the footprint of a 3D solid. Stack a known cross-sectional shape at every x, and integrating the area gives the volume.

 Lesson 6

The Disc Method

Revolve a 2D region around a line, and every cross section becomes a circle. Cross sections specialize to one shape.

 Lesson 7

The Washer Method

When the region being revolved doesn't touch the axis, every slice sweeps out a ring instead of a solid disc — subtract the hole.

 Lesson 8

Arc Length of a Smooth Curve

Every technique so far has measured area or volume under a curve. This one measures the curve itself — how long is it, if you walked along it?

Unit 9: Parametric, Polar & Vector-Valued Functions

Lesson 1

Parametric Equations

Unit 8 closed by measuring a path traced over time. Unit 9 opens by describing that path directly — x and y each as their own function of a parameter t.

 Lesson 2

Second Derivatives and Arc Length

dy/dx was itself built from two rates against t. Differentiating it again — and measuring how far the point actually travels — both lean on that same trick.

 Lesson 3

Vector-Valued Functions

x(t) and y(t) have been two separate functions so far. Bundling them into one arrow-valued function r(t) makes differentiating and integrating a curve feel like differentiating a single object.

 Lesson 4

Motion in Space

Unit 8 tracked a particle moving along a single line. Vector-valued functions let the same ideas describe a particle moving through the plane.

 Lesson 5

Polar Coordinates

Every curve so far has located a point by how far right and how far up. Polar coordinates locate it by how far out and which way — a parametric curve in disguise.

 Lesson 6

Area of Polar Regions

Unit 8 swept rectangles sideways to build area under a curve. A polar region is swept a different way — with thin wedges, pivoting around the pole.

Unit 10: Infinite Sequences and Series

Lesson 1

Convergence and Geometric Series

Adding infinitely many numbers sounds like it should always blow up to infinity. Sometimes the running total settles on a single, finite number instead.

 Lesson 2

The Divergence Test and Integral Test

Most series don't have a clean formula like the geometric series. These two tests trade the exact sum for a faster yes-or-no answer.

 Lesson 3

p-Series and Comparison Tests

One family of series is so well understood it becomes a ruler — every other series can be measured against it.

 Lesson 4

The Alternating Series Test

The harmonic series diverges. Just flip every other sign, and it converges — a genuinely different mechanism for taming an infinite sum.

 Lesson 5

The Ratio Test and Absolute Convergence

Factorials and exponentials defeat comparison tests. The ratio test measures a series against itself instead.

 Lesson 6

The Alternating Series Error Bound

Knowing exactly how far a partial sum can be from the true answer, without computing infinitely many terms.

 Lesson 7

Taylor Polynomials and the Lagrange Error Bound

A polynomial matching a function's derivatives at one point approximates it nearby — and the error can be capped without knowing the true value.

 Lesson 8

Radius and Interval of Convergence

A power series behaves differently for every x plugged in. The radius of convergence draws the boundary.

 Lesson 9

Taylor Series and Power Series Representations

Let the polynomial's degree run to infinity, and — for the right functions — the approximation error vanishes completely. The final lesson of the course.
