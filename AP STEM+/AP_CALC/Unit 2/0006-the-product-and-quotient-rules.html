The Product and Quotient Rules

Why (f·g)′ is not f′·g′ — and the one rule that looks almost, but not quite, like the other.

← Lesson 5 Lesson 7 → Contents Glossary →

It's tempting to guess that the derivative of a product is the product of the derivatives — it isn't. Multiplying two functions together tangles their rates of change, and untangling that requires a rule with two terms, not one.

1. The Product Rule

Rule Product Rule: (f·g)′ = f′·g + f·g′. In words: derivative of the first times the second, plus the first times the derivative of the second.
Worked Example For p(x) = x²·sin(x): let f(x) = x² (so f′(x) = 2x) and g(x) = sin(x) (so g′(x) = cos(x)). By the Product Rule: p′(x) = 2x·sin(x) + x²·cos(x).

2. The Quotient Rule

Rule Quotient Rule: (f/g)′ = (f′·g − f·g′) / g². Same two cross-terms as the Product Rule, but subtracted instead of added, and divided by g squared.

The numerator looks almost like the Product Rule's — same two pieces, f′g and fg′ — which is exactly why it's easy to mix the two rules up. The subtraction, and the g² on the bottom, are what make this one different.

Worked Example For q(x) = sin(x) / (x + 2): let f(x) = sin(x) (so f′(x) = cos(x)) and g(x) = x + 2 (so g′(x) = 1). By the Quotient Rule: q′(x) = [cos(x)·(x + 2) − sin(x)·1] / (x + 2)².

Check your recall

1. (f·g)′ equals:

The Product Rule adds two cross-terms — derivative of the first times the second, plus the first times the derivative of the second. Multiplying the two derivatives together (f′g′) is a common but incorrect guess.

2. (f/g)′ equals:

The Quotient Rule's numerator is the Product Rule's numerator with a minus sign in place of the plus — f′g − fg′ — and the sign matters: swapping the order of subtraction flips the whole answer's sign.

3. For p(x) = x²·sin(x), p′(x) = 2x·sin(x) + x²·___.

With f(x) = x² and g(x) = sin(x), the Product Rule's second term is f(x)·g′(x) = x²·cos(x).

These two rules get confused more than almost anything else in this unit, because they share the same two cross-terms. If a homework problem's answer looks like it's off by a sign, that mix-up is the first thing worth checking — and worth asking about if it isn't obvious which rule was needed.
