Related Rates

Two quantities are locked together by an equation. Differentiate that equation with respect to time, and the rate of one tells you the rate of the other.

← Lesson 2 Lesson 4 → Contents Glossary →

Lesson 2's chain — position, velocity, acceleration — was one quantity feeding into the next through time. Related rates is the same idea turned sideways: two different quantities, tied together by a single equation, both changing as time passes.

1. The five-step strategy

Formula — The procedure (1) Draw a diagram and label every changing quantity. (2) Write an equation relating those quantities — usually geometry (area, volume, Pythagorean theorem). (3) Differentiate implicitly with respect to t, treating every quantity as a function of time. (4) Substitute the known values and rates. (5) Solve for the rate you want. The equation from step 2 must hold at every instant, not just the one you care about — that's what makes differentiating it valid.
Worked Example — Expanding circle A circle's area is A = πr². Differentiating both sides with respect to t: dA/dt = 2πr · dr/dt. If r = 4 cm and dr/dt = 3 cm/s, then dA/dt = 2π(4)(3) = 24π ≈ 75.4 cm²/s — notice r's numeric value had to be plugged in only after differentiating, never before.

2. The sliding ladder

A 13-foot ladder leans against a wall. Its base and top are related by the Pythagorean theorem: x² + y² = 13², where x is the base's distance from the wall and y is the top's height. As the base slides away from the wall at a fixed rate, the top slides down — and related rates tells you exactly how fast.

Formula — Differentiating implicitly Differentiating x² + y² = 169 with respect to t: 2x·(dx/dt) + 2y·(dy/dt) = 0, so dy/dt = −(x/y)·(dx/dt). The minus sign is doing real work here — it says y decreases whenever x increases, exactly what "the top slides down as the base slides out" means.

Drag the point along the arc to reposition the ladder. The base slides away from the wall at a fixed dx/dt = 2 ft/s; the readout computes how fast the top is sliding down at that exact position.

Check your recall

1. In the related-rates strategy, implicit differentiation with respect to t should happen:

Plugging in a specific numeric value before differentiating "freezes" that variable, turning it into a constant whose derivative is 0 — destroying the very relationship you need. Numbers go in only after the derivative is taken.

2. Differentiating A = πr² with respect to t gives dA/dt = ___ · dr/dt.

The power rule and chain rule together give d/dt[πr²] = π · 2r · dr/dt = 2πr · dr/dt — r is being treated as a function of t, so its derivative dr/dt tags along by the chain rule.

3. For the sliding ladder, as the base slides away from the wall (dx/dt > 0), the sign of dy/dt is:

dy/dt = −(x/y)·(dx/dt), and x, y, dx/dt are all positive throughout the slide (both base distance and height stay positive until the ladder hits the ground) — so dy/dt is negative the entire time, matching "the top continuously slides down."
