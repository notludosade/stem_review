Straight-Line Motion

Position, velocity, and acceleration are three views of the same story — each one the derivative of the last.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

Lesson 1 said a derivative is a rate of change, with units. Motion along a line is the single richest example of that idea — three quantities stacked in a chain of derivatives, each one telling a different part of the same story.

1. Position, velocity, and acceleration

Formula Let s(t) be an object's position at time t. Then velocity is v(t) = s′(t), and acceleration is a(t) = v′(t) = s″(t). Velocity is the rate position changes; acceleration is the rate velocity changes. Each arrow in that chain is just one more derivative.
Formula — Speed, and the sign of velocity Speed is |v(t)| — velocity with its sign stripped off. Velocity's sign gives direction: v(t) > 0 means moving in the positive direction, v(t) < 0 means moving in the negative direction, and v(t) = 0 means momentarily at rest.

Try It — Position, Velocity, and Acceleration Together

s(t) = t³ − 6t² + 9t (meters), t ∈ [0, 5] seconds. This is a classic "back and forth" motion: the object moves, stops, reverses, stops again, and reverses once more.

2. Speeding up vs. slowing down

Formula — The rule An object is speeding up when velocity and acceleration have the same sign (both positive or both negative), and slowing down when they have opposite signs. This is the one fact in this lesson that trips people up — it's about matching signs, not about whether acceleration itself is positive.
Worked Example For s(t) = t³ − 6t² + 9t: v(t) = 3t² − 12t + 9, a(t) = 6t − 12. At t = 0.5: v(0.5) = 3.75 > 0 and a(0.5) = −9 < 0 — opposite signs, so the object is slowing down, even though acceleration is negative (a negative acceleration doesn't automatically mean "slowing down"; it depends on velocity's sign too).

Check your recall

1. An object has v(t) = −4 and a(t) = −2 at some instant. The object is:

Velocity and acceleration are both negative — the same sign — so the object is speeding up, just in the negative direction. "At rest" would require v(t) = 0, and "changing course" would require v(t) to actually cross zero, neither of which is given here.

2. s(t) = t² − 4t models a position. The object is momentarily at rest when v(t) = ___.

"Momentarily at rest" is the defining condition v(t) = 0 — for this s(t), v(t) = 2t − 4 = 0 gives t = 2, the instant the object pauses before (presumably) reversing.

3. An object has v(t) = 3 and a(t) = −5 at some instant. The object is:

Velocity is positive and acceleration is negative — opposite signs — so the object is slowing down. It's still moving forward right now (v(t) ≠ 0), just decelerating, which rules out "at rest," and "changing shape" isn't a motion concept at all.
