1. M(t) is the mass of ice remaining in kilograms, t minutes after melting starts. The units of M′(t) are:

A derivative's units are always (units of output)/(units of input): kilograms per minute. Flipping the ratio, multiplying instead of dividing, and squaring the input's units (that's acceleration's unit, one derivative further) are the three distractors.

2. W(t) is a company's revenue in dollars, t days after a product launch. Suppose W′(10) = 450. Answer both parts:

(a) At t = 10, revenue is:

(b) Fill in the blank: revenue is growing by about $___ per day at t = 10.

(a) A positive derivative always means the quantity is increasing at that instant, regardless of context. (b) W′(10) = 450 dollars/day is the instantaneous rate at day 10 — over one more day, revenue is predicted to grow by approximately that same $450, treating the rate as roughly constant over such a short step.

3. An object has v(t) = −2 and a(t) = −6 at some instant. The object is:

Velocity and acceleration are both negative — the same sign — so the object is speeding up, just in the negative direction. "At rest" would require v(t) = 0, which isn't the case here.

4. A particle's position is s(t) = t³ − 12t² + 45t (meters). Answer both parts:

(a) v(t) equals:

(b) Fill in the blank: the object is first momentarily at rest (the smaller of two such times) at t = ___.

(a) Differentiating term by term: d/dt[t³] = 3t², d/dt[−12t²] = −24t, d/dt[45t] = 45, giving v(t) = 3t² − 24t + 45. (b) Setting v(t) = 0: 3t² − 24t + 45 = 0 → t² − 8t + 15 = 0 → (t − 3)(t − 5) = 0, so t = 3 or t = 5 — the smaller value is 3.

5. Which statement correctly describes numeric substitution in a related-rates problem?

Substituting any numeric value before the implicit differentiation step turns that quantity into a constant, silently discarding its rate of change — differentiation with respect to t must come first, with numbers plugged in only afterward.

6. A 10-foot ladder leans against a wall, so x² + y² = 100, where x is the base's distance from the wall and y is the height of the top. At the instant x = 6 ft and y = 8 ft, with dx/dt = 1 ft/s, answer both parts:

(a) Differentiating x² + y² = 100 with respect to t gives:

(b) Fill in the blank: dy/dt = ___ ft/s.

(a) Differentiating x² + y² = 100 implicitly with respect to t, treating both x and y as functions of t, gives 2x·(dx/dt) + 2y·(dy/dt) = 0. (b) Solving for dy/dt: dy/dt = −(x/y)·(dx/dt) = −(6/8)(1) = −3/4 ft/s — the top slides down at 3/4 ft/s.

7. If a tangent line to a curve lies entirely above the curve (touching it only at the point of tangency), the curve must be:

A concave-down curve bends away from below its tangent lines, so the curve sits below the tangent everywhere except the point of tangency itself — exactly the description given. A concave-up curve does the opposite, lying above its tangents.

8. Let f(x) = √x and a = 9, so f(9) = 3. Answer both parts:

(a) f′(9) equals:

(b) Fill in the blank: using L(x) = f(9) + f′(9)(x − 9), the linearization estimate for √9.3 is L(9.3) = ___.

(a) f′(x) = 1/(2√x). At x = 9: f′(9) = 1/(2·3) = 1/6. (b) L(9.3) = 3 + (1/6)(9.3 − 9) = 3 + (1/6)(0.3) = 3 + 0.05 = 3.05.

9. Direct substitution into lim f(x)/g(x) yields ∞/∞. Fill in the blank: since this is also an ___ form, just like 0/0, L'Hôpital's Rule may be applied.

∞/∞ has no defined value on its own, just like 0/0 — both are called indeterminate forms, and both license L'Hôpital's Rule.

10. Consider limx→0 (1 − cos(3x))/x², which plugs in to 0/0. Answer both parts:

(a) After one application of L'Hôpital's Rule, this limit becomes:

(b) Fill in the blank: the limit's exact value is ___.

(a) Differentiating top and bottom: d/dx[1 − cos(3x)] = 3sin(3x), d/dx[x²] = 2x, giving lim (3sin(3x))/(2x). (b) Plugging in x = 0 still gives 0/0 — indeterminate again — so a second application is needed: d/dx[3sin(3x)] = 9cos(3x), d/dx[2x] = 2, giving lim (9cos(3x))/2 = 9(1)/2 = 9/2 = 4.5.
