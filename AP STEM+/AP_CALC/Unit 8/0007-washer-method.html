The Washer Method

When the region being revolved doesn't touch the axis, every slice sweeps out a ring instead of a solid disc — subtract the hole.

← Lesson 6 Lesson 8 → Contents Glossary →

1. A disc with a hole

Method — Washer Method About the x- or y-axis Revolve the region between f(x) (outer boundary) and g(x) (inner boundary) around the x-axis. Each slice sweeps out a washer: a disc of radius R(x) = f(x) with a smaller disc of radius r(x) = g(x) removed. A(x) = π[R(x)² − r(x)²]. V = π ∫ab [f(x)² − g(x)²] dx — the disc method with a second, subtracted term.
Worked Example Revolve the region between y = √x (outer) and y = x/2 (inner) around the x-axis. They meet at x = 0 and x = 4. V = π∫₀⁴ [x − x²/4] dx = π[x²/2 − x³/12]₀⁴ = π[8 − 16/3] = 8π/3.

2. Washers about a different axis

Method — Washer Method About Other Axes As with the disc method, if the axis of revolution is a line y = k rather than the x-axis, both radii shift by the same amount: R(x) = f(x) − k and r(x) = g(x) − k. The formula is otherwise unchanged.
Worked Example Revolve the same region around y = −1. R(x) = √x + 1, r(x) = x/2 + 1. V = π∫₀⁴ [(√x+1)² − (x/2+1)²] dx = π∫₀⁴ [2√x − x²/4] dx = π[32/3 − 16/3] = 16π/3.

3. Watching the ring change size

Slide x to move the slice. Both the outer and inner radius change, so the ring's width — not just its size — changes too.

Check your recall

1. The washer method's cross-sectional area formula is:

The outer disc's area and the inner disc's area are each squared separately before subtracting — squaring the difference of the radii first would give a different, incorrect quantity.

2. Revolving the region between y = √x and y = x/2 around the x-axis gives a volume of ___ (use "pi" for π)

π∫₀⁴[x − x²/4]dx = π[8 − 16/3] = 8π/3, using √x as the outer radius and x/2 as the inner radius.

3. A washer cross section differs from a disc cross section when:

If the inner boundary sits away from the axis, revolving it sweeps out a hole in the middle of every disc — that gap is exactly what turns a disc into a washer.

OpenStax Calculus Volume 1, §6.2 "Determining Volumes by Slicing" presents the washer method directly alongside the disc method as the case with an inner boundary.

The washer method is the disc method plus one extra step: find the inner radius the same way you found the outer one, then subtract its square. Ask for an example revolved around the y-axis, using horizontal slices instead of vertical ones, if that orientation feels less automatic.
