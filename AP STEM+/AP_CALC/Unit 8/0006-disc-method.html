The Disc Method

Revolve a 2D region around a line, and every cross section becomes a circle. Lesson 5's slicing idea specializes to one shape.

← Lesson 5 Lesson 7 → Contents Glossary →

1. Revolving around the axis

Method — Disc Method About the x- or y-axis Revolve the region under y = f(x) on [a, b] around the x-axis. Each slice sweeps out a disc of radius R(x) = f(x), so A(x) = πR(x)². V = π ∫ab [f(x)]² dx. Revolving a region around the y-axis instead gives the same formula with x and y swapped: V = π ∫cd [f(y)]² dy.
Worked Example Revolve the region under y = √x on [0, 4] around the x-axis. R(x) = √x, so V = π∫₀⁴ (√x)² dx = π∫₀⁴ x dx = π[x²/2]₀⁴ = 8π.

2. Revolving around a different line

Method — Disc Method About Other Axes If the axis of revolution isn't the x- or y-axis but a parallel line like y = k, the radius is the distance from the curve to that line: R(x) = f(x) − k. The volume integral is otherwise unchanged: V = π ∫ab [f(x) − k]² dx.
Worked Example Revolve the same region (y = √x, [0, 4]) around the line y = −1 instead. R(x) = √x − (−1) = √x + 1. V = π∫₀⁴ (√x + 1)² dx = π∫₀⁴ (x + 2√x + 1) dx = π[8 + 32/3 + 4] = 68π/3. Moving the axis away from the curve makes every disc bigger, so the volume grows.

3. Watching a slice become a disc

Slide x to move the slice. The radius R(x) = √x sweeps out a disc when the region is revolved around the x-axis.

Check your recall

1. Revolving the region under y = f(x) around the x-axis gives a volume of:

Each disc's area is π times its radius squared, and the radius at each x is simply f(x) — squaring before integrating is what distinguishes this from the flat area formula.

2. Revolving y = √x on [0, 4] around the x-axis gives a volume of ___ (use "pi" for π)

π∫₀⁴x dx = π[x²/2]₀⁴ = 8π — the radius squared is exactly x since R(x) = √x.

3. Revolving the same region around y = −1 instead of the x-axis changes the radius to:

The radius is always the distance from the curve to the axis of revolution — with the axis moved to y = −1, that distance grows by 1 compared to revolving around the x-axis.

A disc is just a circular cross section — everything from Lesson 5 still applies, only the shape formula changed to A = πR². Ask for a y-axis revolution example if revolving around a vertical line feels less automatic than a horizontal one.
