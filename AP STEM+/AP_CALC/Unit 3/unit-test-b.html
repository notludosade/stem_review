1. Differentiate h(x) = sin(x⁴). h′(x) equals:

Outside function sin(u) differentiates to cos(u), evaluated at u = x⁴; inside function x⁴ differentiates to 4x³. Multiplying gives 4x³cos(x⁴) — not a wrong-trig-function, sign-flipped, or missing-inner-derivative version.

2. Let p(x) = (2x³ + 1)³. Answer both parts.

(a) p′(x) equals:

(b) Fill in the blank: Evaluate p′(1). p′(1) = ___

(a) The general power rule gives 3(2x³ + 1)² times the inner derivative 6x², for 18x²(2x³ + 1)² — not 3(2x³ + 1)² (missing the inner derivative), 18x²(2x³ + 1)³ (wrong outer exponent), or 9x²(2x³ + 1)² (an arithmetic slip in the coefficient). (b) At x = 1, 2x³ + 1 = 3, so p′(1) = 18(1)(3)² = 18·9 = 162.

3. Fill in the blank: For the curve 4x² + y² = 20, implicit differentiation gives dy/dx = ___ (in terms of x and y)

Differentiating both sides: 8x + 2yy′ = 0, so y′ = −8x/(2y) = −4x/y.

4. The curve xy² + x³ = 5 passes through the point (1, 2). Answer both parts.

(a) Differentiating implicitly, y′ (in general form) equals:

(b) Fill in the blank: the slope of the tangent line at (1, 2) is ___ (as a fraction)

(a) Differentiating: y² + 2xyy′ + 3x² = 0 (Product Rule on the xy² term gives y² + 2xyy′), so y′(2xy) = −(y² + 3x²), giving y′ = −(y² + 3x²)/(2xy) — not with a flipped sign, a missing y² term from the product rule, or a doubled denominator. (b) At (1, 2): y′ = −(4 + 3)/(2·1·2) = −7/4.

5. Let f(x) = x³ + 3x. Since f(1) = 4, (f⁻¹)′(4) equals:

f′(x) = 3x² + 3, so f′(1) = 3 + 3 = 6, and (f⁻¹)′(4) = 1/f′(1) = 1/6 — the reciprocal of f′(1), not f′(1) itself (6), and not the result of dropping the +3 term (1/3) or another miscalculation (1/9).

6. Let f(x) = x⁵ + x − 2, so f(1) = 1 + 1 − 2 = 0. Answer both parts.

(a) f′(x) equals:

(b) Fill in the blank: (f⁻¹)′(0) = ___ (as a fraction)

(a) Differentiating term by term gives f′(x) = 5x⁴ + 1. (b) f′(1) = 5(1) + 1 = 6, so (f⁻¹)′(0) = 1/f′(1) = 1/6.

7. Fill in the blank: d/dx[tan⁻¹x] = ___

d/dx[tan⁻¹x] = 1/(1 + x²) — derived from cos²(tan⁻¹x) = 1/(1 + x²) via the right-triangle picture, with no minus sign anywhere since tan⁻¹x has no vertical tangents.

8. Let g(x) = sin⁻¹(x/2). Answer both parts.

(a) g′(x) equals:

(b) Fill in the blank: g′(0) = ___

(a) By the Chain Rule, d/dx[sin⁻¹(x/2)] = (1/2)/√(1 − (x/2)²), which simplifies to 1/√(4 − x²) — not the plain arcsin derivative with no chain factor (1/√(1 − x²)), an extra factor of 2 (2/√(4 − x²)), or a chain factor applied only outside the square root ((1/2)/√(1 − x²)). (b) g′(0) = 1/√(4 − 0) = 1/2.

9. For k(x) = x·tan⁻¹(x), the outermost rule to apply is:

x and tan⁻¹(x) are two non-constant factors multiplied together — the outermost operation is multiplication, so the Product Rule goes first, even though differentiating tan⁻¹(x) itself needs a separate rule afterward.

10. For f(x) = 3x⁵ + 2x² − 5x, answer both parts.

(a) f″(x) equals:

(b) Fill in the blank: f‴(x) = ___

(a) f′(x) = 15x⁴ + 4x − 5. Differentiating again: 15x⁴ → 60x³, 4x → 4, and −5 → 0, giving f″(x) = 60x³ + 4 — not with the constant term carried over incorrectly (60x³ − 5), a wrong leading coefficient (15x³ + 4), or a forgotten exponent drop (60x² + 4). (b) Differentiating f″(x) again: 60x³ → 180x², 4 → 0, so f‴(x) = 180x².
