Separation of Variables

Euler's Method approximates. This lesson solves — a technique that finds an exact formula for every solution of certain differential equations at once, then narrows to one using an initial condition.

← Lesson 3 Lesson 5 → Contents Glossary →

1. Separable equations and the general solution

Method — Separation of Variables A differential equation is separable if it can be written dy/dx = f(x)g(y). Rearrange so every y is on one side and every x on the other: (1/g(y)) dy = f(x) dx. Integrate both sides independently, then solve for y — the arbitrary constant from integration produces the whole general solution, a family of curves.
Worked Example Solve dy/dx = xy. Separate: (1/y) dy = x dx. Integrate both sides: ln|y| = x²/2 + C₁. Exponentiate: |y| = ex²/2 + C₁ = eC₁ex²/2. Renaming the constant A = ±eC₁: y = Aex²/2 — the general solution, one curve for every value of A.

2. Particular solutions from an initial condition

Method — Finding the Particular Solution A general solution is a whole family. An initial condition — a known point (x₀, y₀) the solution passes through — pins down the one constant that fits, giving the single particular solution matching that condition.
Worked Example Continuing dy/dx = xy with y(0) = 5: substitute into y = Aex²/2: 5 = Ae⁰ = A. So y = 5ex²/2 is the one member of the family passing through (0, 5).
