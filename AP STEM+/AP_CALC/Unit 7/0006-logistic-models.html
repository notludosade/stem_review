Logistic Models with Differential Equations

Exponential growth never slows down — real populations do, as resources run out. The logistic model builds a limit into the differential equation itself.

← Lesson 5 Contents Glossary →

1. The logistic differential equation

Definition — Logistic Differential Equation dP/dt = rP(1 − P/K), where r is the growth rate and K is the carrying capacity — the maximum sustainable population. When P is small compared to K, (1 − P/K) ≈ 1 and growth looks nearly exponential. As P approaches K, (1 − P/K) approaches 0 and growth slows to a stop.
Fact — The Logistic Solution Solving by separation of variables (partial fractions required — Unit 6's tools resurface here) gives P(t) = K·P₀·ert / (K − P₀ + P₀ert). As t → ∞, ert dominates and the ratio approaches K — the population levels off at the carrying capacity no matter where it started, as long as P₀ > 0.
Worked Example — Reading the Equation Without Solving It dP/dt = 0.4P(1 − P/500). The carrying capacity is K = 500. Growth is fastest exactly when the right side is maximized — calculus (differentiate the right side with respect to P and set it to 0) shows this happens at P = K/2 = 250, the inflection point of the S-shaped logistic curve.

2. Watching the S-curve approach its capacity

With r = 0.5 and P₀ = 5 fixed, slide the carrying capacity K and watch the curve level off there — always with the same S-shape, always slowing fastest as it crosses K/2.

1. In dP/dt = rP(1 − P/K), the constant K represents:

K is the population level the factor (1 − P/K) drives growth toward zero at — the maximum size the environment can sustain.

2. The logistic curve grows fastest — its inflection point — at P = ___

Maximizing rP(1 − P/K) with respect to P places the peak growth rate exactly halfway to the carrying capacity, at P = K/2 — the midpoint of the S-curve.

3. As t → ∞, the logistic solution P(t) approaches:

The e^(rt) term dominates both numerator and denominator as t grows, and the ratio simplifies to K — the population levels off at the carrying capacity rather than growing without bound, unlike the plain exponential model.
