Exponential Models with Differential Equations

"Rate of change proportional to the current amount" is the single most common phrase in applied differential equations — and separation of variables solves it in three lines, every time.

← Lesson 4 Lesson 6 → Contents Glossary →

1. Solving dy/dt = ky

Method — The Exponential Model Any quantity whose rate of change is proportional to its current value satisfies dy/dt = ky. Separating and integrating exactly as in Lesson 4: (1/y) dy = k dt → ln|y| = kt + C₁ → y = y₀ekt, where y₀ = y(0) is the initial amount. k > 0 gives growth; k < 0 gives decay.
Worked Example — Population Doubling A population grows at a rate proportional to its size, starting at 200 and reaching 400 after 3 years. Find k: 400 = 200e3k → 2 = e3k → k = ln(2)/3 ≈ 0.231. The model is P(t) = 200e0.231t.
Worked Example — Radioactive Decay A substance with decay constant k = −0.05 (per year) starts at 80 grams. After t years, the amount remaining is A(t) = 80e−0.05t. At t = 20: A(20) = 80e−1 ≈ 29.4 grams — less than half remains, since the half-life ln(2)/0.05 ≈ 13.9 years has passed.

Check your recall

1. The differential equation dy/dt = ky solves to:

Separating and integrating dy/dt = ky always produces an exponential with k in the exponent multiplied by t, scaled by the initial value y₀ — the defining shape of proportional growth or decay.

2. In y = y₀e^(kt), a negative value of k describes ___

A negative exponent constant shrinks the quantity over time rather than growing it — the signature of decay, as in the radioactive-decay example.

3. A population grows from 200 to 400 in 3 years under dP/dt = kP. The value of k is:

400 = 200e^(3k) reduces to 2 = e^(3k), and taking the natural log of both sides gives 3k = ln(2), so k = ln(2)/3.
