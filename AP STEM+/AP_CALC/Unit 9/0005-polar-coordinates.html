Polar Coordinates

Every curve so far has located a point by how far right and how far up. Polar coordinates locate it by how far out and which way — and that turns out to be a parametric curve in disguise, with θ playing the role of t.

← Lesson 4 Lesson 6 → Contents Glossary →

1. Distance and angle instead of right and up

Definition — Polar Coordinates A point is written (r, θ): r is its distance from the origin (the pole), and θ is the angle from the positive x-axis. A polar curve r = f(θ) traces a point whose distance from the origin depends on the angle. Converting to rectangular coordinates uses x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ — which is exactly the parametric pair x(θ), y(θ), with θ as the parameter instead of t.
Method — Differentiating a Polar Curve Since a polar curve is parametric with x(θ) = f(θ)cos θ and y(θ) = f(θ)sin θ, Lesson 1's formula applies unchanged: dy/dx = (dy/dθ) / (dx/dθ). Nothing new is needed — only the product rule, applied to x(θ) and y(θ) before dividing.
Worked Example r = 1 + cos θ (a cardioid). x(θ) = (1 + cos θ)cos θ, y(θ) = (1 + cos θ)sin θ. dx/dθ = −sin θ cos θ − (1 + cos θ)sin θ = −sin θ(2cos θ + 1). dy/dθ = −sin²θ + (1 + cos θ)cos θ = cos θ + cos 2θ. At θ = π/2: dx/dθ = −1(0 + 1) = −1, dy/dθ = 0 + (−1) = −1, so dy/dx = 1 at the point (0, 1).

2. Watching r trace the curve as θ sweeps

Slide θ from 0 to 2π. The point traces the cardioid the same way a parametric point traces x(t), y(t) — just with r = f(θ) setting the distance from the pole at each angle.

Check your recall

1. Converting a polar curve r = f(θ) to rectangular form uses:

x = r cos θ and y = r sin θ come straight from right-triangle trigonometry: r is the hypotenuse, θ the angle from the positive x-axis.

2. For a polar curve, dy/dx = (dy/dθ) / (dx/dθ) treats ___ as the parameter

x(θ) = f(θ)cos θ and y(θ) = f(θ)sin θ are both functions of θ, so θ plays exactly the role t played in Lessons 1–2.

3. To differentiate y(θ) = f(θ)sin θ with respect to θ, the correct tool is the:

y(θ) is a product of two functions of θ — f(θ) and sin θ — so differentiating it requires the product rule: f'(θ)sin θ + f(θ)cos θ.
