Second Derivatives and Arc Length

dy/dx was itself built from two rates against t. Differentiating it again — and measuring how far the point actually travels — both lean on that same trick.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

1. Second derivatives and concavity

Method — The Parametric Second Derivative dy/dx is itself a function of t. To find d²y/dx², differentiate it with respect to t, then divide by dx/dt again — the same move as before, one derivative deeper: d²y/dx² = [d/dt(dy/dx)] / (dx/dt). The sign of d²y/dx² gives concavity exactly as it does for y = f(x): positive means concave up, negative means concave down.
Worked Example Continuing Lesson 1's curve: x(t) = t² − 2t, y(t) = t³ − 3t, so dy/dx = (3t² − 3)/(2t − 2). Differentiating that quotient with respect to t: d/dt(dy/dx) = [(6t)(2t−2) − (3t²−3)(2)] / (2t−2)² = (6t² − 12t + 6)/(2t−2)² = 3/2. Dividing by dx/dt = 2t − 2 gives d²y/dx² = 3/[2(2t−2)]. At t = 2: d²y/dx² = 3/4 > 0, so the curve is concave up there — matching the dy/dx = 9/2 found in Lesson 1.

2. Arc length of a parametric curve

Method — The Parametric Arc Length Formula Over a short interval dt, the point moves dx horizontally and dy vertically, covering distance √(dx² + dy²). Summing (integrating) those tiny distances from t = a to t = b gives the total length of the path: L = ∫ₐᵇ √[(dx/dt)² + (dy/dt)²] dt. This is the same formula Unit 8 used for distance traveled — there it measured motion over time; here it measures the length of the curve itself, regardless of what t represents.
Worked Example — Sanity Check x(t) = 3cos t, y(t) = 3sin t for t in [0, π] traces the upper half of a circle of radius 3. dx/dt = −3sin t, dy/dt = 3cos t, so √[(dx/dt)² + (dy/dt)²] = √(9sin²t + 9cos²t) = 3 — constant speed. L = ∫₀^π 3 dt = 3π. That matches known geometry: half the circumference of a radius-3 circle is (1/2)(2π·3) = 3π.

Notice the arc length integral never mentions x or y directly — only their rates against t. That's what makes it work for loops and self-crossings where y isn't a function of x at all.
