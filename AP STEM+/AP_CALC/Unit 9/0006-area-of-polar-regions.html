Area of Polar Regions

Unit 8 swept rectangles sideways to build area under y = f(x). A polar region is swept a different way entirely — with thin wedges, pivoting around the pole instead of sliding along an axis.

← Lesson 5 Contents Glossary →

1. A thin wedge instead of a thin rectangle

Definition — Polar Area Element A thin wedge spanning a small angle dθ, at radius r, is nearly a triangle (or circular sector) with area (1/2)r²dθ — the same fraction of a full circle's area (πr²) that dθ is of a full turn (2π). Summing these wedges from θ = a to θ = b gives the area enclosed by one polar curve: A = (1/2)∫ₐᵇ r² dθ = (1/2)∫ₐᵇ [f(θ)]² dθ.
Method — Area Between Two Polar Curves Where an outer curve r = f(θ) and inner curve r = g(θ) overlap between θ = a and θ = b, the wedge between them has area (1/2)([f(θ)]² − [g(θ)]²)dθ — outer wedge minus inner wedge, the polar analog of Unit 8's "top minus bottom." A = (1/2)∫ₐᵇ ([f(θ)]² − [g(θ)]²) dθ. The bounds a and b must be the θ-values where the two curves actually intersect.
Worked Example Area enclosed by one full loop of the cardioid r = 1 + cos θ, θ from 0 to 2π: A = (1/2)∫₀^(2π) (1 + cos θ)² dθ = (1/2)∫₀^(2π) (1 + 2cos θ + cos²θ) dθ. Since ∫₀^(2π) cos θ dθ = 0 and ∫₀^(2π) cos²θ dθ = π: A = (1/2)(2π + 0 + π) = 3π/2.

2. Watching the wedges accumulate

Slide θ forward. Each wedge from the pole to the cardioid adds (1/2)r²dθ to the running total.

1. The area enclosed by one polar curve r = f(θ) from θ = a to θ = b is:

Each thin wedge has area (1/2)r²dθ, matching the fraction (dθ/2π) of the full circle's area πr². Integrating that gives the (1/2)∫[f(θ)]²dθ formula.

2. The area enclosed by the full cardioid r = 1 + cos θ is ___

A = (1/2)∫₀^(2π)(1 + cos θ)² dθ = (1/2)(2π + 0 + π) = 3π/2, using ∫cos θ dθ = 0 and ∫cos²θ dθ = π over a full period.

3. For the area between two polar curves, the bounds a and b must be:

Just as Unit 8's "area between curves" needed the x-values of intersection as bounds, the polar version needs the θ-values where f(θ) = g(θ) — the angles at which the region begins and ends.

The wedge formula (1/2)r²dθ has no x or y in it at all — area accumulates directly from angle and distance.
