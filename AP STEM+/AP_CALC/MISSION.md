# Mission: AP Calculus BC

## Why

Taking AP Calculus BC at school right now, and want a second, deeper pass alongside the classroom — lessons should track a typical AP Calc BC classroom's pace and sequence (the official College Board Course and Exam Description), not run ahead or behind it. Beyond that personal driver, this workspace is also being built to the bar of a free, standalone resource: anyone following the same BC curriculum should be able to pick up a lesson cold and learn from it, not just a student who already has classroom context. That means lessons write to a general learner, cite primary sources instead of assuming a specific teacher's framing, and never depend on outside context to make sense.

## Success looks like

- Can state what a limit is, evaluate one from a graph, a table, or algebraically, and explain why the algebraic answer must agree with the numerical one.
- Can identify and classify discontinuities (removable, jump, infinite) and justify continuity at a point using the three-part definition.
- Can compute derivatives from the limit definition and from the derivative rules (power, product, quotient, chain), including for implicit and inverse functions.
- Can apply derivatives to real contextual problems: related rates, motion (position/velocity/acceleration), optimization, and curve analysis (increasing/decreasing, concavity, extrema) using the Mean Value Theorem and First/Second Derivative Tests.
- Can compute definite and indefinite integrals, connect them to accumulation and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, and apply integration to area, volume, and average value problems.
- Can set up and solve separable differential equations, including exponential growth/decay and logistic growth.
- Can differentiate and integrate parametric, polar, and vector-valued functions, and compute arc length and related quantities for each.
- Can determine convergence or divergence of infinite series using the standard BC tests, and construct Taylor/Maclaurin polynomials with an error bound.
- Comfortable enough with the full BC free-response format (justification, units, proper notation) to self-assess against released College Board scoring guidelines.

## Constraints

- Lessons should track a typical AP Calculus BC classroom pace and unit ordering — the official College Board Course and Exam Description (Units 1–10) is the backbone, not a self-designed sequence.
- Write for a general audience, not just this learner: no inside references to a specific school, teacher, or personal schedule inside lesson content itself (personal pacing notes belong in `NOTES.md`, not in the lessons).
- Time is shared with the parallel Precalculus workspace (`../../Precalculus/`) — both tracks continue at once; neither is paused for the other.
- Prior foundation: precalculus fundamentals (functions, transformations, composition, inverses) are already covered in the Precalculus workspace and can be assumed as background, with a pointer back there if a specific gap surfaces.

## Out of scope

- AP Calculus AB pacing or the AB exam specifically — this is the full BC track (BC already contains everything in AB).
- Multivariable calculus, linear algebra, or any topic beyond the official BC Course and Exam Description.
- College-credit/AP-score logistics (score reporting, credit transfer policies) — this workspace is about learning the material, not test administration.
