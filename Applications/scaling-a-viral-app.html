Scaling a Viral App Overnight

10pm: a creator with four million followers posts a video using your app. By 10:15 your normal 500 requests/second is 22,000/second and climbing. You have maybe ten minutes before this either becomes a great story or a very bad one.

The Concept, From Cloud Computing A Two ideas do all the real work here: elasticity (an auto scaling group that adds servers automatically as load rises, and removes them once it falls) and a load balancer sitting in front of that fleet, spreading incoming requests across whichever servers are currently healthy and available.

Why one bigger server doesn't save you

The instinctive fix — "give the server more CPU and RAM" — is vertical scaling, and it has a hard ceiling: there's a biggest instance size your provider sells, and you can't provision it, reboot onto it, and warm caches back up in the ten minutes you actually have. Horizontal scaling sidesteps the ceiling entirely: instead of one bigger server, an auto scaling group launches more copies of the same modest-sized server, and the load balancer immediately starts routing new requests to them.

Why the auto scaling group's maximum matters as much as its minimum Without a cap, a runaway spike (or a bug generating retry storms) can scale a fleet — and a bill — without limit. The maximum is a deliberate circuit breaker: past it, requests start queuing or getting rejected instead of the account taking unbounded damage. A real incident response plan decides in advance whether "some users see errors" or "the bill has no ceiling" is the worse outcome — and picks the maximum accordingly.

Check your understanding

1. Why does horizontal scaling handle a 44x traffic spike better than vertical scaling?

Vertical scaling caps out at the largest instance size a provider offers and often needs a restart; horizontal scaling just launches more of what you already have.

2. What's the real purpose of setting a maximum on an auto scaling group, not just a minimum?

The maximum is a deliberate circuit breaker on cost and blast radius, not a formality.
