1. What does "the wall" refer to in the traditional Dev/Ops split?

The wall is an organizational handoff, not a technical or network boundary.

2. Fill in the blank: the team historically judged on uptime and reliability rather than feature velocity is called ___.

Operations owns production stability, in contrast to Dev's focus on shipping change quickly.

3. Why can a team install every tool this course covers and still not "have DevOps"?

Tools can automate an unchanged, still-siloed process just as easily as a genuinely shared one — culture is what actually changes.

4. Fill in the blank: a review of an incident focused on the system and process, not on which individual to blame, is called a ___ postmortem.

Blameless postmortems assume a reasonable decision made with the information available at the time usually isn't the root cause.

5. What makes a deployment pipeline "automated," rather than just a written process?

Consistent, repeatable automation — not documentation alone — is what defines the pipeline.

6. A pipeline builds an application once, runs its test suite against that exact build, then deploys it. Answer both parts:

(a) The packaged, versioned output of the build stage is called a...

(b) Fill in the blank: deploying the exact tested artifact instead of ___ from source at each stage avoids the risk of something changing in between.

Reusing the exact tested artifact guarantees what ships is exactly what was verified, instead of rebuilding and risking drift.

7. An auto scaling group can add ten servers in minutes, but a deployment ticket still takes three days to clear Ops's queue. Answer both parts:

(a) This mismatch is best described as...

(b) The underlying cause of the slow ticket queue is best described as...

As infrastructure got faster, the human handoff at the wall increasingly stood out as the bottleneck.

8. Classify each practice. Answer both parts:

(a) Trusting engineers to deploy their own code is an example of...

(b) A CI server that automatically runs a test suite is an example of...

Trust and shared ownership are organizational choices; a CI server is a specific piece of software that supports them.

9. Answer both parts about incident review:

(a) A blameless postmortem's core premise is that a reasonable decision made with the information available at the time...

(b) Instead of blame, a blameless postmortem focuses on...

Blameless postmortems look for systemic fixes rather than an individual to blame.

10. Order and naming. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the deployment pipeline begins when a developer's change is ___ to a shared repository.

(b) Fill in the blank: watching whether a deployed change is actually healthy for users afterward is the job of ___, covered in Unit 6.

The pipeline runs from an initial commit through to ongoing observability of the running system.
