1. A pipeline packages every passing change into a release, but a human still clicks a button before it ships. What is this?

Keeping a human approval step before release is exactly what defines Continuous Delivery.

2. Fill in the blank: whether a pipeline has one of these manual approval checkpoints is the entire difference between Continuous Delivery and Continuous Deployment — it's called a ___.

Presence or absence of the release gate is the whole distinction between the two practices.

3. What are the two environments in a blue-green deployment, and what does each one do?

Blue serves live traffic while green is prepared and verified before the instant switch.

4. What does "traffic shifting" mean in the context of a canary release?

Traffic shifting expands the new version's share only as it continues to look healthy.

5. Why might a team choose to roll forward instead of rolling back after a bad deploy?

Roll-forward is sometimes the only safe option when a rollback would leave the system in a mismatched state.

6. A team removes its manual release button so every change that passes CI ships automatically. Answer both parts:

(a) This team is now practicing...

(b) The pipeline stages before this point (build, test, package) are, compared to before the change...

Only the presence of the final manual step changes between Delivery and Deployment.

7. A team keeps a manual approval step specifically to coordinate a new feature's release with a marketing launch date. Answer both parts:

(a) This is an example of a...

(b) Keeping this gate does NOT necessarily mean...

A kept gate can reflect a legitimate business reason rather than a lack of trust in the pipeline.

8. Right after switching all traffic from blue to green, a bug appears in green. Answer both parts:

(a) Reverting back to blue is fast mainly because...

(b) Before the revert, how many users were exposed to the bug?

Blue-green's all-or-nothing switch means every user is exposed the instant it flips.

9. A team without mature per-slice monitoring reaches for blue-green instead of canary for a risky release. Answer both parts:

(a) This is because canary releases specifically require...

(b) Without that monitoring, a problem in a small canary slice would likely be...

Canary's advantage only pays off with monitoring precise enough to detect a problem in a small slice.

10. A broken feature is wrapped in a flag, and the team flips it off the moment errors spike. Answer both parts:

(a) Compared to a rollback or roll-forward, this response is typically...

(b) This fast response was only possible because...

A feature flag only helps during an incident if it was already in place before the incident happened.
