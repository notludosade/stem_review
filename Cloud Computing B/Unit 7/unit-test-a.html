Unit 7 Test — Version A

Putting It Together. 10 questions covering every lesson in this unit. Score 90% or higher to clear this unit.

← Course Contents Version B →

1. According to this unit, what do the DORA metrics measure?

The DORA metrics are the shared measuring stick this unit uses to evaluate pipeline design choices.

2. Fill in the blank: the manual approval step a regulated fintech pipeline keeps — while a fast-moving startup's pipeline might remove it entirely — is called a ___.

Whether to keep a release gate is a trust and risk decision, not a fixed rule.

3. Why does a high-traffic consumer app's pipeline design lean on canary releases by default?

Scale changes the stakes of exposure, which is exactly why canary fits a high-traffic app's blast-radius concerns.

4. Why can't a team safely remove a release gate its current test suite and monitoring haven't yet earned?

A release gate should be earned by pipeline trustworthiness, not removed on a fixed schedule.

5. What is an anti-pattern?

An anti-pattern keeps recurring precisely because it looks reasonable each time it happens.

6. A team automated its deploy pipeline three years ago and hasn't revisited its alert thresholds since, even as the system grew tenfold. Answer both parts:

(a) This situation is best described as...

(b) The lesson's point about toil is that it...

Toil doesn't stay solved on its own — it has to be kept in check as the system it serves keeps growing.

7. Fill in the blank: a fully automated deployment pipeline that still requires a week-long manual ticket queue for approval has simply automated the ___ from Lesson 1, without actually removing it.

The wall is an organizational handoff — automating the steps on either side of it doesn't remove the handoff itself.

8. A postmortem document singles out one on-call engineer by name as the cause of an outage. What does this represent?

Naming an individual instead of examining the process is exactly what a blameless postmortem is meant to avoid.

9. Why does this course argue that adopting every tool it covers doesn't guarantee a team "has DevOps"?

This closes the loop from Unit 1, Lesson 2 — culture, not tooling, is what actually removes the wall.

10. Fill in the blank: good alerting and self-healing orchestration both work to shrink the DORA metric called ___ — the time between an incident starting and service being restored.

Time to restore service is one of the four DORA metrics, and it's exactly what fast detection and recovery improve.
