Conservative Forces and Potential Energy Functions

mgh and ½kx² were given as facts in Physics 1. They're antiderivatives — and the derivative that undoes them recovers the force itself.

← Lesson 8 Unit 4, Lesson 10 → Contents Glossary →

A force is conservative if the work it does depends only on starting and ending position, never on the path taken between them. For such forces, it's possible to define a potential energy function U(x) that stores the "would-be work" as a function of position alone.

Force from Potential Energy For a conservative force in one dimension: F(x) = −dU/dx. Equivalently, U(x) is built so that W = −ΔU: the work done by the force equals the negative of the change in potential energy. U is (up to sign and a choice of reference point) an antiderivative of −F.
Why the derivative recovers the force U(x) was defined by integrating −F(x) in the first place: U(x) = −∫F(x)dx (+ a chosen constant fixing U = 0 somewhere). Differentiating undoes that integration exactly, by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus — F = −dU/dx isn't a new fact to memorize, it's just running the definition backward.
Example: gravity and springs, verified Near Earth's surface, U(y) = mgy. Then F(y) = −dU/dy = −mg, meaning the force points downward with magnitude mg — exactly weight, with the sign confirming it's a restoring pull toward y = 0. For a spring, U(x) = ½kx². Then F(x) = −dU/dx = −kx — exactly Hooke's law, the restoring force from Unit 6 of Physics 1, now derived rather than quoted.

Equilibrium points, read off the graph

Wherever dU/dx = 0, the force is zero — an equilibrium point. This is the same "critical point" idea from AP Calc Unit 5: a local minimum of U (a "well") is a stable equilibrium, since the force pushes back toward it on either side; a local maximum is unstable, since the force pushes further away.

1. A potential energy function is U(x) = 4x². What is the force F(x)?

F = −dU/dx = −d/dx(4x²) = −8x.

2. At a point where dU/dx = 0, the force on the object is:

Since F = −dU/dx, a zero slope in U means zero force — an equilibrium point, stable if it's a local minimum of U and unstable if it's a local maximum.

3. Which of the following forces is NOT conservative?

Kinetic friction's work depends on the total path length traveled, not just the start and end points (a longer, winding path loses more energy to friction than a straight one) — the defining sign of a non-conservative force. No potential energy function exists for it.

4. Fill in the blank: since F = −dU/dx, the potential energy function U(x) is (up to a sign and constant) an ___ of the force.

U(x) = −∫F(x)dx + C — U is built by integrating −F, so differentiating U recovers −F, i.e. F = −dU/dx.
