The Work-Energy Theorem, Derived

Physics 1 handed you Wnet = ΔKE as a given fact. It falls directly out of F = ma and a single u-substitution — the same trick from AP Calc Unit 6, Lesson 7.

← Lesson 7 Lesson 9 → Contents Glossary →

The Derivation Start from Lesson 7's integral, with F = ma from Newton's second law: W = ∫F dx = ∫ma dx = ∫m (dv/dt) dx. Rewrite dv/dt · dx as dv/dt · (dx/dt) dt = v dv (chain rule, dx = v dt), turning the integral over x into an integral over v: W = ∫v₀v mv dv = ½mv² − ½mv₀² = ΔKE.
Why this is u-substitution wearing a physics costume Setting u = v(x) and noticing du = (dv/dx)dx would look exactly like a standard AP Calc u-substitution problem. Here the same move is done implicitly through the chain rule dx = v dt: the variable of integration changes from x to v because that substitution is what makes the integral solvable in one step, exactly the way u-substitution turns a hard integral into an easy one.
The Work-Energy Theorem Wnet = ΔKE = ½mv² − ½mv₀², where Wnet is the work done by the net force. This holds for any force — constant, position-dependent, even non-conservative like friction — because nothing in the derivation assumed a particular form for F(x).

1. Which substitution turns ∫ma dx into an integral that directly yields ½mv²?

a dx = (dv/dt)(v dt) = v dv, changing the variable of integration from x to v — the key step that makes the work integral evaluate directly to a kinetic energy difference.

2. A 3 kg object starts at rest and a net force does 24 J of work on it. What is its final speed?

W = ΔKE = ½mv² − 0 → 24 = ½(3)v² → v² = 16 → v = 4 m/s.

3. The derivation of Wnet = ΔKE assumed which of the following about the net force F?

Every step used only F = ma and the chain rule — no assumption about F's functional form was needed, so the theorem holds universally for the net force.

4. Fill in the blank: rewriting (dv/dt) dx as v dv relies on the ___, since dx = (dx/dt) dt = v dt.

Substituting dx = v dt is exactly an application of the chain rule connecting position, velocity, and time.
