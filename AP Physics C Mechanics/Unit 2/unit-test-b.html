1. Why does solving ΣF = m d²x/dt² for x(t) require two independent initial conditions rather than one?

Integrating a(t) once introduces one constant (fixed by v₀); integrating again introduces a second (fixed by x₀) — exactly matching the equation's second order.

2. The differential equation m d²x/dt² = 0 (zero net force) has general solution x(t) = x₀ + vt. What does this confirm?

x(t) = x₀ + vt describes motion at constant velocity v (which could be zero, but need not be) — Newton's first law is a special case, not a separate law.

3. Fill in the blank: an object of mass 8 kg falls under gravity (g = 9.8 m/s²) with linear drag coefficient b = 4 kg/s. Its terminal velocity is ___ m/s.

v_term = mg/b = 8(9.8)/4 = 19.6 m/s.

4. To solve m dv/dt = −bv, the equation is rearranged to dv/v = −(b/m) dt before integrating both sides. This technique is called:

Grouping every v-term on one side and every t-term on the other, then integrating each side independently, is the defining move of separation of variables.

5. Fill in the blank: an equation like x₁ + x₂ = L, forced by a physical connection such as an inextensible string, is called a ___ equation — differentiating it links the two objects' accelerations too.

A constraint equation captures a geometric restriction on position that, once differentiated twice, restricts acceleration as well.

6. A 3 kg object experiences a constant net force of 15 N, starting with v₀ = 2 m/s and x₀ = 0. Answer both parts:

(a) What is v(5), the velocity after 5 s?

(b) Fill in the blank: x(5) = ___ m.

a = F/m = 15/3 = 5 m/s². (a) v(5) = 2 + 5(5) = 27 m/s. (b) x(5) = 2(5) + ½(5)(5²) = 10 + 62.5 = 72.5 m.

7. An object moving through a resistive medium has only linear drag acting on it, m dv/dt = −bv, with v₀ = 8 m/s, b = 1.5 kg/s, m = 3 kg. Answer both parts:

(a) What form does v(t) take?

(b) Fill in the blank: v(2) = ___ m/s (to two decimal places).

(a) Separation of variables gives v(t) = v₀e^(−(b/m)t). (b) b/m = 0.5, so v(2) = 8e^(−1) ≈ 2.94 m/s.

8. An Atwood machine has m₁ = 3 kg and m₂ = 9 kg (g = 9.8 m/s²). Answer both parts:

(a) The magnitude of the system's acceleration is:

(b) Fill in the blank: the string tension is ___ N.

(a) a = (m₂ − m₁)g/(m₁ + m₂) = (6)(9.8)/12 = 4.9 m/s². (b) T = m₁(g + a) = 3(9.8 + 4.9) = 3(14.7) = 44.1 N.

9. A 4 kg block sits on a frictionless table and is connected by a string over a pulley at the table's edge to a hanging 1 kg mass (g = 9.8 m/s²). Answer both parts:

(a) Which expression gives the system's acceleration?

(b) Fill in the blank: the system's acceleration is ___ m/s².

Only the hanging mass's weight drives the system: a = m₂g/(m₁ + m₂) = 1(9.8)/5 = 1.96 m/s².

10. For two objects connected by a single ideal (massless string, frictionless pulley) connection, answer both parts:

(a) Why is it correct to use one tension value T for both objects' equations, rather than two different tensions?

(b) Fill in the blank: if m₁'s acceleration has magnitude 3.1 m/s², the magnitude of m₂'s acceleration is ___ m/s².

(a) An ideal (massless) string over a frictionless, massless pulley carries the same tension throughout. (b) The constraint a₁ = −a₂ (equal magnitude, opposite sign) means m₂'s acceleration also has magnitude 3.1 m/s².
