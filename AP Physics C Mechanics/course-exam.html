1. A particle has position x(t) = 5t² − 2t (meters). What is v(3)?

v(t) = 10t − 2. v(3) = 30 − 2 = 28 m/s.

2. The definite integral of a(t) over a time interval equals:

Since a = dv/dt, the definite integral of a(t) over an interval gives the net change in velocity, Δv.

3. Fill in the blank: r(t) = (3t) î + (2t²) ĵ (meters). The particle's speed at t = 1 s is ___ m/s.

v(t) = 3î + 4tĵ. At t = 1: v = (3, 4), speed = √(9+16) = 5 m/s.

4. Solving ΣF = m d²x/dt² for x(t) requires how many independent initial conditions?

Each integration step introduces one constant of integration; a second-order equation needs two known values (typically x₀ and v₀) to fix both.

5. Fill in the blank: an object of mass 4 kg falls with linear drag coefficient b = 2 kg/s (g = 9.8 m/s²). Its terminal velocity is ___ m/s.

v_term = mg/b = 4(9.8)/2 = 19.6 m/s.

6. An Atwood machine has m₁ = 2 kg and m₂ = 8 kg (g = 9.8 m/s²). What is the magnitude of the system's acceleration?

a = (m₂ − m₁)g/(m₁ + m₂) = (6)(9.8)/10 = 5.88 m/s².

7. Fill in the blank: a force F(x) = 8x N acts on an object from x = 0 to x = 3 m. The work done is ___ J.

W = ∫₀³ 8x dx = 4x² |₀³ = 4(9) = 36 J.

8. A 4 kg object starts at rest, and a net force does 72 J of work on it. What is its final speed?

W = ½mv² → 72 = ½(4)v² → v² = 36 → v = 6 m/s.

9. A potential energy function is U(x) = 5x² − 3x. What is F(2)?

F(x) = −dU/dx = −(10x − 3). F(2) = −(20 − 3) = −17 N.

10. Fill in the blank: a force F(t) = 40t N acts on an object from t = 0 to t = 0.5 s. The impulse delivered is ___ N·s.

J = ∫₀^0.5 40t dt = 20t² |₀^0.5 = 20(0.25) = 5 N·s.

11. A 3 kg cart moving at 8 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary 1 kg cart. What is their combined final velocity?

Momentum is conserved: 3(8) + 1(0) = 24 = (3+1)v_f → v_f = 6 m/s.

12. Why does a rocket accelerate in deep space, with no external force acting on it?

Rockets work by momentum exchange with their own ejected exhaust — exactly why they function in a vacuum.

13. A wheel spins counterclockwise, as viewed from where you're standing. By the right-hand rule, its angular momentum vector points:

Curling the right hand's fingers counterclockwise points the thumb out of the page, toward the viewer.

14. Fill in the blank: a uniform rod of mass 15 kg and length 2 m rotates about one end. Its moment of inertia is ___ kg·m².

I = ⅓ML² = ⅓(15)(4) = 20 kg·m².

15. A net torque of 18 N·m acts on a wheel with moment of inertia 3 kg·m². What is its angular acceleration?

α = τ/I = 18/3 = 6 rad/s².

16. The general solution to d²x/dt² = −ω²x is:

The general solution needs a phase constant φ; A sin(ωt) is only the special case where φ = −π/2.

17. Fill in the blank: a mass m = 8 kg is attached to a spring with k = 128 N/m. Its angular frequency ω = ___ rad/s.

ω = √(k/m) = √(128/8) = √16 = 4 rad/s.

18. At maximum displacement (x = A) during SHM, the energy split is:

At x = A, velocity is momentarily zero, so all the energy ½kA² is stored as potential energy.

19. If the separation between two masses doubles, the gravitational force between them becomes:

F ∝ 1/r², so doubling r divides F by 2² = 4.

20. Kepler's second law (equal areas in equal times) follows most directly from:

Since gravity always points along r, τ = r×F = 0, so L = mr²(dθ/dt) is conserved — giving constant dA/dt.

21. Which formula gives escape velocity from a planet's surface (mass M, radius R)?

v_esc comes from setting total energy E = 0: ½mv² − GMm/R = 0 → v = √(2GM/R).

22. A problem asks for a block's speed at a specific position on a frictionless track of unknown shape. The fastest method is:

Energy conservation gives speed at a position directly, regardless of the track's exact shape, as long as it's frictionless.

23. Of a solid sphere and a thin hoop released together from rest on the same incline, which wins the race to the bottom?

The solid sphere's smaller shape factor c = 2/5 gives it a larger acceleration a = g sinθ/(1+c), regardless of mass.

24. Which best describes the relationship between AP Physics 1 and this course?

Every Physics 1 formula reappears in this course as a special case of a more general calculus-based result.
