Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion

E = ½kA² isn't a separate fact to memorize — substitute x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ) into KE + U and a single trig identity does the rest.

← Lesson 16 Lesson 18 → Contents Glossary →

The Derivation With x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ) and v(t) = −Aω sin(ωt + φ):
KE(t) = ½mv² = ½mA²ω² sin²(ωt + φ)
U(t) = ½kx² = ½kA² cos²(ωt + φ)
Since mω² = k (Lesson 16's definition): KE(t) + U(t) = ½kA²[sin²(ωt + φ) + cos²(ωt + φ)] = ½kA² — constant for all t, by the Pythagorean identity.
Why the Pythagorean identity is doing all the work sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 is the one fact making this proof go through: no matter how KE and U individually oscillate between 0 and their maximum, their sum is locked to exactly ½kA² at every instant. This is a genuinely calculus/trig-flavored proof of a result Physics 1 could only verify with specific numbers, never in general.
Reading off extremes At maximum displacement (x = ±A), v = 0: all energy is potential, U = ½kA², KE = 0. At equilibrium (x = 0), speed is maximum: all energy is kinetic, KE = ½kA², U = 0. The maximum speed is found by setting ½mvmax² = ½kA²: vmax = A√(k/m) = Aω.

1. At the equilibrium position (x = 0) during SHM, the energy split is:

At x = 0, U = ½kx² = 0, and speed is at its maximum, so all the energy ½kA² is kinetic.

2. Which identity makes KE(t) + U(t) come out constant for all t?

KE(t) + U(t) = ½kA²[sin²(ωt+φ) + cos²(ωt+φ)], and the Pythagorean identity collapses the bracket to exactly 1.

3. A mass oscillates with amplitude 0.2 m and spring constant k = 50 N/m, mass m = 2 kg. What is its maximum speed?

v_max = A√(k/m) = 0.2√(50/2) = 0.2√25 = 0.2(5) = 1 m/s.

4. In terms of k and A, the total mechanical energy of an SHM oscillator is:

E = ½kA², constant throughout the motion — reached at maximum displacement where all energy is potential.
