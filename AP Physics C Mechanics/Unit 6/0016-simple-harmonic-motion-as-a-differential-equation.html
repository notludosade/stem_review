Simple Harmonic Motion as a Differential Equation

Physics 1 gave you x(t) = A cos(ωt) as a fact to plug numbers into. Here it's the solution to a differential equation — and checking that solution is nothing but taking two derivatives.

← Unit 5, Lesson 15 Lesson 17 → Contents Glossary →

A restoring force F = −kx (Hooke's law) combined with Newton's second law gives ma = −kx, or d²x/dt² = −(k/m)x. Defining ω² = k/m, this becomes the defining differential equation of simple harmonic motion.

The SHM Differential Equation and Its General Solution d²x/dt² = −ω²x. The general solution is x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ), with two constants — amplitude A and phase φ — fixed by the two initial conditions x₀ and v₀. A plain sine, x(t) = A sin(ωt), is only correct for the special initial condition x(0) = 0; writing "x = A sin(ωt)" as if it always applies is a common and incorrect shortcut.
Why the solution checks out by direct substitution x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ) → dx/dt = −Aω sin(ωt + φ) → d²x/dt² = −Aω² cos(ωt + φ) = −ω²[A cos(ωt + φ)] = −ω²x. The second derivative reproduces −ω² times the original function — exactly the equation demands, for any choice of A and φ. This is the AP Calc BC skill "verify a function solves a differential equation" (Unit 7, Lesson 1), applied here instead of assumed.
Matching φ to initial conditions Released from rest at maximum displacement (x₀ = A, v₀ = 0): x(0) = A cos(φ) = A requires φ = 0, giving the familiar x(t) = A cos(ωt). Released from equilibrium moving in the positive direction (x₀ = 0, v₀ > 0): x(0) = A cos(φ) = 0 requires φ = −π/2, and x(t) = A cos(ωt − π/2) = A sin(ωt) — the sine form is correct only for this specific starting condition, not universally.

1. What is the general solution to d²x/dt² = −ω²x?

The general solution needs a phase constant φ; A sin(ωt) is only the special case where φ = −π/2, matching x(0) = 0.

2. Substituting x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ) into d²x/dt² gives:

Two derivatives of cosine bring down a factor of −ω² and return to cosine: d²x/dt² = −Aω²cos(ωt+φ) = −ω²x, confirming the differential equation.

3. An object is released from equilibrium (x₀ = 0) moving in the positive direction. Which form correctly describes its motion?

x(0) = 0 with positive initial velocity corresponds to φ = −π/2 in A cos(ωt + φ), which simplifies to A sin(ωt) — correct specifically because of this starting condition, not as a universal rule.

4. Fill in the blank: in the SHM differential equation d²x/dt² = −ω²x arising from Hooke's law, ω² = ___.

Starting from ma = −kx, dividing by m gives a = −(k/m)x, matching −ω²x with ω² = k/m.
