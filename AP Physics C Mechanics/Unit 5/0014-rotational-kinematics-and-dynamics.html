Rotational Kinematics and Dynamics

Every idea from Unit 1 and Unit 2 has a rotational twin — θ(t), ω(t), α(t) are the same derivative chain, and τ = Iα is the same differential equation as F = ma.

← Lesson 13 Lesson 15 → Contents Glossary →

Rotational Kinematics, by the Same Calculus as Unit 1 ω(t) = dθ/dt (angular velocity), α(t) = dω/dt = d²θ/dt² (angular acceleration) — literally Lesson 1's definitions with x replaced by θ. Every technique from Unit 1 (differentiating, integrating, finding when ω = 0) transfers directly.
Newton's Second Law for Rotation τnet = Iα — the same second-order differential equation structure as Unit 2's ΣF = ma, with torque replacing force, moment of inertia replacing mass, and angular acceleration replacing linear acceleration. Solving it for θ(t) needs the same two initial conditions, θ₀ and ω₀.

Nothing here is a new pattern to memorize — it's the identical calculus, relabeled.
Example θ(t) = 2t³ − 3t² (radians). ω(t) = dθ/dt = 6t² − 6t. At t = 2 s: ω(2) = 24 − 12 = 12 rad/s. α(t) = 12t − 6; α(2) = 18 rad/s². If this object's moment of inertia is I = 4 kg·m², the net torque producing this motion at t = 2 s is τ = Iα = 4(18) = 72 N·m.

1. Which quantity is the rotational analog of velocity?

ω = dθ/dt directly parallels v = dx/dt — the first derivative of the position-like quantity.

2. A wheel has θ(t) = 3t² − t. What is its angular acceleration α(t)?

ω(t) = 6t − 1, so α(t) = dω/dt = 6 rad/s² — constant, since ω is linear in t.

3. A net torque of 20 N·m acts on a wheel with moment of inertia 5 kg·m². What is its angular acceleration?

α = τ/I = 20/5 = 4 rad/s², directly paralleling a = F/m.

4. Fill in the blank: angular acceleration α = d²θ/dt² is the ___ derivative of angular position, exactly paralleling how linear acceleration relates to position.

ω is the first derivative of θ, and α is the derivative of ω, making it the second derivative of θ — the exact rotational mirror of Lesson 1.
