Conservation of Momentum

Physics 1 stated momentum conservation as a fact about isolated systems. It follows directly from Newton's third law and a single integral that cancels itself out.

← Lesson 10 Lesson 12 → Contents Glossary →

The Derivation For two objects interacting only with each other (an isolated system), Newton's third law says the force object 1 exerts on object 2, F12(t), and the force object 2 exerts on object 1, F21(t), satisfy F12(t) = −F21(t) at every instant. Integrating both sides over the same time interval: ∫F12 dt = −∫F21 dt, i.e. Δp2 = −Δp1, so Δp1 + Δp2 = 0 — total momentum is unchanged.
Why this needed Lesson 10's impulse integral Newton's third law only guarantees the forces are equal and opposite instant by instant — it says nothing directly about total momentum. It's the integral (Lesson 10) that turns an instant-by-instant force relationship into a statement about accumulated change over the whole interaction, however complicated the forces are moment to moment.
Elastic and Perfectly Inelastic Collisions Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together, vf = (m₁v₁ + m₂v₂)/(m₁ + m₂), and kinetic energy is not conserved (some is lost to deformation/heat). Elastic: both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved — combining the two conservation equations gives v₁′ = ((m₁−m₂)v₁ + 2m₂v₂)/(m₁+m₂) and v₂′ = ((m₂−m₁)v₂ + 2m₁v₁)/(m₁+m₂).

1. Conservation of momentum in an isolated two-object system follows most directly from:

F₁₂(t) = −F₂₁(t) at every instant (Newton's third law); integrating both sides over time gives Δp₁ = −Δp₂, so total momentum is unchanged.

2. A 3 kg cart moving at 4 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary 1 kg cart. What is their combined final velocity?

Momentum is conserved: 3(4) + 1(0) = 12 = (3+1)v_f → v_f = 3 m/s.

3. In a perfectly elastic collision, which quantity is conserved in addition to momentum?

An elastic collision is defined by conservation of both total momentum and total kinetic energy — the two conditions together are what determine both final velocities uniquely.

4. Fill in the blank: momentum conservation applies to an isolated system because the forces during the interaction are ___ to the system (they cancel in pairs by Newton's third law), not from any outside source.

Internal forces (object 1 on object 2 and vice versa) always come in equal-and-opposite pairs, so they cancel when computing the total momentum change of the whole system — only a net external force could change total momentum.
