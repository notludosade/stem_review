1. A graph has 6 edges. What is the sum of all vertex degrees?

By the Handshake Theorem, the sum of degrees always equals 2|E| = 2(6) = 12.

2. Which of these is true of every simple graph?

Simple graphs disallow self-loops by definition; isolated vertices, disconnected graphs, and graphs that aren't complete are all still perfectly valid simple graphs.

3. A graph has vertices {P,Q,R,S} and edges {P-Q, Q-R, R-S, S-P}. What kind of sequence is P, Q, R, S, P?

S-P is a listed edge, and the sequence only repeats the starting vertex at the very end — exactly the definition of a cycle.

4. Fill in the blank: a graph with 7 vertices and no edges has ___ connected components.

With no edges, no two vertices are mutually reachable, so each of the 7 vertices is its own isolated component.

5. A tree has 12 edges. How many vertices does it have?

A tree on n vertices has n − 1 edges, so 12 edges means n − 1 = 12, giving n = 13.

6. Consider possible vertex degree sequences. Answer both parts:

(a) Which degree sequence is IMPOSSIBLE for any simple graph?

(b) Fill in the blank: by the Handshake Theorem, the sum of all degrees must equal 2 times the number of ___.

(a) A degree sum must always be even (2|E|); 9 is odd, so {3,3,3} can never be the degree sequence of any simple graph. (b) The sum of degrees is always 2|E|, which is why it must always be even.

7. A graph on 10 vertices has 3 connected components of sizes 4, 3, and 3. Answer both parts:

(a) How many vertices are NOT reachable from a fixed vertex in the size-4 component?

(b) Fill in the blank: since components partition the vertex set, 4 + 3 + 3 = ___.

(a) The other two components (3+3 = 6 vertices) have no edges connecting them to the size-4 component, so none of those 6 are reachable. (b) The three component sizes must add up to the total vertex count, 10.

8. Consider a tree. Answer both parts:

(a) Removing a single edge from a tree always results in:

(b) Fill in the blank: a tree on 20 vertices has exactly ___ edges.

(a) A tree has no cycle to provide an alternate route, so removing any edge splits it into exactly two disconnected pieces. (b) n − 1 = 20 − 1 = 19.

9. Consider graph coloring. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the chromatic number of any tree with at least one edge?

(b) Fill in the blank: a 7-cycle (an odd cycle) has chromatic number ___.

(a) A tree has no cycles, so alternating 2 colors outward from any vertex never causes a clash. (b) Any odd cycle forces a 3rd color, since 2-coloring can't close the loop without a conflict at the seam.

10. A graph has vertices {W,X,Y,Z} and edges {W-X, X-Y, Y-Z} only. Answer both parts:

(a) Is there a cycle in this graph?

(b) Fill in the blank: since this graph is connected and has no cycle, it is technically a ___.

(a) There's no edge Z-W, so the chain W-X-Y-Z never closes into a loop. (b) Connected and acyclic is exactly the definition of a tree — this one happens to be a simple 4-vertex path.
