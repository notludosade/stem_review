1. A graph has 9 edges. What is the sum of all vertex degrees?

By the Handshake Theorem, the sum of degrees always equals 2|E| = 2(9) = 18.

2. Which of these is true of every simple graph?

Simple graphs disallow multiple edges between the same pair of vertices by definition; equal degree, forced cycles, and ordered-pair edges (that's a directed graph) are not requirements.

3. A graph has vertices {M,N,O,P} and edges {M-N, N-O, O-P, P-M}. What kind of sequence is M, N, O, P, M?

P-M is a listed edge, and the sequence only repeats the starting vertex at the very end — exactly the definition of a cycle.

4. Fill in the blank: a graph with 9 vertices and no edges has ___ connected components.

With no edges, no two vertices are mutually reachable, so each of the 9 vertices is its own isolated component.

5. A tree has 15 edges. How many vertices does it have?

A tree on n vertices has n − 1 edges, so 15 edges means n − 1 = 15, giving n = 16.

6. Consider possible vertex degree sequences. Answer both parts:

(a) Which degree sequence is IMPOSSIBLE for any simple graph?

(b) Fill in the blank: by the Handshake Theorem, the sum of all degrees must equal 2 times the number of ___.

(a) A degree sum must always be even (2|E|); 9 is odd, so {4,3,2} can never be the degree sequence of any simple graph. (b) The sum of degrees is always 2|E|, which is why it must always be even.

7. A graph on 12 vertices has 3 connected components of sizes 5, 4, and 3. Answer both parts:

(a) How many vertices are NOT reachable from a fixed vertex in the size-5 component?

(b) Fill in the blank: since components partition the vertex set, 5 + 4 + 3 = ___.

(a) The other two components (4+3 = 7 vertices) have no edges connecting them to the size-5 component, so none of those 7 are reachable. (b) The three component sizes must add up to the total vertex count, 12.

8. Consider a tree. Answer both parts:

(a) Adding a single edge between two vertices of a tree always results in:

(b) Fill in the blank: a tree on 15 vertices has exactly ___ edges.

(a) The two vertices already had a unique path between them (it's a tree); adding a direct edge closes that path into exactly one cycle. (b) n − 1 = 15 − 1 = 14.

9. Consider graph coloring. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the chromatic number of a cycle with an even number of vertices (e.g. a 6-cycle)?

(b) Fill in the blank: the complete graph K₆ (every pair of its 6 vertices connected) has chromatic number ___.

(a) An even cycle can alternate 2 colors all the way around and close the loop without any clash. (b) Every vertex of a complete graph is adjacent to every other, so all 6 need distinct colors.

10. A graph has vertices {M,N,O,P} and edges {M-N, N-O, O-P} only. Answer both parts:

(a) Is there a cycle in this graph?

(b) Fill in the blank: since this graph is connected and has no cycle, it is technically a ___.

(a) There's no edge P-M, so the chain M-N-O-P never closes into a loop. (b) Connected and acyclic is exactly the definition of a tree — this one happens to be a simple 4-vertex path.
