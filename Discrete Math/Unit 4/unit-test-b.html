1. Flipping a fair coin 4 times, how many outcomes are in the sample space?

Each flip independently has 2 outcomes, so by the multiplication principle, 4 flips give 2·2·2·2 = 16 total outcomes.

2. A jar has 15 marbles: 6 red, 5 blue, 4 green. One marble is drawn at random. What is P(blue)?

P(blue) = |favorable| / |total| = 5/15 = 1/3.

3. Among 300 students, 180 study Math, 60 study Art, and 30 study both. Given that a randomly chosen student studies Math, what is P(studies Art | studies Math)?

P(Art | Math) = P(Art ∩ Math) / P(Math) = (30/300) / (180/300) = 30/180 = 1/6.

4. Fill in the blank: by Axiom 2, P(S) = ___, where S is the full sample space.

Something in the sample space always happens, so P(S) = 1 (certainty).

5. A random variable X takes value 6 with probability 0.3 and value −3 with probability 0.7. What is E[X]?

E[X] = 6(0.3) + (−3)(0.7) = 1.8 − 2.1 = −0.3.

6. Let S = {1,...,8} with equally likely outcomes, E1 = {1,2,3}, E2 = {6,7}. Answer both parts:

(a) What is P(E1 ∪ E2)?

(b) Fill in the blank: computing P(E1 ∪ E2) by simply adding P(E1) + P(E2) is only valid because E1 and E2 are ___.

(a) P(E1) = 3/8, P(E2) = 2/8, and since E1 ∩ E2 = ∅, P(E1 ∪ E2) = 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8. (b) Additivity (Axiom 3) requires mutual exclusivity — otherwise the overlap would be double-counted.

7. Among 400 people, 250 own a smartphone; of those 250, 100 also own a smartwatch. Answer both parts:

(a) What is P(Smartwatch | Smartphone)?

(b) Fill in the blank: P(Smartwatch ∩ Smartphone) = ___ / 400.

(a) P(Smartwatch | Smartphone) = 100/250 = 2/5. (b) 100 of the 400 people own both, directly given.

8. Let P(A) = 0.6, P(B) = 0.3, P(A ∩ B) = 0.18. Answer both parts:

(a) Are A and B independent?

(b) Fill in the blank: if instead P(A ∩ B) = 0.25, A and B would be ___.

(a) Independence requires P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B), which holds exactly here. (b) 0.25 ≠ 0.6 × 0.3 = 0.18, so the events would no longer be independent — they'd be dependent.

9. A game costs $4 to play. You win $12 with probability 0.25, and win nothing otherwise. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the expected net profit per play?

(b) Fill in the blank: by linearity of expectation, playing this game twice has a total expected net profit of $___.

(a) Expected winnings = 12(0.25) = 3; net profit = 3 − 4 = −$1. (b) Linearity of expectation: E[X₁ + X₂] = E[X₁] + E[X₂] = −1 + (−1) = −$2.

10. A committee of 2 is randomly selected from 6 men and 3 women (9 people total, all equally likely committees). Answer both parts:

(a) What is P(both selected are women)?

(b) Fill in the blank: the total number of equally-likely committees is C(9,2) = ___.

(a) Favorable outcomes: C(3,2) = 3 (all-women committees). Total: C(9,2) = 36. P = 3/36 = 1/12. (b) C(9,2) = 9!/(2!·7!) = 36.
