1. A license plate has 2 letters followed by 3 digits, each character chosen independently (26 letters, 10 digits, repetition allowed). How many license plates are possible?

Each of the 5 characters is an independent choice: 26 · 26 · 10 · 10 · 10 = 676,000, by the multiplication principle.

2. A student must take exactly one foreign language section: 4 Spanish sections or 2 French sections (no section counts as both). How many ways can they satisfy this requirement?

The two groups of sections are mutually exclusive alternatives, so the addition principle applies: 4 + 2 = 6.

3. In how many ways can 4 of 9 distinct paintings be hung in a row, where order matters?

Order matters, so this is a permutation: P(9,4) = 9 · 8 · 7 · 6 = 3,024.

4. Fill in the blank: 6! = ___.

6! = 6·5·4·3·2·1 = 720.

5. A hand of 5 cards is dealt from a standard 52-card deck; order is irrelevant. Which expression counts the number of possible hands?

Since a hand's order doesn't matter, this is a combination, C(52,5), not a permutation.

6. A 4-person subcommittee, with no distinct roles, is chosen from 10 people. Answer both parts:

(a) Which counting tool applies?

(b) Fill in the blank: C(10,4) = ___.

(a) With no distinct roles, swapping two committee members produces the same committee, so this counts combinations, not permutations. (b) C(10,4) = 10!/(4!·6!) = 210.

7. Consider the fact that C(6,2) = C(5,1) + C(5,2). Answer both parts:

(a) Which identity explains this?

(b) Fill in the blank: by the symmetry identity, C(9,2) = C(9,___).

(a) Pascal's identity splits "choose r from n" into whether a fixed item is included or excluded, matching this exact pattern. (b) Symmetry gives C(9,2) = C(9, 9−2) = C(9,7).

8. Row 4 of Pascal's triangle is 1, 4, 6, 4, 1. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the coefficient of x²y² in the expansion of (x+y)⁴?

(b) Fill in the blank: the full expansion is x⁴ + 4x³y + ___x²y² + 4xy³ + y⁴.

(a)–(b) The Binomial Theorem pairs row 4's coefficients directly with each term, and the middle term's coefficient is C(4,2) = 6.

9. A password has 4 characters, each independently chosen from 36 options (26 letters + 10 digits), repetition allowed. Answer both parts:

(a) How many such passwords are possible?

(b) Fill in the blank: if repetition were NOT allowed, the count becomes P(36,4) = 36 × 35 × 34 × ___.

(a) 36⁴ = 1,679,616 by the multiplication principle with repetition allowed. (b) Without repetition, each successive character loses one more option: 36, 35, 34, 33.

10. A 3-person team is chosen from 6 candidates, with no distinct roles. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the correct count?

(b) Fill in the blank: by symmetry, C(6,3) = C(6,___).

(a) With no distinct roles, C(6,3) = 6!/(3!·3!) = 20. (b) Symmetry gives C(6,3) = C(6, 6−3) = C(6,3) — this case is self-symmetric, since 3 = 6−3.
