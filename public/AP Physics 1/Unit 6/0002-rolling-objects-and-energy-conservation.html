Rolling Objects and Energy Conservation

Why a solid ball always beats a hoop down the same ramp — proven directly from energy conservation, no race required.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

Energy Conservation for a Rolling Object For an object rolling without slipping down a ramp (static friction present but doing no work — Unit 5, Lesson 6), mechanical energy is conserved: mgh = ½mvcm² + ½Iω². Using the rolling constraint ω = vcm/R (Unit 5, Lesson 6) lets everything be written in terms of vcm alone.
Deriving a shape-independent speed formula Writing I = cMR² for some shape-dependent constant c (c = ½ for a solid disk, c = 1 for a hoop, c = ⅖ for a solid sphere — Unit 5, Lesson 3), and substituting ω = vcm/R: mgh = ½mvcm² + ½(cmR²)(vcm/R)² = ½mvcm²(1 + c). The mass cancels entirely, giving vcm = √[2gh / (1 + c)] — the final speed depends only on height and the shape constant c, never on mass or radius.
Why a smaller c means a faster roll A larger c (more rotational inertia relative to mass and size, like a hoop's c = 1) means more of the available gravitational PE is diverted into spinning rather than translating, per the 1/(1+c) factor — so a hoop (c = 1) reaches the bottom slower than a solid disk (c = ½), which in turn is slower than a solid sphere (c = ⅖), regardless of their masses or radii. A frictionless block that slides without rotating at all (c = 0) is fastest of all, since 100% of its PE converts to translational KE.
Example: comparing shapes down the same ramp Released from rest at h = 1 m: a sliding block (c=0) reaches v = √(2·9.8·1) ≈ 4.43 m/s. A solid sphere (c=⅖) reaches v = √(2·9.8·1/1.4) ≈ 3.74 m/s. A solid disk (c=½) reaches v = √(2·9.8·1/1.5) ≈ 3.61 m/s. A hoop (c=1) reaches v = √(2·9.8·1/2) ≈ 3.13 m/s — confirming the order block > sphere > disk > hoop, independent of any specific mass or radius chosen.

1. A solid sphere and a hoop of different masses and radii are released from rest at the same height on identical ramps. Which reaches the bottom with greater speed?

v_cm = √[2gh/(1+c)] depends only on the shape constant c (⅖ for a sphere vs. 1 for a hoop), not on mass or radius — the sphere's smaller c gives it a higher final speed.

2. Why does mass cancel out of the final speed formula for a rolling object on a ramp?

mgh = ½mv²(1+c) has mass as a common factor on both sides once I is written as cMR², so it cancels regardless of the specific shape.

3. A frictionless block that slides (without any rotation) down the same ramp compares to a rolling solid sphere released from the same height how?

With c = 0, the sliding block's formula reduces to v = √(2gh), the fastest possible case, since no energy is diverted into rotational KE.

4. Fill in the blank: energy conservation with the constraint ω = v_cm/R applies specifically to objects ___ down a ramp.

Rolling without slipping is what makes static friction do zero work, preserving mechanical energy conservation while still allowing the object to spin up.
