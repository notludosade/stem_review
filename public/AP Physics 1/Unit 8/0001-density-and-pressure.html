Density and Pressure

Two quantities that let us describe fluids without tracking every individual molecule.

← Unit 7, Lesson 5 Lesson 2 → Contents Glossary →

Density Density ρ = m/V — mass per unit volume, in kg/m³. Unlike mass, density is an intrinsic property of a material: a small block and a large block of the same solid aluminum have the same density, even though their masses and volumes differ.
Pressure Pressure P = F/A — force per unit area, in pascals (1 Pa = 1 N/m²), where F is the force acting perpendicular to a surface. Unlike force, pressure doesn't have a direction of its own within a fluid at rest — it pushes equally in every direction at a given point (fluids can't sustain a shear force at rest, unlike solids).
Why the same force can feel very different depending on area Standing on one foot versus lying flat applies the same weight (force), but concentrates it over a much smaller area when standing — resulting in far higher pressure on the ground beneath your foot. This is exactly why snowshoes work: spreading the same body weight over a much larger area lowers the pressure enough to avoid sinking into snow, even though the total force (your weight) hasn't changed at all.
Pressure in a Fluid at Depth Pressure increases with depth in a fluid: P = P0 + ρgh, where P0 is the pressure at the surface (often atmospheric pressure) and h is depth below that surface. Deeper fluid must support the weight of everything above it, so pressure grows linearly with depth.
Where P = P₀ + ρgh comes from Consider a column of fluid of cross-sectional area A extending from the surface down to depth h. Its weight is mg = (ρV)g = ρ(Ah)g, and this weight must be supported by the pressure difference across the column's area: (P − P0)A = ρAhg, so P − P0 = ρgh. This is the same logic as any object's weight being supported by whatever's beneath it (Unit 2) — here, applied to a column of fluid instead of a solid object.
Example: pressure at the bottom of a pool A pool is 3 m deep, with water density ρ = 1000 kg/m³ and atmospheric pressure P0 = 101,325 Pa at the surface. Pressure at the bottom: P = 101,325 + (1000)(9.8)(3) = 101,325 + 29,400 = 130,725 Pa — about 29% higher than atmospheric pressure alone.
Example: finding density A metal block has mass 270 g and volume 100 cm³ = 1×10⁻⁴ m³. Converting mass to kg (0.27 kg): ρ = 0.27/1×10⁻⁴ = 2700 kg/m³ — matching the known density of aluminum, identifying the metal.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Why does standing on one foot produce more pressure on the ground than lying flat, given the same body weight?

Pressure = force/area, so the same force over a smaller area (standing on one foot) produces higher pressure than over a larger area (lying flat).

2. Pressure at a depth h in a fluid with surface pressure P₀ increases because:

P = P₀ + ρgh comes directly from the weight of the fluid column above a given depth needing to be supported by pressure.

3. A block has mass 500 g and volume 200 cm³. What is its density in kg/m³?

0.5 kg / (200×10⁻⁶ m³) = 0.5/0.0002 = 2500 kg/m³.

4. Fill in the blank: force divided by the area it acts over, P = F/A, defines ___.

Pressure is force per unit area, distinct from force itself since it accounts for how that force is distributed.
