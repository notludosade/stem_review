1. A system undergoes simple harmonic motion. Which condition must its restoring force satisfy?

SHM is defined specifically by a restoring force proportional to displacement, F = −kx, with the minus sign ensuring the force always opposes displacement.

2. Fill in the blank: a mass in simple harmonic motion completes one full oscillation every 0.25 s. Its frequency f is ___ Hz.

Frequency is the reciprocal of period: f = 1/T = 1/0.25 = 4 Hz.

3. A mass on a spring is pulled back 4 cm and released, completing each cycle in 0.8 s. If instead it's pulled back 12 cm and released, its period will be:

The period of a spring-mass system, T = 2π√(m/k), never depends on amplitude — a larger pull-back distance means proportionally more restoring force and speed at every point, and the two effects cancel.

4. A pendulum is released from rest at a large angle (60°) instead of a small angle (5°). Compared to the small-angle prediction T = 2π√(L/g), the actual period at 60° will be:

The restoring force involves sin θ, only approximately proportional to θ (and thus to displacement) for small angles — at 60° the approximation breaks down and the true period runs noticeably longer than T = 2π√(L/g) predicts.

5. A shock absorber returns a car's suspension to equilibrium as quickly as possible, with no bouncing at all. This is an example of:

Critical damping is defined as the fastest possible return to equilibrium without any oscillation, distinct from the slower overdamped case and the still-oscillating underdamped case.

6. A 4 kg mass is attached to a spring with spring constant k = 100 N/m. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the period of oscillation?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the spring constant were quadrupled to 400 N/m (mass unchanged), the new period would be ___ s (to 2 decimal places).

(a) T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(4/100) = 2π(0.2) ≈ 1.26 s. (b) Since T ∝ 1/√k, quadrupling k divides T by √4 = 2, giving 1.26/2 ≈ 0.63 s.

7. A simple pendulum has length L = 1.0 m, swinging at a small angle near Earth's surface (g = 9.8 m/s²). Answer both parts:

(a) What is its period?

(b) If the bob's mass were doubled (length and angle unchanged), the period would:

(a) T = 2π√(L/g) = 2π√(1/9.8) ≈ 2π(0.319) ≈ 2.01 s. (b) Mass cancels entirely out of T = 2π√(L/g), so doubling the bob's mass leaves the period unchanged.

8. A 0.2 kg mass oscillates on a spring with k = 50 N/m and amplitude A = 0.1 m. Answer both parts:

(a) At which position x is the system's kinetic energy at its maximum?

(b) Fill in the blank: the total mechanical energy E of the system is ___ J.

(a) At equilibrium, potential energy is zero, so all the system's fixed total energy is kinetic there — the point of maximum speed. (b) E = ½kA² = ½(50)(0.1)² = 0.25 J, constant throughout the motion.

9. A 0.5 kg mass oscillates on a spring with k = 20 N/m and amplitude A = 0.2 m. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the maximum speed of the mass, vmax?

(b) Fill in the blank: the speed of the mass at x = 0.1 m is ___ m/s (to 2 decimal places).

(a) vmax = A√(k/m) = 0.2√(20/0.5) = 0.2√40 ≈ 1.26 m/s. (b) v = √[(k/m)(A² − x²)] = √[40(0.04 − 0.01)] = √1.2 ≈ 1.10 m/s.

10. A swing has a natural period of 2 s. Answer both parts:

(a) To build up amplitude most efficiently through resonance, a person should push the swing:

(b) Fill in the blank: dramatic amplitude buildup from matching a driving frequency to a system's natural frequency is called ___.

(a) Pushing in rhythm with the swing's own 2 s natural period adds energy every cycle, building amplitude efficiently. (b) This phenomenon is called resonance.
