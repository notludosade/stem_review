1. Which three constraints does the iron triangle say a project balances, where fixing any two forces the third to move?

The iron triangle names what a project delivers, how long it takes, and what it consumes as three linked constraints.

2. A team is estimating how long "redesign the office building's HVAC system" will take, but the task is too large to estimate directly. What should they do first?

A task this large is too big to schedule or estimate directly — decomposing it into a WBS makes each piece tractable.

3. Two tasks remain: Task A, which is on the critical path, and Task B, which isn't. The team only has resources to speed up one. Which choice actually moves the project's finish date earlier?

The project's finish date is determined by the critical path; speeding up an off-path task like Task B doesn't shorten the timeline.

4. A team identifies that a key engineer might leave the project sometime in the next six months. Is this currently a "risk" or an "issue"?

Risk management is specifically about the "might," before it becomes a "did" — a possible departure that hasn't happened is a risk, not an issue.

5. Fill in the blank: filling out a risk register once and filing it away treats it as paperwork instead of what it's meant to be — a ___ tool that forces an advance answer to what the team would actually do if a risk occurs.

A risk register's real value comes from the advance thinking it forces, not from its existence as a compliance artifact.

6. A project has held scope, schedule, and budget all fixed under heavy pressure for months. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is this situation described as especially risky?

(b) Fill in the blank: this fourth, less-discussed variable is called ___, and it tends to absorb pressure invisibly when the other three constraints can't move.

(a) Nobody explicitly agrees to this trade-off, which makes it the most dangerous kind since it's easy to miss until consequences surface. (b) Quality is the fourth variable that quietly absorbs pressure.

7. A project's original schedule assumed a permit would be approved in two weeks; it actually took two months. Answer both parts:

(a) What should the team do with the schedule once this becomes clear?

(b) Fill in the blank: a ___ chart is the standard tool for visualizing which tasks can run in parallel and which chain of dependencies drives the finish date.

(a) Updating immediately lets the team react early instead of discovering the slip only at the original deadline. (b) A Gantt chart is the standard way of visualizing task timing and dependencies.

8. A risk register lists a common, minor packaging defect alongside a rare but catastrophic equipment failure. Answer both parts:

(a) Why does rating both risks on likelihood and impact matter more than ranking them by likelihood alone?

(b) Fill in the blank: even though the equipment failure is rare, it still deserves a response plan because of its severe ___.

(a) Both dimensions together let a team correctly prioritize risks. (b) A rare-but-severe risk can outweigh a common-but-minor one because of its impact.

9. A firm decides a low-likelihood, low-impact risk isn't worth the cost of addressing directly, and records that decision deliberately. Answer both parts:

(a) Which of the four standard risk responses is this?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the firm had instead redesigned the plan so the risk couldn't occur at all, that response would be called ___.

(a) Acceptance is a deliberate, recorded decision to proceed without further action, appropriate for low-likelihood, low-impact risks. (b) Changing the plan so a risk can't occur is called avoidance.

10. A client asks to add a new requirement mid-project without discussing schedule or budget. Answer both parts:

(a) What does naming the trade-off explicitly actually accomplish in this conversation?

(b) Fill in the blank: this request is really an increase in the project's ___ — what it must deliver — even though it wasn't framed that way.

(a) Naming the trade-off makes the cost of a scope change visible instead of letting it get absorbed invisibly. (b) A new requirement, however it's framed, is a scope increase.
