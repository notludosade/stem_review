1. A senior in college, about to graduate, sits for an exam covering broad undergraduate math, science, and general engineering principles. What does passing this exam grant?

The FE is an early knowledge check taken at or near graduation; it earns the EIT/EI title, not the full PE license.

2. An engineer has several years of documented, qualifying work experience under a licensed PE and is ready to test for the full Professional Engineer license. What does the PE exam specifically assess?

The PE tests judgment — knowing which valid approach fits a real situation — which can only be verified after years of supervised experience, unlike the FE's earlier knowledge check.

3. One engineer has become the person their organization always turns to for a specific, narrow failure-mode analysis; another engineer is comfortable working across many subsystems and disciplines at once. Which path does each represent?

A specialist develops deep expertise in one narrow area; a generalist develops working competence across many areas instead.

4. A strong individual engineer is promoted into a management role and struggles despite being excellent at hands-on technical work. What does the lesson say explains this?

Deep focus and independent problem-solving drive individual technical excellence, but they aren't the same skills that make someone an excellent manager.

5. Fill in the blank: a five-year career plan is a forecast made with today's best information, and like a project schedule, it should be treated as a ___ document that gets revised as new information comes in.

Treating an early plan as permanently fixed regardless of what's actually learned defeats the same purpose a rigid, un-updated project schedule would.

6. Two engineers can both correctly calculate a beam's required strength right out of school. Answer both parts about why licensure isn't granted from a single test:

(a) What does the PE exam, combined with its experience requirement, verify that the FE alone cannot?

(b) Fill in the blank: engineering ___ — knowing which valid approach fits a given situation, and recognizing the limits of your own competence — is built through years of supervised practice, not from passing an exam alone.

(a) The PE and its experience requirement test real-world judgment, not just calculation ability. (b) This kind of judgment specifically cannot be verified any earlier than after years of supervised practice.

7. A software engineer at a private tech company has worked for fifteen years without ever obtaining a PE license. Answer both parts:

(a) What does this indicate, based on the lesson?

(b) Fill in the blank: licensure is ___-specific rather than universal — many engineers in software, private R&D, or internal-facing work never need a PE at all.

(a) The legal requirement for a PE stamp is tied to specific kinds of public-facing work, not every engineering job. (b) This makes licensure role-dependent, not a universal requirement.

8. An engineer spends five years as a deep technical specialist, then moves into a management role, and later returns to individual technical work. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the lesson say about a career that moves like this?

(b) Fill in the blank: treating an early career choice among these three paths as ___ adds pressure that doesn't reflect how careers in this field typically unfold.

(a) Movement between these paths is common in practice. (b) None of these choices needs to be treated as locked in forever.

9. An engineer is one year into their first job and hasn't yet thought about whether their role provides PE-qualifying experience. Answer both parts:

(a) Why does the lesson say this needs attention now rather than later?

(b) Fill in the blank: confirming a role provides qualifying supervised ___ needs to happen within the first year or two of a career, not discovered as a gap at year four.

(a) Since qualifying experience accumulates over years, checking early avoids a costly late discovery. (b) The specific kind of supervised experience the PE requires is what needs early confirmation.

10. A five-year career plan lists specific years for building portfolio work, taking on project responsibility, and choosing a career direction — but ethical judgment doesn't get its own dedicated year. Answer both parts:

(a) Why not?

(b) Fill in the blank: disclosing conflicts of interest and documenting concerns in writing apply from the very first day and never stop applying — ethical judgment is a constant ___ to every other year of the plan, not a phase of its own.

(a) Unlike skills that build up over specific years, ethical judgment is relevant at every stage simultaneously. (b) It functions as an ongoing backdrop rather than a single stage in the plan.
