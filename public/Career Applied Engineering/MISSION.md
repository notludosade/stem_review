# Mission: Career Applied Engineering

## Why
Technical skill alone doesn't get an engineer hired, promoted, or trusted with bigger responsibility — professional practice does. This course covers the parts of an engineering career that Engineering 1 and discipline-specific courses don't: how to present your work, get through the interview process, operate effectively inside a real organization, manage a project, and navigate licensure and long-term career growth.

## Success looks like
- Can build a resume and portfolio that actually represents engineering work to a hiring manager
- Comfortable with the structure of technical interviews — design/whiteboard problems and behavioral questions alike — and has a repeatable approach to both
- Understands how to write clear technical documentation and communicate with non-engineers (managers, clients, other departments)
- Knows the basic vocabulary and tools of project management: scope/schedule/budget trade-offs, work breakdown, risk
- Understands what the FE and PE exams actually test, and has a framework for thinking about long-term career paths (specialist vs. generalist vs. management)

## Constraints
- Assumes Engineering 1 (or equivalent) — this course is about career and professional practice, not engineering fundamentals
- U.S.-centric where career/licensure specifics are unavoidable (the FE/PE exam system, NSPE ethics) — general professional-practice content (interviews, documentation, project management) applies more broadly
- Practical and applied — this is not a management theory or MBA-style course

## Out of scope
- Deep project management methodology (PMP-level certification content, detailed scheduling software) — this course covers the basics an early-career engineer needs, not a project management specialization
- Discipline-specific career content (e.g. how to interview specifically for a structural engineering role) — kept general enough to apply across disciplines
- Graduate school or research career paths — focused on industry practice
