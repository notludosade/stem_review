1. A wave has frequency 6 Hz and wavelength 0.4 m. What is its speed?

v = fλ = (6)(0.4) = 2.4 m/s. Adding f and λ instead of multiplying gives 6.4; dividing f by λ gives 15; dividing λ by f gives 0.067 — none of those is the wave speed relationship.

2. Which of the following is an example of a transverse wave?

A wave on a string oscillates perpendicular to its direction of travel — transverse. Sound, spring-compression waves, and ultrasound are all longitudinal: the medium oscillates parallel to the direction of travel.

3. A string of length 1.2 m has wave speed 240 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) What is its fundamental frequency?

(b) Fill in the blank: the fixed points of maximum displacement in a standing wave are called ___.

(a) The fundamental wavelength is λ₁ = 2L = 2(1.2) = 2.4 m, so f₁ = v/λ₁ = 240/2.4 = 100 Hz. (b) Antinodes are the points of maximum displacement; nodes are the fixed points of zero displacement.

4. An astronaut on the Moon's surface strikes two rocks together right next to a colleague's helmet, but the colleague hears nothing through the near-vacuum lunar environment (unless the helmets are in direct contact). This is because:

Sound is a pressure disturbance transmitted by particles bumping into their neighbors; with essentially no atmosphere, there is nothing to carry the compressions and rarefactions, so no sound wave can travel between the astronauts through the vacuum.

5. Answer both parts about the Doppler effect:

(a) A police car's siren sounds higher-pitched as it speeds toward you and lower-pitched as it speeds away. This occurs because:

(b) A source emits a 600 Hz tone while approaching a stationary listener at 34 m/s (speed of sound v = 340 m/s). What frequency does the listener hear?

(a) Approaching motion crowds wavefronts closer together (shorter received wavelength → higher frequency); receding motion stretches them apart (lower frequency). (b) f_obs = f_source · v/(v − v_source) = 600 · 340/(340−34) = 600 · 340/306 ≈ 667 Hz — higher than the emitted 600 Hz, as expected for an approaching source.

6. Fill in the blank: diffraction — the bending of waves around obstacles or through openings — becomes most noticeable when the size of the opening or obstacle is comparable to the wave's ___.

A wave passing through an opening much larger than its wavelength barely bends at all; one passing through an opening comparable to its own wavelength spreads out dramatically.

7. Answer both parts about interference:

(a) When two identical waves overlap exactly out of phase (crest meets trough), the principle of superposition predicts:

(b) Fill in the blank: two speakers emit identical in-phase waves of wavelength 3 m. At a point where one speaker's sound has traveled 4.5 m farther than the other's, this produces ___ interference (constructive or destructive).

(a) Out-of-phase superposition subtracts displacements, potentially all the way to zero — destructive interference. (b) 4.5 m = 1.5 × 3 m = (1 + 1/2)λ, a half-integer number of wavelengths, so the waves arrive out of phase — destructive interference (a quiet spot).

8. Answer both parts about the double-slit experiment:

(a) Dark fringes (destructive interference) occur where the path difference between the two slits equals:

(b) Fill in the blank: slits separated by d = 0.015 mm produce a first-order (m = 1) bright fringe at θ = 2°. The wavelength is λ ≈ ___ nm (nearest 10).

(a) d sin θ = mλ gives bright fringes (whole-number path difference); d sin θ = (m + 1/2)λ gives dark fringes (half-integer path difference). (b) λ = d sin θ/m = (1.5×10⁻⁵)(sin 2°)/1 ≈ (1.5×10⁻⁵)(0.0349) ≈ 5.235×10⁻⁷ m ≈ 520 nm.

9. Which best explains why a soap bubble shows shifting rainbow colors, while a thick pane of glass under the same white light looks uniformly gray?

The interference condition depends on the extra path length 2t compared to each wavelength. In a thin film this changes gently with thickness, producing clean color bands; in a thick film it changes so rapidly that the colors wash out to gray.

10. Answer both parts about diffraction gratings:

(a) Compared to a simple double slit, a diffraction grating with thousands of lines produces:

(b) A diffraction grating has 2000 lines per millimeter. What is its slit spacing d?

(a) With thousands of lines, only precise angles satisfying d sin θ = mλ keep light from every line in phase simultaneously; slightly off-angle light cancels almost completely, producing much sharper, brighter fringes than a two-slit setup. (b) d = 1 mm/2000 = 0.0005 mm = 5×10⁻⁷ m.
