Faraday's Law

A changing magnetic field creates an electric current out of nowhere — the discovery that makes generators, transformers, and induction cooktops possible.

← Lesson 3 Lesson 5 → Contents Glossary →

Magnetic Flux ΦB = BA cos θ (unit: weber, Wb), where B is field strength, A is the area of the loop, and θ is the angle between the field and the loop's normal (perpendicular) direction. Flux measures how much magnetic field "passes through" a loop.
Faraday's Law of Induction ε = −N(ΔΦB/Δt), where N is the number of loops (turns) in a coil. A changing magnetic flux through a loop induces an EMF, and therefore a current if the loop is part of a closed circuit.
Why it's specifically change in flux that matters, not flux itself A steady, unchanging magnetic field passing through a stationary loop induces nothing at all — no matter how strong that field is — because Faraday's law depends explicitly on ΔΦB/Δt, the rate of change. This can happen three ways: the field strength itself changes over time, the loop's area changes (as it's stretched or shrunk), or the angle between the loop and field changes (as the loop rotates) — any of these alters flux over time and induces an EMF, which is exactly the principle exploited by electric generators (Lesson 6) that rotate a loop within a fixed field.
Example: flux through a loop A circular loop of area 0.05 m² sits perpendicular to a 0.4 T field (θ = 0°). ΦB = BA cos θ = (0.4)(0.05)(1) = 0.02 Wb.
Example: induced EMF from changing flux A single loop's flux changes from 0.02 Wb to 0.05 Wb over 0.1 s. ε = −N(ΔΦB/Δt) = −(1)(0.05 − 0.02)/0.1 = −(0.03)/0.1 = −0.3 V — the magnitude of the induced EMF is 0.3 V (the negative sign's direction meaning is the subject of Lesson 5).

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. A loop of wire sits in a very strong but perfectly constant magnetic field. What EMF is induced?

Since ΔΦ_B/Δt = 0 for an unchanging flux, no EMF is induced no matter how strong the steady field is.

2. Which of the following would NOT change the magnetic flux through a stationary, fixed-size loop?

A steady field, unchanging area, and unchanging angle together mean zero change in flux, and therefore zero induced EMF.

3. A single-loop coil's flux changes from 0.01 Wb to 0.04 Wb over 0.2 s. What is the magnitude of the induced EMF?

ε = N(ΔΦ_B/Δt) = (1)(0.04 − 0.01)/0.2 = 0.03/0.2 = 0.15 V.

4. Fill in the blank: the quantity Φ_B = BA cos θ, measuring how much magnetic field passes through a loop, is called ___.

It's the rate of change of magnetic flux, not flux itself, that induces an EMF according to Faraday's law.
