1. Find the energy of a photon with frequency 7.5×10¹⁴ Hz (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s).

E = hf = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(7.5×10¹⁴) ≈ 4.97×10⁻¹⁹ J. Forgetting to multiply by f leaves just h; using f as the answer ignores Planck's constant entirely.

2. Two light sources shine on the same metal: Source A has frequency above the metal's threshold frequency but very low intensity; Source B has frequency below threshold but very high intensity. Which ejects photoelectrons?

Each photon interacts individually with one electron. A single photon above threshold carries enough energy (hf ≥ φ) to eject one electron immediately; below threshold, no number of individually-too-weak photons can add up.

3. Answer both parts about photons:

(a) Find the momentum of a photon with wavelength 250 nm.

(b) Fill in the blank: light exhibiting both wave-like interference and particle-like discrete energy exchange is an example of wave-particle ___.

(a) p = h/λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/(250×10⁻⁹) ≈ 2.65×10⁻²⁷ kg·m/s. (b) Wave-particle duality: both descriptions are simultaneously valid, each surfacing depending on what's measured.

4. Find the energy of the n = 5 level in hydrogen (Eₙ = −13.6 eV/n²).

E₅ = −13.6/5² = −13.6/25 ≈ −0.54 eV. Dividing by 5 instead of 25 gives −2.72; forgetting to divide gives the ground-state value −13.6; −0.85 eV is actually the n = 4 level.

5. Answer both parts about atomic spectra:

(a) A dense, hot solid emits a continuous rainbow of wavelengths, but an isolated low-pressure gas of the same element emits only sharp discrete lines. Why?

(b) An electron in hydrogen drops from n = 3 (E₃ = −1.51 eV) to n = 1 (E₁ = −13.6 eV). What is the energy of the emitted photon?

(a) Sharply defined, discrete energy levels mean only specific transition energies (and thus wavelengths) are possible — blurred-together levels in a dense solid instead produce a continuum. (b) E_photon = E₃ − E₁ = −1.51 − (−13.6) = 12.09 eV.

6. Fill in the blank: when a photon collides with a free electron and loses energy (gaining wavelength) depending on the scattering angle, this phenomenon is called ___ scattering, and it proved photons carry real momentum.

Compton scattering obeys the same conservation-of-momentum bookkeeping as a particle collision, and the measured wavelength shift only matches predictions if the photon carries real momentum p = h/λ.

7. Answer both parts about the de Broglie wavelength:

(a) A proton and an electron travel at the same speed. Which has the larger de Broglie wavelength, and why?

(b) Fill in the blank: an electron (m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg) moves at 8×10⁶ m/s. Its de Broglie wavelength is λ ≈ ___ picometers (1 pm = 10⁻¹² m; nearest whole number).

(a) λ = h/(mv) shrinks as mass grows; at equal speed, the lighter electron has the larger wavelength. (b) λ = h/(mv) = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/((9.11×10⁻³¹)(8×10⁶)) ≈ 9.09×10⁻¹¹ m = 91 pm.

8. Answer both parts about radioactive decay:

(a) Carbon-14 (Z = 6, A = 14) decays into nitrogen-14 (Z = 7, A = 14). What type of decay is this?

(b) A sample starts with 200 g of an isotope with a half-life of 6 years. How much remains after 18 years?

(a) Z increases by 1 (6→7) while A stays the same (14→14) — the signature of beta decay (a neutron converts to a proton, emitting an electron). (b) 18 years = 3 half-lives. N = 200(1/2)³ = 200/8 = 25 g.

9. Uranium-235 and uranium-238 both have 92 protons but differ in neutron count. They are best described as:

Both have Z = 92 (92 protons, defining the element uranium) but differ in neutron count and therefore mass number A — exactly the definition of isotopes.

10. Answer both parts about mass-energy equivalence:

(a) Splitting a very light nucleus (like helium) into smaller pieces would NOT release energy, while splitting uranium does. Why?

(b) Fill in the blank: a fission reaction has a mass defect of 2.5×10⁻²⁸ kg. Using E = Δmc² (c = 3×10⁸ m/s), the energy released is ___ ×10⁻¹¹ J (just the decimal coefficient).

(a) Binding energy per nucleon peaks near iron; moving toward that peak releases energy. Helium splitting further moves away from the peak (no energy release), while uranium splitting moves toward it (energy released). (b) E = Δmc² = (2.5×10⁻²⁸)(3×10⁸)² = (2.5×10⁻²⁸)(9×10¹⁶) = 2.25×10⁻¹¹ J.
