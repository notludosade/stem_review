Phase Changes and Latent Heat

Ice at 0°C and water at 0°C are wildly different amounts of energy away from each other — even though a thermometer can't tell them apart.

← Lesson 3 Lesson 5 → Contents Glossary →

Latent Heat Q = mL, where L is the latent heat of the phase change (J/kg) — the energy needed to change one kilogram of a substance from one phase to another without any change in temperature. Melting/freezing uses the latent heat of fusion, Lf; boiling/condensing uses the latent heat of vaporization, Lv.
Why temperature stays flat during a phase change During melting or boiling, every joule of heat added goes entirely into breaking the intermolecular bonds that hold a solid's or liquid's structure together, not into speeding up molecular motion. Since temperature (Lesson 2) reflects average molecular kinetic energy, and kinetic energy isn't increasing during the phase change, temperature holds perfectly steady until the phase change is complete — only then does added heat resume raising the temperature.
Example: melting ice How much heat is needed to melt 0.5 kg of ice at 0°C into water at 0°C? Using Lf = 334,000 J/kg for water: Q = mLf = (0.5)(334,000) = 167,000 J — and notice this doesn't change the temperature at all, only the phase.
Example: a multi-stage heating problem Find the total heat needed to turn 1 kg of ice at −10°C into water at 20°C. This requires three stages: (1) warming ice from −10°C to 0°C using cice = 2100 J/(kg·°C): Q₁ = (1)(2100)(10) = 21,000 J; (2) melting at 0°C: Q₂ = (1)(334,000) = 334,000 J; (3) warming water from 0°C to 20°C using cwater = 4186 J/(kg·°C): Q₃ = (1)(4186)(20) = 83,720 J. Total: Q = 21,000 + 334,000 + 83,720 = 438,720 J — notice the melting stage alone dwarfs both temperature-changing stages combined.

1. Why does a substance's temperature stay constant while it melts or boils, even as heat continues to be added?

Since temperature reflects average molecular kinetic energy, and phase-change energy goes into breaking bonds rather than speeding up molecules, temperature holds flat until the phase change finishes.

2. How much heat is needed to boil 2 kg of water at 100°C into steam, using L_v = 2,260,000 J/kg?

Q = mL_v = (2)(2,260,000) = 4,520,000 J.

3. In a temperature-versus-heat-added graph for a substance being steadily heated from solid to gas, the two flat (zero-slope) plateaus correspond to:

The flat plateaus are exactly the melting and boiling phase-change regions, where Q = mL applies instead of Q = mcΔT.

4. Fill in the blank: the energy per kilogram required to change a substance's phase without changing its temperature is called ___.

Latent heat of fusion applies to melting/freezing; latent heat of vaporization applies to boiling/condensing.
