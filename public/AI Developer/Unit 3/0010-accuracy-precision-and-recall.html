Accuracy, Precision, and Recall

"Percent correct" sounds like the obvious way to score a classifier — until the classes aren't evenly split, where it can be badly misleading.

Lesson 11 → Contents Glossary →

Suppose spam is genuinely rare — 5% of emails, 95% legitimate. A model that predicts "not spam" for every single email, never even looking at the content, is right 95% of the time. That number, accuracy, sounds great and is completely useless — the model catches zero spam.

Python — scikit-learn 
from sklearn.metrics import accuracy_score, precision_score, recall_score, confusion_matrix

predictions = model.predict(X_val)

print(confusion_matrix(y_val, predictions))
# [[190   2]     true negatives, false positives
#  [  4   14]]   false negatives, true positives

print(accuracy_score(y_val, predictions))    # 0.97 — looks great
print(precision_score(y_val, predictions))   # 0.88 — of predicted spam, 88% really was
print(recall_score(y_val, predictions))      # 0.78 — of real spam, 78% got caught

The confusion matrix's four cells give both better metrics their names. Precision asks: of everything the model flagged as spam, what fraction actually was spam? It's hurt by false positives — legitimate emails wrongly flagged. Recall asks: of all the real spam that existed, what fraction did the model actually catch? It's hurt by false negatives — real spam that slipped through unflagged. The always-predict-"not spam" model from above would score 0.0 recall despite its 95% accuracy — recall is what exposes the flaw that accuracy alone hides completely.

Definition Accuracy is the percent of all predictions that were correct. Precision is, of everything predicted positive, the fraction that truly was positive. Recall is, of everything truly positive, the fraction the model actually caught.
Why it matters here This closes Unit 3. It's also the direct payoff of Lesson 8's mention that the 0.5 decision threshold can be moved deliberately — raising it trades recall for precision, lowering it trades the other way, and knowing which one a real problem actually needs is exactly why these three numbers exist instead of just one.
