1. At a junction, currents of 9 A and 4 A flow in, and a single current flows out along the only other branch. What is that outgoing current?

ΣI_in = ΣI_out, so the single outgoing branch must carry 9 + 4 = 13 A.

2. Traversing a battery from its + terminal to its − terminal contributes to the loop-rule sum as:

Moving from + to − across a battery moves toward lower potential — a drop of −ε in the loop sum; moving − to + would instead be a gain of +ε.

3. In a series RC charging circuit, a long time after the switch closes (t → ∞, capacitor essentially fully charged), the current in the circuit is:

I(t) = (ε/R)e^(−t/RC) → 0 as t → ∞: once Q/C ≈ ε, there's essentially no net voltage left to push current through R.

4. Doubling both R and C simultaneously changes the time constant τ = RC by a factor of:

τ = RC is a product, so (2R)(2C) = 4RC — doubling each factor multiplies the product by 2 × 2 = 4.

5. A charged capacitor discharges through a resistor. Which of the following correctly describes its voltage as a function of time?

Discharging solves dQ/dt = −Q/(RC), giving exponential decay V(t) = V₀e^(−t/RC) — the first choice is charging's formula, the second is a linear (not exponential) decay, and the third ignores the discharge entirely.

6. A single loop contains a 24 V battery in series with a 5 Ω resistor and a 3 Ω resistor. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: applying the loop rule, the current in this loop is ___ A.

(b) The voltage drop across the 5 Ω resistor is:

(a) ε − IR₁ − IR₂ = 0 ⟹ I = 24/(5+3) = 3 A. (b) V = IR = 3(5) = 15 V.

7. A series RC circuit has R = 1200 Ω and C = 2500 μF, driven by ε = 18 V. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the time constant is τ = ___ s.

(b) At t = τ, the capacitor voltage is approximately:

(a) τ = RC = (1200)(2500×10⁻⁶) = 3 s. (b) V(τ) = 18(1 − e⁻¹) ≈ 18(0.632) ≈ 11.38 V.

8. A capacitor charged to V₀ = 16 V discharges through R = 2000 Ω and C = 300 μF. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: at t = 0.6 s (= τ), V ≈ ___ V (2 decimal places).

(b) If C were instead doubled (R unchanged), the time constant would:

(a) τ = RC = (2000)(300×10⁻⁶) = 0.6 s, so t = 0.6 s = τ: V = 16e⁻¹ ≈ 16(0.368) ≈ 5.89 V. (b) τ = RC is a direct product, so doubling C doubles τ.

9. A series RC circuit has R = 4000 Ω and C = 300 μF. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the time constant is τ = ___ s.

(b) If the resistance were instead cut in half (C unchanged), the time constant would:

(a) τ = RC = (4000)(300×10⁻⁶) = 1.2 s. (b) τ = RC is a direct product, so halving R halves τ.

10. A capacitor charges toward V_max = 36 V with τ = 4 s. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: at t = 8 s (= 2τ), V ≈ ___ V (2 decimal places).

(b) After about how many time constants has a charging capacitor reached approximately 95% of V_max?

(a) V(2τ) = 36(1 − e⁻²) ≈ 36(0.865) ≈ 31.13 V. (b) 1 − e⁻³ ≈ 1 − 0.050 = 0.950, so about 95% complete at 3τ; 5τ is the "practically done" convention at ≈99.3%.
