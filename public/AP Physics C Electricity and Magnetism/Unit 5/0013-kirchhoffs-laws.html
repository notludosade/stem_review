Kirchhoff's Laws

Series and parallel formulas only get you so far. Two bookkeeping rules — charge in equals charge out, and energy gained equals energy lost — solve any circuit, however tangled.

← Lesson 12 Lesson 14 → Contents Glossary →

Definition — Junction Rule (KCL) At any junction (node) in a circuit, ΣI_in = ΣI_out — the total current flowing in must equal the total current flowing out. This is charge conservation: charge can't pile up or vanish at a junction, so whatever flows in has to flow back out.
Definition — Loop Rule (KVL) Around any closed loop in a circuit, Σ(voltage changes) = 0. Traversing a battery from − to + contributes +ε (a gain); traversing a resistor in the direction of assumed current contributes −IR (a drop). This is energy conservation: a charge that returns to its starting point has gained and lost equal amounts of potential energy along the way.
Why You Need Both Laws for General Circuits Series and parallel formulas are really shortcuts that already have Kirchhoff's laws baked into their derivation — but they only work when a circuit can be reduced step by step into single equivalent resistors. Circuits with multiple batteries, or a "bridge" topology that can't be untangled into pure series/parallel chunks, need the junction and loop rules applied directly: assign unknown currents, write one junction equation per independent node and one loop equation per independent loop, then solve the resulting system algebraically.
Worked Example — Applying Both Rules A circuit has two batteries and three resistors forming two loops. Labeling three branch currents I₁, I₂, I₃ meeting at a junction, the junction rule gives I₁ = I₂ + I₃ immediately. Writing the loop rule around each of the two loops (one equation per loop, tracking +ε for each battery traversed − to + and −IR for each resistor in the assumed current direction) produces two more equations — three equations, three unknowns, solvable by substitution or matrix methods.
