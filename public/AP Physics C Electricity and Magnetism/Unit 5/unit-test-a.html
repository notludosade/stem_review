1. At a junction, currents of 5 A and 7 A flow in, and a single current flows out along the only other branch. What is that outgoing current?

ΣI_in = ΣI_out, so the single outgoing branch must carry 5 + 7 = 12 A.

2. Traversing a resistor in the direction opposite to the assumed current direction contributes to the loop-rule sum as:

Traversing a resistor with the assumed current gives −IR (a drop); traversing against it gives +IR (a rise) — the sign flips with the direction of travel, not with the resistor itself.

3. In a series RC charging circuit, immediately after the switch closes (t = 0, capacitor uncharged), the current in the circuit is:

At t = 0, Q = 0, so the capacitor contributes no opposing voltage yet; the full ε drops across R, giving I(0) = ε/R, the maximum current the circuit ever carries.

4. The SI units of the time constant τ = RC work out to:

Ω = V/A and F = C/V, so Ω·F = (V/A)(C/V) = C/A = s — the product of resistance and capacitance is always a time.

5. A capacitor charged to V₀ discharges through a resistor with no battery in the loop. The current in this circuit:

I(t) = (V₀/R)e^(−t/RC): the current is largest the instant discharging begins (the full V₀ drives it through R) and decays toward zero as the capacitor empties.

6. A single loop contains a 15 V battery in series with a 3 Ω resistor and a 2 Ω resistor. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: applying the loop rule, the current in this loop is ___ A.

(b) The voltage drop across the 3 Ω resistor is:

(a) ε − IR₁ − IR₂ = 0 ⟹ I = 15/(3+2) = 3 A. (b) V = IR = 3(3) = 9 V.

7. A series RC circuit has R = 800 Ω and C = 2500 μF, driven by ε = 10 V. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the time constant is τ = ___ s.

(b) At t = τ, the capacitor voltage is approximately:

(a) τ = RC = (800)(2500×10⁻⁶) = 2 s. (b) V(τ) = 10(1 − e⁻¹) ≈ 10(0.632) ≈ 6.32 V.

8. A capacitor charged to V₀ = 12 V discharges through R = 4000 Ω and C = 250 μF. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: at t = 2 s (= 2τ), V ≈ ___ V (2 decimal places).

(b) If R were instead doubled (C unchanged), the time constant would:

(a) τ = RC = (4000)(250×10⁻⁶) = 1 s, so t = 2 s = 2τ: V = 12e⁻² ≈ 12(0.135) ≈ 1.62 V. (b) τ = RC is a direct product, so doubling R doubles τ.

9. A series RC circuit has R = 6000 Ω and C = 150 μF. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the time constant is τ = ___ s.

(b) If the capacitance were instead quadrupled (R unchanged), the time constant would:

(a) τ = RC = (6000)(150×10⁻⁶) = 0.9 s. (b) τ = RC is a direct product, so quadrupling C quadruples τ.

10. A capacitor charges toward V_max = 25 V with τ = 5 s. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: at t = 10 s (= 2τ), V ≈ ___ V (2 decimal places).

(b) By convention, after how many time constants is an RC circuit considered practically "fully" charged or discharged?

(a) V(2τ) = 25(1 − e⁻²) ≈ 25(0.865) ≈ 21.62 V. (b) By t = 5τ, e^(−5) ≈ 0.0067, so charging is over 99% complete and discharging has under 1% of V₀ left — the standard engineering convention for "done."
