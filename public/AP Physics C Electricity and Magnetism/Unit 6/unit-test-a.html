1. A positive charge moves in the +x direction through a field B pointing in the +y direction. Using F = qv × B, the force on the charge points:

v × B = x̂ × ŷ = ẑ, and q is positive, so F = qv × B points in the +z direction.

2. A charged particle moves in a circle inside a uniform magnetic field, with no other forces acting on it. Its kinetic energy:

F·v = 0 for a magnetic force at every instant, so it can never do work on the particle — speed and kinetic energy stay constant no matter how long it orbits.

3. A straight current-carrying wire sits in a uniform magnetic field, oriented parallel to B (θ = 0° between the wire and B). The force on the wire is:

F = BIL sinθ is zero when the wire is parallel to B (θ = 0°) and maximal when perpendicular (θ = 90°) — the opposite of what "parallel means reinforcement" might suggest.

4. A current loop's torque is given by τ = IAB sinθ, where θ is measured from the loop's normal vector to B. Torque is maximized when:

sinθ is largest at θ = 90°, which is when the normal vector is perpendicular to B — equivalently, when the loop's own plane is parallel to B.

5. Ampère's law, ∮B·dl = μ₀I_enc, is the magnetic-field analog of which electrostatics tool?

Gauss's law relates flux through a closed surface to enclosed charge; Ampère's law relates circulation around a closed loop to enclosed current — the same symmetry trick (choosing a surface or loop where the field is constant) makes both trivial to apply.

6. A charge q = 4×10⁻⁶ C moves at v = 3×10⁵ m/s perpendicular to a field B = 0.25 T. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the force on the charge is F = ___ N.

(b) If instead v points in +x and B points in +y, and the charge is positive, the force points:

(a) F = qvB sinθ = (4×10⁻⁶)(3×10⁵)(0.25)(sin 90°) = 0.3 N. (b) x̂ × ŷ = ẑ, and q is positive, so F points in +z.

7. A proton (q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C, m = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg) moves at v = 2.4×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to B = 0.4 T. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the orbit radius is r ≈ ___ cm (2 decimal places).

(b) If B were instead doubled (v, q, m unchanged), the radius would:

(a) r = mv/(qB) = (1.67×10⁻²⁷)(2.4×10⁶)/[(1.6×10⁻¹⁹)(0.4)] ≈ 0.0626 m ≈ 6.26 cm. (b) r = mv/(qB) is inversely proportional to B, so doubling B cuts r in half.

8. A wire segment L = 0.5 m carries I = 8 A perpendicular to B = 0.4 T. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the force on the wire is F = ___ N.

(b) If this same wire were instead bent into a closed loop carrying the same current in the same uniform field, the net force on the loop would be:

(a) F = BIL sinθ = (0.4)(8)(0.5)(sin 90°) = 1.6 N. (b) In a uniform field, forces on opposite parts of any closed loop always cancel — net force is zero even though net torque generally isn't.

9. A long straight wire carries I = 12 A. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: at r = 0.06 m, the field is B ≈ ___ ×10⁻⁴ T (enter just the coefficient).

(b) If the current were doubled and the distance from the wire were also doubled, B would:

(a) B = μ₀I/(2πr) = (4π×10⁻⁷)(12)/[2π(0.06)] = 0.4×10⁻⁴ T. (b) B ∝ I/r, so doubling I multiplies B by 2 while doubling r divides B by 2 — the net effect is B unchanged.

10. An ideal solenoid has n = 900 turns/m carrying I = 6 A. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the interior field is B ≈ ___ ×10⁻³ T (2 decimal places).

(b) Doubling the number of turns per unit length n (I unchanged) would cause the interior field to:

(a) B = μ₀nI = (4π×10⁻⁷)(900)(6) ≈ 6.79×10⁻³ T. (b) B = μ₀nI is a direct product, so doubling n doubles B.
