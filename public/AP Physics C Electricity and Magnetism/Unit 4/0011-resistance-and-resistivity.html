Resistance and Resistivity

Unlike capacitance, which depended only on shape, a wire's resistance depends on both its geometry and the material it's made of.

← Lesson 10 Lesson 12 → Contents Glossary →

Definition — Resistance and Resistivity Ohm's law: V = IR holds for "ohmic" materials, where R stays constant across a wide range of V and I. For a uniform wire, R = ρL/A: length L (more material to push through, more resistance), cross-sectional area A (a wider path, less resistance), and resistivity ρ — an intrinsic property of the material itself, independent of the wire's shape. Copper has low ρ (a good conductor); nichrome has ρ nearly 100× higher (deliberately used in heating elements).
Method — Resistivity Depends on Temperature For most conductors, resistivity rises with temperature (more thermal jostling of the lattice means more collisions slowing drifting electrons down): ρ(T) ≈ ρ₀[1 + α(T − T₀)], where α is the material's temperature coefficient of resistivity and T₀ is a reference temperature (often 20°C). This is why a light bulb's filament resistance is noticeably higher glowing hot than at room temperature.
Why Resistance Isn't Just a Material Property Contrast this with capacitance (Lesson 7), which depended only on geometry (C = ε₀A/d), with the material playing no role for a vacuum-gap capacitor. Resistance depends on both: geometry through L and A, and material through ρ. Two wires of the same copper but different lengths have different resistance; two wires of the same length and thickness but different metals also have different resistance. Both factors matter simultaneously.
Worked Example A copper wire (ρ = 1.68×10⁻⁸ Ω·m) is 1 m long with cross-sectional area 2×10⁻⁷ m². R = ρL/A = (1.68×10⁻⁸)(1)/(2×10⁻⁷) = 1.68×10⁻⁸/2×10⁻⁷ = 0.084 Ω.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. If a wire's length is doubled while its cross-sectional area and material stay the same, its resistance:

R = ρL/A is directly proportional to L, so doubling the length doubles the resistance.

2. Fill in the blank: a copper wire (ρ = 1.68×10⁻⁸ Ω·m) is 1 m long with cross-sectional area 2×10⁻⁷ m². Its resistance is ___ Ω.

R = ρL/A = (1.68×10⁻⁸)(1)/(2×10⁻⁷) = 0.084 Ω.

3. Resistivity ρ, unlike resistance R, depends primarily on:

Resistivity characterizes the material — two different-shaped wires of the same metal share the same ρ even though their resistances R = ρL/A differ.

4. If a wire's cross-sectional area is doubled while its length and material stay the same, its resistance:

R = ρL/A is inversely proportional to A, so doubling the cross-sectional area halves the resistance.
