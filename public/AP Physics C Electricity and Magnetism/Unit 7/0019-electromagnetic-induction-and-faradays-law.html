Electromagnetic Induction and Faraday's Law

A magnet sitting still next to a loop of wire does nothing at all. Move it — or move the loop, or just rotate one relative to the other — and a current appears with no battery in sight. That's the entire idea: a changing magnetic flux induces an EMF.

Recall — Magnetic Flux Magnetic flux Φ_B = ∫B·dA, the same "field through a surface" bookkeeping Unit 1 used for electric flux. For a uniform field B through a flat loop of area A, this reduces to Φ_B = BA cos θ, where θ is the angle between B and the loop's normal vector. Unit 6 already used this to state that magnetic field lines never terminate — the net flux through any closed surface is always zero. This lesson is about flux through an open surface bounded by a loop, and what happens when that flux changes.
Definition — Faraday's Law EMF = −dΦ_B/dt, or for a coil of N tightly wound turns, EMF = −N dΦ_B/dt. A changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an EMF around that circuit, which drives a current if the circuit is closed. The minus sign encodes a direction — which way the induced current actually flows — and that direction is the entire subject of Lenz's law, next lesson. For now, focus on magnitude: no change in flux, no induced EMF, no matter how strong the field itself is.
Worked Derivation — Motional EMF, EMF = BLv A conducting rod of length L slides with speed v along two parallel rails, through a uniform field B perpendicular to the plane the rails sweep out. Two ways to reach the same result. Via Faraday's law: in time dt the rod sweeps out extra area dA = L(v dt), so dΦ_B = B·L·v·dt and EMF = dΦ_B/dt = BLv. Via the magnetic force directly: each free charge q in the moving rod feels a force qv×B along the rod's length, equivalent to an effective electric field of magnitude vB pushing charge from one end to the other; integrating that field along the rod's length L gives EMF = ∫(v×B)·dl = BLv. Same answer, two derivations — motional EMF isn't a separate law, it's Faraday's law (or the magnetic force law) applied to one specific, concrete geometry.
Why "Changing" Is Doing All the Work Flux Φ_B = BA cos θ has three ingredients, and changing any one of them induces an EMF: change B (an electromagnet ramping its current up or down near a stationary loop), change A (the sliding-rod case above — the circuit's effective area grows as the rod moves), or change θ (a coil rotating inside a fixed field — this is exactly how a generator turns mechanical spin into an alternating EMF). A powerful stationary magnet next to a stationary loop, however strong, induces nothing whatsoever — flux that isn't changing contributes zero to −dΦ_B/dt.
Worked Example A circular loop of radius 0.1 m (A = πr² ≈ 0.0314 m², N = 1) sits in a magnetic field that ramps uniformly from 0.2 T to 0.8 T over 0.5 s, perpendicular to the loop (θ = 0). dB/dt = (0.8 − 0.2)/0.5 = 1.2 T/s, so |EMF| = A(dB/dt) = (0.0314)(1.2) ≈ 0.0377 V — about 37.7 mV, entirely from the field changing, with the loop itself never moving.

1. According to Faraday's law, an EMF is induced in a loop whenever:

Faraday's law is EMF = −dΦ_B/dt — it's the rate of change of flux that matters, not the flux's mere presence.

2. Fill in the blank: a loop of area 0.05 m² sits in a field that ramps uniformly from 0.1 T to 0.5 T over 2 s, perpendicular to the loop. The induced EMF magnitude is ___ ×10⁻² V (enter just the coefficient, e.g. 3.77).

dB/dt = (0.5 − 0.1)/2 = 0.2 T/s. |EMF| = A(dB/dt) = (0.05)(0.2) = 0.01 V = 1.00×10⁻² V.

3. A rod of length L slides with speed v perpendicular to a uniform field B, along frictionless rails. The motional EMF it generates is:

Whether derived from dΦ_B/dt or from integrating the magnetic force qv×B along the rod, motional EMF works out to EMF = BLv.

4. A strong bar magnet is held perfectly still, a few centimeters from a stationary wire loop. What EMF does the loop experience?

Faraday's law responds to dΦ_B/dt. A stationary magnet next to a stationary loop produces a constant, unchanging flux — its derivative is zero, so the induced EMF is zero.
