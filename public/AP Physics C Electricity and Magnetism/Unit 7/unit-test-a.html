1. A loop of area 0.02 m² sits in a uniform magnetic field that increases from 0.3 T to 0.9 T over 3 s, perpendicular to the loop. What is the induced EMF?

dB/dt = (0.9 − 0.3)/3 = 0.2 T/s. EMF = A(dB/dt) = (0.02)(0.2) = 0.004 V = 4×10⁻³ V.

2. A conducting rod of length 0.4 m slides at 3 m/s perpendicular to a uniform 0.5 T field, along frictionless rails. What is the motional EMF?

EMF = BLv = (0.5)(0.4)(3) = 0.6 V.

3. The current in a solenoid is decreasing, so the magnetic flux through a nearby, coaxial, stationary loop is decreasing too. By Lenz's law, the induced current in the loop:

Lenz's law: the induced current opposes the change in flux. Since flux is decreasing, the induced current flows to reinforce it — creating a field in the same direction as the original, weakening field.

4. An RL circuit has L = 0.2 H and R = 25 Ω. What is its time constant τ?

τ = L/R = 0.2/25 = 0.008 s = 8 ms.

5. Maxwell added a term to Ampère's law so it would give a consistent magnetic field regardless of which surface, bounded by the same loop, was used to compute enclosed current — including a surface passing between a charging capacitor's plates. What is this added term called?

Maxwell's displacement current term, μ₀ε₀ dΦ_E/dt, supplies exactly the "missing" contribution wherever a changing electric flux — rather than literal charge — crosses the chosen surface, making Ampère's law consistent no matter which surface is used.

6. A circular loop of radius 0.15 m (A ≈ 0.0707 m²) sits in a magnetic field that increases uniformly from 0.3 T to 0.9 T over 3 s, perpendicular to the loop. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the induced EMF magnitude is ___ ×10⁻² V (enter just the coefficient).

(b) If the loop's radius were instead doubled (field rate unchanged), the induced EMF would:

(a) dB/dt = (0.9 − 0.3)/3 = 0.2 T/s. EMF = A(dB/dt) = (0.0707)(0.2) ≈ 0.01414 V ≈ 1.41×10⁻² V. (b) Area scales with r², so doubling the radius quadruples A and therefore quadruples the EMF at fixed dB/dt.

7. A conducting rod of length 0.6 m slides along frictionless rails through a uniform 0.8 T field, perpendicular to the rails' plane. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: if the rod's speed is 5 m/s, the motional EMF is ___ V.

(b) If the rod's speed were instead tripled (B and L unchanged), the motional EMF would:

(a) EMF = BLv = (0.8)(0.6)(5) = 2.4 V. (b) EMF = BLv is directly proportional to v, so tripling v triples the EMF.

8. A loop with resistance 4 Ω experiences an induced EMF of 2 V due to a changing magnetic flux. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the induced current in the loop is ___ A.

(b) If the magnetic flux through the loop were instead changing in the opposite sense (increasing rather than decreasing), the direction of the induced current would:

(a) I = EMF/R = 2/4 = 0.5 A. (b) Lenz's law direction depends on which way flux is changing — reversing that change reverses the induced current's direction.

9. A coil has self-inductance L = 0.5 H, with current through it changing at dI/dt = 4 A/s. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the magnitude of the self-induced EMF is ___ V.

(b) This same coil is placed in series with a 10 Ω resistor and a battery, forming an RL circuit. Its time constant τ is:

(a) EMF = L(dI/dt) = (0.5)(4) = 2 V. (b) τ = L/R = 0.5/10 = 0.05 s = 50 ms.

10. An electromagnetic wave in vacuum has frequency f = 6×10¹⁴ Hz. Use c = 3×10⁸ m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: its wavelength is ___ ×10⁻⁷ m (enter just the coefficient).

(b) This wave's existence — E and B fields propagating through empty space with no charge or current present — follows directly from combining which two of Maxwell's equations?

(a) λ = c/f = (3×10⁸)/(6×10¹⁴) = 5×10⁻⁷ m. (b) A changing B induces a circulating E (Faraday's law), and in vacuum a changing E induces a circulating B (Ampère–Maxwell law with I_enc = 0) — together they let the two fields regenerate each other indefinitely.
