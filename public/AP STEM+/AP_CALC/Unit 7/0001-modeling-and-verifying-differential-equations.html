Modeling and Verifying Differential Equations

1. What a differential equation is

Definition — Differential Equation A differential equation is an equation relating a function to one or more of its derivatives — for example, dy/dx = 2y. A solution is any function that makes the equation true. Unlike an algebraic equation, whose solution is a number, a differential equation's solution is a whole function.

2. Modeling situations with differential equations

Method — Translating Words into an Equation Look for a rate ("grows," "decays," "changes at a rate proportional to") and identify what it's proportional to. "The rate of change of y is proportional to y itself" becomes dy/dx = ky for some constant k. "The population grows at a rate proportional to the difference between the population and a carrying capacity" becomes dP/dt = k(K − P) — a preview of Lesson 6's logistic model.
Worked Example "A cup of coffee cools at a rate proportional to the difference between its temperature and the room's 70° temperature." Let T be the coffee's temperature at time t. The rate of change dT/dt is proportional to (T − 70), so dT/dt = k(T − 70), with k < 0 since the coffee is cooling toward 70°, not away from it.

3. Verifying a proposed solution

Method — Verifying a Solution To check whether a function solves a differential equation, differentiate the proposed function and substitute both it and its derivative into the equation. If the two sides match for every x, it's a solution.
Worked Example Does y = 3e2x solve y′ − 2y = 0? Differentiating: y′ = 6e2x. Substituting: 6e2x − 2(3e2x) = 6e2x − 6e2x = 0. The two sides match, so yes — y = 3e2x is a solution.

1. "The rate of change of y is proportional to y itself" translates to:

"Proportional to y" means the rate equals a constant times y — dy/dx = ky is exactly that statement written as an equation.

2. Substituting y = 3e²ˣ and y′ = 6e²ˣ into y′ − 2y gives 6e²ˣ − 6e²ˣ = ___

The two terms cancel exactly, confirming the differential equation holds for every x — the hallmark of a valid solution.

3. A differential equation's solution is:

Unlike an algebraic equation like 2x = 6, whose solution is a single number, a differential equation is satisfied by an entire function — often a whole family of them, as Lesson 4 will show.
