1. A replacement texture loads with no error, but appears stretched and mirrored on the model. What is the most likely cause?

A valid image file can still look wrong if it doesn't correspond correctly to the model's UV layout.

2. Fill in the blank: a small mod built specifically to reconcile two other mods' conflicting asset changes is called a ___ ___.

Compatibility patches are one of the most common mod categories on any large mod list.

3. Why might a replacement voice line that plays back perfectly clearly still cause a visible problem in-game?

Audio sync problems come from timing mismatches, not from whether the audio itself plays back cleanly.

4. Fill in the blank: a localization mod created entirely by a fan community, rather than the original publisher, is called a ___ ___.

Fan translations have made many otherwise-unlocalized games playable in new languages through community effort alone.

5. A dagger deals 5 damage at 4 attacks per second; a hammer deals 20 damage at 1 attack per second. How do their DPS values compare?

5 × 4 = 20 and 20 × 1 = 20 — identical DPS despite very different individual stats.

6. Two separate risks can break an asset swap even without any error message appearing. Answer both parts:

(a) What must a replacement texture generally match, beyond just being a valid image?

(b) What happens if the engine can't find the replacement due to a wrong file name or path?

Format, UV layout, and exact naming/path all have to line up — a mismatch on any of them produces a silent, not loud, failure.

7. A game uses a commercially licensed music track. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is redistributing that original track inside a mod's download risky?

(b) What is a safer alternative for a modder who wants to change the music?

Replacing licensed music sidesteps the licensing problem; redistributing the original track does not.

8. A modder wants to lower the risk and skill required for their first mod. Answer both parts:

(a) Which category of mod is generally the lowest-risk, lowest-skill option?

(b) Why is this category also considered low legal risk?

Data-driven mods are low-risk on both technical and legal fronts, for the same underlying reason — only values change, nothing copyrighted is touched.

9. A modder wants to change what items a boss enemy can drop and at what odds. Answer both parts:

(a) What data structure controls this?

(b) Does changing this typically require any new assets or scripts?

Drop tables are a classic data-driven target — editable with no new assets or scripting at all.

10. Unit 4 covered three asset-replacement categories. Answer both parts:

(a) Which category most directly risks a licensing problem beyond the base game's own copyright?

(b) Which category is generally the safest and simplest to attempt first?

Licensed audio carries the clearest extra legal risk; data-driven changes remain the simplest and safest category throughout the unit.
