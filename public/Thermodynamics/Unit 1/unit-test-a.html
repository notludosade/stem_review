1. Systems X and Y are each separately found to be in thermal equilibrium with system Z, though X and Y are never placed in direct contact with each other. What can you conclude?

This is exactly the zeroth law: equilibrium with a common third system (here Z, playing the role of a thermometer) guarantees equilibrium between X and Y, with no direct contact required.

2. An oven is set to 375°F. What is this in Celsius, to the nearest whole degree?

°C = (5/9)(°F − 32) = (5/9)(343) ≈ 190.6°C ≈ 191°C.

3. A thermally insulated, gas-filled cylinder has its piston pushed in by an external force, compressing the gas and raising its temperature. Using this course's sign convention (W positive when done BY the system), which best describes Q and W for the gas?

Insulation blocks heat, so Q = 0. The piston moves inward under an external push, so the gas is compressed rather than expanding — work is done on the gas, making W (work done by the gas) negative.

4. A concrete wall (k = 1.7 W/(m·K)) has area 3 m² and thickness 0.15 m, with a 12 K temperature difference across it. What is the conduction rate?

Q̇ = kAΔT/L = (1.7)(3)(12)/(0.15) = 61.2/0.15 = 408 W.

5. Which heat transfer mechanism specifically requires the bulk motion of a fluid, and therefore cannot occur through a vacuum?

Convection is specifically bulk fluid motion carrying heat; conduction needs contact but no bulk flow, and radiation needs no medium at all.

6. A lab sample is measured at 10°C. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: in Kelvin, this temperature is ___ K.

(b) If the sample's temperature then rises by 25 Celsius degrees, its Kelvin temperature rises by:

(a) K = °C + 273.15 = 10 + 273.15 = 283.15 K. (b) Celsius and Kelvin share the same size degree, offset by a fixed constant, so any change in °C equals the same change in K.

7. A gas moves from state 1 to state 2 via Process I, absorbing Q = 180 J while doing W = 60 J of work. A second experiment moves the same gas between the same two states via Process II, this time doing W = 90 J of work. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: ΔU for Process I is ___ J.

(b) What must Q be for Process II, given that ΔU must match Process I's value (since ΔU depends only on the states, not the path)?

(a) ΔU = Q − W = 180 − 60 = 120 J. (b) U is a state function, so Process II must reach the same ΔU = 120 J: Q = ΔU + W = 120 + 90 = 210 J.

8. A steel plate (k = 50 W/(m·K)) has area 0.05 m² and thickness 0.02 m, with an 80 K temperature difference across it. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the conduction rate is ___ ×10³ W.

(b) If the plate's area were instead tripled (all else unchanged), the conduction rate would:

(a) Q̇ = kAΔT/L = (50)(0.05)(80)/(0.02) = 200/0.02 = 10,000 W = 10×10³ W. (b) Q̇ is directly proportional to A, so tripling A triples Q̇.

9. A thermometer reads 25°C when placed against object A, and also reads 25°C when placed against object B (A and B never touch each other directly). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: this common reading, expressed in Kelvin, is ___ K.

(b) By the zeroth law, are A and B in thermal equilibrium with each other?

(a) K = °C + 273.15 = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K. (b) That's the zeroth law directly, with the thermometer playing the role of the common system C.

10. Consider the three heat transfer mechanisms together. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: exactly ___ of the three mechanisms (conduction, convection, radiation) require a physical medium to be present.

(b) Which single mechanism is the odd one out — the only one that can cross a total vacuum?

(a) Conduction and convection both require a physical medium; only radiation is electromagnetic and needs none. (b) Radiation follows the Stefan–Boltzmann T⁴ law and is the only mechanism of the three that can cross a vacuum.
