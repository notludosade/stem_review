The Ideal Gas Law and Thermodynamic Processes

One equation ties pressure, volume, and temperature together for a gas. Holding any one of them fixed while the other two change traces out a distinctly-shaped path — and those paths get names you'll use for the rest of the course.

← Lesson 5 Unit 3, Lesson 7 → Contents Glossary →

Definition — The Ideal Gas Law PV = nRT, where P is pressure (Pa), V is volume (m³), n is the amount of gas (moles), T is absolute temperature (K, never Celsius or Fahrenheit here), and R ≈ 8.314 J/(mol·K) is the universal gas constant. It's called "ideal" because it assumes gas particles have negligible size and no intermolecular forces — an excellent approximation for most gases at ordinary pressures and temperatures (Unit 6 revisits where this assumption breaks down).
Method — Naming the Standard Processes Holding one of P, V, or T fixed while a gas changes state traces a specific path on a PV diagram (pressure on the vertical axis, volume on the horizontal axis): isobaric (constant P) traces a horizontal line; isochoric (constant V, also called isovolumetric) traces a vertical line; isothermal (constant T) traces a curve where P ∝ 1/V, a hyperbola. Every process this course studies is one of these three, or a combination of them end to end.
Why the PV Diagram Becomes the Main Tool A PV diagram doesn't just describe a gas's state (a single point, since T follows from P and V via PV = nRT) — it will turn out, starting next lesson, to also encode the work a process does as the area beneath its path. That double duty — showing both where a gas is and how much work getting there cost — is why nearly every remaining lesson in this course draws one.
Worked Example — Solving for an Unknown State Variable n = 2 mol of an ideal gas occupies V = 0.05 m³ at T = 300 K. Find the pressure.
P = nRT/V = (2)(8.314)(300)/(0.05) = 4988.4/0.05 ≈ 99,768 Pa ≈ 99.8 kPa (a bit below atmospheric pressure, ≈101.3 kPa).

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. In an isochoric process, which variable is held constant?

"Isochoric" (also isovolumetric) means constant volume — the process traces a vertical line on a PV diagram, since V never changes.

2. Fill in the blank: n = 1.5 mol of an ideal gas occupies V = 0.03 m³ at T = 250 K. Its pressure is P = ___ kPa.

P = nRT/V = (1.5)(8.314)(250)/(0.03) = 3117.75/0.03 = 103,925 Pa ≈ 103.9 kPa.

3. On a PV diagram, an isothermal process (constant T) traces out:

With T fixed, PV = nRT means PV = constant, so P = (nRT)/V ∝ 1/V — exactly the shape of a hyperbola, not a straight line.

4. Which process is represented by a horizontal line on a PV diagram?

A horizontal line on a PV diagram means P stays the same while V changes — exactly the definition of isobaric.
