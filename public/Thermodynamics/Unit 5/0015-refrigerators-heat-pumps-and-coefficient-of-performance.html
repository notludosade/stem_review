Refrigerators, Heat Pumps, and Coefficient of Performance

Definition — Coefficient of Performance A refrigerator uses work input W to pump heat Q_c out of a cold space (what you want) and exhausts Q_h = Q_c + W to a hot reservoir. Its coefficient of performance, COP_R = Q_c/W, measures cold-space heat removed per unit of work spent. A heat pump is the identical device with the goal flipped — Q_h delivered to a warm space is what you want — so COP_HP = Q_h/W. Since Q_h = Q_c + W, the two are related by COP_HP = COP_R + 1.
Why COP Isn't Called "Efficiency" Unlike e = W/Q_h (always less than 1), COP_R and COP_HP are routinely greater than 1 — a COP of 3 means 3 J of heat moved per 1 J of work spent, which sounds like it's "beating" 100%. It isn't a violation of anything: COP measures heat moved per work spent, not work out per heat in, so there's no reason it should be capped at 1. This is exactly why a heat pump can be a more efficient way to heat a house than a resistive electric heater (COP effectively 1, converting work straight to heat) — it moves existing heat rather than manufacturing it from scratch.
Worked Example — Refrigerator and Heat Pump COP A refrigerator removes Q_c = 150 J from its cold interior using W = 50 J of work input. Find COP_R, and the equivalent COP_HP for the same cycle.
COP_R = Q_c/W = 150/50 = 3.0.
Q_h = Q_c + W = 150 + 50 = 200 J, so COP_HP = Q_h/W = 200/50 = 4.0 — matching COP_R + 1 = 3.0 + 1 = 4.0 exactly.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. A refrigerator's coefficient of performance, COP_R, is defined as:

COP_R specifically measures the useful output (cold-space heat removed) per unit of work paid for it — the refrigerator's whole job.

2. Fill in the blank: a refrigerator removes Q_c = 240 J using W = 60 J of work. COP_R = ___.

COP_R = Q_c/W = 240/60 = 4.

3. For the same physical cycle, how are COP_HP and COP_R related?

Since Q_h = Q_c + W, dividing through by W gives Q_h/W = Q_c/W + 1, i.e. COP_HP = COP_R + 1.

4. Fill in the blank: the Carnot (maximum theoretical) coefficient of performance for a refrigerator is COP_R,Carnot = T_c/(T_h − T_c). For T_h = 300 K and T_c = 250 K, COP_R,Carnot = ___.

COP_R,Carnot = T_c/(T_h − T_c) = 250/(300 − 250) = 250/50 = 5.
