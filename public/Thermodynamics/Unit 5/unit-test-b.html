1. An engine absorbs Q_h = 1100 J and exhausts Q_c = 682 J per cycle. What is its efficiency?

e = 1 − Q_c/Q_h = 1 − 682/1100 = 1 − 0.62 = 0.38.

2. A Carnot engine operates between T_h = 900 K and T_c = 540 K. What is e_Carnot?

e_Carnot = 1 − T_c/T_h = 1 − 540/900 = 1 − 0.60 = 0.40.

3. A refrigerator removes Q_c = 400 J using W = 80 J of work. What is COP_R?

COP_R = Q_c/W = 400/80 = 5.0.

4. If Q_c decreases while Q_h stays fixed, an engine's efficiency e = 1 − Q_c/Q_h:

A smaller Q_c/Q_h ratio means less of the absorbed heat is being wasted as exhaust, directly raising e.

5. As T_h grows very large (T_h → ∞), with T_c fixed, e_Carnot = 1 − T_c/T_h approaches:

As T_h → ∞, the ratio T_c/T_h → 0, so e_Carnot → 1 — though an infinitely hot reservoir is itself physically unattainable.

6. An engine has efficiency e = 0.35 and absorbs Q_h = 1600 J per cycle. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the work it delivers is W = ___ J.

(b) If Q_h were instead tripled while e stayed fixed at 0.35, W would:

(a) W = e × Q_h = (0.35)(1600) = 560 J. (b) W = eQ_h is directly proportional to Q_h at fixed e, so tripling Q_h triples W.

7. A Carnot engine operates between T_h = 680 K and T_c = 340 K. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: e_Carnot = ___ (as a decimal, e.g. 0.5).

(b) A real engine operating between these same two temperatures will have an actual efficiency that is:

(a) e_Carnot = 1 − 340/680 = 1 − 0.5 = 0.5. (b) Carnot's theorem sets this as the absolute ceiling for any engine operating between these two temperatures.

8. A device removes Q_c = 450 J from a cold space using W = 90 J of work. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: treating this as a heat pump instead, COP_HP = ___.

(b) This device's COP_R (refrigerator mode) must be:

(a) Q_h = Q_c + W = 450 + 90 = 540 J, so COP_HP = Q_h/W = 540/90 = 6. (b) COP_R = Q_c/W = 450/90 = 5, matching COP_HP − 1 = 6 − 1 = 5.

9. A Carnot engine has e_Carnot = 0.4 while operating with a hot reservoir at T_h = 500 K. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: solving e_Carnot = 1 − T_c/T_h for T_c gives T_c = ___ K.

(b) If this same engine instead used a different working substance (steam instead of an ideal gas) but the same T_h and T_c, its Carnot efficiency would be:

(a) T_c = T_h(1 − e_Carnot) = 500(1 − 0.4) = 500(0.6) = 300 K. (b) Carnot's theorem guarantees the ceiling depends only on the two reservoir temperatures, never on what the working substance happens to be.

10. A Carnot refrigerator operates between T_h = 340 K and T_c = 300 K. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: using COP_R,Carnot = T_c/(T_h − T_c), COP_R,Carnot = ___.

(b) On a much hotter day, T_h rises while T_c (the desired interior temperature) stays fixed. COP_R,Carnot would:

(a) COP_R,Carnot = 300/(340 − 300) = 300/40 = 7.5. (b) A larger T_h − T_c gap shrinks the ratio T_c/(T_h − T_c) — exactly why air conditioners struggle more on the hottest days.
