1. Flipping a fair coin 3 times, how many outcomes are in the sample space?

Each flip independently has 2 outcomes, so by the multiplication principle, 3 flips give 2·2·2 = 8 total outcomes.

2. A jar has 12 marbles: 5 red, 4 blue, 3 green. One marble is drawn at random. What is P(red)?

P(red) = |favorable| / |total| = 5/12.

3. Among 200 students, 120 study Spanish, 50 study French, and 20 study both. Given that a randomly chosen student studies Spanish, what is P(studies French | studies Spanish)?

P(French | Spanish) = P(French ∩ Spanish) / P(Spanish) = (20/200) / (120/200) = 20/120 = 1/6.

4. Fill in the blank: for any event E, 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ ___.

Probability is bounded between impossibility (0) and certainty (1).

5. A random variable X takes value 4 with probability 0.25 and value −2 with probability 0.75. What is E[X]?

E[X] = 4(0.25) + (−2)(0.75) = 1 − 1.5 = −0.5.

6. Let S = {1,...,6} with equally likely outcomes, E1 = {1,2}, E2 = {3,4}. Answer both parts:

(a) What is P(E1 ∪ E2)?

(b) Fill in the blank: computing P(E1 ∪ E2) by simply adding P(E1) + P(E2) is only valid because E1 and E2 are ___.

(a) P(E1) = 2/6, P(E2) = 2/6, and since E1 ∩ E2 = ∅, P(E1 ∪ E2) = 2/6 + 2/6 = 4/6 = 2/3. (b) Additivity (Axiom 3) requires mutual exclusivity — otherwise the overlap would be double-counted.

7. Among 500 people, 200 own a car; of those 200, 150 also own a bike. Answer both parts:

(a) What is P(Bike | Car)?

(b) Fill in the blank: P(Bike ∩ Car) = ___ / 500.

(a) P(Bike | Car) = 150/200 = 3/4. (b) 150 of the 500 people own both a car and a bike, directly given.

8. Let P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5, P(A ∩ B) = 0.2. Answer both parts:

(a) Are A and B independent?

(b) Fill in the blank: if instead P(A ∩ B) = 0.3, A and B would be ___.

(a) Independence requires P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B), which holds exactly here. (b) 0.3 ≠ 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2, so the events would no longer be independent — they'd be dependent.

9. A game costs $3 to play. You win $9 with probability 0.2, and win nothing otherwise. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the expected net profit per play?

(b) Fill in the blank: by linearity of expectation, playing this game twice has a total expected net profit of $___.

(a) Expected winnings = 9(0.2) = 1.8; net profit = 1.8 − 3 = −$1.20. (b) Linearity of expectation: E[X₁ + X₂] = E[X₁] + E[X₂] = −1.2 + (−1.2) = −$2.40.

10. A committee of 3 is randomly selected from 5 men and 4 women (9 people total, all equally likely committees). Answer both parts:

(a) What is P(all 3 selected are women)?

(b) Fill in the blank: the total number of equally-likely committees is C(9,3) = ___.

(a) Favorable outcomes: C(4,3) = 4 (all-women committees). Total: C(9,3) = 84. P = 4/84 = 1/21. (b) C(9,3) = 9!/(3!·6!) = 84.
