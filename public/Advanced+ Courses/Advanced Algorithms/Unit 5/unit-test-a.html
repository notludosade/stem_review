1. A tree has root R (weight 5) with two leaf children A (weight 3) and B (weight 4). Using the include/exclude tree DP for maximum weight independent set, what is the maximum weight achievable?

Since A and B are only adjacent to R (not to each other), selecting both leaves gives 3+4=7, beating selecting R alone (5). All three can't be picked together, since each leaf is adjacent to R.

2. Answer both parts about the tree DP algorithm:

(a) Why must every child of v be fully processed before v's own include/exclude values can be computed?

(b) Fill in the blank: the tree DP's total runtime is Θ(V) because each ___ in the tree contributes only O(1) work exactly once, across all vertices combined.

(a) The parent's recurrence literally sums and compares its children's include/exclude values, so those values must already exist — forcing post-order (children-before-parent) traversal. (b) Summing that constant per-edge work across the whole tree is exactly what gives the Θ(V) total.

3. Fill in the blank: checking whether city k is already in a bitmask, and adding city k to that bitmask, are both bitwise operations that run in O(___) time, independent of how many cities have been visited.

mask & (1<<k) and mask | (1<<k) are both single machine-word operations, regardless of how large the mask or how many cities are already marked.

4. In a 3-city TSP instance (cities 0, 1, 2) with dist(0,1)=10, dist(0,2)=15, dist(1,2)=20, and dp[{0}][0] = 0. Answer both parts:

(a) What does dp[{0,1}][1] equal?

(b) Fill in the blank: given dp[{0,1}][1] = 10, dp[{0,1,2}][2] = dp[{0,1}][1] + dist(1,2) = 10 + 20 = ___.

(a) The only way to have visited exactly {0,1} and be standing at city 1 is having come directly from city 0, so dp[{0,1}][1] = dp[{0}][0] + dist(0,1) = 0 + 10 = 10. (b) Extending that partial tour to also visit city 2, arriving last, adds dist(1,2) = 20, giving 30.

5. Bitmask DP solves TSP in Θ(n²·2ⁿ) time, a large improvement over Θ(n!) brute force. Why doesn't this contradict TSP's status as an NP-hard problem?

2ⁿ growth is exponential no matter what polynomial factor multiplies it, so this remains fully consistent with no known polynomial-time algorithm existing for TSP.

6. For matrices A₁ (10×100), A₂ (100×5), A₃ (5×50), answer both parts:

(a) What is the total cost of computing (A₁A₂)A₃?

(b) Fill in the blank: the alternative parenthesization A₁(A₂A₃) costs ___ total — ten times more expensive, despite computing the identical final matrix.

(a) A₁A₂ costs 10·100·5=5,000 (producing a 10×5 matrix), then (A₁A₂)A₃ costs 10·5·50=2,500 — total 7,500. (b) A₂A₃ costs 100·5·50=25,000 (producing a 100×50 matrix), then A₁(A₂A₃) costs 10·100·50=50,000 — total 75,000.

7. Fill in the blank: the matrix chain DP tries every possible split point k, rather than always splitting at a fixed midpoint (as ordinary merge sort does), because the optimal split isn't known in ___.

Since there's no way to know which split point is cheapest ahead of time, every candidate k must be tried and compared — unlike merge sort's fixed halfway split.

8. Answer both parts about the matrix chain DP's structure:

(a) Why must the DP's outer loop iterate by increasing chain length, rather than by increasing i or j directly?

(b) Fill in the blank: dp[i][i] = ___, since a single matrix requires no multiplication at all.

(a) Every dp[i][j] recurrence reads dp[i][k] and dp[k+1][j] for strictly shorter chains, so those must already be solved — length-based ordering is what guarantees that. (b) A chain of one matrix needs zero multiplications, so its cost is trivially 0.

9. Which matrix M satisfies [F(n+1), F(n)]ᵀ = M · [F(n), F(n−1)]ᵀ for the Fibonacci recurrence?

Multiplying [[1,1],[1,0]] by [F(n), F(n−1)]ᵀ gives F(n+1)=F(n)+F(n−1) in the top entry and F(n)=F(n) in the bottom — exactly matching the recurrence and its carry-over term.

10. Answer both parts about matrix exponentiation:

(a) What is the runtime of computing F(n) via matrix exponentiation, and why is it better than the ordinary Θ(n) Fibonacci DP?

(b) Fill in the blank: any fixed-order linear recurrence can be advanced one step at a time by multiplying by a fixed ___ matrix, generalizing the 2×2 Fibonacci case used here.

(a) Repeated squaring computes Mⁿ using only O(log n) matrix multiplications, a dramatic improvement once n is astronomically large. (b) This companion-matrix construction extends the same trick to any fixed-order linear recurrence, not just Fibonacci.
