1. Which statement about nilpotent operators is correct?

Since every eigenvalue of a nonzero nilpotent operator is 0, and 0 being an eigenvalue means a nontrivial kernel, the operator can never be injective, hence never invertible.

2. Fill in the blank: the chain independence proof isolates each coefficient in turn, from c₀ down to c_(k−1), using decreasing powers of N at each step — the pattern is a proof by ___.

Each step reduces to a smaller version of the same argument, applied to one fewer term — the hallmark of an inductive proof.

3. What is J₂(−3), the 2×2 Jordan block for eigenvalue −3?

The superdiagonal entry is always exactly +1, regardless of the sign of the eigenvalue on the diagonal.

4. For an eigenvalue with algebraic multiplicity 4, if there is only 1 Jordan block for it, what is that block's size?

A single block's size must account for the entire algebraic multiplicity by itself — 4.

5. In the Principal Axis Theorem, what does D represent?

D is the diagonal matrix from A's spectral decomposition A=QDQᵀ, holding A's real eigenvalues.

6. For M=[[0,2,0],[0,0,3],[0,0,0]] and v=(0,0,1). Answer both parts:

(a) What is Mv?

(b) M²v = (6,0,0). Fill in the blank: M³v = (0,0,___).

(a) Mv reads off column 3 of M (scaled by v's entry), giving (0,3,0). (b) M³v = M(6,0,0) = 6·(column 1 of M) = (0,0,0) — the chain terminates.

7. For E=[[0,1],[−1,2]] (eigenvalue 1, algebraic multiplicity 2), taking v=(1,0). Answer both parts:

(a) What is Nv=(E−I)v?

(b) Ordering w₁=Nv=(−1,−1), w₂=v gives Jordan block J₂(___).

(a) (E−I)=[[−1,1],[−1,1]], and (E−I)(1,0) = (−1,−1). (b) The eigenvalue is 1, giving block J₂(1)=[[1,1],[0,1]].

8. For Q(x₁,x₂)=−5x₁²−4x₁x₂−5x₂² (matrix A=[[−5,−2],[−2,−5]], eigenvalues −7,−3). Answer both parts:

(a) What is Q in principal-axis coordinates?

(b) Fill in the blank: since both coefficients are negative, Q is ___ definite.

(a) The eigenvalues −7 and −3 become the coefficients directly. (b) All-negative eigenvalues is exactly the negative-definite condition.

9. Why is N=(T−λI)|Kλ nilpotent? Answer both parts:

(a) What is the key fact?

(b) Fill in the blank: T restricted to Kλ then equals N + ___I.

(a) Lesson 12's kernel-chain stabilization is exactly what guarantees this power of (T−λI) vanishes on all of Kλ. (b) Rearranging N=(T−λI)|Kλ gives T|Kλ=N+λI.

10. Why does T(w₁)=λw₁ exactly, with no extra term (the base of the chain)? Answer both parts:

(a) What is the key fact?

(b) Fill in the blank: this makes w₁ a genuine ___ of T, not merely a generalized one.

(a) N^k=0 is exactly what kills the N(w₁) term. (b) T(w₁)=λw₁ with no leftover is precisely the definition of an ordinary eigenvector.
