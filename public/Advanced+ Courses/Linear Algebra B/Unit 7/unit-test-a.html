1. If N is nilpotent, what is true of its eigenvalues?

Nᵏ=0 combined with an eigenvalue relation forces λᵏ=0, hence λ=0 — the only possible eigenvalue for any nilpotent operator.

2. Fill in the blank: applying N^(k−1) to the chain's dependency relation isolates the term c₀·N^(k−1)v, forcing c₀=0 since N^(k−1)v is assumed ___.

The chain is defined by N^(k−1)v being nonzero — that's exactly what's needed to conclude c₀=0 from c₀N^(k−1)v=0.

3. What is J₃(5), the 3×3 Jordan block for eigenvalue 5?

A Jordan block has the eigenvalue on the diagonal and exactly 1 (not 0, and not extra 1's) on the superdiagonal.

4. For an eigenvalue with algebraic multiplicity 5, if the Jordan blocks for it have sizes 3 and 2, how many total blocks are there for that eigenvalue?

3+2=5 matches the algebraic multiplicity, using exactly 2 blocks.

5. According to the Principal Axis Theorem, Q(x)=xᵀAx becomes, in coordinates y=Qᵀx:

The change of coordinates y=Qᵀx eliminates every cross term, leaving a pure sum of squares weighted by A's eigenvalues.

6. For M=[[0,1,0],[0,0,1],[0,0,0]] and v=(0,0,1). Answer both parts:

(a) What is Mv?

(b) M²v = (1,0,0). Fill in the blank: M³v = (0,0,___).

(a) Mv reads off column 3 of M, giving (0,1,0). (b) M³v = M(1,0,0) = column 1 of M = (0,0,0) — the chain terminates.

7. For D=[[2,1],[−4,6]] (eigenvalue 4, algebraic multiplicity 2), taking v=(1,0). Answer both parts:

(a) What is Nv=(D−4I)v?

(b) Ordering w₁=Nv=(−2,−4), w₂=v gives Jordan block J₂(___).

(a) (D−4I)=[[−2,1],[−4,2]], and (D−4I)(1,0) = (−2,−4). (b) The eigenvalue is 4, so the resulting block is J₂(4)=[[4,1],[0,4]].

8. For Q(x₁,x₂)=6x₁²+6x₁x₂+6x₂² (matrix A=[[6,3],[3,6]], eigenvalues 9,3). Answer both parts:

(a) What is Q in principal-axis coordinates?

(b) Fill in the blank: since both coefficients are positive, Q is ___ definite.

(a) The Principal Axis Theorem uses A's eigenvalues (9, 3) as the coefficients directly. (b) All-positive eigenvalues is exactly the positive-definite condition.

9. In the proof that Kλ is T-invariant. Answer both parts:

(a) What is shown about (T−λI)v for v∈Kλ?

(b) Fill in the blank: this uses the fact T = (T−λI) + ___I.

(a) Tracking the exponent shows (T−λI)^(k+1)v=0, so (T−λI)v stays in Kλ. (b) Splitting T as (T−λI)+λI is what lets both invariance facts combine into T-invariance.

10. In the chain-to-Jordan-block proof, for i≥2, T(wᵢ) = N(wᵢ)+λwᵢ = wᵢ₋₁+λwᵢ. Answer both parts:

(a) What identifies N(wᵢ) with wᵢ₋₁?

(b) Fill in the blank: this contributes a 1 in row i−1 and λ in row i of column i — exactly matching Jk(λ)'s ___ entries.

(a) The chain's own definition (wᵢ=N^(k−i)v) is exactly what pins down N(wᵢ)=wᵢ₋₁. (b) The superdiagonal is precisely where Jordan blocks place their 1's.
