1. Cauchy-Schwarz states that for any u,v in an inner product space:

The discriminant argument proves an upper bound, not equality and not a reversed (≥) inequality.

2. Fill in the blank: the inner product axiom ⟨v,v⟩≥0, with equality only when v=0, is called ___.

Positive-definiteness is exactly this axiom, and it's what makes ‖v‖=√⟨v,v⟩ a sensible notion of length.

3. If u₁,…,uₖ are nonzero and pairwise orthogonal, what can be concluded?

Orthogonality of nonzero vectors is enough to force independence directly — nothing here requires spanning, unit norm, or a specific count.

4. For v₁=(1,0,1), v₂=(0,1,1), Gram-Schmidt gives u₁=(1,0,1) and u₂ = (−1/2, 1, ___).

⟨v₂,u₁⟩=1, ⟨u₁,u₁⟩=2, so u₂ = (0,1,1) − (1/2)(1,0,1) = (−1/2, 1, 1/2).

5. Which statement about W⊥ is always true for a subspace W of finite-dimensional V?

The direct sum decomposition V=W⊕W⊥ is the lesson's main theorem — W⊥ need not match W's dimension, and it's never empty (0 is always in it).

6. For u=(3,4,0), v=(4,3,0) in ℝ³. Answer both parts:

(a) What is ⟨u,v⟩?

(b) ‖u‖=‖v‖=5. Cauchy-Schwarz predicts |⟨u,v⟩| ≤ ___.

(a) ⟨u,v⟩ = 3(4)+4(3)+0(0) = 12+12 = 24. (b) ‖u‖‖v‖ = 5×5 = 25, and indeed 24 ≤ 25.

7. For v₁=(2,0,0), v₂=(1,1,0). Answer both parts:

(a) What is u₁?

(b) u₂ = (0, ___, 0).

(a) u₁ = v₁ = (2,0,0) always. (b) ⟨v₂,u₁⟩=2, ⟨u₁,u₁⟩=4, so u₂ = (1,1,0) − (1/2)(2,0,0) = (0,1,0).

8. Suppose v ∈ W ∩ W⊥. Answer both parts about why v must be 0:

(a) What is the key step?

(b) Fill in the blank: ⟨v,v⟩=0 forcing v=0 uses the inner product axiom of ___-definiteness.

(a) Since v belongs to both W and W⊥, it must be orthogonal to itself. (b) Positive-definiteness is exactly what turns ⟨v,v⟩=0 into v=0.

9. For W=span{(1,0,0)} and v=(3,4,5). Answer both parts:

(a) What is projW(v)?

(b) v − projW(v) = (0, 4, ___).

(a) projW(v) = (⟨v,e₁⟩/⟨e₁,e₁⟩)e₁ = 3(1,0,0) = (3,0,0). (b) (3,4,5)−(3,0,0) = (0,4,5), orthogonal to (1,0,0) as required.

10. In the Triangle Inequality's proof, ‖u+v‖² = ‖u‖²+2⟨u,v⟩+‖v‖². Answer both parts:

(a) Which step bounds this by (‖u‖+‖v‖)²?

(b) Fill in the blank: after bounding, ‖u‖²+2‖u‖‖v‖+‖v‖² is recognized as the expansion of (‖u‖+‖v‖)^___.

(a) Cauchy-Schwarz bounds ⟨u,v⟩ ≤ ‖u‖‖v‖, directly bounding the cross term. (b) a²+2ab+b² is the standard expansion of (a+b)².
