1. Which equation defines the adjoint T* of an operator T?

The adjoint's defining property moves T from the first slot to the second slot of the inner product — not a claim about T* equaling ±T.

2. Fill in the blank: since v≠0, ‖v‖² is strictly ___, which is required to divide both sides of λ‖v‖² = conj(λ)‖v‖² and conclude λ equals its own conjugate (i.e. is real).

Positive-definiteness guarantees ‖v‖²>0 for any nonzero v, which is exactly what licenses dividing both sides by it.

3. According to the Spectral Theorem, every self-adjoint operator on a finite-dimensional inner product space has:

The theorem's exact guarantee is an orthonormal eigenbasis — stronger than merely a basis, and eigenvalues are always real (Lesson 16), not absent.

4. For A=[[6,3],[3,6]], what is the larger eigenvalue?

det(A−λI) = (6−λ)²−9 = λ²−12λ+27 = (λ−9)(λ−3), giving eigenvalues 9 and 3.

5. Why does the SVD construction use AᵀA instead of A directly?

Since A may not be square (and so may have no eigenvalues of its own), AᵀA — always square and symmetric — is what makes the Spectral Theorem usable at all.

6. For A=[[6,3],[3,6]], eigenvalue 9 has eigenvector (1,1). Answer both parts:

(a) What is the eigenvector for eigenvalue 3?

(b) ⟨(1,1),(1,−1)⟩ = ___.

(a) (A−3I)=[[3,3],[3,3]] gives eigenvector (1,−1). (b) 1(1)+1(−1) = 0, confirming orthogonality of distinct-eigenvalue eigenvectors.

7. For A=[[6,3],[3,6]] (eigenvalues 9, 3), answer both parts about A=QDQᵀ:

(a) What is D?

(b) Checking QᵀQ=I, the (1,1) entry of QᵀQ equals (1/√2)² + (1/√2)² = ___.

(a) D has the eigenvalues on its diagonal, largest first by convention. (b) (1/√2)²+(1/√2)² = 1/2+1/2 = 1, confirming Q's first column has unit norm.

8. For A=[[2,0],[0,1],[0,0]], AᵀA=[[4,0],[0,1]]. Answer both parts:

(a) What are the singular values of A?

(b) Fill in the blank: since both singular values are nonzero, rank(A) = ___.

(a) σᵢ=√λᵢ, so √4=2 and √1=1. (b) The number of nonzero singular values equals rank(A) — here, 2.

9. In the adjoint uniqueness proof, after showing ⟨v,S₁(w)−S₂(w)⟩=0 for all v. Answer both parts:

(a) What specific choice of v finishes the argument?

(b) Fill in the blank: ‖S₁(w)−S₂(w)‖²=0 forces S₁(w)−S₂(w) = ___, by positive-definiteness.

(a) Substituting v=S₁(w)−S₂(w) turns the general orthogonality statement into a direct statement about that vector's own squared norm. (b) A vector with norm 0 must itself be 0.

10. In the Spectral Theorem's induction, W=span{e₁}⊥. Answer both parts:

(a) What must be verified about W before the inductive hypothesis can be applied?

(b) Fill in the blank: W has dimension n − ___, one less than V — exactly what lets the induction proceed to a smaller case.

(a) T-invariance is exactly what's needed for T restricted to W to be a well-defined operator the inductive hypothesis can apply to. (b) dim(W) = n − 1, since e₁ alone is removed from the full n dimensions.
