1. Which condition defines T as self-adjoint?

Self-adjoint means T equals its own adjoint — moving T from one slot of the inner product to the other changes nothing.

2. Fill in the blank: the orthogonality proof for distinct eigenvalues uses the fact (proved earlier in the same lesson) that every eigenvalue of a self-adjoint operator is ___, so conj(λ₂) = λ₂ can be substituted directly.

Because λ₂ is already known to be real, its conjugate equals itself — the step that turns conj(λ₂)⟨v₁,v₂⟩ into λ₂⟨v₁,v₂⟩.

3. In matrix form, the Spectral Theorem says every real symmetric matrix A factors as:

The exact factorization is A = QDQᵀ — the transpose (equivalently Q⁻¹, since Q is orthogonal) on the right, not just Q or a sum.

4. For A=[[6,3],[3,6]], what is the smaller eigenvalue?

Wait — check carefully: det(A−λI) = (6−λ)²−9 = λ²−12λ+27 = (λ−9)(λ−3), giving eigenvalues 9 and 3, so the smaller eigenvalue is 3, not 2.

5. In the SVD, what does Σ contain?

Σ is the diagonal-structured matrix of nonnegative singular values, sorted largest to smallest — the eigenvectors live in U and V instead.

6. For A=[[6,3],[3,6]], eigenvalue 3 has eigenvector (1,−1). Answer both parts:

(a) What is the eigenvector for eigenvalue 9?

(b) ⟨(1,−1),(1,1)⟩ = ___.

(a) (A−9I)=[[−3,3],[3,−3]] gives eigenvector (1,1). (b) 1(1)+(−1)(1) = 0, confirming orthogonality.

7. For A=[[6,3],[3,6]], answer both parts about the induction behind its spectral decomposition:

(a) After peeling off e₁ (unit eigenvector for λ=9), what is dim(W) for W=span{e₁}⊥?

(b) Fill in the blank: the base case of the induction (dimension ___) is what finishes constructing the eigenbasis inside W.

(a) dim(V)=2, so W has dimension 2−1=1. (b) A 1-dimensional space is exactly the induction's base case.

8. For A=[[3,0],[0,1],[0,0]], AᵀA=[[9,0],[0,1]]. Answer both parts:

(a) What are the singular values of A?

(b) Fill in the blank: since both singular values are nonzero, rank(A) = ___.

(a) σᵢ=√λᵢ, so √9=3 and √1=1. (b) Two nonzero singular values means rank(A)=2.

9. In the proof that T* exists, [S]=[T]ᵀ is proposed as the adjoint. Answer both parts:

(a) What identity confirms ⟨T(v),w⟩ = ⟨v,S(w)⟩?

(b) Fill in the blank: this identification of ⟨v,w⟩ with a dot product is only valid in an ___ basis.

(a) The dot-product transpose identity is exactly what the existence proof relies on. (b) Coordinates relative to an orthonormal basis are exactly what make ⟨v,w⟩ compute as a plain dot product.

10. In the Spectral Theorem's proof, T restricted to W is used in the inductive step. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is T|W still self-adjoint?

(b) Fill in the blank: T|W is only a well-defined operator on W in the first place because W was shown to be T-___.

(a) The universal quantifier "for all v,w in V" already covers the special case where both happen to lie in W. (b) T-invariance guarantees T never sends a W-vector outside W, making the restriction well-defined.
