1. According to the Exchange Lemma, if {v₁,…,v₅} spans V and {w₁,…,w₆} ⊆ V, what can you conclude?

The Exchange Lemma says an independent set can never outnumber a spanning set (m ≤ n). Since 6 > 5, {w₁,…,w₆} is forced to be dependent — it cannot be independent.

2. Fill in the blank: dim(V) is defined as the common size of every ___ of V, a definition justified by the Exchange Lemma's corollary.

Dimension only makes sense as a single number because every basis has the same size — the fact the Exchange Lemma's corollary proves.

3. Is U ∩ W always a subspace of V, for any two subspaces U, W of V?

0 is in both U and W, and vectors staying in both under addition and scaling follows immediately from U and W each being individually closed — no special relationship between U and W is required.

4. If dim(U) = 5, dim(W) = 3, and dim(U+W) = 6, what is dim(U∩W)?

Rearranging dim(U+W) = dim(U)+dim(W)−dim(U∩W) gives dim(U∩W) = 5+3−6 = 2.

5. Why is φB: V → Fⁿ called an isomorphism rather than just a linear map?

Isomorphism specifically means a linear bijection — φB qualifies because it has the two-sided inverse ψ, built directly from Lesson 3's existence-and-uniqueness theorem.

6. In ℝ³, let {e₁,e₂,e₃,e₁+e₂+e₃} span V (n=4) and let {e₁−e₂, e₂−e₃} be independent (m=2). Answer both parts:

(a) What does the Exchange Lemma guarantee here?

(b) Fill in the blank: writing w₁ = e₁−e₂ = 1·e₁ + (−1)·e₂ + 0·e₃ + 0·(e₁+e₂+e₃), the nonzero coefficient on e₁ means e₁ can validly be ___ for w₁ in the spanning set during the exchange.

(a) The lemma requires m ≤ n whenever a size-m independent set sits alongside a size-n spanning set; 2 ≤ 4 holds with room to spare. (b) A nonzero coefficient on e₁ is exactly what licenses solving for e₁ and swapping it out.

7. In ℝ⁴, let U = span{(1,0,0,0),(0,1,0,0)} and W = span{(0,0,1,0),(0,0,0,1)}. Answer both parts:

(a) What is dim(U∩W)?

(b) Fill in the blank: dim(U+W) = dim(U) + dim(W) − dim(U∩W) = 2 + 2 − 0 = ___.

(a) Any vector in both U and W must have its first two AND last two coordinates all zero, forcing the zero vector — dim(U∩W) = 0. (b) 2+2−0 = 4, matching U+W = ℝ⁴.

8. Let B = {(2,1), (1,1)}, a basis of ℝ². Answer both parts about finding [v]B for v = (7,1) = a(2,1) + b(1,1):

(a) What is a?

(b) Fill in the blank: [v]B = (6, ___).

(a)–(b) Solving 2a+b=7 and a+b=1 by subtraction gives a=6, then b=1−6=−5. Check: 6(2,1)+(−5)(1,1) = (12,6)+(−5,−5) = (7,1). ✓

9. Let B = {(1,2), (3,4)} and B′ = the standard basis of ℝ². Answer both parts:

(a) What is column 1 of PB←B′ (the coordinates of (1,0) relative to B)?

(b) For v = (4,6), [v]B′ = (4,6), and [v]B = PB←B′(4,6) = (1, ___).

(a) Solving (1,0)=a(1,2)+b(3,4) gives a+3b=1, 2a+4b=0 → a=−2b, so −2b+3b=1 → b=1, a=−2. (b) Solving (4,6)=a(1,2)+b(3,4) directly gives a=1, b=1, so [v]B=(1,1).

10. In proving dimension is well-defined for bases B₁ (size n) and B₂ (size m), applying the Exchange Lemma with B₁ spanning and B₂ independent gives m ≤ n. Answer both parts:

(a) What additional application is needed to conclude n = m?

(b) Fill in the blank: m ≤ n and n ≤ m together, by the ___ property of ≤, force m = n.

(a) Since both B₁ and B₂ are simultaneously spanning and independent (both are bases), the lemma applies in both directions. (b) Two inequalities pointing opposite directions force equality — antisymmetry of ≤.
