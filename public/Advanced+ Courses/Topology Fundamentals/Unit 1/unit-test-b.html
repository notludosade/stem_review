1. Why must a topology τ on X contain both ∅ and X, according to axiom (T1)?

(T1) is a base-case requirement, not a restriction — every topology, however large or small, must include these two sets.

2. On a two-point set X = {a, b}, how many open sets does the indiscrete topology have, compared to the discrete topology?

Indiscrete: {∅, X}, just 2 sets. Discrete: 𝒫(X) = {∅, {a}, {b}, X}, all 4 subsets — the maximum possible.

3. Fill in the blank: basis axiom (B1) requires that ___ of X lies in at least one basis element of ℬ.

Every point of X must be covered by some basis element — without this, the union of all basis elements couldn't reconstruct X itself.

4. Which of the following collections of open intervals also generates τstd on ℝ, the same topology as all open intervals (a, b)?

Density of ℚ in ℝ lets rational-endpoint intervals reach every point any irrational-endpoint interval could, so this smaller basis generates the identical topology.

5. Fill in the blank: whether a set F counts as closed depends entirely on whether F's ___ belongs to τ.

The complement's openness is the entire test — closed has no independent, direct definition.

6. Consider proving "arbitrary intersection of closed sets is closed" for a general topological space. Answer both parts:

(a) Which open-set axiom does this proof rely on?

(b) Fill in the blank: De Morgan converts the complement of an arbitrary intersection of closed sets into an arbitrary ___ of open sets, open by (T2).

(a) Since intersections of closed sets correspond to unions of their complements, the arbitrary-union axiom (T2) is what's actually doing the work. (b) X \ ⋂F_α = ⋃(X \ F_α), an arbitrary union of open sets.

7. Let A = [−1, 4) in (ℝ, τstd). Answer both parts:

(a) What is cl(A)?

(b) Fill in the blank: the point ___ has to be added to A to reach its closure, since it is a limit point of A not already contained in A.

(a) A already contains −1, but not 4, so the smallest closed set containing A must add 4, giving [−1, 4]. (b) The missing endpoint is 4.

8. Let A = (−2, 6] in (ℝ, τstd). Answer both parts:

(a) What is int(A)?

(b) Fill in the blank: formally, int(A) is defined as the ___ of every open set contained in A.

(a) 6 has no ε-ball staying inside A (it spills above 6), so it's excluded, giving (−2, 6). (b) Interior is the union of all open subsets of A.

9. Let A = (−2, 6] in (ℝ, τstd), continuing from Question 8. Answer both parts:

(a) Using cl(A) = [−2, 6] and int(A) = (−2, 6), what is ∂A?

(b) Fill in the blank: the boundary formula ∂A = cl(A) \ int(A) is computed by ___ the interior from the closure.

(a) [−2,6] minus (−2,6) leaves exactly the two endpoints. (b) Subtracting (set difference) the interior from the closure is the defining formula.

10. Consider verifying that ℝ itself belongs to τstd. Answer both parts:

(a) Which axiom directly requires ℝ ∈ τstd?

(b) Fill in the blank: checking the ε-ball definition directly, ℝ satisfies it ___, since every ε-ball around every point of ℝ is automatically already contained in ℝ.

(a) Axiom (T1) directly requires X (here, ℝ) to belong to the topology. (b) The check is trivial: no ε-ball around any real number can ever escape ℝ.
