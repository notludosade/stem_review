1. Why does the definition of continuity avoid requiring images of open sets to be open?

The x² example is the standard counterexample showing images of opens can fail to be open, which is exactly why the definition uses preimages instead.

2. In the proof that topological continuity implies ε-δ continuity, what specific open set V is chosen around f(a)?

The ε-ball around f(a) is exactly the open set whose preimage, guaranteed open by topological continuity, supplies the needed δ.

3. Fill in the blank: preimages ___ cleanly with unions and intersections for any function whatsoever, which is exactly why the topology axioms transfer well through them.

f⁻¹(⋃V_α) = ⋃f⁻¹(V_α) and f⁻¹(V₁ ∩ V₂) = f⁻¹(V₁) ∩ f⁻¹(V₂) hold for any f — that commuting behavior is the entire reason preimages work so well.

4. What does it mean for f : X → Y to be a homeomorphism?

All three conditions — bijective, continuous, continuous inverse — are required for a genuine homeomorphism.

5. Fill in the blank: the worked example proves (−1, 1) ≅ ℝ using the explicit homeomorphism f(x) = ___.

f(x) = x/(1−x²) is the explicit map shown to be a continuous bijection with continuous inverse between (−1,1) and ℝ.

6. Consider Y = [2, 8) with the subspace topology from (ℝ, τstd). Answer both parts:

(a) Is [2, 4) open in Y?

(b) Fill in the blank: this shows openness is always ___ to whichever space is under discussion, not an absolute property of a set.

(a) (0,4) ∩ [2,8) = [2,4), satisfying the subspace definition despite failing ℝ's own ε-ball test at 2. (b) Openness is always relative to the ambient space in question.

7. Consider proving τY satisfies (T3), finite intersections stay open. Answer both parts:

(a) Which identity does the proof rely on?

(b) Fill in the blank: the proof concludes U₁ ∩ U₂ ∈ τ using axiom (___) applied to the ambient space X.

(a) Distributing the intersection over the two Y-intersections is the identity used. (b) (T3), finite intersections of open sets stay open, applied first in X.

8. Suppose g : X → ℝ is continuous and only ever outputs integers. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the codomain-restriction theorem let you conclude about g : X → ℤ (subspace topology from ℝ)?

(b) Fill in the blank: the proof shows f⁻¹(U ∩ Z) = f⁻¹(U), since continuity only ever depends on the ___ of open sets, unaffected by the codomain's exact size.

(a) Shrinking the codomain to a superset of the image (here, ℤ) preserves continuity. (b) Preimages of open sets are the entire basis of the continuity definition.

9. Suppose two spaces X and Y are homeomorphic. Which of these is guaranteed to transfer from X to Y?

Only topological properties — those defined purely through open sets — are guaranteed to transfer across a homeomorphism; boundedness and distance are not among them.

10. Let X = {1, 2, 3} with the discrete topology, and let f : X → ℝ send 1 ↦ 5, 2 ↦ 5, 3 ↦ 100. Answer both parts:

(a) Is f continuous?

(b) Fill in the blank: f⁻¹({100}) = {___}, a single-point set — automatically open in X, since X is discrete.

(a) Discreteness of the domain makes any function continuous, regardless of injectivity. (b) f⁻¹({100}) = {3}, and every subset of a discrete space is open.
