1. X is connected exactly when:

Connectedness is the absence of any valid separation — a pair of nonempty, disjoint, open sets covering X.

2. Why does a nontrivial clopen subset C of X (C ≠ ∅, X) immediately produce a separation?

C open plus X\C open (from C being closed) gives exactly the two nonempty disjoint open sets a separation requires.

3. Fill in the blank: in the proof that continuous images of connected spaces stay connected, a hypothetical separation of Y is pulled back to the domain X via ___ of U and V.

Preimages of U and V under f are what get shown to form a contradictory separation of X.

4. What is required of the domain of a path γ in the definition of path-connectedness?

A path is always parametrized by [0,1], continuous into whatever space X is being studied.

5. Fill in the blank: no path exists from L to C in the topologist's sine curve, so S is connected but not ___.

Path-connected is exactly the stronger property S fails to have, despite being connected.

6. Consider why path-connected implies connected uses a proof by contradiction. Answer both parts:

(a) What is assumed at the start of the proof?

(b) Fill in the blank: γ⁻¹(U) and γ⁻¹(V) are shown nonempty because γ(0) = x ∈ U and γ(___) = y ∈ V.

(a) The proof assumes a separation exists and derives a contradiction from it. (b) γ(0) = x and γ(1) = y are the two path endpoints landing in U and V respectively.

7. Let h(x) = x³ − 6x + 2 on [2, 3]. h(2) = −2, h(3) = 11. Answer both parts:

(a) Does the topological IVT guarantee some c ∈ (2,3) with h(c) = 5?

(b) Fill in the blank: h([2,3]) is guaranteed to be an interval because it's the continuous image of the ___ set [2,3].

(a) 5 lies strictly between −2 and 11, so IVT guarantees a c with h(c) = 5. (b) [2,3] being connected is what makes h([2,3]) connected, hence an interval.

8. Consider the relationship between this course's open-set separation and Real Analysis A's closure-based separation. Answer both parts:

(a) What lets the two definitions be shown equivalent for subsets of ℝ?

(b) Fill in the blank: this is why this course's Lesson 7 could reuse the connected-subsets-are-intervals proof from Real Analysis A ___, unchanged.

(a) Since each piece of a separation is the complement of the other, both are automatically closed too, making the two separation notions coincide. (b) Real Analysis A, Unit 3, Lesson 18 is the exact source theorem reused.

9. Consider the "connected" half of the topologist's sine curve proof. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is C = {(x, sin(1/x)) : 0 < x ≤ 1} connected?

(b) Fill in the blank: since L ⊆ cl(C), and a set sitting between a connected set and its ___ is itself connected, S = C ∪ L is connected.

(a) C is a continuous image of the connected interval (0,1], hence connected. (b) Closure — any set between C and cl(C) inherits connectedness.

10. Let X be some connected topological space with no metric structure at all, and f : X → ℝ continuous with f(p) = 10 and f(q) = −4 for two points p, q ∈ X. Answer both parts:

(a) Does the generalized IVT corollary still apply, even though X has no distance function?

(b) Fill in the blank: since ___ lies strictly between f(p) = 10 and f(q) = −4, the corollary guarantees some point of X maps to it.

(a) The corollary needs only connectedness of the domain and continuity of f — no metric on X is required. (b) 0 lies strictly between −4 and 10, so the corollary applies to it.
