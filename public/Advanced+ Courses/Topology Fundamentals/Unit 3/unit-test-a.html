1. A separation of X is:

All four conditions — nonempty, disjoint, open, union equals X — are required for a genuine separation.

2. Which statement about clopen sets and connectedness is correct?

A nontrivial clopen set C immediately supplies a separation, C and its complement — so ruling those out is exactly equivalent to connectedness.

3. Fill in the blank: the theorem "continuous image of connected is connected" requires f to be ___, so that nonempty open subsets of Y have nonempty preimages in X.

Surjectivity is exactly what guarantees f⁻¹(U) and f⁻¹(V) are both nonempty in the proof.

4. What does it mean for a path γ to go from x to y in a space X?

A path is a continuous function out of [0,1] into X, with its endpoints landing on the two specified points.

5. Fill in the blank: the topologist's sine curve S = C ∪ L is ___ but not path-connected — showing the two properties are not equivalent in general.

Connected is the property S retains despite having no path from the wall segment L into the oscillating curve C.

6. Consider the proof that path-connected implies connected. Answer both parts:

(a) Suppose X = U ∪ V is a separation, and γ is a path from x ∈ U to y ∈ V. What do γ⁻¹(U) and γ⁻¹(V) form?

(b) Fill in the blank: this is a contradiction because [0,1] is a ___ interval, so no separation of it can exist.

(a) γ⁻¹(U), γ⁻¹(V) are open, disjoint, nonempty, and cover [0,1], forming a genuine separation. (b) [0,1] being connected is exactly what rules that separation out, completing the proof by contradiction.

7. Let g(x) = x³ − 3x + 1 on [1, 2]. g(1) = −1, g(2) = 3. Answer both parts:

(a) Does the topological IVT guarantee a root of g in (1, 2)?

(b) Fill in the blank: g([1,2]) must itself be an ___ of ℝ, since it's the continuous image of a connected set, guaranteeing every value between g(1) and g(2) — including 0 — is attained.

(a) Since 0 is strictly between −1 and 3, IVT guarantees a root in (1,2). (b) g([1,2]) is connected (continuous image of connected [1,2]), hence an interval, so it contains 0.

8. Consider how this course's separation definition (open sets U, V) compares to Real Analysis A's (closures). Answer both parts:

(a) Why do the two definitions pick out exactly the same connected sets in ℝ?

(b) Fill in the blank: as a result, this course reuses Real Analysis A's proof unchanged that connected subsets of ℝ (with ≥2 points) are exactly the ___s.

(a) Since disjoint open pieces of a subspace are complements of each other, they're automatically closed too, making the two separation definitions coincide. (b) Interval remains the exact characterization.

9. In the proof that S = C ∪ L is not path-connected, what ultimately blocks any hypothetical path from crossing from C into L? Answer both parts:

(a) What behavior of sin(1/x) is the key obstruction?

(b) Fill in the blank: since the path's second coordinate can't settle on a limit as t → t*⁺, this directly contradicts the assumed ___ of the path at t*.

(a) The infinite oscillation near x = 0 is exactly what prevents any path from having a limit there. (b) Continuity of the path at t* is exactly what the failed limit contradicts.

10. Let X be the topologist's sine curve S (connected, per Lesson 8), and let f : S → ℝ be continuous with f taking the value −3 at one point and 7 at another. Answer both parts:

(a) Does the generalized IVT corollary guarantee some point of S maps to 2?

(b) Fill in the blank: the generalized corollary only requires the domain to be ___ — path-connectedness is never actually needed.

(a) The corollary only needs connectedness of the domain, which S has, regardless of path-connectedness. (b) Connected is the only hypothesis the corollary actually uses.
