1. Why does the product topology on ℝ × ℝ agree with the standard topology on ℝ² generated by open disks?

Mutual containment between the two bases' elements is what guarantees they generate the same topology.

2. What is the smallest-topology characterization of the product topology?

Since every basis rectangle U × V = π_X⁻¹(U) ∩ π_Y⁻¹(V) is forced to be open once both projections are continuous, the product topology is the minimal topology achieving that.

3. Fill in the blank: the quotient map q : X → Y is automatically ___, since the quotient topology's openness test is defined to match the continuity condition exactly.

Continuity of q holds by construction — no separate check is ever needed.

4. In the proof that [0,1]/(0~1) ≅ S¹, what role does the "quotient-map universal property" play?

Since f(0) = f(1), f factors through the quotient as a well-defined continuous map f̄ on Y — that's exactly the universal property being used.

5. Fill in the blank: the cylinder is built by identifying (0,y) ~ (1,y) — matching direction, ___ — leaving the top and bottom edges free.

No flip is exactly what distinguishes the cylinder's gluing from the Möbius strip's.

6. Consider proving ℬ = {U × V} satisfies basis axiom (B1). Answer both parts:

(a) What must be shown for (B1) to hold?

(b) Fill in the blank: since X ∈ τ_X and Y ∈ τ_Y by axiom (___) in each factor, X × Y itself is a basis rectangle containing every point.

(a) Covering every point of X × Y with some basis rectangle is exactly what (B1) requires. (b) (T1) guarantees X and Y are each open in their own topologies, making X × Y itself a valid basis element.

7. Consider verifying the quotient topology satisfies axiom (T3). Answer both parts:

(a) What identity does the proof rely on?

(b) Fill in the blank: since q⁻¹(V₁) and q⁻¹(V₂) are each open in X, their ___ intersection is too (axiom T3 for X), showing V₁ ∩ V₂ belongs to the quotient topology.

(a) Preimages commute with intersections for any function. (b) Finite intersections of open sets stay open by (T3), applied within X.

8. Consider the torus construction's two identification rules, (0,y)~(1,y) and (x,0)~(x,1). Answer both parts:

(a) Do either of these identifications include a flip?

(b) Fill in the blank: the resulting surface is the standard topological model of the surface of a ___.

(a) Neither identification in the torus construction involves a flip. (b) The torus is exactly the surface-of-a-donut shape this construction produces.

9. Consider why Lesson 5's [0,2π) → S¹ map fails to be a homeomorphism, in light of the compact-to-Hausdorff theorem. Answer both parts:

(a) Which hypothesis of the theorem does [0, 2π) fail to satisfy?

(b) Fill in the blank: replacing [0, 2π) with the compact quotient [0,1]/(0~1) fixes exactly ___ hypothesis, letting the theorem apply.

(a) Compactness of the domain is exactly what [0,2π) lacks. (b) A single hypothesis — compactness — is all that changes between the failed and successful examples.

10. Consider tracing a line down the center of each surface. Answer both parts:

(a) On a cylinder, does a center-line loop return to its starting point on the same face it began on?

(b) Fill in the blank: on a Möbius strip, the same loop returns to its start on the ___ face from where it began, because of the single flip in the construction.

(a) The cylinder's two sides are genuinely distinguishable, so a center loop stays on one of them. (b) The flip is exactly what sends the Möbius strip's center loop to the opposite face upon return.
