1. What does it mean for two loops γ₀, γ₁ based at x₀ to be homotopic?

Homotopy is exactly this continuous-deformation relation, keeping the basepoint fixed throughout.

2. Which of these spaces is simply connected?

The disk's convexity lets every loop contract to a point, making it simply connected — unlike S¹ or T², both of which have nontrivial fundamental groups.

3. Fill in the blank: a loop's homotopy class on S¹ is completely determined by its ___ — how many times, and in which direction, it winds around.

Winding number is the integer invariant classifying loops on S¹ up to homotopy.

4. In the h(x) = −(x−1)² + 8 on K = [−1,3] synthesis example, what two facts together show h(K) contains its own supremum?

Compactness-preservation, Heine-Borel, and "closed sets contain their limit points" together replace the maximizing-sequence argument entirely.

5. Fill in the blank: π₁(T²) is isomorphic to ___, since a loop can wind independently around the torus's tube and through its hole.

ℤ×ℤ reflects the two independent winding directions available on a torus.

6. Consider the straight-line homotopy H(s,t) = (1−t)γ(s) + t·x₀ on D². Answer both parts:

(a) What is H(s,1)?

(b) Fill in the blank: since H(s,0) = γ(s) and H(s,1) = x₀, H is a homotopy from γ to the ___ loop.

(a) At t=1, the formula reduces to x₀. (b) This exhibits γ as homotopic to the constant loop at x₀.

7. Consider verifying τ = {∅, {red}, {red, green}, X} on X = {red, green, blue} is a topology. Answer both parts:

(a) What must be checked?

(b) Fill in the blank: no ___ is ever needed to perform this check — the axioms only reference the sets in τ directly.

(a) Direct verification of (T1)–(T3) against the listed sets is all that's needed. (b) No metric or distance is required anywhere in the check.

8. Let p(x) = x³ − x + 3 on [−2, −1]. p(−2) = −8+2+3 = −3, p(−1) = −1+1+3 = 3. Answer both parts:

(a) Does the topological IVT guarantee a root of p in (−2,−1)?

(b) Fill in the blank: p([−2,−1]) is connected because it's the continuous image of the ___ interval [−2,−1].

(a) 0 lies strictly between −3 and 3, so IVT applies. (b) [−2,−1] being connected is what makes its continuous image connected too.

9. Consider how continuity connects Units 2 and 4 of this course. Answer both parts:

(a) Which theorem from Unit 2 gets reused directly in Unit 4's Extreme Value Theorem proof?

(b) Fill in the blank: continuity preserving compactness, proved in ___, is what powers the Extreme Value Theorem's proof.

(a) Preimage continuity from Lesson 4 underlies the compactness-preservation theorem used in EVT. (b) Unit 4's compactness-preservation theorem is what powers EVT directly.

10. Consider the relationship between this course and Differential Geometry, per the Looking Ahead box. Answer both parts:

(a) How does Differential Geometry's question relate to this course's opening question?

(b) Fill in the blank: every space built in this course's Unit 5 — the circle, torus, and sphere — is itself an example of a ___, the central object Differential Geometry studies next.

(a) This course removed distance from Real Analysis A's picture; Differential Geometry adds a (possibly curved) distance-like structure back on top of topology. (b) Manifold is the term for these smooth-structured topological spaces.
