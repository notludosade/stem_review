1. A loop in X based at x₀ is:

A loop is exactly a path that starts and ends at the same basepoint.

2. What does it mean for X to be simply connected?

Simply connected means path-connected with a trivial fundamental group — every loop contracts to a point.

3. Fill in the blank: π₁(S¹) is isomorphic to ___, with a loop's homotopy class determined entirely by its winding number.

ℤ (the integers) classifies loops on S¹ by winding number.

4. In the f(x) = 3x − 5 synthesis example, what does checking f⁻¹((a,b)) is open accomplish?

The preimage check and the ε-δ check certify the identical fact, by Lesson 4's equivalence theorem.

5. Fill in the blank: the torus and the sphere are both compact and connected, so only the ___ (trivial for the sphere, ℤ×ℤ for the torus) can distinguish them.

The fundamental group is the invariant capable of telling these two surfaces apart.

6. Consider why every loop in the disk D² is homotopic to a constant loop. Answer both parts:

(a) What property of D² makes the straight-line homotopy H(s,t) = (1−t)γ(s) + t·x₀ stay inside D² for every t?

(b) Fill in the blank: as a result, π₁(D², x₀) is ___ — every loop contracts.

(a) Convexity guarantees the straight-line path between any two points of D² stays inside D². (b) A trivial fundamental group is the direct consequence.

7. Consider the topology τ = {∅, {red}, {red, green}, X} on X = {red, green, blue}. Answer both parts:

(a) Why couldn't Real Analysis A's original open-set definition describe this example?

(b) Fill in the blank: this course's Lesson 1 topology ___ apply directly, with no distance required at all.

(a) No distance function exists on X, ruling out an ε-ball definition. (b) The abstract topology axioms need no such distance, applying to this example without modification.

8. Let g(x) = x³ − 4x² + 2 on [0, 1]. g(0) = 2, g(1) = 1 − 4 + 2 = −1. Answer both parts:

(a) Does the topological IVT guarantee a root of g in (0,1)?

(b) Fill in the blank: g([0,1]) must be an ___ of ℝ, since it's a continuous image of the connected set [0,1].

(a) 0 lies strictly between 2 and −1, so IVT guarantees a root. (b) The continuous image of a connected set is connected, hence an interval in ℝ.

9. Consider the overall structure of this course, as traced in Lesson 21. Answer both parts:

(a) What does Unit 1 abstract into axioms?

(b) Fill in the blank: the circle, torus, and Möbius strip were built as quotients in ___, later reused for the fundamental group discussion.

(a) Unit 1's topology axioms are abstracted directly from ε-ball open sets' closure properties. (b) Unit 5 built these quotient spaces, later revisited in Unit 7.

10. Consider what Differential Geometry studies, per the Looking Ahead box. Answer both parts:

(a) What are its central objects?

(b) Fill in the blank: the added smooth structure is what makes ___ — derivatives, curvature, geodesics — possible on these spaces.

(a) Smooth manifolds are Differential Geometry's central objects. (b) Calculus — derivatives, curvature, geodesics — is exactly what the smooth structure enables.
