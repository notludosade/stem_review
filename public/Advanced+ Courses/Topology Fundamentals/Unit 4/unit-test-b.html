1. What must be true of a subcover for it to demonstrate compactness of X?

A finite subcover — finitely many sets from the original cover that still cover X — is exactly what compactness requires to exist.

2. In the proof that closed subsets of compact spaces are compact, why is the complement X \ F discarded from the final subcover?

X \ F is disjoint from F by construction, so it plays no role once attention narrows back to covering F specifically.

3. Fill in the blank: for subsets of ℝ, this lesson's open-cover Heine-Borel theorem reaches the identical closed-and-bounded characterization that ___ proved via sequential compactness.

Real Analysis A is the course whose sequential-compactness proof reached the same conclusion by a different route.

4. Why does the bisection proof of [a,b]'s compactness use proof by contradiction?

The proof assumes a cover with no finite subcover exists and derives a contradiction once the shrinking intervals fall inside a single open set containing their limit point.

5. Fill in the blank: the proof that continuous images of compact spaces are compact pulls an open cover of Y back to X using ___ of the covering sets.

Preimages of the open cover of Y form an open cover of X, to which X's compactness is then applied.

6. Consider the set (0, 1] and the open cover {(1/n, 2) : n = 1, 2, 3, …}. Answer both parts:

(a) Does this cover have a finite subcover of (0, 1]?

(b) Fill in the blank: this confirms (0,1] is not compact, consistent with Heine-Borel since (0,1] is bounded but not ___.

(a) A largest finite index N always leaves a gap (0, 1/N] uncovered. (b) (0,1] fails Heine-Borel's closedness requirement — it's missing its limit point 0.

7. In the proof that compact ⇒ bounded for K ⊆ ℝⁿ, consider the cover {B(0, m) : m = 1, 2, 3, …}. Answer both parts:

(a) What does compactness of K extract from this cover?

(b) Fill in the blank: taking M as the ___ of m₁, …, m_k shows K ⊆ B(0, M), establishing boundedness.

(a) A finite subcover is exactly what compactness guarantees exists. (b) The maximum of the finitely many radii bounds K within a single ball.

8. Let K = [−2, 4] ⊆ ℝ (compact) and g(x) = x² − 2x. Answer both parts:

(a) Does the topological EVT guarantee g attains a minimum on K?

(b) Fill in the blank: the minimum case of the proof is obtained by applying the maximum argument to ___ g instead of g.

(a) EVT guarantees both extremes are attained under continuity and compactness. (b) Applying the max-attainment argument to −g recovers the minimum for g.

9. Consider what fact replaces the maximizing-sequence machinery in this course's EVT proof. Answer both parts:

(a) Which theorem from earlier in this course is directly reused?

(b) Fill in the blank: the proof also reuses Real Analysis A's ___ of supremum to show every ε-ball around s meets f(X).

(a) Compactness-preservation plus Heine-Borel together replace the sequence-extraction machinery. (b) The epsilon characterization of supremum from Real Analysis A is reused directly.

10. Consider the box [0, 10] × [0, 10] × [0, 10] ⊆ ℝ³. Answer both parts:

(a) Is this box compact?

(b) Fill in the blank: this box is a product of ___ compact intervals [0,10], and a finite product of compact spaces is compact.

(a) Closed and bounded in ℝ³ means compact by Heine-Borel. (b) Three copies of [0,10] multiplied together, each individually compact.
