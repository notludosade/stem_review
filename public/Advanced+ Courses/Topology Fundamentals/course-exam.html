Topology: Fundamentals — Course Exam

1. Which of the following must be true of any topology τ on a set X?

The three topology axioms — (T1) ∅, X ∈ τ, (T2) arbitrary unions, (T3) finite intersections — are the only requirements.

2. A collection ℬ of subsets of X is a valid basis if:

Basis axioms (B1) and (B2) are exactly these two conditions, guaranteeing the generated collection of unions forms a genuine topology.

3. Fill in the blank: the closure of A = (−2, 6) in (ℝ, τstd) is ___ (using bracket notation).

The closure adds both missing endpoints, since every closed set containing (−2,6) must also contain −2 and 6.

4. f : X → Y is continuous exactly when:

Continuity is defined via preimages of open sets, since images of open sets need not stay open.

5. What is required, beyond f being a continuous bijection, for f to be a homeomorphism?

A continuous bijection with a discontinuous inverse (like [0,2π)→S¹) is not a homeomorphism — continuity of f⁻¹ is a genuinely separate requirement.

6. Fill in the blank: the subspace topology on Y ⊆ X consists of every set of the form U ∩ Y — the ___ of Y with an open set of X.

Intersection with an ambient open set is exactly the subspace topology's defining test.

7. A separation of X is a pair of sets U, V such that:

All four conditions together define a separation — connectedness is the absence of any such pair.

8. What does the topologist's sine curve demonstrate?

The topologist's sine curve is connected but has no path from its wall segment into its oscillating curve — the standard counterexample to the converse implication.

9. Fill in the blank: the topological proof of IVT relies on the fact that a continuous image of a ___ set is itself connected.

Connectedness is preserved by continuous images, which is the central fact IVT's topological proof leans on.

10. X is compact when:

Compactness requires a finite subcover to exist within every possible open cover, however large.

11. According to Heine-Borel, compact subsets of ℝⁿ are exactly those that are:

Closed and bounded together are exactly equivalent to compactness in ℝⁿ.

12. Fill in the blank: the topological Extreme Value Theorem requires f to be continuous on a ___ set to guarantee its max and min are attained.

Compactness is the essential hypothesis — bounded alone is not enough, as (0,1) shows.

13. The basis for the product topology on X × Y consists of sets of the form:

Open rectangles U × V are the basis for the product topology.

14. In the quotient topology on Y = X/~, a set V ⊆ Y is open exactly when:

The quotient topology's openness test is defined entirely via the preimage under the quotient map q.

15. Fill in the blank: the Möbius strip differs from the cylinder construction by including a ___ in the left-right edge identification.

A single reversed (flipped) identification is the entire difference between the two constructions.

16. What does the Hausdorff property guarantee about sequence limits?

Hausdorff is exactly the property that rules out a sequence converging to two distinct points at once.

17. Which implication in the separation axiom hierarchy is correct?

The hierarchy runs strictly one direction from normal down to T1, with no implication reversing in general.

18. Fill in the blank: since every metric automatically generates a ___ topology, a non-Hausdorff space can never be metrizable.

Hausdorff is a necessary consequence of having any metric at all, via the disjoint-ball construction.

19. What does it mean for a space to be simply connected?

Simply connected means path-connected with a trivial fundamental group — every loop contracts to a point.

20. What is the general pattern by which this course's theorems generalize Real Analysis A's?

Replacing ℝ-specific machinery with open-set reasoning is the consistent pattern across every generalized theorem in this course.

21. Fill in the blank: the course that picks up next, studying smooth manifolds — topological spaces with an added smooth structure — is ___.

Differential Geometry is the natural next course, adding smooth structure back onto the topological spaces this course built.
