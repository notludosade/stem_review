1. What is the contrapositive of "If m is a multiple of 4, then m is even"?

Negate and swap both parts: ¬(m even) → ¬(m is a multiple of 4), i.e., m is odd → m is not a multiple of 4.

2. To prove "if n is a perfect square, then n is not negative" by contrapositive, what should you prove instead?

Negate and swap: ¬(n not negative) → ¬(n is a perfect square), i.e., n is negative → n is not a perfect square.

3. Fill in the blank: reaching a statement together with its own negation while reasoning from ¬S is called reaching a ___.

A contradiction shows the assumption ¬S was false, so S must be true.

4. A contradiction proof of "if x is even, then x² is even" assumes which two statements simultaneously?

Contradiction proofs of P → Q assume P and ¬Q together — here, "x is even" (P) and "x² is odd" (¬Q).

5. Fill in the blank: the √2 proof assumes p/q is written in ___, meaning p and q share no common factor greater than 1.

Lowest terms is the assumption that later gets violated when both p and q turn out even, producing the contradiction.

6. Consider proving "if n² is odd, then n is odd." Answer both parts:

(a) A contrapositive proof of this claim would directly prove which statement?

(b) Fill in the blank: a contradiction proof of the same claim would instead begin by assuming n² is odd and n is ___, simultaneously.

(a) Contrapositive negates and swaps: n even → n² even. (b) Contradiction assumes the hypothesis (n² odd) and the negated conclusion (n even) together.

7. Continuing the √2 proof after p² = 2q². Answer both parts:

(a) What is concluded about p at this step?

(b) Fill in the blank: substituting p = 2k into p² = 2q² gives 4k² = 2q², so q² = ___ k².

(a) By Lesson 7's theorem, p² even forces p even. (b) 4k² = 2q² divides by 2 to give q² = 2k², setting up the same argument for q.

8. In Euclid's proof, let N = p₁p₂⋯pₙ + 1 for an assumed complete list of primes. Answer both parts:

(a) Why can no prime on the assumed list divide N?

(b) Fill in the blank: the contradiction arises because some prime p would have to divide N − p₁p₂⋯pₙ = ___, which is impossible for any prime.

(a) The "+1" guarantees remainder 1 against every listed prime. (b) p divides both N and p₁⋯pₙ, so it divides their difference, 1 — impossible since every prime exceeds 1.

9. Consider the claim: "if 3n + 2 is odd, then n is odd." Answer both parts:

(a) What is the contrapositive?

(b) Fill in the blank: assuming n = 2k (even), 3n + 2 = 6k + 2 = 2(___).

(a) Contrapositive negates and swaps: n even → 3n+2 even. (b) 6k + 2 = 2(3k + 1), confirming 3n + 2 is even whenever n is.

10. Consider proving: "if n² is divisible by 3, then n is divisible by 3." Answer both parts:

(a) To prove this by contradiction, which two statements would you assume simultaneously?

(b) Fill in the blank: if n is not divisible by 3, then n = 3k+1 or n = 3k+2 — in either case, n² leaves remainder ___ when divided by 3 (never 0).

(a) Contradiction assumes the hypothesis and negated conclusion together. (b) Both (3k+1)² and (3k+2)² expand to a multiple of 3 plus 1, so n² can never be divisible by 3 when n isn't — the needed contradiction.
