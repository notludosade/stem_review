Dividing Polynomials

Dividing by a single monomial is the exponent laws in reverse, applied one term at a time.

← Lesson 8 Lesson 10 → Contents Glossary →

This lesson covers dividing a polynomial by a single monomial (a one-term expression like 3x). It uses exactly the quotient-of-powers law from Lesson 7 — just applied separately to each term of the polynomial on top.

Divide each term separately

Rule To divide a polynomial by a monomial, split the fraction into one fraction per term, then simplify each piece with the laws of exponents: (A + B) / C = A/C + B/C. Every term gets divided — not just the first one, the same "everything, not just the first term" warning from adding and subtracting polynomials.

Step through dividing (6x³ − 9x² + 3x) by 3x below, one term at a time.

(6x³ − 9x² + 3x) ÷ 3x

Click "Next Step" to begin.

Worked example, written out (6x³ − 9x² + 3x) ÷ 3x = 6x³/3x − 9x²/3x + 3x/3x = 2x² − 3x + 1.
Check by multiplying back: 3x(2x² − 3x + 1) = 6x³ − 9x² + 3x ✓
Why checking by multiplying always works Division undoes multiplication — if A ÷ B = C, then B × C must equal A back again, the same relationship that lets you check 20 ÷ 4 = 5 by confirming 4 × 5 = 20. Multiplying your quotient back against the original divisor is the fastest way to catch a dropped term or sign error before it becomes a wrong final answer.
A coefficient doesn't have to divide evenly (4x² − 6x) ÷ 8x = 4x²/8x − 6x/8x = x/2 − 3/4. Fractions in the answer are completely normal here — nothing requires the coefficients to divide evenly, unlike the whole-number division from grade school this lesson otherwise resembles.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Divide: (8x⁴ + 12x²) ÷ 4x²

8x⁴/4x² = 2x², and 12x²/4x² = 3, giving 2x² + 3.

2. Divide: (15x³ − 10x² + 5x) ÷ 5x

Every term divides by 5x: 15x³/5x = 3x², −10x²/5x = −2x, and 5x/5x = 1 — including that last term, easy to accidentally drop.

3. To check that (10x² − 4x) ÷ 2x = 5x − 2 is correct, what should you do?

Since division undoes multiplication, multiplying the divisor by your quotient should reproduce the original polynomial.

4. Fill in the blank: (4x² − 6x) ÷ 8x = ___ (as an expression with a fraction).

4x²/8x = x/2 and −6x/8x = −3/4, giving x/2 − 3/4 — the coefficients don't have to divide evenly.

5. Fill in the blank: this lesson's method — splitting into one fraction per term — works for dividing by a single ___ (a one-term expression).

A monomial is a single term; this term-by-term split only works cleanly when the divisor is one term rather than several.
