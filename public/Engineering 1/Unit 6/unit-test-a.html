1. In the water-pipe analogy for electricity, what does voltage correspond to?

Voltage is the electrical "pressure" pushing charge through a circuit, just as water pressure pushes water through a pipe.

2. Fill in the blank: the equation V = I × R, relating voltage, current, and resistance, is called ___.

Ohm's law is the electrical equivalent of Newton's second law for mechanics — the starting point for nearly every basic circuit calculation.

3. A wall switch is flipped to "off," breaking the circuit loop to a lamp. What happens to the current through the lamp?

A circuit needs a closed loop to carry current — breaking the loop anywhere drops current to zero everywhere in that loop.

4. Fill in the blank: a 9-volt battery is connected across a 3-ohm resistor. The current flowing through the resistor is ___ amps.

I = V / R = 9 / 3 = 3 amps, directly from Ohm's law.

5. Two 6-ohm resistors are connected in series across a 12-volt battery. What is the total resistance?

Series resistances simply add: R_total = 6 + 6 = 12 ohms.

6. An old-style string of holiday lights is wired in series. One bulb burns out, and the whole string goes dark. Answer both parts:

(a) Why does a single failed bulb take down the entire series string?

(b) Fill in the blank: in a series circuit, total resistance is simply the ___ of each individual resistance.

Because a series circuit has only one path, breaking it anywhere breaks it everywhere downstream, dropping current to zero for the entire loop.

7. Modern household wiring connects each outlet and light fixture in parallel rather than in series. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is this reliability advantage important?

(b) Fill in the blank: three 6-ohm resistors connected in parallel have a total resistance of ___ ohms.

Independent paths mean one component failing doesn't remove the path for every other component; 1/R = 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/2, so R = 2 ohms.

8. A microphone converts continuously varying sound pressure into a continuously varying voltage, smoothly matching louder and softer sounds with no discrete jumps. Answer both parts:

(a) What type of signal does this describe?

(b) Fill in the blank: a dimmer switch that smoothly adjusts brightness through every intermediate level, rather than jumping between fixed steps, produces an ___ signal.

An analog signal varies continuously and can take on any value within a range, with no discrete steps between values.

9. A digital signal picks up a moderate amount of electrical noise as it travels down a long cable, yet the receiver still correctly reads every 0 and 1. Answer both parts:

(a) Why does this noise not corrupt the digital signal's meaning, while similar noise would corrupt an analog signal?

(b) Fill in the blank: a digital signal takes on only a fixed set of discrete values — almost always just two, representing binary 0 and ___.

An analog signal's exact value directly carries the information, so any noise corrupts it — a digital signal only needs a coarse threshold decision, tolerating far more noise.

10. A digital audio file stored on a phone is converted into a continuously varying voltage that drives the phone's speaker. Answer both parts:

(a) What is this conversion process called?

(b) Fill in the blank: the reverse process — sampling a microphone's continuous voltage at regular intervals and rounding each sample to the nearest digital level — is called ___ conversion.

DAC reverses ADC — a smartphone typically does both, converting analog input to digital for processing, then back to analog to drive a speaker or screen.
