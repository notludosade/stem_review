1. A physicist wants to understand why a certain alloy conducts heat the way it does. An engineer is asked to design a heat sink for a processor using that same alloy. What is the key difference between their two goals?

Science pursues true, general explanations; engineering must turn something like that explanation into a working solution under real, specific limits.

2. Fill in the blank: a bridge design with an unlimited budget and unlimited time is trivial to solve — real engineering problems are defined by the ___ placed on cost, materials, time, and safety.

Remove every constraint and almost any engineering problem becomes easy — constraints are what make the problem real.

3. Which of the following is one of the seven SI base units?

The kilogram is a base unit; newtons, joules, and watts are all derived units built out of the base units.

4. Fill in the blank: converting 5 feet to meters using (1 m / 3.281 ft) works because that conversion factor equals exactly ___, since the numerator and denominator describe the same physical length.

A conversion factor is really just the number 1 in disguise, which is exactly why multiplying by it doesn't change the underlying quantity.

5. What two properties fully describe a force?

A force is a vector — it isn't fully specified without both how strong it is and which way it points.

6. Fill in the blank: the force that resists relative sliding between two surfaces in contact, directed opposite to the sliding, is called ___.

Friction always opposes relative motion (or attempted motion) between two contacting surfaces.

7. Which of the four material families is described as extremely stiff and hard under compression, but brittle — failing suddenly with little warning?

Ceramics resist compression well but have almost no ability to deform before fracturing, which is what makes them brittle.

8. Fill in the blank: a material that fractures suddenly at or just past its yield point, with little or no visible deformation beforehand, is described as ___.

Brittle failure gives almost no visible warning before it happens, unlike ductile materials that visibly deform first.

9. A warehouse worker holds a heavy box perfectly still for two minutes, exerting a large upward force the entire time. According to the physics definition of work, how much work does the worker do on the box?

Physics defines work as force acting through a displacement — no displacement means no work, no matter how much effort is felt.

10. Fill in the blank: no machine has ever been built that produces more energy than it consumes — a so-called perpetual motion machine — because the law of ___ of energy forbids it.

Conservation of energy means energy can change form but never be created from nothing — ruling out any machine that outputs more than it takes in.

11. In the water-pipe analogy for electricity, what does voltage correspond to?

Voltage is the "push" behind current, the same way pressure is the push behind water flow — current maps to flow rate, resistance to a narrowed pipe.

12. Fill in the blank: the equation V = I × R, relating voltage, current, and resistance, is called ___.

Ohm's law is the fundamental relationship tying voltage, current, and resistance together in any simple circuit.

13. A local building code requires that structural steel meet ASME standards. Which statement correctly distinguishes the code from the standard in this example?

A code is law; a standard is the technical detail a code often points to — the code makes following that standard mandatory in this case.

14. Fill in the blank: codes and standards are overwhelmingly written or revised in direct response to documented ___, so future engineers don't have to relearn the same lesson the hard way.

Most codes exist because something failed first — they encode hard-won lessons so they don't have to be relearned by later engineers.

15. The original Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapsed in November 1940, just four months after opening. What actually caused the collapse?

An ordinary wind, well within what the bridge should have handled, triggered a growing aerodynamic instability rather than any dramatic overload event.

16. Fill in the blank: the bridge's twisting motion changed how it shed wind, which changed the aerodynamic force on it, which fed back into more twisting — a self-reinforcing pattern called positive ___.

Positive feedback is what turned a small twist into a self-amplifying, growing oscillation until the bridge failed.
