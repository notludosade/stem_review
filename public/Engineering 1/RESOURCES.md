# Engineering 1 Resources

## Knowledge

- [NCEES FE Reference Handbook](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/FE-Reference-Handbook-9.5.pdf)
  The free reference handbook used in the actual Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) licensing exam — covers units, statics, materials, circuits, and ethics in one authoritative document. Use for: quick-lookup formulas and definitions that match real professional practice.
- [MIT OpenCourseWare — 2.001 Mechanics & Materials I](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-001-mechanics-materials-i-fall-2006/)
  Full MIT course materials on statics, stress, and strain. Use for: a rigorous second pass on the statics/materials units when the intro-level treatment here needs more depth.
- [Engineering LibreTexts — Statics](https://eng.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Mechanical_Engineering/Engineering_Statics)
  Free, open statics textbook. Use for: worked equilibrium and truss problems beyond what's covered here.
- [NIST Special Publication 811 — Guide for the Use of the International System of Units (SI)](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811)
  The authoritative US reference for SI units and dimensional conventions. Use for: settling any ambiguity about unit notation.
- [Practical Engineering (YouTube, Grady Hillhouse)](https://www.youtube.com/@PracticalEngineeringChannel)
  A civil engineer explaining real infrastructure and failures in accessible video form. Use for: intuition-building on materials, failure modes, and safety factors.
- [Real Engineering (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/@RealEngineering)
  Explainer videos spanning mechanical, electrical, and systems engineering topics. Use for: seeing how disciplines connect on real projects.
- [NSPE Code of Ethics for Engineers](https://www.nspe.org/resources/ethics/code-ethics)
  The National Society of Professional Engineers' code of ethics, the standard reference in US engineering practice. Use for: the ethics and public-responsibility lesson.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/AskEngineers](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskEngineers/)
  Active community of working engineers and students across all disciplines. Use for: asking which specialization fits a given interest, or getting a practitioner's take on a real design trade-off.

## Gaps

- No single free resource covers circuits, statics, and ethics together at introductory depth — this course deliberately synthesizes across the sources above rather than following one text end to end.
