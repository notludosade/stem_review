1. A tension force pulls at a specific angle on one end of a cable. What two properties are needed to fully describe this force?

A force is a vector — it isn't fully specified without both how strong it is and which way it points.

2. Fill in the blank: a crate resting on a ramp doesn't slide because a force acting along the ramp's surface, resisting sliding, is called ___.

Friction acts opposite to the direction of sliding or potential sliding, and is easy to accidentally omit from a free-body diagram.

3. Why is a free-body diagram drawn for a single isolated object, rather than the entire scene around it?

Isolating one object at a time keeps the diagram focused strictly on forces acting on it, avoiding a common source of directional mix-ups.

4. Fill in the blank: for an object in equilibrium, the sum of all moments about any point must equal ___.

ΣM = 0 about any point is the moment half of the equilibrium condition — without it, an object with balanced forces could still spin.

5. Using a longer wrench to loosen a stuck bolt makes the job easier with the same hand force. Why?

M = F × d — the same hand-force applied at a larger distance from the bolt produces a larger moment, more turning effect for the same effort.

6. An 8-meter beam rests on two supports, one at each end. A 400 N weight sits 3 meters from the left support. Answer both parts:

(a) Which support carries the larger share of the load, and why?

(b) Fill in the blank: taking moments about the left support, the right support's force must equal ___ N.

(a) The support closer to the load bears more of the total weight, confirmed by moment equilibrium. (b) ΣM (about the left support) = 0 → R_right × 8 m = 400 N × 3 m → R_right = 150 N, and by force equilibrium the left support carries the remaining 250 N.

7. A hinged four-bar rectangle can collapse into a parallelogram without any rod changing length, but a hinged triangle can't deform at all. Answer both parts:

(a) What does this demonstrate about why trusses are built from triangles?

(b) Fill in the blank: a truss member being stretched from both ends, like a rope, is said to be in ___.

(a) Unlike a hinged rectangle, which can collapse into a parallelogram, a hinged triangle is geometrically locked in shape — no rigid joints are required. (b) Tension members are stretched; compression members are squeezed — the two possible states for an axially-loaded truss member.

8. At a given truss joint, every member force passes through that same single point. Answer both parts:

(a) Why does this mean only the two force-equilibrium equations apply there, not a moment equation?

(b) Fill in the blank: the method of joints can immediately solve any joint with at most ___ unknown member forces.

(a) Since every force at a joint passes through that same point, there's no perpendicular distance for any of them to create a moment about it. (b) With only two equilibrium equations available at each joint, at most two unknowns can be solved for at once.

9. A steel rod with a cross-sectional area of 0.001 m² carries an axial force of 5,000 N. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the stress in the rod is σ = F / A = 5,000 N / 0.001 m² = ___ MPa.

(b) A thick steel column and a thin steel wire are both subjected to the same 1,000 N pull. Why does the wire snap while the column barely notices?

(a) σ = F / A = 5,000 / 0.001 = 5,000,000 Pa = 5 MPa. (b) Stress normalizes force by area — the same force spread across far less area produces much higher stress in the thin wire.

10. Rubber has a much lower modulus of elasticity (E) than steel. Answer both parts:

(a) What does this low E value indicate about how rubber behaves under stress, compared to steel?

(b) Fill in the blank: the fractional change in a member's length under load, with no units, is called ___.

(a) E is the constant of proportionality between stress and strain — a low E means the material stretches easily under stress. (b) ε = ΔL / L₀ — a ratio of two lengths, so the units cancel and strain is dimensionless.
