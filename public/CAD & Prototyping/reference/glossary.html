Unit 1: Sketches and Parametric Modeling

Geometric constraint

Fixes the relationship between sketch entities without specifying a number — coincident, horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, tangent, equal, symmetric.

Dimensional constraint

Fixes an actual measurement on a sketch — a distance, length, radius, diameter, or angle.

Degrees of freedom (DOF)

The number of independent ways geometry is still free to move. A 2D point has 2 DOF (x, y). A sketch is fully constrained when 0 DOF remain.

Underdefined / fully defined / overdefined

A sketch with DOF > 0 is underdefined (some geometry can still move); DOF = 0 is fully defined; a sketch with contradictory constraints is overdefined, and CAD software flags it as an error rather than resolving it automatically.

Parametric modeling

Dimensions and features are driven by named parameters and formulas instead of fixed numbers, so changing one parameter updates every dependent dimension automatically.

Extrude

Pushes a 2D sketch profile a specified distance perpendicular to its sketch plane, sweeping out a solid.

Revolve

Rotates a 2D sketch profile around a specified axis, sweeping out a solid of revolution.

Feature tree (history tree)

The sequential, ordered record of every feature applied to a model. Each feature builds on the geometry that existed at that point in the tree; editing an earlier feature forces later features to recompute.

Unit 2: Assemblies and Constraints

Assembly / mate

An assembly is a collection of separate parts combined with mates — constraints that fix or limit how each part can move relative to the others.

Coincident mate

Forces two faces, planes, or points to touch flush against each other. Typically removes 3 DOF for a planar face-to-face mate: 1 translation + 2 rotations.

Concentric mate

Forces two cylindrical or circular features to share the same axis. Typically removes 4 DOF: 2 radial translations + 2 tilt rotations.

Distance mate / angle mate

A distance mate fixes a specific offset between two features (removes 1 DOF); an angle mate fixes a specific angular relationship (removes 1 DOF).

6 degrees of freedom

A free rigid body in 3D space has 6 DOF: 3 translational (x, y, z) and 3 rotational (about x, y, z).

Redundant mate

A mate that removes a degree of freedom already removed by an earlier mate, and agrees with it. Harmless, but worth noticing.

Parent / child feature

A parent feature is one a later (child) feature depends on for its geometry. Editing or deleting a parent forces every child to recompute, and can break a child that referenced specific geometry no longer present.

Design intent

The plan for how a model is supposed to behave as it's edited — which surfaces, edges, and relationships should stay meaningful as parameters change.

Unit 3: Tolerances and Fits

Nominal size / tolerance

Nominal size is a dimension's target value. Tolerance is the allowable range of variation around it, e.g. 25.00 ± 0.05mm means limits of 24.95mm to 25.05mm.

Tolerance stack-up

When several toleranced dimensions combine in a chain, their tolerances add together. Worst-case: T_total = T₁ + T₂ + T₃ + … RSS (statistical): T_total = √(T₁² + T₂² + …).

Clearance fit

The hole's smallest allowed size is still larger than the shaft's largest allowed size — a guaranteed gap. Used for parts that must slide or rotate freely.

Interference fit

The hole's largest allowed size is still smaller than the shaft's smallest allowed size — the shaft is always larger, requiring force to assemble and held by friction.

Transition fit

The hole's and shaft's tolerance ranges overlap — an individual assembled pair could land as a slight clearance or a slight interference.

GD&T (Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing)

A standardized symbolic language (ASME Y14.5) for specifying allowable variation in a part's form, orientation, and location, beyond what plain ± dimensions can express.

Datum

A theoretically exact reference (a plane, axis, or point), established from a real feature on the part, that other GD&T tolerances are measured from.

Flatness / perpendicularity / position

Flatness (form) controls a surface's deviation from a perfect plane. Perpendicularity (orientation) controls deviation from exactly 90° relative to a datum. Position (location) controls how far a feature's axis may deviate from its true location, typically as a cylindrical Ø tolerance zone.

Unit 4: Materials and Manufacturing Constraints

Yield strength

The stress at which a material begins to permanently (plastically) deform.

Stiffness / modulus of elasticity (E)

Resistance to elastic (recoverable) deformation under load — a property independent of strength.

Density / specific strength

Density is mass per unit volume. Specific strength (strength ÷ density) and specific stiffness (E ÷ density) are the relevant comparisons whenever a design is weight-limited.

Draft angle

A slight taper (typically 1–3° per side) on a molded or cast part's vertical walls, letting it release from its mold without sticking.

Minimum wall thickness

The thinnest a wall can be made and still reliably fill, print, or cut without warping, breaking, or failing to form — process-dependent.

Undercut

A feature that geometrically blocks a given manufacturing process from producing a part cleanly — a hook a mold can't pull past, an overhang a printer can't print without support, or a shape a laser cutter can't reach.

DFM (Design for Manufacturability)

Designing a part around a specific manufacturing process's real physical limits from the start, rather than discovering them after the fact.

Stress (σ) / Factor of safety (FS)

Stress is internal force divided by cross-sectional area: σ = F/A. Factor of safety is yield strength divided by expected stress: FS = yield strength / expected stress.

Unit 5: 3D Printing Workflows

FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling)

Builds a part by extruding molten thermoplastic filament layer by layer, from the bottom up, each layer fusing to the one below it.

Layer height / infill

Layer height is the thickness of each deposited layer (commonly 0.1–0.3mm). Infill is the density of the internal lattice inside a part, from 0% (hollow) to 100% (solid).

Supports

Temporary printed material generated under overhanging geometry, removed after printing — needed because every new layer must have something solid beneath it.

Print orientation

How a part is rotated on the print bed, affecting support needs, layer-bond strength direction relative to expected load, and print time.

Slicer

Software that converts a 3D model into layer-by-layer toolpath instructions for the printer, based on settings like layer height and infill.

Number of layers

number of layers = part height / layer height. A direct driver of print time.

Overhang angle

Measured from horizontal in this course. Surfaces within about 45° of vertical (steeper than 45° from horizontal) commonly self-support without added print supports.

Bridging

Printing a horizontal span between two supported points with nothing underneath — the 0°-from-horizontal extreme of an overhang, relying on the filament cooling fast enough not to sag.

Unit 6: Laser Cutting and Finishing

Kerf

The width of material actually removed by the laser as it cuts — typically 0.1–0.3mm. A cut part is smaller than its drawn outline by roughly the kerf.

Vector cutting/scoring vs. raster engraving

Vector work follows a drawn path at full power (cutting) or reduced power (scoring). Raster engraving scans line by line to burn a grayscale image into the surface.

Finger joint / tab

Interlocking rectangular protrusions and matching slots cut into two panels' edges, letting them lock together at a right angle without fasteners.

Living hinge

A flexible joint cut into rigid sheet material as a dense pattern of short parallel slits, letting the material bend along that line.

Kerf compensation

Drawing a tab oversized and a slot undersized by the kerf width (draw tab = w + kerf, draw slot = w − kerf), so the finished cut parts land on the true nominal width w. Uncompensated, a tab and slot drawn at the same nominal width mismatch by 2 × kerf.
