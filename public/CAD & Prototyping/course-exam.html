1. A 2D sketch line has 4 DOF. A coincident constraint pins one endpoint to a fixed point (−2 DOF), and an angle dimension is added (−1 DOF). How many DOF remain?

4 − 2 − 1 = 1 DOF remains — the free endpoint can still slide along a fixed direction from the pinned point, since no length has been set.

2. Fill in the blank: a plate has width = 200mm, margin = 20mm, and 6 evenly spaced holes. Using spacing = (width − 2×margin) / (holes − 1), the spacing is ___ mm.

spacing = (200 − 40) / 5 = 160 / 5 = 32mm.

3. Editing an early sketch in a feature tree causes three later features to fail to rebuild. What does this most directly demonstrate?

The feature tree is a dependency chain — editing an early feature can cascade through everything built on top of it.

4. A bolt's shaft must share its axis with the hole it passes through. Which mate type creates this relationship?

Concentric mates align two cylindrical features to a shared axis.

5. Fill in the blank: a part starts at 6 DOF. A coincident mate (−3 DOF) and a distance mate (−1 DOF) and an angle mate (−1 DOF) are applied, with no overlap between them. ___ DOF remain.

6 − 3 − 1 − 1 = 1.

6. Why is it good practice to reference a part's original, stable faces rather than a fillet's rounded edge when placing a new sketch?

Cosmetic features like fillets are among the most likely to be revised — referencing stable geometry avoids fragile downstream dependencies.

7. Fill in the blank: three dimensions stack end to end: 14.00 ± 0.02mm, 9.00 ± 0.03mm, and 6.00 ± 0.015mm. The worst-case total tolerance is ___ mm.

Worst-case stack-up sums the individual tolerances: 0.02 + 0.03 + 0.015 = 0.065mm.

8. A hole ranges from 20.00mm to 20.03mm. A shaft ranges from 20.01mm to 20.04mm. What type of fit is this?

The hole's 20.00–20.03mm range and the shaft's 20.01–20.04mm range overlap, so an assembled pair could land as either a slight clearance or a slight interference.

9. A hole is located with ±0.10mm in both x and y instead of a true position tolerance. Roughly what is the maximum deviation allowed along the diagonal direction?

√(0.10² + 0.10²) = √0.02 ≈ 0.141mm — about 41% more than the 0.10mm allowed along either axis alone.

10. A material barely deflects under load, yet fractures suddenly at a fairly low stress with little warning. Which two properties does this describe?

Barely deflecting means high stiffness; fracturing at low stress means low strength — the two properties are independent.

11. Why doesn't draft angle matter for an FDM-printed part the way it does for an injection-molded one?

Draft angle solves a mold-release problem specific to mold- and cast-based processes — it isn't relevant to a process that never uses a pull-apart tool.

12. Fill in the blank: a part carries 90N over a cross-section 6mm × 2.5mm (15mm²), made from a material with 60 MPa yield strength. Stress = 6 MPa, so FS = 60 / 6 = ___.

σ = 90N / (15×10⁻⁶ m²) = 6×10⁶ Pa = 6 MPa. FS = 60 / 6 = 10.

13. Why does print orientation affect a part's strength, independent of any slicer setting?

Layer-to-layer bonds are inherently weaker than within-layer bonds, so orientation decides whether a load peels layers apart or loads them in-plane.

14. Fill in the blank: a part is 60mm tall, printed at 0.25mm layer height. It needs ___ layers.

number of layers = 60 / 0.25 = 240.

15. Using this course's convention (angle measured from horizontal), which of these is the hardest case for FDM printing to handle without support?

A 0°-from-horizontal bridge is the extreme case, relying entirely on the filament cooling fast enough not to sag.

16. Why does a laser-cut part always come out slightly smaller than its drawn vector outline?

Kerf is real, predictable material removal along the cut path — typically 0.1 to 0.3mm.

17. Fill in the blank: kerf = 0.18mm, nominal joint width = 12.00mm. The tab should be drawn at ___ mm for a snug post-cut fit.

Draw tab = nominal + kerf = 12.00 + 0.18 = 12.18mm.

18. A design requires a true compound-curved surface. What does this mean for choosing between laser cutting and 3D printing, regardless of cost or batch size?

A compound curve is a hard geometric constraint — laser cutting is fundamentally limited to flat stock, so no cost or time advantage can substitute for a process that simply can't produce the required shape.
