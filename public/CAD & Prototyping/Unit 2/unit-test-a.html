Unit 2 Test — Version A

Assemblies and Constraints — mates, degrees of freedom in an assembly, and design intent in the feature tree. 10 points across every lesson in this unit — 5 questions worth 1 point, 5 worth 1 point split across two parts. Score 80% or higher to clear this unit.

1. A drawer slide needs a fixed 3mm gap maintained between the drawer's back panel and the cabinet's rear wall — no contact, no rotation, just a set offset. Which mate type fits this?

A distance mate fixes a specific offset between two features without forcing contact — exactly what a deliberate 3mm gap needs.

2. Fill in the blank: unlike a sketch constraint, which controls geometry within one 2D sketch, an assembly mate controls the relative position and ___ between separate 3D parts.

Mates define both where parts sit relative to each other and what motion, if any, remains between them.

3. A part starts at 6 DOF. A concentric mate (−4 DOF) and an angle mate (−1 DOF) are applied, with no overlap between them. How many DOF remain?

6 − 4 − 1 = 1 DOF remains.

4. Fill in the blank: a free rigid part in 3D space, before any mates are applied, has ___ total degrees of freedom.

3 translational + 3 rotational = 6.

5. A designer deletes a sketch that a later extrude feature was built from. What happens to that extrude feature?

A child feature depends on its parent's geometry — deleting the parent sketch removes what the extrude was built from, so it fails to rebuild.

6. A hinge pin must share its axis with a hinge barrel, but still be free to spin and slide along that axis. Answer both parts:

(a) Which single mate achieves exactly this — shared axis, with spin and axial slide both still free?

(b) Fill in the blank: this mate removes ___ degrees of freedom from the pin's original 6.

(a) Concentric aligns the axes without restricting axial slide or spin. (b) It removes 4 DOF (2 radial translations + 2 tilt rotations), leaving 2 (slide and spin) — exactly what a hinge pin needs.

7. After applying every intended mate, a part can still be dragged slightly in the assembly window. Answer both parts:

(a) What does this indicate about the part?

(b) Fill in the blank: to be fully constrained, this part's remaining degrees of freedom must equal ___.

(a) A part that can still be dragged has DOF > 0 — under-constrained. (b) Fully constrained requires exactly 0 DOF remaining.

8. A mounting-hole sketch is drawn referencing a fillet's rounded edge. Later, that fillet's radius is increased enough to eliminate the original edge shape. Answer both parts:

(a) What happens to the mounting-hole sketch?

(b) Fill in the blank: the plan for which relationships in a model should stay meaningful as it's edited is called ___.

(a) A child feature anchored to a specific parent geometry fails when that geometry changes shape. (b) This deliberate planning around what should stay meaningful under edits is design intent.

9. A part already has 0 DOF remaining after its applied mates. A new mate is added that would fix the exact same position the part is already sitting at. Answer both parts:

(a) What is this new mate called?

(b) Fill in the blank: if that new mate instead demanded a different position than the one already established, it would be called ___ instead.

(a) A mate that agrees with an already-removed freedom is redundant, not an error. (b) A mate that disagrees with an already-removed freedom is conflicting, and gets flagged as an error.

10. A designer is deciding where in the feature tree to place a decorative chamfer on a bracket that already has several downstream features planned. Answer both parts:

(a) What is generally the safer choice, and why?

(b) Fill in the blank: a feature that a later feature depends on for its geometry is called that later feature's ___.

(a) Cosmetic features are among the most likely to be revised, so placing them last reduces how much later work is put at risk. (b) The depended-upon feature is called the parent.
