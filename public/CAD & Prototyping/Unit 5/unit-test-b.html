1. Which of these would eliminate the need for support material under a given overhang, without adding any support structure at all?

Reorientation can turn a problematic overhang into a bed-supported flat face, avoiding the need for support material entirely — neither infill, layer height, nor speed changes the underlying geometry.

2. Fill in the blank: a part is 27mm tall, printed at 0.09mm layer height. It needs ___ layers.

number of layers = 27 / 0.09 = 300.

3. Using this course's convention (angle measured from horizontal), which overhang is most likely to self-support without added support material?

Surfaces steeper than about 45° from horizontal (closer to vertical) commonly self-support — 70° is well within that range.

4. Fill in the blank: FDM stands for ___.

Fused Deposition Modeling builds a part by extruding molten filament layer by layer, fusing each layer to the one below.

5. What causes stringing — thin wisps of plastic between separate features on a print?

Stringing results from oozing filament during nonprinting travel moves, not from a mechanical fault or infill/layer-height settings.

6. A designer increases a part's infill from 15% to 80%. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the general relationship between infill percentage and strength, according to this unit?

(b) What is the direct cost of this change?

Higher infill adds material and time for comparatively smaller strength gains once past a moderate percentage, since the perimeter walls already carry much of the load.

7. A part needs more bending strength without a large increase in print time or material. Answer both parts:

(a) Which change is often the more material-efficient choice?

(b) Fill in the blank: this reasoning mirrors basic ___ theory, where material farther from the neutral axis contributes more to bending resistance.

(a) Outer walls, being farther from the neutral axis, contribute disproportionately to bending strength. (b) The reasoning mirrors beam theory directly.

8. A well-tuned PLA printer with good part cooling is bridging a gap. Answer both parts:

(a) What commonly cited range describes a reliable maximum bridging distance for this setup?

(b) Fill in the blank: reliable bridging depends heavily on part ___, which helps the filament solidify before it can sag.

(a) 5–10mm is a commonly cited representative range for well-tuned PLA, though it's genuinely process-dependent, not a fixed universal number. (b) Fan-driven part cooling speeds up solidification, directly improving bridging reliability.

9. A part height is 45mm, printed at 0.15mm layer height. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: this part needs ___ layers.

(b) What is the slicer's overall job in producing this print?

(a) 45 / 0.15 = 300. (b) The slicer translates geometry and settings into the toolpath the printer actually follows.

10. A long unsupported horizontal span in a design exceeds the printer's reliable bridging distance. Answer both parts:

(a) What is a CAD-level fix that avoids relying purely on slicer settings?

(b) Fill in the blank: like print orientation, this kind of redesign is a ___ decision made in CAD, with no added material or print-time cost by itself.

(a) Breaking one long bridge into several shorter ones keeps each individual span within a reliable distance. (b) Like orientation, this is a "free" geometry decision, not a slicer setting.
