1. Why does FDM printing require support material under a steep overhang?

FDM builds strictly bottom-up — a new layer with nothing solid beneath it has nothing to fuse to.

2. Fill in the blank: a part is 36mm tall, printed at 0.18mm layer height. It needs ___ layers.

number of layers = 36 / 0.18 = 200.

3. Using this course's convention (angle measured from horizontal), which overhang typically needs added support material to print cleanly?

Surfaces shallower than about 45° from horizontal generally need support — 20° is well below that threshold.

4. Fill in the blank: rotating a part on the print bed before slicing, which affects support needs, layer-bond strength direction, and print time, is called print ___.

Orientation is a free decision — no geometry change required — that affects all three factors at once.

5. Why is warping generally more of a concern with ABS than PLA?

Greater cooling shrinkage means more internal stress pulling at the part's corners and edges during printing.

6. A designer switches a part's layer height from 0.24mm to 0.12mm. Answer both parts:

(a) What happens to the surface finish on the part's angled surfaces?

(b) What is the direct cost of this change?

Halving layer height roughly doubles the layer count for a fixed part height, trading print time for finer surface detail.

7. A part loaded mainly in bending needs more strength. Answer both parts:

(a) Which change is often more material-efficient: increasing wall (perimeter) count, or increasing infill percentage?

(b) Fill in the blank: material located farther from a part's ___ contributes more to resisting bending than material near it.

(a) This mirrors basic beam theory, where outer material does more work resisting bending. (b) The neutral axis is the reference line this reasoning is measured from.

8. A design requires a horizontal span between two supported walls, with nothing underneath. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the printer rely on to make this span print successfully?

(b) Fill in the blank: printing a fully unsupported horizontal span like this is called ___.

(a) Bridging relies entirely on the filament cooling fast enough to hold its own shape across the gap. (b) This is called bridging.

9. A part height is 50mm. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: at 0.20mm layer height, this part needs ___ layers.

(b) Why isn't the added print time from switching to a finer layer height exactly proportional to the increase in layer count?

(a) 50 / 0.20 = 250 layers. (b) More layers means more vertical moves and layer changes, but each layer's horizontal travel doesn't shrink just because it got thinner.

10. A part has a downward-facing corner that exceeds the reliable overhang angle in its current orientation. Answer both parts:

(a) What is generally a more reliable fix than just adding more support material and cleaning it up afterward?

(b) Fill in the blank: catching this kind of issue in CAD, before the first print attempt, is an application of the same ___ logic used for molding and machining.

(a) Redesigning the geometry (chamfer or reorientation) eliminates the problem outright, rather than papering over it. (b) This is the same DFM logic applied specifically to FDM's constraints.
