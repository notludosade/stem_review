Exponential Growth and Decay

"Rate of change proportional to the current amount" is the single most common phrase in applied differential equations — and Unit 1's separation of variables solves it in three lines, every time.

← Unit 1, Lesson 3 Lesson 5 → Contents Glossary →

1. Solving dy/dt = ky

Method — The Exponential Model Any quantity whose rate of change is proportional to its current value satisfies dy/dt = ky. Separating and integrating: (1/y) dy = k dt → ln|y| = kt + C₁ → y = y₀ekt, where y₀ = y(0) is the initial amount. k > 0 gives growth; k < 0 gives decay.
Worked Example — Population Doubling A population grows at a rate proportional to its size, starting at 200 and reaching 400 after 3 years. Find k: 400 = 200e3k → 2 = e3k → k = ln(2)/3 ≈ 0.231. The model is P(t) = 200e0.231t.
Worked Example — Radioactive Decay and Half-Life A substance with decay constant k = −0.05 (per year) starts at 80 grams. After t years, A(t) = 80e−0.05t. The half-life — the time for half the substance to remain — solves ½ = ekt½, giving t½ = ln(2)/|k| ≈ 13.9 years. At t = 20, A(20) = 80e−1 ≈ 29.4 grams — less than half remains, since more than one half-life has passed.

Check your recall

1. The differential equation dy/dt = ky solves to:

Separating and integrating dy/dt = ky always produces an exponential with k in the exponent multiplied by t, scaled by the initial value y₀.

2. In y = y₀e^(kt), a negative value of k describes ___

A negative exponent constant shrinks the quantity over time rather than growing it — the signature of decay.

3. A population grows from 200 to 400 in 3 years under dP/dt = kP. The value of k is:

400 = 200e^(3k) reduces to 2 = e^(3k); taking the natural log of both sides gives 3k = ln(2), so k = ln(2)/3.

4. Fill in the blank: for y = y₀e^(kt) with k > 0, the doubling time is t = ___

Setting y₀e^(kt) = 2y₀ gives e^(kt) = 2, so kt = ln(2) and t = ln(2)/k — independent of y₀, which is why "doubling time" is a fixed constant for a given k.
