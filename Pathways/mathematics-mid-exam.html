Mathematics — Mid-Pathway Exam

24 questions spanning Precalculus and AP Calculus BC — everything the first half of this pathway covered. Unlocks once both course exams are passed. Score 75% or higher to pass and unlock the second half of the route: Real Analysis A and B.

1. What is the domain of f(x) = 1/(x − 3)?

The denominator is undefined (zero) exactly at x = 3, so that's the only excluded value.

2. Fill in the blank: in (f ∘ g)(x) = f(g(x)), the function applied FIRST to x is ___.

Composition reads inside-out: g acts on x first, then f acts on that result.

3. For an odd-degree polynomial with a positive leading coefficient, as x → −∞, f(x) →

Odd-degree, positive leading coefficient: the ends point in opposite directions, down on the left, up on the right.

4. A rational function has a vertical asymptote at x = a only if:

If a factor cancels between numerator and denominator, x = a is a removable hole, not a vertical asymptote — the denominator being zero alone isn't sufficient.

5. Fill in the blank: log_b(xy) = ___ (the product rule for logarithms, in terms of log_b(x) and log_b(y)).

The log of a product is the sum of the logs: log_b(xy) = log_b(x) + log_b(y).

6. Solve 2^x = 16.

16 = 2⁴, and since the bases match, x = 4.

7. Fill in the blank: sin(π/6) = ___.

π/6 = 30°, and sin(30°) = 1/2.

8. What is the period of tan(x)?

Unlike sine and cosine (period 2π), tangent repeats every π because it's a ratio that resets faster.

9. Fill in the blank: sin²θ + cos²θ = ___.

The Pythagorean identity, true for every real θ.

10. How many solutions does sin(x) = 0 have on the interval [0, 2π)?

Sine is zero at 0 and π within [0, 2π) — note 2π itself is excluded by the half-open interval.

11. Fill in the blank: for the 2×2 matrix [[a, b], [c, d]], the determinant is ___.

det = ad − bc, the product of the main diagonal minus the product of the other diagonal.

12. The nth term of a geometric sequence with first term a and common ratio r is:

Each term multiplies the previous by r, so after n−1 multiplications from the first term, you get a·r^(n−1).

13. Fill in the blank: lim(x→0) sin(x)/x = ___.

A famous special limit, provable via the squeeze theorem — note this is a 0/0 indeterminate form resolved by the limit, not by naive substitution.

14. d/dx[x⁵] =

Power rule: bring the exponent down as a coefficient, then reduce the exponent by 1.

15. Fill in the blank: the product rule states d/dx[f·g] = ___ (in terms of f, g, f′, g′).

d/dx[fg] = f′g + fg′ — differentiate each factor in turn, keeping the other one along for the ride.

16. If h(x) = f(g(x)), then h′(x) =

The chain rule: differentiate the outer function at the inner function's value, then multiply by the inner function's derivative.

17. Fill in the blank: d/dx[arcsin(x)] = ___.

d/dx[arcsin(x)] = 1/√(1 − x²), valid for −1 < x < 1.

18. A critical point of f occurs where:

Both a zero derivative and an undefined derivative (like a sharp corner or vertical tangent) produce critical points — a common error is forgetting the "undefined" case.

19. By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, if F′ = f, then ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx =

The definite integral equals the antiderivative evaluated at the upper limit minus the lower limit.

20. Fill in the blank: the general solution to dy/dt = ky (exponential growth/decay) is y = ___.

Separating and integrating gives y = Ce^(kt), where C is set by the initial condition.

21. The area between two curves f(x) and g(x) (with f above g) from x = a to x = b is:

Top-minus-bottom, integrated across the interval, gives the enclosed area.

22. Fill in the blank: for r(t) = ⟨x(t), y(t)⟩, the speed |r′(t)| equals ___ (in terms of dx/dt and dy/dt).

Speed is the magnitude of the velocity vector: √[(dx/dt)² + (dy/dt)²].

23. The geometric series Σ a·rⁿ (n = 0 to ∞) converges if and only if:

Strict inequality: at r = 1 the series diverges (sum of a's), and at r = −1 it oscillates without converging.

24. A series that converges when the absolute values of its terms are summed is called:

Absolute convergence is the stronger condition — a series that converges only without the absolute values is conditionally convergent, not absolutely convergent.
