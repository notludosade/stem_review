1. Separating the variables

Definition — Separable Equation A first-order equation is separable if it can be written dy/dx = f(x)g(y) — a pure function of x times a pure function of y. Rearranging puts every y-term (including dy) on one side and every x-term (including dx) on the other: dy/g(y) = f(x) dx. Integrating both sides independently solves it.
Method — Solving a Separable Equation (1) Rewrite dy/dx as a fraction and separate: dy/g(y) = f(x) dx. (2) Integrate both sides: ∫ dy/g(y) = ∫ f(x) dx. (3) Solve for y if possible, and don't forget the constant of integration — combining both sides' constants into one + C is standard. (4) If an initial condition is given, substitute it in to solve for C, turning the general solution into one particular curve.
Worked Example Solve dy/dx = xy, y(0) = 3. Separate: dy/y = x dx. Integrate: ln|y| = x²/2 + C₁. Exponentiate: y = ex²/2 + C₁ = Cex²/2 (absorbing eC₁ into a new constant C). Apply y(0) = 3: 3 = Ce⁰ = C. The particular solution is y = 3ex²/2.

3. General vs. particular solutions

Fact — General and Particular Solutions Integrating a separated equation leaves an arbitrary constant C, producing a general solution — an entire family of curves, one for every value of C. An initial condition pins down C to one specific value, giving the particular solution that passes through that one point.

Check your recall

1. Which equation is separable?

dy/dx = x²y is already a pure function of x (x²) times a pure function of y (y), so it factors as f(x)g(y). The other three involve x and y added together before any other operation applies, and no algebra rearranges a sum like that into a product of separate functions.

2. Fill in the blank: separating dy/dx = x/y means moving y to the left as ___ = x dx.

Cross-multiplying dy/dx = x/y gives y dy = x dx — every y-term (including dy) on the left, every x-term (including dx) on the right.

3. Solving dy/dx = xy with y(0) = 3 gives:

Separating and integrating gives y = Ce^(x²/2); applying y(0) = 3 pins C = 3, giving y = 3e^(x²/2).

4. Fill in the blank: a solution family written with an arbitrary constant C, before any initial condition is applied, is called the ___ solution.

The general solution is the whole family of curves indexed by C; an initial condition selects one member of that family, the particular solution.
