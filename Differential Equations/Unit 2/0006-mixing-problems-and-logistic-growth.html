Mixing Problems and Logistic Growth

Two more first-order models close out the unit: a tank whose contents change as fluid flows in and out, and a growth equation that — unlike Lesson 4's — builds a limit into itself.

← Lesson 5 Unit 3, Lesson 7 → Contents Glossary →

1. Mixing problems

Method — Setting Up a Mixing Problem For a tank holding a dissolved substance, track the amount A(t) with dA/dt = (rate in) − (rate out). Rate in is (incoming concentration)×(inflow rate). Rate out is (current concentration)×(outflow rate) = [A(t)/V(t)]×(outflow rate), where V(t) is the tank's volume at time t — constant if inflow and outflow rates match.
Worked Example A 100-gallon tank starts with 5 lb of dissolved salt. Brine with 2 lb/gal enters at 3 gal/min; the well-mixed solution leaves at the same rate, so volume stays at 100 gal. Rate in = 3(2) = 6 lb/min. Rate out = 3(A/100) = 0.03A lb/min. This gives dA/dt + 0.03A = 6 — first-order linear, solved with Unit 1's integrating factor: μ = e0.03t, giving A(t) = 200 + Ce−0.03t. Applying A(0) = 5: C = −195, so A(t) = 200 − 195e−0.03t.

2. The logistic differential equation

Definition — Logistic Differential Equation dP/dt = rP(1 − P/K), where r is the growth rate and K is the carrying capacity — the maximum sustainable population. When P is small compared to K, (1 − P/K) ≈ 1 and growth looks nearly exponential, matching Lesson 4. As P approaches K, (1 − P/K) approaches 0 and growth slows to a stop — the missing ingredient in the plain exponential model.
Fact — The Logistic Solution Solving by separation of variables (partial fractions required) gives P(t) = K·P₀·ert / (K − P₀ + P₀ert). As t → ∞, ert dominates and the ratio approaches K — the population levels off at the carrying capacity no matter where it started, as long as P₀ > 0. Growth is fastest at the curve's inflection point, P = K/2.

3. Watching the S-curve approach its capacity

With r = 0.5 and P₀ = 5 fixed, slide the carrying capacity K and watch the curve level off there — always slowing fastest as it crosses K/2.

Check your recall

1. A mixing problem's governing equation always takes the form dA/dt = :

The amount of dissolved substance changes exactly by what flows in minus what flows out per unit time — a direct accounting equation, not a product or a ratio.

2. Fill in the blank: for the 100-gallon tank example (2 lb/gal brine entering at 3 gal/min, well-mixed outflow at 3 gal/min), the governing equation is dA/dt + 0.03A = ___

Rate in = 3 gal/min × 2 lb/gal = 6 lb/min, and rate out = 3(A/100) = 0.03A, giving dA/dt = 6 − 0.03A, or dA/dt + 0.03A = 6.

3. In dP/dt = rP(1 − P/K), the constant K represents:

K is the population level the factor (1 − P/K) drives growth toward zero at — the maximum size the environment can sustain.

4. The logistic curve grows fastest — its inflection point — at P = ___

Maximizing rP(1 − P/K) with respect to P places the peak growth rate exactly halfway to the carrying capacity, at P = K/2.

5. As t → ∞, the logistic solution P(t) approaches:

The e^(rt) term dominates both numerator and denominator as t grows, and the ratio simplifies to K — unlike the plain exponential model, the population levels off instead of growing without bound.
