Newton's Law of Cooling

A quantity's rate of change proportional to itself gave Lesson 4's pure exponential. Shift what it's proportional to — the gap from room temperature, not the temperature itself — and the same separation technique still solves it.

← Lesson 4 Lesson 6 → Contents Glossary →

1. The model

Definition — Newton's Law of Cooling An object's temperature T changes at a rate proportional to the difference between T and the surrounding (ambient) temperature Ta: dT/dt = k(T − Ta). Ta is treated as constant. If T starts above Ta, the object cools; if it starts below Ta, the same equation describes it warming up — either way, k < 0, since the gap T − Ta always shrinks toward zero.
Method — Solving Let u = T − Ta. Then du/dt = dT/dt = k(T − Ta) = ku — exactly Lesson 4's equation. So u = u₀ekt, which means T(t) = Ta + (T₀ − Ta)ekt, where T₀ = T(0).
Worked Example Coffee at 200°F sits in a 70°F room; k = −0.1 (per minute). T(t) = 70 + 130e−0.1t. After 10 minutes: T(10) = 70 + 130e−1 ≈ 70 + 47.8 = 117.8°F. To find how long until it reaches 100°F: 100 = 70 + 130e−0.1t → e−0.1t = 30/130 → t = ln(130/30)/0.1 ≈ 14.7 minutes.

2. Watching the gap close

Coffee starts at 200°F in a 70°F room. Slide the cooling constant |k| and watch how fast the temperature settles toward ambient — and how long it takes to reach 100°F.

Try It — T(t) = 70 + 130e^(−|k|t)
|k| = 0.10

Check your recall

1. Newton's Law of Cooling states:

The rate of change is proportional to the gap between T and the ambient temperature T_a, not to T alone — that's what distinguishes it from Lesson 4's plain exponential model.

2. Fill in the blank: whether an object is warming or cooling toward T_a, the constant k in dT/dt = k(T − T_a) is always ___

Whether T starts above or below T_a, the gap T − T_a shrinks toward 0 over time — which requires k < 0 in both directions.

3. As t → ∞, T(t) = T_a + (T₀ − T_a)e^(kt) approaches:

Since k < 0, e^(kt) → 0 as t → ∞, so T(t) approaches T_a + 0 = T_a — the object equilibrates with its surroundings.

4. Fill in the blank: a pie at 180°F in a 70°F kitchen with k = −0.05 (per minute) has temperature T(t) = 70 + 110e^(−0.05t). At t = 15 minutes, T(15) ≈ ___ °F (nearest whole number).

T(15) = 70 + 110e^(−0.75) ≈ 70 + 110(0.4724) ≈ 70 + 51.96 ≈ 122°F.
