Homogeneous Equations with Constant Coefficients

Every equation in Units 1–2 was first-order — one constant of integration, one initial condition. Second-order equations need a genuinely new guess, and produce a genuinely bigger family of solutions.

← Unit 2, Lesson 6 Lesson 8 → Contents Glossary →

1. The equation and the guess

Definition — Second-Order Linear Homogeneous Equation A second-order linear homogeneous equation with constant coefficients has the form ay″ + by′ + cy = 0, where a, b, c are constants and a ≠ 0. "Homogeneous" means the right side is 0 — no standalone forcing term.
Method — The Exponential Guess Try y = erx. Then y′ = rerx and y″ = r²erx. Substituting: ar²erx + brerx + cerx = 0. Since erx is never zero, divide it out: ar² + br + c = 0 — the characteristic equation. Every root r gives a solution erx.
Worked Example Solve y″ − 5y′ + 6y = 0. Characteristic equation: r² − 5r + 6 = 0 → (r − 2)(r − 3) = 0 → r = 2, 3 — two distinct real roots. Each gives a solution, and (as Lesson 8 will justify) the general solution combines both: y = C₁e2x + C₂e3x.

2. Two constants, not one

Why This Matters A first-order equation's general solution carries exactly one arbitrary constant, pinned down by one initial condition. A second-order equation's general solution carries exactly two arbitrary constants, C₁ and C₂ — because it takes two independent building-block solutions to describe every possible solution, and two initial conditions (typically y(x₀) and y′(x₀)) to pin both constants down. Dropping one of the two constants is one of the most common errors in this unit.

1. Substituting y = e^(rx) into ay″ + by′ + cy = 0 and dividing out e^(rx) leaves:

y = re^(rx) and y″ = r²e^(rx) substituted in give ar²e^(rx) + bre^(rx) + ce^(rx) = 0; since e^(rx) ≠ 0, dividing it out leaves the characteristic equation ar² + br + c = 0.

2. Fill in the blank: for y″ − 5y′ + 6y = 0, the general solution is y = C₁e^(2x) + C₂e^(___x)

The characteristic equation r² − 5r + 6 = 0 factors as (r − 2)(r − 3) = 0, giving roots 2 and 3.

3. The general solution of a second-order linear homogeneous equation has how many arbitrary constants?

Exactly two independent building-block solutions combine (with two constants, C₁ and C₂) to form every solution — matching the two initial conditions a second-order IVP needs.

4. Fill in the blank: for y″ + 3y′ + 2y = 0, the characteristic equation r² + 3r + 2 = 0 factors as (r + 1)(r + 2) = 0, so one of its roots is r = ___

(r + 1)(r + 2) = 0 gives r = −1 or r = −2 — either one is a correct root of this characteristic equation.
