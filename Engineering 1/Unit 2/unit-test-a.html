1. Which of the following is one of the seven SI base units?

The kilogram (mass) is one of the seven SI base units; newtons, joules, and watts are all derived units built from combinations of the base units.

2. Fill in the blank: converting 5 feet to meters using (1 m / 3.281 ft) works because that conversion factor equals exactly ___, since the numerator and denominator describe the same physical length.

Multiplying by a fraction that equals 1 changes the units without changing the actual quantity being measured.

3. A student calculates a car's travel time and gets an answer with units of meters instead of seconds. What does this tell them, according to dimensional analysis?

A physically correct equation must have matching units on both sides — a result in the wrong units is hard proof a mistake happened, even before checking the number.

4. Fill in the blank: how close a measurement is to the true value is called ___, a property that is independent of how tightly clustered repeated measurements are.

Accuracy measures closeness to the true value; precision measures consistency and reported digit count — the two are independent properties.

5. Why did NASA lose the Mars Climate Orbiter in 1999?

The failure was a systems-level unit mismatch between two teams' software, not a single arithmetic slip.

6. Consider the measured values 0.0420 m and 3,006 kg. Answer both parts:

(a) How many significant figures does 0.0420 have?

(b) How many significant figures does 3,006 have?

(a) Leading zeros are never significant, but a trailing zero after a decimal point is — 4, 2, and 0 all count. (b) Zeros between nonzero digits are always significant — all four digits count.

7. Answer both parts about rounding a calculator result to the correct number of significant figures or decimal places:

(a) Multiplying 2.5 (2 sig figs) by 4.168 (4 sig figs) gives 10.42 on a calculator. How should the result be reported?

(b) Adding 22.6 and 9.34 gives 31.94 on a calculator. Following the addition rule, how should this be reported?

(a) Multiplication and division results are capped at the fewest significant figures among the inputs. (b) Addition and subtraction results are capped at the fewest decimal places among the inputs.

8. Answer both parts about Fermi problems:

(a) What is a Fermi problem?

(b) Fill in the blank: this style of chained-estimate problem is named after physicist Enrico ___.

(a) Fermi problems break an unknown quantity into a chain of estimable sub-quantities that multiply or divide together. (b) Fermi was known for producing surprisingly accurate on-the-spot estimates this way.

9. A five-step Fermi estimate has each individual guess potentially off by a factor of 2. Answer both parts:

(a) Why doesn't the final answer typically end up off by a factor of 32 (2⁵)?

(b) What range of accuracy does a well-constructed Fermi estimate typically achieve, compared to the true value?

Independent errors in different directions tend to offset one another, keeping the final estimate within roughly a factor of 3-10 of the true value rather than compounding multiplicatively.

10. A rough estimate early in a design suggests a proposed battery would need to weigh 200 kg to meet a requirement. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is this estimate valuable before any detailed circuit design begins?

(b) Fill in the blank: an order-of-___ estimate only aims to identify the right power of 10, not an exact value.

Getting within the right power of 10 is a far lower, far more achievable bar than exact precision, and is often all that's needed to sanity-check a design direction early.
