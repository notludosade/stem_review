1. Which of the following is one of the seven SI base units?

The second (time) is one of the seven SI base units; pascals, hertz, and coulombs are all derived units built from combinations of the base units.

2. Fill in the blank: converting 10 inches to centimeters using (2.54 cm / 1 in) works because that conversion factor equals exactly ___, since the numerator and denominator describe the same physical length.

Multiplying by a fraction that equals 1 changes the units without changing the actual quantity being measured.

3. A student calculates a rocket's acceleration and the units come out as meters, not meters per second squared. What does this tell them, according to dimensional analysis?

A physically correct equation must have matching units on both sides — a result in the wrong units is hard proof a mistake happened, even before checking the number.

4. Fill in the blank: a thermometer that reads consistently 3 degrees above the true temperature every time is precise, but lacks ___.

Accuracy measures closeness to the true value; precision measures consistency — a consistently biased reading is precise but not accurate.

5. What specifically went wrong in the Mars Climate Orbiter failure, distinguishing it from a simple arithmetic mistake?

The failure was a systems-level unit mismatch between two teams' software, not a single arithmetic slip.

6. Consider the measured values 0.00650 m and 5,004 kg. Answer both parts:

(a) How many significant figures does 0.00650 have?

(b) How many significant figures does 5,004 have?

(a) Leading zeros are never significant, but a trailing zero after a decimal point is — 6, 5, and 0 all count. (b) Zeros between nonzero digits are always significant — all four digits count.

7. Answer both parts about rounding a calculator result to the correct number of significant figures or decimal places:

(a) Multiplying 3.2 (2 sig figs) by 5.146 (4 sig figs) gives 16.4672 on a calculator. How should the result be reported?

(b) Adding 8.6 and 2.45 gives 11.05 on a calculator. Following the addition rule, how should this be reported?

(a) Multiplication and division results are capped at the fewest significant figures among the inputs. (b) Addition and subtraction results are capped at the fewest decimal places among the inputs.

8. Answer both parts about Fermi problems:

(a) What best describes a Fermi problem?

(b) Fill in the blank: this technique is named after physicist Enrico ___, known for producing accurate on-the-spot estimates.

(a) Fermi problems break an unknown quantity into a chain of estimable sub-quantities that multiply or divide together. (b) Fermi was known for producing surprisingly accurate on-the-spot estimates this way.

9. A chain of four Fermi-estimate guesses, each potentially off by a factor of 2, is used to estimate the number of coffee shops in a city. Answer both parts:

(a) Why doesn't the final estimate typically end up off by 2⁴ = 16×?

(b) About how close to the true value does a well-constructed Fermi estimate typically land?

Independent errors in different directions tend to offset one another, keeping the final estimate within roughly a factor of 3-10 of the true value rather than compounding multiplicatively.

10. Before running detailed structural calculations, an engineer roughly estimates that a proposed bridge component would need to be twice as thick as any available material allows. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is catching this early, with only a rough estimate, valuable?

(b) Fill in the blank: this kind of estimate only aims to get the right order of ___ — is the true answer closer to 50, 500, or 5,000 — not an exact figure.

Getting within the right power of 10 is a far lower, far more achievable bar than exact precision, and is often all that's needed to sanity-check a design direction early.
