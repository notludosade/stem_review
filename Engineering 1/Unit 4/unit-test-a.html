Materials — common engineering materials, material selection trade-offs, and why things break. 10 points across every lesson in this unit — 5 questions worth 1 point, 5 worth 1 point split across two parts. Score 90% or higher to clear this unit.

1. Which of the four material families is described as extremely stiff and hard under compression, but brittle — failing suddenly with little warning?

Ceramics are stiff and excellent under compression, but brittle — they fracture suddenly with little warning, unlike ductile metals.

2. Fill in the blank: a material that fractures suddenly at or just past its yield point, with little or no visible deformation beforehand, is described as ___.

Brittle materials give none of the visible warning that ductile materials give before they finally break.

3. Why is concrete almost always paired with steel reinforcement in construction, given concrete's own properties?

Pairing brittle concrete with ductile steel means visible bending appears before a catastrophic, sudden failure would otherwise occur.

4. Fill in the blank: a material made of two or more combined materials chosen to get a property profile neither has alone — like carbon fiber strands in an epoxy matrix — is called a ___.

Composites combine materials to get a property profile, like high stiffness at low weight, that neither component achieves alone.

5. Aircraft skin is typically made from aluminum, or increasingly carbon-fiber composite, rather than steel — even though steel is stronger in absolute terms. Why?

Strength or stiffness per unit weight often matters more than raw strength, especially for anything that has to fly.

6. Mild steel and a ceramic are both loaded under increasing stress until failure. Answer both parts:

(a) Which best describes mild steel's stress-strain curve?

(b) Fill in the blank: unlike steel, a ceramic's curve rises linearly and then ___ almost immediately after leaving the linear region, with no flat warning section.

Steel's long flat region reflects ductile behavior that gives warning before failure; a ceramic's abrupt end reflects brittle behavior with no such warning.

7. A touring bike, built to survive years of abuse, and a professional racing bike reasonably use different frame materials for the exact same part. Answer both parts:

(a) Why can this be true, even though both bikes need the same part to do the same job?

(b) Fill in the blank: in vehicles and portable devices, extra ___ carries an ongoing cost (fuel, battery life, fatigue), not just an upfront one, making it a critical selection criterion.

"Best" only makes sense once the specific application's priorities are known — that's exactly what makes material selection a trade-off, not a lookup.

8. An engineer is selecting a material for an engine exhaust component. Answer both parts:

(a) What happens first in the "eliminate, then rank" selection method, applied to this component?

(b) Fill in the blank: only after hard requirements eliminate unsuitable options does the engineer ___ the remaining materials against whichever criteria — cost, corrosion resistance, weight — matter most for this application.

Elimination happens before ranking, so remaining trade-offs are only weighed among materials that could actually work in the first place.

9. A metal paperclip is bent back and forth repeatedly, each bend applying stress well below what would snap it in one motion — yet it eventually breaks. Answer both parts:

(a) What failure mode does this demonstrate?

(b) Fill in the blank: fatigue is dangerous because a part can pass every static load test and still fail in service, since static tests apply a load once while fatigue depends on how many ___ a load repeats over the part's lifetime.

Fatigue grows a microscopic crack a little further with every cycle, until it suddenly propagates — a static test that applies load only once can miss it entirely.

10. A steel bracket sits in a humid outdoor environment for years before finally snapping suddenly, with almost no warning bend beforehand. Answer both parts:

(a) How does corrosion most directly make this later fracture more likely, given stress = F / A?

(b) Fill in the blank: sudden failure that propagates from an existing flaw or crack, especially in brittle materials, with little or no warning deformation, is called ___.

Corrosion both raises effective stress by shrinking area and creates a crack-like flaw — often becoming the very starting point a later fracture propagates from.
