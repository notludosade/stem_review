# Mission: Engineering 1

## Why
A broad, interdisciplinary introduction to engineering — what engineers actually do, how they think, and how the major disciplines (mechanical, electrical, civil, chemical) differ — before specializing into any one of them. Plays the same role for engineering that Precalculus plays for Calculus: a shared foundation everything else builds on.

## Success looks like
- Can describe the engineering design process and apply it to a real, open-ended problem
- Comfortable with SI units, dimensional analysis, significant figures, and order-of-magnitude estimation
- Can draw a free-body diagram, apply equilibrium, and reason about stress/strain and material failure at an introductory level
- Understands the basic vocabulary of materials, energy/systems thinking, and circuits well enough to recognize which discipline a given problem belongs to
- Understands why codes, standards, safety factors, and engineering ethics exist — not as bureaucracy, but as hard-won lessons from real failures

## Constraints
- Interdisciplinary survey, not a deep dive into any one discipline — depth comes in later, discipline-specific courses
- Assumes Precalculus (trigonometry, vectors, basic algebra) but not Calculus — mechanics here is algebra/trig-based, not calculus-based
- Broad enough to prepare a student to choose a specialization with real information, not guesswork

## Out of scope
- Calculus-based mechanics (dynamics with derivatives, differential equations) — deferred to discipline-specific follow-on courses
- Deep dives into any single discipline's core curriculum (circuit design, structural analysis, thermodynamics in full) — this course surveys, it doesn't specialize
- Software/programming — covered separately in the Computer Programming strand
