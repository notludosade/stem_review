1. What two properties fully describe a force?

A force is a vector — it isn't fully specified without both how strong it is and which way it points.

2. Fill in the blank: the force that resists relative sliding between two surfaces in contact, directed opposite to the sliding, is called ___.

Friction acts opposite to the direction of sliding or potential sliding, and is easy to accidentally omit from a free-body diagram.

3. Why is a free-body diagram drawn for a single, isolated object rather than the entire scene around it?

Isolating one object at a time keeps the diagram focused strictly on forces acting on it, avoiding a common source of directional mix-ups.

4. Fill in the blank: for an object in equilibrium, the sum of all forces in any direction must equal ___.

ΣF = 0 in every direction is the force half of the equilibrium condition — a nonzero sum would mean acceleration, by Newton's second law.

5. Using a longer wrench to loosen a stuck bolt makes the job easier with the same hand force. Why?

M = F × d — the same hand-force applied at a larger distance from the bolt produces a larger moment, more turning effect for the same effort.

6. A 6-meter beam rests on two supports, one at each end. A 300 N weight sits 2 meters from the left support. Answer both parts:

(a) Which support carries the larger share of the load, and why?

(b) Fill in the blank: taking moments about the left support, the right support's force must equal ___ N.

(a) The support closer to the load bears more of the total weight, confirmed by moment equilibrium. (b) ΣM (about the left support) = 0 → R_right × 6 m = 300 N × 2 m → R_right = 100 N, and by force equilibrium the left support carries the remaining 200 N.

7. A truss is built entirely from triangular panels connected at pin joints. Answer both parts:

(a) Why are triangles, specifically, the fundamental shape in truss design?

(b) Fill in the blank: a truss member being squeezed from both ends, like a column holding up a roof, is said to be in ___.

(a) Unlike a hinged rectangle, which can collapse into a parallelogram, a hinged triangle is geometrically locked in shape. (b) Compression members are squeezed; tension members are stretched — the two possible states for an axially-loaded truss member.

8. A truss joint has exactly two unknown member forces meeting at it. Answer both parts:

(a) Why does the method of joints use only the two force equations (ΣFₓ = 0, ΣFy = 0) at this joint, and not a moment equation?

(b) Fill in the blank: a joint can be solved immediately by the method of joints only if it has at most ___ unknown member forces.

(a) Since every force at a joint passes through that same point, there's no perpendicular distance for any of them to create a moment about it. (b) With only two equilibrium equations available at each joint, at most two unknowns can be solved for at once.

9. A steel rod with a cross-sectional area of 0.002 m² carries an axial force of 6,000 N. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the stress in the rod is σ = F / A = 6,000 N / 0.002 m² = ___ MPa.

(b) Why does a thin wire snap under a force that a thick column barely notices, even though both experience the same force?

(a) σ = F / A = 6,000 / 0.002 = 3,000,000 Pa = 3 MPa. (b) Stress normalizes force by area — the same force spread across far less area produces much higher stress in the thin wire.

10. A material has a very high modulus of elasticity (E), such as steel (~200 GPa). Answer both parts:

(a) What does this high E value indicate about how the material behaves under stress?

(b) Fill in the blank: the fractional change in a member's length under load, ΔL / L₀, with no units, is called ___.

(a) E is the constant of proportionality between stress and strain — a high E means the material resists stretching, i.e., it's stiff. (b) ε = ΔL / L₀ — a ratio of two lengths, so the units cancel and strain is dimensionless.
