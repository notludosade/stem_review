Dielectrics

Slide an insulator between a capacitor's plates and capacitance always goes up — but whether voltage falls or charge rises depends entirely on whether a battery is still attached.

← Lesson 8 Unit 4, Lesson 10 → Contents Glossary →

Definition — Dielectric Constant A dielectric is an insulating material that, filling the gap between a capacitor's plates, increases capacitance by a factor κ (the material's dielectric constant, κ ≥ 1): C = κC₀, where C₀ is the capacitance with vacuum (or air) in the gap. Microscopically, the dielectric's molecules polarize in the field, creating bound surface charges that partially cancel the free charges' field inside the material — reducing E for a given amount of free charge, which is what lets more charge fit per volt.
Method — Two Scenarios That Give Different Answers Whether inserting a dielectric changes V or Q depends on what stays fixed. Isolated capacitor (battery disconnected first, so Q is fixed): C rises to κC₀, so V = Q/C falls to V₀/κ, E falls to E₀/κ, and stored energy falls to U₀/κ (the field does positive work pulling the dielectric in). Battery-connected capacitor (V is held fixed by the battery): C still rises to κC₀, but now Q = CV rises to κQ₀ (the battery pushes more charge on to maintain V), E = V/d is unchanged (V and d are both fixed), and stored energy rises to κU₀ (the battery supplies the extra energy).
Why Both Can't Be True at Once Q = CV is one equation with three quantities — fixing any two of {Q, V} while C changes automatically determines the third's response, so "does V drop or does Q rise?" isn't answerable without first knowing which one is being held fixed by the circuit. Always check whether the battery is still connected before predicting what a dielectric does.
Worked Example — Isolated Capacitor An isolated capacitor (Q fixed at 2 nC) initially has C₀ = 5 pF and V₀ = Q/C₀ = 400 V. A dielectric with κ = 4 is inserted. New capacitance: C = κC₀ = 20 pF. New voltage: V = Q/C = (2×10⁻⁹)/(20×10⁻¹²) = 100 V — exactly V₀/κ = 400/4, as expected with Q held fixed.

1. A dielectric with κ = 3 is inserted into an isolated capacitor (battery disconnected first, Q fixed). The voltage:

With Q fixed, C rises to 3C₀, so V = Q/C falls to V₀/3.

2. The same κ = 3 dielectric is instead inserted while the capacitor stays connected to its battery (V fixed). The charge:

With V fixed by the battery, C rises to 3C₀, so Q = CV rises to 3Q₀ — the battery supplies the extra charge.

3. Fill in the blank: a capacitor with C₀ = 5 pF has a κ = 4 dielectric inserted. Its new capacitance is ___ pF.

C = κC₀ = 4(5 pF) = 20 pF — capacitance always rises by the factor κ, regardless of which scenario you're in.

4. Microscopically, why does inserting a dielectric into an isolated (Q fixed) capacitor reduce the field E between the plates?

Polarization creates bound charge at the dielectric's surfaces, oriented to oppose the free charges' field — the net field inside is reduced, but not eliminated (κ is finite).
