1. A charge of 12 C flows past a point in a wire over 4 s. What is the average current?

I = ΔQ/Δt = 12/4 = 3 A.

2. A copper wire (n ≈ 8.5×10²⁸ /m³) carries I = 1 A through A = 1×10⁻⁶ m² (q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C). What is the drift velocity?

v_d = I/(nqA) = 1/[(8.5×10²⁸)(1.6×10⁻¹⁹)(1×10⁻⁶)] = 1/(1.36×10⁴) ≈ 7.35×10⁻⁵ m/s.

3. An aluminum wire (ρ = 2.65×10⁻⁸ Ω·m) is 3 m long with cross-sectional area 5×10⁻⁷ m². What is its resistance?

R = ρL/A = (2.65×10⁻⁸)(3)/(5×10⁻⁷) = 7.95×10⁻⁸/5×10⁻⁷ ≈ 0.159 Ω.

4. Two wires are made of the same metal but have different lengths and different thicknesses. Which quantity must still be identical between them?

Resistivity is an intrinsic material property, unaffected by shape — resistance R = ρL/A differs between the wires since L and A differ.

5. Two resistors, 10 Ω and 15 Ω, are connected in parallel. What is the equivalent resistance?

1/R_eq = 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6, so R_eq = 6 Ω.

6. A wire carries I = 4 A through a cross-sectional area of 2×10⁻⁶ m². Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the current density is ___ A/m².

(b) If the same current instead flowed through a wire with twice the cross-sectional area, the current density would:

(a) J = I/A = 4/(2×10⁻⁶) = 2×10⁶ A/m². (b) J is inversely proportional to A at fixed I, so doubling A halves J.

7. A copper wire (n ≈ 8.5×10²⁸ /m³) carries I = 3 A through A = 2×10⁻⁶ m² (q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the drift velocity is approximately ___ ×10⁻⁴ m/s (enter just the coefficient).

(b) Even though this drift velocity is tiny, a switch elsewhere in the circuit takes effect almost instantly because:

(a) v_d = I/(nqA) = 3/[(8.5×10²⁸)(1.6×10⁻¹⁹)(2×10⁻⁶)] = 3/(2.72×10⁴) ≈ 1.10×10⁻⁴ m/s. (b) No single electron needs to traverse the wire — the field's near-light-speed propagation gets every electron moving together almost immediately.

8. A copper wire (ρ = 1.68×10⁻⁸ Ω·m) is 4 m long with cross-sectional area 8×10⁻⁷ m². Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: its resistance is ___ Ω.

(b) If this wire's length were instead doubled (area unchanged), its resistance would:

(a) R = ρL/A = (1.68×10⁻⁸)(4)/(8×10⁻⁷) = 6.72×10⁻⁸/8×10⁻⁷ = 0.084 Ω. (b) R is directly proportional to length, so doubling L doubles R.

9. Two resistors, 5 Ω and 20 Ω, are connected in parallel. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the equivalent resistance is ___ Ω.

(b) If these same two component values were capacitors instead of resistors, wired in parallel, the combination rule would be:

(a) 1/R_eq = 1/5 + 1/20 = 4/20 + 1/20 = 5/20 = 1/4, so R_eq = 4 Ω. (b) Capacitors and resistors combine oppositely: parallel resistors add reciprocally, but parallel capacitors add directly (it's series capacitors that add reciprocally).

10. A 2 Ω resistor is in series with a parallel combination of 6 Ω and 3 Ω. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the total equivalent resistance of this network is ___ Ω.

(b) If a 12 V battery drives this combination, the total current supplied by the battery is:

(a) The parallel pair reduces to 1/(1/6 + 1/3) = 1/(1/6+2/6) = 1/(1/2) = 2 Ω; adding the series 2 Ω resistor gives R_eq = 2 + 2 = 4 Ω. (b) I = V/R_eq = 12/4 = 3 A.
