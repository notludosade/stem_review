1. A charge of 20 C flows past a point in a wire over 5 s. What is the average current?

I = ΔQ/Δt = 20/5 = 4 A.

2. A copper wire (n ≈ 8.5×10²⁸ /m³) carries I = 2 A through A = 1×10⁻⁶ m² (q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C). What is the drift velocity?

v_d = I/(nqA) = 2/[(8.5×10²⁸)(1.6×10⁻¹⁹)(1×10⁻⁶)] = 2/(1.36×10⁴) ≈ 1.47×10⁻⁴ m/s.

3. A copper wire (ρ = 1.68×10⁻⁸ Ω·m) is 5 m long with cross-sectional area 4×10⁻⁷ m². What is its resistance?

R = ρL/A = (1.68×10⁻⁸)(5)/(4×10⁻⁷) = 8.4×10⁻⁸/4×10⁻⁷ ≈ 0.21 Ω.

4. Two wires, made of different metals, happen to have exactly the same length and the same cross-sectional area. Which quantity is most likely to differ between them?

Resistivity is set by the material itself; two different metals generally have different ρ even with identical geometry, so R = ρL/A differs too.

5. Two resistors, 12 Ω and 4 Ω, are connected in parallel. What is the equivalent resistance?

1/R_eq = 1/12 + 1/4 = 1/12 + 3/12 = 4/12 = 1/3, so R_eq = 3 Ω.

6. A wire carries I = 6 A through a cross-sectional area of 3×10⁻⁶ m². Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the current density is ___ A/m².

(b) If the same current instead flowed through a wire with three times the cross-sectional area, the current density would:

(a) J = I/A = 6/(3×10⁻⁶) = 2×10⁶ A/m². (b) J is inversely proportional to A at fixed I, so tripling A cuts J to a third.

7. A copper wire (n ≈ 8.5×10²⁸ /m³) carries I = 2 A through A = 2×10⁻⁶ m² (q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the drift velocity is approximately ___ ×10⁻⁵ m/s (enter just the coefficient).

(b) Even though this drift velocity is tiny, a switch elsewhere in the circuit takes effect almost instantly because:

(a) v_d = I/(nqA) = 2/[(8.5×10²⁸)(1.6×10⁻¹⁹)(2×10⁻⁶)] = 2/(2.72×10⁴) ≈ 7.35×10⁻⁵ m/s. (b) No single electron needs to traverse the wire — the field's near-light-speed propagation gets every electron moving together almost immediately.

8. An aluminum wire (ρ = 2.65×10⁻⁸ Ω·m) is 2 m long with cross-sectional area 1×10⁻⁷ m². Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: its resistance is ___ Ω.

(b) If this wire's length were instead tripled (area unchanged), its resistance would:

(a) R = ρL/A = (2.65×10⁻⁸)(2)/(1×10⁻⁷) = 5.3×10⁻⁸/1×10⁻⁷ = 0.53 Ω. (b) R is directly proportional to length, so tripling L triples R.

9. Two resistors, 10 Ω and 40 Ω, are connected in parallel. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the equivalent resistance is ___ Ω.

(b) If these same two component values were capacitors instead of resistors, wired in parallel, the combination rule would be:

(a) 1/R_eq = 1/10 + 1/40 = 4/40 + 1/40 = 5/40 = 1/8, so R_eq = 8 Ω. (b) Capacitors and resistors combine oppositely: parallel resistors add reciprocally, but parallel capacitors add directly (it's series capacitors that add reciprocally).

10. A 3 Ω resistor is in series with a parallel combination of 8 Ω and 8 Ω. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the total equivalent resistance of this network is ___ Ω.

(b) If a 14 V battery drives this combination, the total current supplied by the battery is:

(a) The parallel pair reduces to 1/(1/8 + 1/8) = 4 Ω; adding the series 3 Ω resistor gives R_eq = 3 + 4 = 7 Ω. (b) I = V/R_eq = 14/7 = 2 A.
