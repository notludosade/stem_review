1. A positive charge moves in the +y direction through a field B pointing in the +x direction. Using F = qv × B, the force on the charge points:

v × B = ŷ × x̂ = −ẑ (reversing the order of x̂ × ŷ = ẑ flips the sign), and q is positive, so F points in the −z direction.

2. A charged particle's speed is tripled while its charge, mass, and the field strength B are all held fixed. What happens to its orbit period T?

T = 2πm/(qB) has no v in it — a faster particle traces a proportionally larger circle (r = mv/(qB) does scale with v) but completes it in exactly the same time.

3. A straight current-carrying wire sits in a uniform magnetic field, oriented perpendicular to B (θ = 90°). Compared to that same wire oriented parallel to B (θ = 0°), the force is:

F = BIL sinθ: sin 90° = 1 gives the maximum force, while sin 0° = 0 gives zero force — perpendicular orientation always produces the strongest force on a current-carrying wire.

4. A current loop sits with its normal vector parallel to B (θ = 0°, its stable equilibrium). If left alone with no other torques acting on it, the loop will:

θ = 0° gives sinθ = 0, so torque vanishes — this is exactly why a real DC motor needs a commutator to flip the current before the loop settles into this equilibrium and stops.

5. The Biot-Savart law, dB = (μ₀/4π)(I dl × r̂)/r², is the magnetic-field analog of which electrostatics tool?

Both laws build up a field by integrating tiny contributions from source elements — dq for the electric field, I dl for the magnetic field — over the full source; Ampère's law, not Biot-Savart, is the one analogous to Gauss's law.

6. A charge q = 2×10⁻⁶ C moves at v = 5×10⁵ m/s perpendicular to a field B = 0.4 T. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the force on the charge is F = ___ N.

(b) If instead v points in +y and B points in +x, and the charge is positive, the force points:

(a) F = qvB sinθ = (2×10⁻⁶)(5×10⁵)(0.4)(sin 90°) = 0.4 N. (b) ŷ × x̂ = −ẑ, and q is positive, so F points in −z.

7. A proton (q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C, m = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg) moves at v = 2×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to B = 0.25 T. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the orbit radius is r ≈ ___ cm (2 decimal places).

(b) If v were instead doubled (B, q, m unchanged), the radius would:

(a) r = mv/(qB) = (1.67×10⁻²⁷)(2×10⁶)/[(1.6×10⁻¹⁹)(0.25)] ≈ 0.0835 m ≈ 8.35 cm. (b) r = mv/(qB) is directly proportional to v, so doubling v doubles r.

8. A wire segment L = 0.25 m carries I = 10 A perpendicular to B = 0.6 T. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the force on the wire is F = ___ N.

(b) For a single straight current-carrying wire (not a closed loop) in a uniform field, what determines whether the force on it is maximum, partial, or zero?

(a) F = BIL sinθ = (0.6)(10)(0.25)(sin 90°) = 1.5 N. (b) F = BIL sinθ depends entirely on the angle θ between the current and B, unlike a closed loop where the net force is always zero regardless of orientation.

9. A long straight wire carries I = 20 A. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: at r = 0.04 m, the field is B ≈ ___ ×10⁻⁴ T (enter just the coefficient).

(b) If only the current were doubled (distance from the wire unchanged), B would:

(a) B = μ₀I/(2πr) = (4π×10⁻⁷)(20)/[2π(0.04)] = 1.0×10⁻⁴ T. (b) B ∝ I at fixed r, so doubling I doubles B.

10. An ideal solenoid has n = 2500 turns/m carrying I = 2 A. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the interior field is B ≈ ___ ×10⁻³ T (2 decimal places).

(b) Outside an ideal long solenoid, the magnetic field is:

(a) B = μ₀nI = (4π×10⁻⁷)(2500)(2) ≈ 6.28×10⁻³ T. (b) The Amperian-loop derivation of B = μ₀nI relies on the field outside an ideal long solenoid being negligible — that's what let the outside contribution drop out of the loop integral.
