1. Spring steel flexes considerably under load and returns to shape, without yielding, over a wide range. Which two properties does this combination best describe?

Spring steel is deliberately both strong and stiff enough to be useful, engineered to flex elastically over a large range without exceeding its yield point.

2. Fill in the blank: strength divided by density is called ___, and is usually the more meaningful comparison whenever a design is weight-limited.

Specific strength normalizes raw strength by density, which is what actually matters for a weight-constrained design.

3. What typical range is a draft angle for an injection-molded wall?

A slight taper of about 1° to 3° per side is typically enough to let a part release from its mold without sticking.

4. Fill in the blank: factor of safety is defined as yield strength divided by the actual expected ___ the part experiences.

FS = yield strength / expected stress.

5. What is one common way to handle a genuine undercut in a molded part, besides redesigning the feature away?

A side-action or lifter lets the mold reach an undercut that a simple straight pull can't — at the cost of a more complex, more expensive mold.

6. A bracket tab carries 120N over a cross-section 4mm × 2mm (8mm²), made from ABS with 40 MPa yield strength. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the stress is σ = F/A = ___ MPa.

(b) Fill in the blank: the factor of safety is FS = yield / stress ≈ ___ (2 decimal places).

(a) σ = 120N / (8×10⁻⁶ m²) = 15×10⁶ Pa = 15 MPa. (b) FS = 40 / 15 ≈ 2.67.

7. A designer is choosing between PLA and ABS for a bracket that will sit in a hot car interior. Answer both parts:

(a) Which material is generally more heat-resistant, at the cost of warping more during printing?

(b) Fill in the blank: ABS's greater tendency to shrink unevenly while cooling makes it more prone to ___ during printing than PLA.

(a) ABS is more heat-resistant but more prone to warping and often needs an enclosed printer. (b) Uneven cooling shrinkage is the direct cause of warping.

8. A printed part's strength is derated to 70% of PLA's rated 60 MPa bulk yield strength before computing FS. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the derated effective yield strength is ___ MPa.

(b) Why is this derating specifically relevant to printed parts, rather than to bulk-cast or machined material?

(a) 0.70 × 60 = 42 MPa. (b) Layer adhesion and infill specifically affect printed parts, which is why the derating logic is tied to the printing process itself.

9. An FDM-printed part's thin wall keeps failing to print reliably. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the most likely cause?

(b) Fill in the blank: minimum wall thickness is ___ — it isn't one fixed number across every manufacturing method.

(a) Walls thinner than a process's minimum reliable thickness tend to warp, break, or fail to form. (b) The minimum depends on the specific process and material, not a single universal figure.

10. A designer computes FS = 1.4 for a printed jig after appropriate derating. Answer both parts:

(a) How does this compare to the common rule-of-thumb range for non-critical prototyping parts?

(b) Fill in the blank: increasing the part's ___ directly reduces stress for a given force, raising the factor of safety.

(a) 1.4 falls below the common 2–4 rule of thumb for non-critical parts. (b) Since σ = F/A, increasing cross-sectional area directly lowers stress for the same force, raising FS.
