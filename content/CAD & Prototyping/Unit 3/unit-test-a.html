1. A dimension is written 18.00 ± 0.06mm. What is the upper limit?

Upper limit = nominal + tolerance = 18.00 + 0.06 = 18.06mm.

2. Fill in the blank: three dimensions stack end to end: 12.00 ± 0.03mm, 18.00 ± 0.04mm, and 5.00 ± 0.01mm. The worst-case total tolerance is ___ mm (2 decimal places).

Worst-case stack-up sums the individual tolerances: 0.03 + 0.04 + 0.01 = 0.08mm.

3. A hole ranges from 15.00mm to 15.02mm. A shaft ranges from 15.05mm to 15.07mm. What type of fit is this?

Since the shaft is always larger than the hole (15.05mm minimum vs. 15.02mm maximum), every valid pair is an interference fit.

4. Fill in the blank: a theoretically exact reference (a plane, axis, or point), established from a real feature on a part, that other GD&T tolerances are measured from, is called a ___.

Datums give GD&T tolerances a fixed, unambiguous origin to measure from.

5. Which GD&T control specifies how far a hole's axis may deviate from its true intended location?

Position tolerancing controls a feature's location relative to datums, typically as a cylindrical Ø tolerance zone.

6. Two dimensions stack end to end: 12.00 ± 0.03mm and 18.00 ± 0.04mm. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the worst-case total tolerance is ___ mm.

(b) Why is the RSS (statistical) stack-up estimate for this chain smaller than the worst-case value?

(a) 0.03 + 0.04 = 0.07mm worst-case. (b) RSS = √(0.03² + 0.04²) = √0.0025 = 0.05mm, smaller because it reflects the low probability of both extremes coinciding.

7. A design needs a shaft that spins freely inside a bushing with zero risk of binding. Answer both parts:

(a) Which fit type guarantees this?

(b) Fill in the blank: if an interference fit were mistakenly used instead, the shaft would ___ rather than spin.

(a) Only a clearance fit guarantees the gap a freely spinning shaft requires. (b) An interference fit would grip the shaft by friction, causing it to seize instead of spin.

8. A hole is located with ±0.06mm in both x and y instead of true position tolerancing. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the maximum deviation allowed along the diagonal direction is √(0.06² + 0.06²) ≈ ___ mm (2 decimal places).

(b) Why is this considered a flaw compared to a true position tolerance of the equivalent size?

(a) √(0.06² + 0.06²) = √0.0072 ≈ 0.085mm. (b) That's about 41% more than the 0.06mm allowed along either axis — a true position tolerance gives a uniform allowance in every direction instead.

9. Three toleranced parts are stacked inside a housing that only has 0.06mm of spare clearance for a moving mechanism, based on nominal dimensions alone. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is checking only nominal dimensions insufficient here?

(b) Fill in the blank: the accumulation of individual part tolerances across a chain of dimensions is called tolerance ___.

(a) Nominal-only checks miss the real worst-case variation that stack-up accounts for. (b) This accumulation is tolerance stack-up.

10. A designer must ensure a mounting hole's axis stays within a fixed distance of its true position, regardless of which direction it drifts. Answer both parts:

(a) Which approach gives a uniform allowance in every direction?

(b) Fill in the blank: a diameter-based position tolerance defines a ___ tolerance zone for the hole's axis.

(a) True position tolerancing defines a round (cylindrical) zone, giving uniform allowance in every direction, unlike a square ± x/± y zone. (b) The zone is cylindrical.
