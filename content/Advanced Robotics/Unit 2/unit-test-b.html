1. A sensor that measures distance to a nearby wall, as opposed to the robot's own joint angle, is:

Exteroceptive sensors measure the external environment, such as distance to a wall; proprioceptive sensors measure the robot's own internal state.

2. Fill in the blank: the number of encoder ticks corresponding to one full wheel revolution is called the encoder's ___.

Resolution describes how finely the encoder can distinguish partial rotations.

3. Answer both parts about a wheel encoder:

(a) A wheel with 0.2 m circumference has an encoder with 400 ticks/revolution. The encoder counts 800 ticks. What distance is estimated?

(b) Fill in the blank: because of possible wheel slip, the encoder's estimate is how far the wheel turned, not necessarily how far the robot actually ___.

(a) Revolutions = 800/400 = 2; distance = 2 × 0.2 m = 0.4 m. (b) A slipping wheel can turn without the robot actually moving that far.

4. What quantity does LIDAR use to convert a laser pulse's round-trip travel time into a distance?

Time-of-flight distance is computed using the speed of light, since that's the speed at which the laser pulse travels.

5. Why might a LIDAR beam pointed at a clean glass window fail to produce a useful return?

Transparent surfaces like glass can let the laser pulse pass through with no clean return, another known LIDAR weakness alongside reflective surfaces.

6. Answer both parts about cameras:

(a) A monocular camera sees a 30 cm box that could be 2 m away, or a 15 cm box that could be 1 m away, both projecting identically. What resolves this ambiguity?

(b) Fill in the blank: a stereo camera resolves this by comparing a point's shift between two images, called the ___.

(a) A single 2D projection cannot distinguish scale from distance without additional information (like a second viewpoint). (b) Disparity between two camera views is what recovers true depth geometrically.

7. Fill in the blank: 'not visible in this camera frame' does not mean 'not present in the scene' — this hiding effect is called ___.

Occlusion means an object is blocked from view by another object, not that it's absent from the scene.

8. A distance sensor's readings for a fixed 3 m target scatter randomly between 2.90 m and 3.10 m across repeated trials, averaging out close to 3.00 m. This scatter is an example of:

Unpredictable scatter around the true value, which partly cancels when averaged, is the definition of random noise rather than bias.

9. What is the correct way to fix a sensor with significant bias (a consistent offset), as opposed to a sensor with high noise?

Averaging reduces noise but does nothing for bias; a consistent offset requires calibration against a known reference to correct.

10. Fill in the blank: the standard mathematical tool for combining multiple sensors by weighing each one's contribution based on trust is called a ___ filter.

The Kalman filter (and its variants) is the standard formal tool for sensor fusion.
