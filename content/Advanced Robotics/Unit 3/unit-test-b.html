1. Why can't GPS alone fully solve the localization problem for most robots?

GPS's meter-scale accuracy and unavailability indoors or in urban canyons means most robots must build their own pose estimate from onboard sensors.

2. Fill in the blank: dead reckoning estimates position from accumulated motion alone, without reference to external ___.

Dead reckoning builds a position estimate purely from accumulated motion, with no external landmark reference.

3. Answer both parts about odometry:

(a) A robot at (0,0,0°) moves 0.5 m forward with no turn in one time step. What is its new estimated x-position?

(b) Fill in the blank: because odometry is built purely from accumulated motion, it naturally defines its own local ___ frame, anchored where tracking began.

(a) With no turn, the full 0.5 m adds directly to x: 0 + 0.5 = 0.5 m. (b) The odom frame is anchored to the robot's starting point, not any fixed external landmark.

4. A robot's heading estimate is off by a small constant angle. What kind of error does this produce over a long, straight drive?

A constant heading error points every increment of motion slightly off course, producing sideways drift that grows roughly with distance covered.

5. What can reduce, but not eliminate, dead-reckoning drift?

A higher-resolution encoder reduces the size of each per-step error, slowing drift, but cannot provide the independent fixed reference needed to eliminate it.

6. Fill in the blank: a representation of the environment as a grid of cells, each estimating obstacle probability, is called an ___.

Occupancy grid is the standard term for this cell-based environment representation.

7. Why is treating unexplored space as 'assumed free' considered a dangerous design choice for an occupancy grid?

Treating unsensed space as safe by default risks planning through areas that could contain unseen obstacles — a real safety concern.

8. Answer both parts about SLAM:

(a) Why does SLAM treat mapping and localization as continuously correcting each other, rather than solving one first?

(b) Fill in the blank: SLAM stands for ___ Localization And Mapping.

(a) Uncorrected drift while mapping would record the same real feature inconsistently across different drifted viewpoints. (b) SLAM = Simultaneous Localization And Mapping.

9. Why does confirming a loop closure typically adjust the entire chain of poses since the loop began, not just the current position?

Distributing the correction across the whole loop keeps the map internally consistent, which is why one loop closure can straighten an entire drifted section of map.

10. Fill in the blank: a loop closure that incorrectly matches two genuinely different real locations, risking map corruption, is called a ___ (two words, or fill just the key adjective).

A false loop closure merges two genuinely different places, which is why candidate matches must be weighed carefully rather than accepted automatically.
