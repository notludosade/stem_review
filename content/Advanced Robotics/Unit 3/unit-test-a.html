1. How does localization differ from the forward kinematics of Unit 1?

Localization estimates a robot's pose in the world using inherently noisy sensor data, unlike the trusted, direct computation of forward kinematics.

2. Fill in the blank: estimating current position by accumulating small measured motions from a known start, with no external landmarks, is called ___.

Dead reckoning builds position estimates purely from accumulated motion.

3. Answer both parts about odometry:

(a) A robot at (0,0,0°) moves 0.3 m forward with no turn in one time step. What is its new estimated x-position?

(b) Fill in the blank: odometry is dead reckoning built specifically from a wheeled robot's ___ readings.

(a) With no turn, the full 0.3 m adds directly to x: 0 + 0.3 = 0.3 m. (b) Odometry specifically uses wheel encoder data.

4. Why does dead-reckoning error compound over a long drive instead of averaging out like repeated measurements of a fixed quantity?

Unlike repeated measurements of one fixed quantity, dead reckoning measures a different increment each step and builds directly on prior error, so errors compound rather than cancel.

5. Fill in the blank: dead-reckoning position drift generally grows roughly in proportion to ___ traveled, not time elapsed alone.

Since each increment of motion carries its own small error, more distance covered means more accumulated error.

6. In an occupancy grid, what does a cell marked 'unknown' mean?

Unknown is a genuine third state representing missing sensor evidence, distinct from confirmed occupancy or confirmed emptiness.

7. How does a single LIDAR ray typically update cells in an occupancy grid?

A LIDAR ray that traveled some distance before returning implies open space along its path and a likely obstacle at the return point.

8. Answer both parts about SLAM:

(a) What problem does SLAM solve that a pre-built map plus dead reckoning alone cannot?

(b) Fill in the blank: SLAM stands for Simultaneous Localization And ___.

(a) SLAM addresses the chicken-and-egg problem of needing a map to localize and needing localization to build an accurate map. (b) SLAM = Simultaneous Localization And Mapping.

9. Fill in the blank: a detected match between a current sensor reading and a previously recorded observation, indicating a revisited location, is called a ___.

Loop closure is the recognition event that gives SLAM an independent reference to correct accumulated drift.

10. What is the risk posed by a false loop closure?

Matching two different real places as if they were the same location can distort the map, which is why candidate loop closures must be weighed carefully.
