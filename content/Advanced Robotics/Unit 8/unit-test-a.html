1. What is an autonomy stack?

The autonomy stack is the layered architecture connecting every capability this course covered, not any single algorithm alone.

2. Fill in the blank: a fault in an early stack layer, like bad localization, does not stay contained — it ___ through planning and control downstream.

Each layer depends on the layers below it, so an early-stage fault propagates through the whole stack rather than staying isolated.

3. Answer both parts about warehouse robot fleets:

(a) What genuinely new problem does coordinating a fleet of many warehouse robots introduce beyond single-robot planning?

(b) Fill in the blank: a situation where robots' individually reasonable plans mutually block each other is called a ___.

(a) Individually correct plans can still conflict without coordination above single-robot planning. (b) Deadlock is the standard term for this mutual-blocking failure.

4. Why is camera-based vSLAM common in consumer robot vacuums specifically?

Cost constraints, not raw technical superiority, drive the vSLAM choice in this product category — the same core SLAM algorithm as elsewhere.

5. Fill in the blank: alongside LIDAR and cameras, self-driving perception stacks commonly add ___, which works well in poor weather and directly measures relative velocity.

Radar complements LIDAR and cameras by working well in poor weather and directly measuring relative velocity, covering different failure modes.

6. Answer both parts about self-driving perception:

(a) Why does a road environment need more than Unit 3's plain occupancy grid?

(b) Fill in the blank: following a detected object's motion over time to predict its future position is called object ___.

(a) Predicting pedestrian or cyclist motion requires classifying and tracking specific objects. (b) Object tracking, combined with detection, enables anticipating future positions.

7. What is a seam failure?

A seam failure occurs at the boundary between components, each individually correct, while their combination produces a wrong outcome.

8. Why is testing each capability in isolation necessary but not sufficient for catching seam failures?

Seam failures arise from mismatched assumptions between components, which per-component testing alone cannot reveal.

9. What was this course's capstone ultimately arguing is the real skill built across all 8 units?

No single unit's material alone solves a real task — the capstone's point is that integration across every unit is the actual skill this course built toward.

10. Why does the capstone's warehouse robot need SLAM rather than localization against a pre-existing map?

Without a pre-existing map, localization alone has nothing to reference — SLAM builds the map and tracks position simultaneously.
