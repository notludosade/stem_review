Unit 5 Test — Version B

Motion Control. 10 questions covering every lesson in this unit. Score 80% or higher to clear this unit.

1. Why is motion control described as sitting between planning and low-level control-loop hardware?

Motion control takes planning's output (a path) and produces the commands the underlying motor control loops execute.

2. What does a trajectory specify that a plain path does not?

A path is a sequence of positions with no schedule; a trajectory adds timing, which motion control actually needs to execute the path.

3. Answer both parts about trajectory generation:

(a) A robot ramps from 0 to 1 m/s at constant acceleration, taking 2 s. Roughly what shape does its velocity-vs-time graph trace during ramp-up, cruise, and ramp-down?

(b) Fill in the blank: trajectory generation typically respects limits on both maximum velocity and maximum ___.

(a) Ramp up, cruise, ramp down produces a trapezoidal shape when velocity is plotted against time. (b) Both velocity and acceleration limits shape a physically achievable trajectory.

4. Why does pure pursuit avoid steering directly at the next fixed waypoint?

A moving lookahead target produces smoother, more stable steering than repeatedly aiming at fixed points along the path.

5. What is the trade-off in choosing a shorter vs. longer lookahead distance in pure pursuit?

Lookahead distance trades path-tracking tightness against steering smoothness.

6. Fill in the blank: a feedback controller commands a correction sized ___ to the measured tracking error, not a fixed correction regardless of error size.

Proportional correction — sizing the response to the size of the error — is the core feedback-control idea applied to path tracking.

7. Answer both parts about tracking error:

(a) A trajectory says the robot should be at (5.0, 2.0) m; localization reports actual position (5.0, 2.2) m. What is the cross-track error magnitude?

(b) Fill in the blank: this error is fed into a feedback controller to command a ___ back toward the trajectory.

(a) The deviation is |2.2 − 2.0| = 0.2 m. (b) Feedback control uses the measured error to command a proportional correction.

8. Fill in the blank: one real-world disturbance that geometric steering alone (like pure pursuit) doesn't directly notice, but feedback correction can catch, is ___.

Wheel slip is a physical disturbance that pure geometric steering doesn't measure directly, but feedback correction responds to via the resulting tracking error.

9. Fill in the blank: modifying a planned path to reduce sharp corners while still respecting obstacles is called trajectory ___.

Trajectory smoothing reshapes a path for gentler, more executable motion without abandoning the obstacle clearance the original path established.

10. Why is trajectory smoothing described as a refinement layered on top of a validated path, rather than a replacement for planning?

Smoothing reshapes geometry near corners, which risks violating obstacle clearance if not re-checked — it builds on a validated path rather than replacing the planning that made it safe.
