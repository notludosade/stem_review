1. A serial robot arm has 5 independently actuated revolute joints. How many degrees of freedom does it have?

Each independently actuated joint contributes exactly 1 DOF.

2. Fill in the blank: for a given set of joint values, forward kinematics always produces exactly ___ end-effector pose.

Forward kinematics is a direct computation with no ambiguity.

3. Why can inverse kinematics have zero, one, or many solutions, unlike forward kinematics?

Running the forward-kinematics relationship backward means solving nonlinear equations, which can yield zero, one, or many valid solutions.

4. Fill in the blank: a sensor measuring distance to an external obstacle, rather than the robot's own internal state, is called ___.

Exteroceptive sensors measure the external environment; proprioceptive sensors measure the robot's own state.

5. How does LIDAR measure distance to a surface?

LIDAR uses time-of-flight: the round-trip time of a laser pulse, converted to distance via the speed of light.

6. Why are complementary sensors generally more powerful to combine than two identical sensors?

Complementary sensors (like a fast-but-drifting encoder and a slower but stable fix) cover each other's specific weaknesses.

7. Fill in the blank: dead-reckoning position drift generally grows roughly in proportion to ___ traveled, not time elapsed alone.

Since each increment of motion carries its own small error, more distance covered means more accumulated error.

8. In an occupancy grid, what does a cell marked 'unknown' represent?

Unknown is a genuine third state representing missing sensor evidence.

9. Fill in the blank: a detected match between a current sensor reading and a previously recorded observation, letting SLAM correct accumulated drift, is called a ___.

Loop closure gives SLAM an independent reference to correct accumulated drift across the whole loop just completed.

10. Why does an obstacle appear larger in configuration space than in the physical world?

Inflating obstacles by the robot's own size lets planning treat the robot as a point while respecting its true extent.

11. Fill in the blank: for A* to guarantee the true lowest-cost path, its heuristic must be ___ — it must never overestimate the true remaining cost.

An admissible heuristic never overestimates, which guarantees A* finds the optimal path.

12. Why is running both global and local planning together standard practice?

Global planning sets an informed route; local planning reacts to what the map couldn't have known — each covers the other's weakness.

13. Fill in the blank: the key thing a trajectory adds that a plain path lacks is ___.

A path is just positions; a trajectory adds a schedule of when the robot should be at each position.

14. In the pure pursuit steering strategy, what does the robot continuously steer toward?

Pure pursuit aims at a point that advances along the path a fixed distance ahead, producing smoother motion than aiming at fixed waypoints.

15. Fill in the blank: the perpendicular deviation between a robot's actual position and its intended position on the trajectory is called ___.

Cross-track error is the standard term for this deviation, which feedback control uses to compute a correction.

16. At any given moment, how many states is a robot's FSM in?

An FSM's robot is always in exactly one named state at a time.

17. Fill in the blank: a behavior tree node that succeeds only if all its children succeed, in order, is called a ___ node.

A Sequence node fails immediately if any child fails, without running the remaining children.

18. Why is purely deliberative control, by itself, insufficient for handling a sudden obstacle?

Building and reasoning over a plan takes time that a sudden hazard doesn't allow for.

19. Why can grasping an object's geometric center fail for an item like a handle-heavy mug?

Grasp planning must reason about actual mass distribution, not just outline shape.

20. Fill in the blank: an object weighing 8 N is gripped with a coefficient of friction μ = 0.8. The minimum normal (grip) force needed to support it is ___ N.

Required normal force ≈ weight / μ = 8 / 0.8 = 10 N.

21. What are the four stages of a manipulation pipeline, in order?

The pipeline runs: perceive the object, plan a grasp and approach, execute the grasp, then place the object.

22. Fill in the blank: the full software architecture connecting a robot's perception, localization, planning, and control into one running system is called the autonomy ___.

Autonomy stack is the standard term for this layered architecture.

23. What is a seam failure?

A seam failure occurs at the boundary between components, each individually correct, while their combination produces a wrong outcome.

24. What genuinely new problem does coordinating a fleet of many warehouse robots introduce beyond single-robot path planning?

Individually correct plans can still conflict without coordination above single-robot planning, risking deadlocks.
