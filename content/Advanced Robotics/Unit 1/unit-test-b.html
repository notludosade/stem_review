1. A conveyor belt moves packages but has no sensing or decision-making loop. Why does this lesson's definition exclude it from being a 'robot' in the fuller sense?

Without sensing feeding into a decision loop, the belt can't adapt to what it detects — the defining gap between a robot and simple automated machinery.

2. Fill in the blank: a series of rigid links connected by movable joints, from base to end-effector, is called a kinematic ___.

Kinematic chain is the standard term for this base-to-tip series of links and joints.

3. A serial robot arm has 6 independently actuated joints (5 revolute, 1 prismatic) and no other moving parts. How many degrees of freedom does it have?

Every independently actuated joint — revolute or prismatic — contributes exactly 1 DOF, so 6 joints give 6 DOF total.

4. Answer both parts about degrees of freedom:

(a) A human arm has roughly 7 DOF, one more than the 6 needed for full pose control at the hand. What does this let you do?

(b) Fill in the blank: a joint value set beyond the minimum needed for full pose control adds ___ to the arm.

(a) The extra DOF allows many joint configurations for the same hand pose, so the elbow can move independently. (b) This extra freedom is called redundancy.

5. Fill in the blank: knowing a robot gripper's position alone does not tell you its full pose — you also need its ___.

Pose combines position and orientation; position alone leaves orientation unknown.

6. A LIDAR sensor reports 'obstacle at 2 m, straight ahead.' Before this is useful for planning a path to a world-coordinate destination, what must happen?

A sensor reading is naturally expressed in the sensor's own frame and must be converted into the world frame, combined with the robot's own pose, before it's usable for world-frame planning.

7. Answer both parts about forward kinematics:

(a) For a 2-link planar arm with L1 = 0.5 m, L2 = 0.5 m, θ1 = 90°, θ2 = 0°, what is the end-effector's y-coordinate?

(b) Fill in the blank: forward kinematics is called 'forward' because it goes from joint values to ___.

(a) y = L1 sin(90°) + L2 sin(90°+0°) = 0.5(1) + 0.5(1) = 1.0 m. (b) It moves in the chain's natural base-to-tip direction, ending at the end-effector's pose.

8. Fill in the blank: for a 2-link planar arm with L1 = 0.6 m and L2 = 0.4 m, the workspace's outer radius is ___ m.

Outer radius = L1 + L2 = 0.6 + 0.4 = 1.0 m.

9. If a target point's distance from the base exceeds L1 + L2 for a 2-link planar arm, what does this mean?

A target beyond L1 + L2 lies outside the arm's maximum reach — geometrically unreachable, so IK has zero solutions.

10. Fill in the blank: the geometric worked example for 2-link inverse kinematics in this unit used the law of ___ to find the elbow angle.

The law of cosines relates the triangle formed by the base, elbow, and target, giving the elbow angle directly from the link lengths and target distance.
