1. Which of these best distinguishes a robot from a simple automated machine like a wind-up toy?

The defining feature is the closed sense-decide-act loop, not the specific hardware used.

2. Fill in the blank: the working tip of a robot's kinematic chain — a gripper, a torch, a nozzle — is called the ___.

The end-effector is the working tip of the kinematic chain, at the far end from the fixed base.

3. A serial robot arm has 4 independently actuated revolute joints and no other moving parts. How many degrees of freedom does it have?

Each independently actuated joint contributes exactly 1 DOF, so 4 joints give 4 DOF.

4. Answer both parts about degrees of freedom:

(a) A 6-DOF arm can reach any position and orientation within its workspace. What does an added 7th DOF provide?

(b) Fill in the blank: this extra freedom is called ___ (the property, not a joint type).

(a) A redundant DOF lets the arm reach the same pose multiple ways, useful for obstacle avoidance or comfort. (b) That property is called redundancy.

5. Fill in the blank: a rigid body's position together with its orientation is called its ___.

Pose is the standard term combining both position and orientation.

6. Why do robots typically use several coordinate frames (world, body, sensor) instead of just one?

A sensor reading is simplest in the sensor frame; a destination is simplest in the world frame — robots convert between frames rather than forcing everything into one.

7. Answer both parts about forward kinematics:

(a) For a 2-link planar arm with L1 = 0.4 m, L2 = 0.4 m, θ1 = 0°, θ2 = 90°, what is the end-effector's x-coordinate?

(b) Fill in the blank: forward kinematics always produces exactly ___ solution(s) for a given set of joint values.

(a) x = L1 cos(0°) + L2 cos(90°) = 0.4(1) + 0.4(0) = 0.4 m. (b) Forward kinematics is a direct computation with no ambiguity.

8. Fill in the blank: for a 2-link planar arm with L1 = 0.5 m and L2 = 0.3 m, the workspace's inner radius is ___ m.

Inner radius = |L1 − L2| = |0.5 − 0.3| = 0.2 m.

9. Why can inverse kinematics have zero, one, or many solutions, unlike forward kinematics?

Running the forward-kinematics relationship backward means solving nonlinear trig equations, which can yield zero, one, or many valid solutions.

10. In 2-link planar IK, the 'elbow up' and 'elbow down' configurations for the same target point are an example of:

Both elbow configurations place the end-effector at the identical target — a genuine multiple-solutions case for inverse kinematics.
