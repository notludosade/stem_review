1. How does this unit define manipulation, as distinct from locomotion?

Manipulation targets a change in something external to the robot; locomotion targets the robot's own pose.

2. Fill in the blank: manipulation's deliberate contact introduces forces and ___ that pure locomotion generally doesn't need to reason about.

Contact-based tasks like grasping introduce friction and force reasoning that avoidance-based locomotion doesn't require.

3. Answer both parts about grippers:

(a) What is a two-finger (parallel-jaw) gripper?

(b) Fill in the blank: an end-effector with movable fingers that close around an object is generally called a ___.

(a) Parallel-jaw grippers are the simplest, most common design. (b) Gripper is the general term for this end-effector category.

4. Why can 'grasp the geometric center' fail for an object like a handle-heavy mug?

Grasp planning must reason about actual mass distribution, not just outline shape, to avoid the object rotating out of an otherwise plausible-looking grip.

5. Fill in the blank: real grasp-planning systems generate several candidate grasps and ___ each one on reachability and expected stability before selecting the best.

Scoring candidate grasps lets a system pick the most promising option rather than committing to the first geometrically plausible one.

6. Answer both parts about friction and grasp stability:

(a) An object weighing 8 N is gripped with μ = 0.8. What is the minimum normal (grip) force needed to support it?

(b) Fill in the blank: when a gripper lifts by squeezing sides, the object's weight is held up entirely by ___ at the contact points.

(a) Required normal force ≈ weight / μ = 8 / 0.8 = 10 N. (b) With no support from underneath, friction is the only force resisting the object's weight.

7. How does a suction (vacuum) gripper hold an object?

Suction grippers rely on air pressure difference created by a vacuum pump, not mechanical squeezing.

8. Fill in the blank: a gripper built from flexible materials that passively deform to match an object's shape is called a ___ (or compliant) gripper.

Soft/compliant grippers conform to shape passively, unlike a rigid gripper's precisely positioned fingers.

9. What are the four stages of a manipulation pipeline, in order?

The pipeline runs: perceive the object, plan a grasp and approach, execute the grasp, then place the object at its destination.

10. Why does a perception error early in the manipulation pipeline tend to cause downstream failures?

Errors propagate downstream: bad perception data leads to a grasp plan based on incorrect information, which can then fail in execution.
