Manipulation Pipelines — Perceive, Plan, Grasp, Place

Every piece from this unit, and several from earlier ones, chained into the one sequence a robot actually runs to pick something up and put it down correctly.

← Lesson 40 Unit 8, Lesson 42 → Contents Glossary →

This unit built up the pieces of manipulation one at a time: end-effector hardware, grasp selection, the physics of a secure grip, specialized tools for hard cases. This closing lesson chains them into the sequence a real manipulation task actually executes, start to finish.

Definition A manipulation pipeline is the ordered sequence of steps a robot runs to complete a pick-and-place task: Perceive (sense and identify the object and its pose — Unit 2's cameras and depth sensors), Plan (select a grasp — Lesson 38 — and a reachable approach path — Unit 1's inverse kinematics and Unit 4's planning), Grasp (execute the approach and close the gripper with appropriate force — Lesson 39), and Place (move the held object to its destination and release it in the intended pose).
Why each stage depends on the ones before it succeeding correctly An error in Perceive (a misjudged object pose from Unit 2's sensor noise) feeds a wrong target into Plan, which can select a grasp that looks sound on faulty information but fails against the real object. An error in Plan (an unreachable or unstable grasp choice) causes Grasp to fail even with perfect execution. This is the same "garbage in, garbage out" chain Unit 3 established for localization feeding into planning, now running through one more stage: perception errors propagate through grasp planning into execution, which is exactly why real systems check for success at each stage — verifying a grasp actually succeeded (Lesson 39's grip-force and contact reasoning) before committing to Place, rather than assuming every step worked.
Example, tying nearly the whole course together A warehouse robot's full pick task: Perceive — a camera and depth sensor identify a specific box's shape and pose on the shelf (Unit 2). Plan — grasp planning selects a suction-cup grasp on the box's flat top face (Lesson 38, Lesson 40), and inverse kinematics finds a reachable arm configuration to approach it (Unit 1), with the overall arm-and-base motion sequenced by this course's motion-control machinery (Unit 5). Grasp — the arm executes the approach, engages suction, and a pressure sensor confirms a secure hold (Lesson 39's grip-security idea, applied to suction rather than friction). Place — the arm moves the box to a drop-off location and releases it, completing the task Lesson 32's finite state machine (or Lesson 34's behavior tree) was coordinating at the decision-making level the whole time.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. What are the four stages of a manipulation pipeline, in order?

The pipeline runs in this order: perceive the object, plan a grasp and approach, execute the grasp, then place the object at its destination.

2. Why does a perception error early in the pipeline (a misjudged object pose) tend to cause downstream failures?

Errors propagate downstream through the pipeline: bad perception data leads to a grasp plan based on incorrect information, which can then fail in execution.

3. Why do real manipulation systems check for success at each pipeline stage, rather than only assuming everything worked and proceeding straight to Place?

Since errors can occur and propagate at any stage, verifying success (like confirming a secure grasp) before committing to the next stage catches failures before they compound.

4. Fill in the blank: the ordered sequence Perceive, Plan, Grasp, and Place that a robot runs to complete a pick-and-place task is called a manipulation ___.

Manipulation pipeline is the standard term for this end-to-end sequence, chaining together perception, planning, and execution.

5. Fill in the blank: the manipulation pipeline stage that involves sensing and identifying an object's shape and pose, relying on Unit 2's cameras and depth sensors, is called ___.

Perceive is the first pipeline stage, and every later stage depends on getting this one right.
