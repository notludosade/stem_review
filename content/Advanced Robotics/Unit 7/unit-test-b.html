Unit 7 Test — Version B

Robotic Manipulation. 10 questions covering every lesson in this unit. Score 80% or higher to clear this unit.

1. Why does manipulation require reasoning about forces and friction in a way pure locomotion generally doesn't?

Grasping requires making and controlling contact, introducing force and friction reasoning that pure navigation's avoid-everything goal doesn't need.

2. Why is 'the end-effector reached a target pose' not the same as manipulation success?

Reaching a target end-effector pose is necessary but not sufficient — manipulation's real goal is the object's resulting state.

3. Answer both parts about grippers:

(a) What is the main trade-off between a two-finger and a three-finger gripper?

(b) Fill in the blank: the simplest, most common gripper design is the two-finger or ___-jaw gripper.

(a) More fingers means more versatility and stability at the cost of complexity. (b) Parallel-jaw is the standard term for this common design.

4. Fill in the blank: a specific contact configuration and approach pose expected to hold an object securely is called a ___.

A grasp is the specific plan grasp planning selects and evaluates from among several candidates.

5. What must a good candidate grasp satisfy, according to this unit?

A grasp candidate needs both a reachable arm configuration and sound mechanics — either alone isn't enough.

6. Answer both parts about friction and grasp stability:

(a) An object weighing 5 N is gripped with μ = 0.5. What is the minimum normal (grip) force needed to support it?

(b) Fill in the blank: μ, the coefficient of friction, is a ___-dependent constant relating grip force to maximum available friction.

(a) Required normal force ≈ weight / μ = 5 / 0.5 = 10 N. (b) The coefficient of friction depends on the two materials in contact.

7. Fill in the blank: a suction gripper sidesteps the rigid-gripper requirement of needing two ___ to grip, by attaching to just one face.

Suction grippers attach to a single face rather than needing opposing surfaces to squeeze between, unlike a rigid two-finger gripper.

8. Why might a produce-sorting task handling delicate, irregularly shaped fruit be better served by a soft gripper than a rigid one?

Passive conformance and gentler force distribution make soft grippers better suited to delicate, irregular objects than a rigid gripper's precisely calculated but unforgiving contact points.

9. Fill in the blank: the manipulation pipeline stage relying on Unit 2's cameras and depth sensors to identify an object's shape and pose is called ___.

Perceive is the first pipeline stage, and every later stage depends on getting this one right.

10. Why do real manipulation systems check for success at each pipeline stage rather than assuming every step worked?

Since errors can occur and propagate at any stage, verifying success before committing to the next stage catches failures before they compound.
