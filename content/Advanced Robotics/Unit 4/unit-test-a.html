1. In a planning graph built from an occupancy grid, what typically becomes a node?

Free cells become nodes connected by edges to their free neighbors; occupied cells are excluded from the graph.

2. Fill in the blank: choosing the cell size of a planning grid is a trade-off between graph fidelity and search speed, known as choosing the grid's ___.

Coarser resolution searches faster but may miss real gaps; finer resolution is more faithful but produces a much larger graph.

3. Answer both parts about configuration space:

(a) Why does an obstacle appear larger in configuration space than in the physical world?

(b) A circular robot has radius 0.2 m. Fill in the blank: for a gap 0.9 m wide between two obstacles, the effective gap in configuration space is ___ m.

(a) Inflating obstacles by the robot's size lets planning treat the robot as a point while still respecting its true extent. (b) 0.9 − 2(0.2) = 0.5 m.

4. Why does Dijkstra's algorithm improve on breadth-first search (BFS) for graphs with unequal edge costs?

BFS assumes equal-cost edges; Dijkstra expands by true accumulated cost, correctly finding the lowest-cost path even with unequal edges.

5. Fill in the blank: a cheap estimate of remaining cost from a node to the goal, used to prioritize promising nodes, is called a ___.

A heuristic estimates remaining cost so search can prioritize nodes that seem closer to the goal.

6. Answer both parts about A* search:

(a) A node has g(n) = 5 and h(n) = 3. What is f(n)?

(b) Fill in the blank: for A* to guarantee the true lowest-cost path, its heuristic must never ___ the true remaining cost.

(a) f(n) = g(n) + h(n) = 5 + 3 = 8. (b) An admissible heuristic never overestimates, which guarantees A* finds the optimal path.

7. Why is straight-line distance a standard admissible heuristic for grid-based robot path planning?

A straight line is the shortest possible route, so any real path around obstacles is at least that long — the heuristic never overestimates.

8. What is the key limitation of a global planner like A* that local obstacle avoidance is designed to address?

A global plan is fixed to the map's knowledge at planning time; local planning reacts to fresh sensor data the map couldn't have captured.

9. What typically triggers full replanning, as opposed to ordinary local obstacle avoidance?

Replanning is reserved for mismatches serious enough that local steering-around isn't sufficient, such as a fully blocked route.

10. Fill in the blank: planning around a moving obstacle well requires reasoning about ___ as well as space, since the obstacle's position changes before the robot arrives.

Moving obstacles turn planning into a problem that includes time, not just a static spatial map.
