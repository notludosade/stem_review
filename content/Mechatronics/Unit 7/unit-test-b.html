1. According to the integration recipe, when should the control strategy (bang-bang vs. PID) be decided?

Control strategy selection comes after sensor and actuator are identified, specifically matched to what the actuator can do.

2. Why would using bang-bang control on a PWM-driven DC fan be a missed opportunity?

When an actuator can genuinely produce smooth output, restricting it to strict on/off leaves real performance on the table.

3. Fill in the blank: in the temperature-controlled fan case study, filtering the noisy thermistor reading before the control calculation uses a simple ___ ___ (two words) from Unit 4.

A simple moving average filter is sufficient smoothing for the slow-drifting thermistor signal in this case study.

4. What is the consequence of getting a control loop’s error sign backward?

A flipped error sign makes the controller correct in the wrong direction, making the system actively worse rather than better.

5. Why is the basic two-sensor line-following logic described as "bang-bang-like"?

On/off sensor readings driving a fixed handful of discrete responses is exactly bang-bang-style control, applied to steering.

6. Fill in the blank: the more advanced line-following approach shows that PID is a completely general strategy for turning any measurable ___ into a corrective actuator output.

PID isn’t tied to temperature specifically — the same formula applies to any measurable error, including lateral line position.

7. In the example pseudocode combining a temperature control loop with a safety override, what happens to the normal proportional-control calculation if the safety condition trips?

The if/else structure means the safety branch and normal control branch are mutually exclusive.

8. What is a simple example of sensor fusion?

Combining multiple readings of the same quantity, like averaging redundant sensors, is a simple, real example of sensor fusion.

9. Fill in the blank: testing a sensor reading alone, before adding any control logic, means printing its raw values and confirming they make physical ___ first.

Confirming raw sensor readings make physical sense is the first isolation step before trusting them in a control loop.

10. An actuator doesn’t respond at all when commanded. What should be checked first, before assuming the control math is wrong?

Wiring and driver issues are far more commonly the culprit than a bug in the control formula itself.
