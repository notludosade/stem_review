1. What is the first step in designing a sensor + actuator + control subsystem?

Everything else in the recipe depends on first knowing what quantity is being measured and controlled.

2. Why is bang-bang control often the correct choice for a relay-driven heater, not just the simplest option?

Matching control strategy to what the actuator can physically do means bang-bang fits a strictly on/off actuator well.

3. Fill in the blank: in the temperature-controlled fan case study, the sensor used is a thermistor voltage divider, and the actuator is a ___-driven DC fan.

A PWM-driven fan is what allows the case study to use smooth proportional speed control rather than strict on/off.

4. Why might a control loop define error as (measured − target) rather than (target − measured), as in some earlier lessons?

What matters is that increasing error produces increasing corrective output in the correct direction for the specific system.

5. In the basic two-sensor line-following example, what information does each sensor provide?

The basic version only reports a simple on-line/off-line state per sensor.

6. Fill in the blank: a more advanced line-following version measures how far off-center the line is as a numeric ___, allowing proportional or PID steering correction.

Turning the sensing into a numeric lateral-position error lets the same PID machinery from Unit 6 apply to steering.

7. Why shouldn’t a safety condition, like a tripped limit switch, be blended into a control loop’s normal weighted calculation?

A tripped safety condition demands an immediate, full override rather than dilution through a blended calculation.

8. Fill in the blank: combining readings from multiple sensors measuring the same or related quantities into a single, more reliable estimate is called sensor ___.

Sensor fusion combines multiple sensors’ information into a single, more trustworthy estimate.

9. What is the recommended first step when bringing up a new integrated sensor + actuator + control system?

Isolating each piece before combining them reveals which layer to investigate first when something goes wrong.

10. A control loop’s output consistently pushes in the wrong direction, making the system worse instead of better. What is the most likely bug?

A controller actively fighting itself is the signature of a flipped error sign — one of the most common integration bugs.
