1. Fill in the blank: in the PID formula, the term Kp × error is the ___ term.

The proportional term scales output directly with the current error.

2. What happens to a PID controller if Kd is set to exactly zero?

Setting any gain to zero removes that term’s contribution — PID with Kd = 0 behaves as a PI controller.

3. Fill in the blank: with Kp = 8 and an error of 2.5 units, the proportional output is ___.

output_P = Kp × error = 8 × 2.5 = 20.

4. What is steady-state error?

Steady-state error is the persistent settled-state gap that pure proportional control often leaves behind.

5. How does adding an integral term eliminate the steady-state error pure proportional control leaves behind?

The integral sum keeps growing for as long as error persists, closing the gap proportional control alone leaves open.

6. Fill in the blank: integral windup happens when a large error persists while the actuator is already ___ and can’t respond any further, yet the integral sum keeps growing anyway.

Windup occurs specifically during actuator saturation, since the integral term has no way to know the actuator is stuck.

7. How does the derivative term help reduce overshoot?

By reacting to a fast-shrinking error in advance, the derivative term pulls output down before overshoot actually occurs.

8. Fill in the blank: applying a moving average or EMA filter (Unit 4) to a sensor reading before it reaches a derivative term is often what prevents output ___.

Filtering the signal first prevents noise from being misread as a fast, genuine rate of change.

9. Fill in the blank: with error = 5, integral sum = 4, rate of change of error = −2, Kp = 2, Ki = 1.5, Kd = 3, the total PID output is ___.

(2×5) + (1.5×4) + (3×−2) = 10 + 6 − 6 = 10.

10. A system’s output becomes jittery and erratic after Kd is increased. What are reasonable responses?

Jittery output from a raised Kd usually means the derivative term is reacting to noise — reducing Kd or filtering the signal are the standard fixes.
