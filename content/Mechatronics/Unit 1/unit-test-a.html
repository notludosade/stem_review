Sensors I — analog vs. digital signals, thermistors, thermocouples, photoresistors, phototransistors, ultrasonic ranging, limit switches, and calibration. 10 questions covering every lesson in this unit. Score 80% or higher to clear this unit.

1. A sensor’s output can smoothly take on any value between its minimum and maximum. What kind of sensor is it?

A continuously varying output over a range, with every intermediate value meaningful, is the definition of an analog signal.

2. Fill in the blank: an analog sensor must connect to one of a microcontroller’s limited ___ pins, while a digital sensor can use almost any general-purpose pin.

ADC (analog-to-digital converter) pins are the scarcer, dedicated resource analog sensors require.

3. An NTC thermistor is heated up. What happens to its resistance?

NTC stands for negative temperature coefficient — resistance falls as temperature rises.

4. Why does a thermistor need to be placed in a voltage divider to be read by a microcontroller?

ADCs sample voltage; a voltage divider is what turns a resistive sensor’s resistance change into a readable voltage change.

5. Fill in the blank: a thermocouple generates a small voltage from a junction of two dissimilar metals due to the ___ effect.

The Seebeck effect is the thermoelectric phenomenon behind a thermocouple’s voltage output.

6. What happens to a photoresistor’s (LDR’s) resistance as light hitting it increases?

More light means lower resistance for a photoresistor — the same inverse relationship as an NTC thermistor with heat.

7. Why is a phototransistor a better choice than an LDR for reading a fast-spinning optical encoder disk?

An LDR’s slow response can’t keep up with rapid transitions; a phototransistor’s much faster, more linear response can.

8. Fill in the blank: an ultrasonic sensor’s echo pin stays HIGH for 400 microseconds. Using 0.0343 cm/microsecond for the speed of sound, the one-way distance is approximately ___ cm.

400 × 0.0343 = 13.72 cm round-trip; divide by 2 for one-way distance: ≈ 6.86 cm.

9. Why is a normally-closed (NC) switch often preferred for a safety-critical cutoff over a normally-open (NO) switch?

With NC wiring, both a triggered switch and a broken wire look like an open circuit — the system fails toward the safe state.

10. Fill in the blank: converting a sensor’s raw electrical output into a real-world unit using known reference readings is called ___.

Calibration relates a sensor’s output to the physical quantity it measures, correcting for manufacturing variation between individual units.
