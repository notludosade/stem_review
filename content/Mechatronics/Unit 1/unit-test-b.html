Unit 1 Test — Version B

Sensors I — analog vs. digital signals, thermistors, thermocouples, photoresistors, phototransistors, ultrasonic ranging, limit switches, and calibration. 10 questions covering every lesson in this unit. Score 80% or higher to clear this unit.

1. A limit switch reports only "pressed" or "not pressed," with nothing meaningful in between. What kind of sensor is it?

Exactly two discrete states with no meaningful in-between reading is the definition of a digital signal.

2. Fill in the blank: because ADC-capable pins are more limited than general-purpose digital pins, planning a sensor layout should treat them as a ___ resource.

ADC pins are far fewer than GPIO pins on most microcontrollers, making them the more constrained resource to plan around.

3. An NTC thermistor is cooled down significantly. What happens to its resistance?

NTC means resistance moves opposite to temperature — cooling raises resistance, since NTC resistance falls only as it heats up.

4. What two components make up the basic voltage divider circuit used to read a thermistor or photoresistor?

A fixed resistor paired with the sensor, tapped at the midpoint, is exactly the divider pattern used for any simple resistive sensor.

5. Fill in the blank: unlike a thermistor, a thermocouple’s tiny output voltage requires an ___ circuit before a microcontroller can read it usefully.

A thermocouple’s microvolt-to-millivolt-scale output needs amplification, unlike a thermistor’s simple voltage-divider readout.

6. Why is an LDR generally a poor choice for a fast, precise light-timing application?

An LDR’s slow, imprecise response makes it unsuitable for fast or precise timing applications, unlike a phototransistor.

7. What is the defining behavior of a phototransistor?

A phototransistor’s light-triggered base current producing a larger controlled current is what distinguishes it from a simple photoresistor.

8. Fill in the blank: an ultrasonic sensor’s echo pin stays HIGH for 1000 microseconds. Using 0.0343 cm/microsecond for the speed of sound, the one-way distance is approximately ___ cm.

1000 × 0.0343 = 34.3 cm round-trip; divide by 2 for one-way distance: ≈ 17.15 cm.

9. Why does an unpressed, unconnected (floating) digital input pin give unreliable readings without a pull-up or pull-down resistor?

A pull-up or pull-down resistor guarantees a defined default state; without one, a floating pin is susceptible to noise.

10. Fill in the blank: a two-point calibration uses ___ known reference readings to build a straight-line mapping to a real-world unit.

Two-point calibration is defined by exactly two trusted reference readings used to interpolate the conversion.
