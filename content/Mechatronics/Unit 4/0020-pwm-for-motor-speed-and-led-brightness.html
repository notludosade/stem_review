Pulse-Width Modulation for Motor Speed and LED Brightness

Duty cycle is the one number that matters — here’s exactly how it maps to real, controllable output.

← Lesson 19 Lesson 21 → Contents Glossary →

The previous lesson introduced PWM's core idea. This lesson gives it exact vocabulary and the arithmetic behind it.

Definition A PWM signal repeats at a fixed frequency (how many on/off cycles occur per second). Within each cycle, the duty cycle is the percentage of that cycle's time spent HIGH rather than LOW. A 25% duty cycle spends one quarter of each cycle HIGH; a 75% duty cycle spends three quarters HIGH.
average voltage ≈ duty cycle × supply voltage
Example A 5 V pin driven at 60% duty cycle delivers an average voltage of 0.60 × 5 = 3.0 V to a DC motor — noticeably slower than full speed, but faster than a 50%-duty 2.5 V average would give.

PWM driving a motor

A real-world caveat: minimum duty deadzone A real DC motor has to overcome internal friction before it turns at all. Below some minimum duty cycle, the average voltage is too low to overcome that friction, and the motor simply doesn't turn — it stays at zero output even though the duty cycle isn't literally zero. The relationship between duty cycle and actual motor speed is only reliably proportional above this deadzone, not all the way down to 0%.

PWM driving an LED

An LED has no comparable mechanical deadzone — its perceived brightness scales quite proportionally with duty cycle, as long as the PWM frequency is high enough (roughly above 60 Hz) that human vision perceives a steady, dimmed glow rather than a visible flicker.

Worked example A PWM signal has a period of 4 ms and stays HIGH for 1 ms of each cycle. Duty cycle = 1 / 4 = 25%. Driving a 5 V LED circuit at 25% duty produces roughly one quarter of full brightness — a clearly dimmer, but steady-looking, glow.

Check your recall

1. A PWM signal has a period of 20 ms and stays HIGH for 5 ms of each cycle. What is its duty cycle?

Duty cycle = HIGH time / period = 5 / 20 = 0.25 = 25%.

2. A 12 V supply drives a PWM signal at 40% duty cycle into a motor. What is the approximate average voltage delivered?

Average voltage ≈ duty cycle × supply voltage = 0.40 × 12 = 4.8 V.

3. Why might a DC motor fail to turn at all at a very low but nonzero PWM duty cycle?

Real motors have a minimum-duty deadzone caused by internal friction — below it, the average voltage isn’t enough to start the motor turning.

4. Fill in the blank: the percentage of each PWM cycle spent HIGH rather than LOW is called the ___ cycle.

Duty cycle is the single number that determines PWM’s effective average output — the fraction of time spent HIGH.

5. Fill in the blank: for an LED to appear as a steady dimmed glow rather than a visible flicker, the PWM frequency generally needs to stay above roughly ___ Hz.

Human vision generally can’t detect flicker above roughly 60 Hz, which is why PWM-dimmed LEDs look steady rather than blinking at typical PWM frequencies.
