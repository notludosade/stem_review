1. How many discrete levels does a 10-bit ADC have?

2^10 = 1024 discrete levels.

2. Fill in the blank: the small, unavoidable rounding difference between a true continuous voltage and an ADC’s nearest reported level is called ___ error.

Quantization error is the inherent rounding cost of representing a continuous voltage with a finite number of discrete levels.

3. Why do many cheap microcontroller boards use PWM instead of a true DAC for analog-like output?

PWM is a practical workaround exploiting the fact that a motor or LED responds to the time-averaged effect of rapid switching.

4. Fill in the blank: a PWM signal with a period of 10 ms that stays HIGH for 3 ms has a duty cycle of ___%.

Duty cycle = HIGH time / period = 3 / 10 = 30%.

5. A 5 V supply drives a PWM signal at 80% duty cycle into an LED circuit. What is the approximate average voltage delivered?

Average voltage ≈ duty cycle × supply voltage = 0.80 × 5 = 4 V.

6. Why might a DC motor fail to turn at all at a very low, nonzero PWM duty cycle?

A real motor has a minimum-duty deadzone caused by internal friction that must be overcome before it turns at all.

7. Fill in the blank: random, unwanted variation in a sensor reading that doesn’t reflect a real physical change is called ___.

Noise arises from several combined sources: electrical interference, quantization, vibration, and sensor jitter.

8. What is the main cost of reducing noise by averaging several fresh samples for every single reading?

Averaging trades speed for smoothness — more samples means more time before a result is ready.

9. What does increasing a moving average filter’s window size (N) generally do?

A larger window averages over more readings, producing smoother but more lagging output.

10. Fill in the blank: in an exponential moving average, a small value of alpha produces heavier smoothing but a ___ response to real changes.

A small alpha weights new readings lightly, smoothing heavily but responding more slowly, the same trade-off a large window size produces.
