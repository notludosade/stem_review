1. What is stall torque?

Stall torque is the torque at zero speed — the motor is still trying to turn and drawing current, just fully loaded down.

2. Fill in the blank: rapidly switching a motor’s supply on and off, rather than holding a lower steady voltage, is the technique called ___, covered fully in Unit 4.

PWM avoids the wasted heat of a series resistor by switching full voltage on and off rapidly instead.

3. What would happen if both switches on the same vertical side of an H-bridge closed at once?

A same-side short is a real failure mode a safe H-bridge design specifically guards against.

4. Fill in the blank: instead of hand-wiring four transistors, motor control typically uses a dedicated motor driver ___ that packages a protected H-bridge.

A motor driver IC bundles protection against shorts and back-EMF into one tested component.

5. A hobby servo receives a repeating pulse close to 1.0 ms wide. Roughly what angle is being commanded?

A roughly 1.0 ms pulse commands close to the servo’s near extreme, close to 0°.

6. Fill in the blank: a standard hobby servo’s control pulse repeats roughly every ___ milliseconds, regardless of the commanded angle.

The 20 ms repetition period is standard, even though pulse width within it varies with the commanded angle.

7. A stepper motor with a 1.8° step angle needs to rotate exactly 360° (one full revolution). How many steps does that take?

360 / 1.8 = 200 steps make up one full revolution.

8. What happens if a stepper motor is pushed past its available torque and stalls?

A stall can cause missed steps invisibly, since the controller has no feedback sensor to detect the mismatch.

9. Fill in the blank: on a continuous rotation servo, a pulse width of about ___ ms commands the servo to stop.

A roughly 1.5 ms pulse corresponds to "stop" on a continuous rotation servo, the same pulse width that means "center" on a standard servo.

10. A 3D printer’s axis needs precise, repeatable multi-turn positioning, well beyond 180° of equivalent rotation. Which actuator fits best?

A stepper’s precise, repeatable, multi-turn positioning without a feedback sensor is exactly what fits a 3D printer axis.
