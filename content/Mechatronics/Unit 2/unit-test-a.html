1. What happens to a DC motor’s speed as its mechanical load increases, up to the point of stalling?

Real motors trade speed for torque as load increases, still drawing current even at stall.

2. Fill in the blank: wiring a variable resistor in series to control a DC motor’s speed is inefficient because it wastes power as ___.

A series resistor burns off unwanted power as heat rather than actually reducing what the motor consumes.

3. In an H-bridge, closing which pair of switches spins a motor in the opposite direction from closing SW1 and SW4?

Closing the opposite diagonal pair (SW2 and SW3) reverses the motor’s connection polarity, spinning it the other way.

4. Fill in the blank: the voltage spike a motor generates when its magnetic field collapses as it’s switched off is called back-___.

Back-EMF is the reason motor driver circuits need protection built in.

5. A hobby servo receives a repeating pulse close to 2.0 ms wide. Roughly what angle is being commanded?

A roughly 2.0 ms pulse commands close to the servo’s far extreme, near 180°.

6. What internal component lets a standard servo motor know its own current shaft angle?

The internal potentiometer gives the servo’s control circuit a continuous reading of its actual current angle.

7. Fill in the blank: a stepper motor with a 1.8° step angle commanded to take 100 steps rotates ___ degrees.

100 steps × 1.8° per step = 180°.

8. Why can a stepper motor track its position without a feedback sensor, unlike a servo?

Position tracking relies on every commanded step actually occurring — a stall can cause silent, undetectable drift.

9. How does a continuous rotation servo differ from a standard servo, given the identical control signal?

Removing the internal angle limit reinterprets the same signal as speed/direction rather than absolute position.

10. Fill in the blank: an actuator that needs to hold a specific angle within a limited range and actively resist being pushed off it is best served by a ___ motor.

A servo’s built-in feedback loop is specifically designed to hold a commanded angle against a resisting load.
