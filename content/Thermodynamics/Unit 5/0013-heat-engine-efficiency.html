Heat Engine Efficiency

Every heat engine, from a car's cylinder to a power plant's turbine, does the same basic trick: pull heat from somewhere hot, dump some of it somewhere cold, and keep the difference as useful work.

← Unit 4, Lesson 12 Lesson 14 → Contents Glossary →

Definition — Heat Engine Efficiency A heat engine is a device that runs a working substance through a repeated cycle (Lesson 9's territory), absorbing heat Q_h from a hot reservoir, exhausting heat Q_c to a cold reservoir, and delivering net work W = Q_h − Q_c. Its efficiency is the fraction of the heat taken in that comes out as useful work: e = W/Q_h = 1 − Q_c/Q_h. Because the Kelvin statement (Lesson 10) forbids Q_c = 0, e is always strictly less than 1 — some heat is always exhausted, never converted entirely to work.
Why e = 1 − Q_c/Q_h Is the Same Fact as the Kelvin Statement e = 1 exactly would require Q_c = 0 — an engine that dumps nothing to a cold reservoir at all, converting 100% of the heat it absorbs into work. That's the literal scenario the Kelvin statement rules out. Every real engine's efficiency ceiling — however clever the design — traces back to this one inequality, not to any particular flaw in a particular engine.
Worked Example — Efficiency of a Simple Engine An engine absorbs Q_h = 800 J from its hot reservoir per cycle and exhausts Q_c = 500 J to its cold reservoir. Find W and e.
W = Q_h − Q_c = 800 − 500 = 300 J.
e = W/Q_h = 300/800 = 0.375 = 37.5% (equivalently, e = 1 − Q_c/Q_h = 1 − 500/800 = 0.375, the same answer either way).

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. In e = 1 − Q_c/Q_h, what does Q_c represent?

Q_c is specifically the heat dumped to the cold reservoir — the "waste" heat that keeps e from ever reaching 1.

2. Fill in the blank: an engine absorbs Q_h = 950 J and exhausts Q_c = 570 J per cycle. Its efficiency is e = ___ (as a decimal, e.g. 0.4).

e = 1 − Q_c/Q_h = 1 − 570/950 = 1 − 0.6 = 0.4.

3. Why can no heat engine ever reach e = 1 (100% efficiency), regardless of engineering improvements?

This is a hard physical law, not an engineering shortfall — the Kelvin statement directly rules out any cyclic engine with Q_c = 0.

4. Fill in the blank: an engine has efficiency e = 0.4 and absorbs Q_h = 900 J per cycle. The work it delivers is W = ___ J.

W = e × Q_h = (0.4)(900) = 360 J.
