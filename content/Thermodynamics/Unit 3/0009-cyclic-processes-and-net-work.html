Cyclic Processes and Net Work

Every real engine repeats the same loop over and over. Closing that loop on a PV diagram turns the whole messy multi-step process into a single number: the area it encloses.

← Lesson 8 Unit 4, Lesson 10 → Contents Glossary →

Definition — Cyclic Process A cyclic process strings together several individual processes (isobaric, isochoric, isothermal, adiabatic legs, in any combination) that end exactly where they began — the same P, V, and T the cycle started with. Because U is a state function, ΔU = 0 over one complete cycle, no matter how complicated the path. The first law then forces Q_net = W_net for the cycle as a whole: whatever net heat went in over the full loop comes back out as net work.
Method — Net Work as Enclosed Area Each leg of a cycle contributes its own W = ∫P dV. Summed over a closed loop, the legs where volume is increasing add positive area and the legs where volume is decreasing subtract area — what's left over is exactly the area enclosed by the loop on a PV diagram. Traversed clockwise, a loop's enclosed area is positive net work done by the gas (the shape of a heat engine cycle). Traversed counterclockwise, the same shape gives negative net work — work is done on the gas overall (a refrigerator or heat pump cycle, previewed in Unit 5).
Why This Is the Bridge to Unit 5's Engines A heat engine, stripped to its thermodynamic essentials, is nothing more than a working substance carried around a closed PV loop over and over — the enclosed area, repeated once per cycle, is the mechanical work the engine delivers. Every heat-engine efficiency calculation in Unit 5 traces back to this single geometric fact: net work out equals the area of the loop the gas traces.
Worked Example — Net Work of a Rectangular Cycle A gas cycles through four legs forming a rectangle on a PV diagram: V from 0.01 m³ to 0.03 m³ at P = 3×10⁵ Pa (isobaric), then P drops to 1×10⁵ Pa at V = 0.03 m³ (isochoric), then V returns to 0.01 m³ at P = 1×10⁵ Pa (isobaric), then P rises back to 3×10⁵ Pa at V = 0.01 m³ (isochoric) — traversed clockwise. Find the net work.
Net work = enclosed area = ΔV × ΔP = (0.03 − 0.01)(3×10⁵ − 1×10⁵) = (0.02)(2×10⁵) = 4000 J, done by the gas, since the loop runs clockwise.
