1. A gas expands at a constant pressure P = 2.5×10⁵ Pa from V₁ = 0.015 m³ to V₂ = 0.045 m³. What is the work done by the gas?

W = PΔV = (2.5×10⁵)(0.045 − 0.015) = (2.5×10⁵)(0.03) = 7500 J.

2. In PVᵞ = constant, γ is defined as:

γ = C_P/C_V, the ratio of heat capacity at constant pressure to heat capacity at constant volume — about 5/3 for a monatomic gas, 7/5 for a diatomic gas.

3. Over one complete thermodynamic cycle, the first law requires:

Since the cycle returns to its starting state, ΔU = 0, and ΔU = Q_net − W_net forces Q_net = W_net.

4. An isothermal process at T = 400 K moves n = 2 mol of ideal gas from V₁ = 0.01 m³ to V₂ = 0.03 m³ (R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)). What is the work done by the gas?

W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁) = (2)(8.314)(400) ln(3) = 6651.2 × 1.0986 ≈ 7307 J.

5. During an adiabatic expansion (compared to an isothermal expansion covering the same ΔV), the gas's pressure:

Because γ > 1, PVᵞ = const is a steeper curve than PV = const, so pressure both rises faster under adiabatic compression and falls faster under adiabatic expansion.

6. An isothermal process at T = 350 K moves n = 1.5 mol of ideal gas from V₁ = 0.02 m³ to V₂ = 0.05 m³. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the work done by the gas is W ≈ ___ J.

(b) If V₂ were increased further (a bigger expansion), W would:

(a) W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁) = (1.5)(8.314)(350) ln(2.5) = 4364.85 × 0.9163 ≈ 3999.5 J. (b) ln(V₂/V₁) is an increasing function of V₂, so a larger expansion always does more work.

7. Air (γ = 1.4) starts at P₁ = 1.2×10⁵ Pa, V₁ = 0.025 m³, and is adiabatically compressed to V₂ = 0.0125 m³. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the final pressure is P₂ ≈ ___ ×10⁵ Pa.

(b) This process's temperature change is best described as:

(a) P₂ = P₁(V₁/V₂)ᵞ = (1.2×10⁵)(2)^1.4 ≈ (1.2×10⁵)(2.639) ≈ 3.17×10⁵ Pa. (b) With Q = 0, ΔU = −W; compression means work is done on the gas (W < 0), so ΔU > 0 and T rises.

8. A rectangular cycle is traversed clockwise with ΔV = 0.015 m³ and ΔP = 1.8×10⁵ Pa. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the net work done by the gas over one cycle is ___ J.

(b) If this same loop were instead traversed counterclockwise, that 2700 J would be:

(a) Net work = enclosed area = ΔV × ΔP = (0.015)(1.8×10⁵) = 2700 J. (b) Reversing the direction of travel flips which legs are expansions and which are compressions, flipping the sign of the net work.

9. A gas carried through one complete cycle absorbs a net Q_net = 3200 J. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: since ΔU = 0 over the cycle, W_net = ___ J.

(b) A positive W_net (net work done by the gas) means this loop, on a PV diagram, is traversed:

(a) ΔU = Q_net − W_net = 0, so W_net = Q_net = 3200 J. (b) A clockwise loop is exactly the convention for positive net work done by the gas — the heat-engine direction.

10. A cyclic process traces a right-triangle-shaped loop on a PV diagram with legs ΔV = 0.02 m³ and ΔP = 1.5×10⁵ Pa. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the net work is W = ½ΔV·ΔP = ___ J.

(b) What makes cyclic processes essential for real engines, as opposed to a single one-way expansion?

(a) A right-triangle's area is half the base times height: W = ½(0.02)(1.5×10⁵) = 1500 J. (b) Only a closed loop lets the same working substance be reused cycle after cycle, which is exactly what makes a continuously running engine possible.
