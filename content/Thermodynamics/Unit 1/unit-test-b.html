1. Systems P and Q are each separately found to be in thermal equilibrium with system R, though P and Q are never placed in direct contact with each other. What can you conclude?

This is exactly the zeroth law: equilibrium with a common third system (here R, playing the role of a thermometer) guarantees equilibrium between P and Q, with no direct contact required.

2. A pizza oven is set to 425°F. What is this in Celsius, to the nearest whole degree?

°C = (5/9)(°F − 32) = (5/9)(393) ≈ 218.3°C ≈ 218°C.

3. A thermally insulated, gas-filled cylinder has its piston pulled outward by an external force, expanding the gas and lowering its temperature. Using this course's sign convention (W positive when done BY the system), which best describes Q and W for the gas?

Insulation blocks heat, so Q = 0. The gas itself is expanding and pushing outward on its surroundings, so the gas does positive work (W > 0) even though it's being pulled by an external force.

4. A brick wall (k = 0.7 W/(m·K)) has area 4 m² and thickness 0.2 m, with an 18 K temperature difference across it. What is the conduction rate?

Q̇ = kAΔT/L = (0.7)(4)(18)/(0.2) = 50.4/0.2 = 252 W.

5. Which heat transfer mechanism specifically requires the bulk motion of a fluid, and therefore cannot occur through a vacuum?

Convection is specifically bulk fluid motion carrying heat; conduction needs contact but no bulk flow, and radiation needs no medium at all.

6. A lab sample is measured at 25°C. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: in Kelvin, this temperature is ___ K.

(b) If the sample's temperature then falls by 10 Celsius degrees, its Kelvin temperature falls by:

(a) K = °C + 273.15 = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K. (b) Celsius and Kelvin share the same size degree, offset by a fixed constant, so any change in °C equals the same change in K.

7. A gas moves from state 1 to state 2 via Process I, absorbing Q = 220 J while doing W = 70 J of work. A second experiment moves the same gas between the same two states via Process II, this time doing W = 40 J of work. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: ΔU for Process I is ___ J.

(b) What must Q be for Process II, given that ΔU must match Process I's value (since ΔU depends only on the states, not the path)?

(a) ΔU = Q − W = 220 − 70 = 150 J. (b) U is a state function, so Process II must reach the same ΔU = 150 J: Q = ΔU + W = 150 + 40 = 190 J.

8. An aluminum plate (k = 205 W/(m·K)) has area 0.04 m² and thickness 0.05 m, with a 60 K temperature difference across it. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the conduction rate is ___ ×10³ W (2 decimal places).

(b) If the plate's thickness were instead doubled (all else unchanged), the conduction rate would:

(a) Q̇ = kAΔT/L = (205)(0.04)(60)/(0.05) = 492/0.05 = 9840 W = 9.84×10³ W. (b) Q̇ is inversely proportional to L, so doubling L halves Q̇.

9. A thermometer reads 30°C when placed against object P, and also reads 30°C when placed against object Q (P and Q never touch each other directly). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: this common reading, expressed in Kelvin, is ___ K.

(b) By the zeroth law, are P and Q in thermal equilibrium with each other?

(a) K = °C + 273.15 = 30 + 273.15 = 303.15 K. (b) That's the zeroth law directly, with the thermometer playing the role of the common system C.

10. Consider the three heat transfer mechanisms together. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: exactly ___ of the three mechanisms (conduction, convection, radiation) can transfer heat with no medium present at all.

(b) Which single mechanism is that one — the one that can cross a total vacuum?

(a) Only radiation needs no medium; conduction and convection both require physical matter to carry the energy. (b) Radiation is electromagnetic and is the only mechanism of the three that can cross a vacuum.
