1. Which pair correctly matches each name to its statement of the second law?

Kelvin is the engine-flavored statement (no 100%-efficient engine); Clausius is the refrigerator-flavored statement (no free "uphill" heat flow) — the two are logically equivalent.

2. A gas absorbs Q = 960 J of heat during a reversible isothermal process at T = 160 K. What is ΔS?

ΔS = Q/T = 960/160 = 6.0 J/K.

3. Why is entropy described as giving physics an "arrow of time," when the microscopic laws underneath it are time-symmetric?

Each microscopic interaction is reversible, but the sheer statistical dominance of high-Ω macrostates makes macroscopic entropy increase overwhelmingly likely going forward in time.

4. A process that dissipates energy (friction, finite-ΔT heat flow) so it cannot be undone without a permanent change elsewhere is called:

Irreversibility is the signature of dissipation — every real-world process is irreversible to some degree; reversibility is only an idealized limit.

5. 1.5 kg of water (c ≈ 4186 J/(kg·K)) is heated from 270 K to 315 K. Using ΔS = mc ln(T₂/T₁), what is ΔS?

ΔS = mc ln(T₂/T₁) = (1.5)(4186) ln(315/270) = 6279 × 0.1542 ≈ 968 J/K.

6. A proposed device claims to transfer Q_c = 300 J from a cold reservoir at T_c = 240 K to a hot reservoir at T_h = 480 K, with zero work input and no other effect. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: if this worked as claimed, the combined entropy change of the two reservoirs (ΔS_cold + ΔS_hot) would be ___ J/K.

(b) Since this total entropy change is negative, this device:

(a) ΔS_cold = −Q_c/T_c = −300/240 = −1.25 J/K; ΔS_hot = +Q_c/T_h = 300/480 = 0.625 J/K; total = −1.25 + 0.625 = −0.625 J/K. (b) A negative total entropy change for an isolated pair of reservoirs is exactly what the second law (ΔS_total ≥ 0) forbids — this restates the Clausius statement numerically.

7. A gas absorbs Q = 770 J of heat during a reversible isothermal process at T = 140 K. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: ΔS = ___ J/K.

(b) If this same Q = 770 J were instead absorbed at a much lower temperature, ΔS would be:

(a) ΔS = Q/T = 770/140 = 5.5 J/K. (b) The same heat has a larger "disordering effect" at low T than at high T, so ΔS grows as T falls.

8. 1.0 kg of water is heated from 290 K to 330 K (c ≈ 4186 J/(kg·K)). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: ΔS = mc ln(T₂/T₁) ≈ ___ J/K.

(b) If both T₁ and T₂ were doubled instead (580 K and 660 K, the same ratio T₂/T₁), ΔS would:

(a) ΔS = (1.0)(4186) ln(330/290) = 4186 × 0.1292 ≈ 540.9 J/K. (b) ln(T₂/T₁) only cares about the ratio, so scaling both temperatures by the same factor leaves ΔS unchanged.

9. 9 distinguishable particles are each independently placed in the left or right half of a box. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the number of microstates matching "4 particles left, 5 right" is Ω = C(9,4) = ___.

(b) Compared to the macrostate "all 9 particles on one side" (Ω = 1), the 4-5 split is:

(a) C(9,4) = 9!/(4!5!) = 126. (b) If every microstate is equally likely, the probability of observing a macrostate is directly proportional to its Ω — 126 microstates vs. 1 means 126 times more likely.

10. A macrostate has Ω = 2000 microstates (k_B ≈ 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: using S = k_B ln Ω, S ≈ ___ J/K (scientific notation, 3 sig figs, e.g. 1.05e-22).

(b) If Ω were instead much smaller, the Boltzmann entropy S = k_B ln Ω would:

(a) S = k_B ln Ω = (1.38×10⁻²³)(ln 2000) = (1.38×10⁻²³)(7.601) ≈ 1.05×10⁻²² J/K. (b) ln is a strictly increasing function, so fewer available microstates always means lower entropy.
