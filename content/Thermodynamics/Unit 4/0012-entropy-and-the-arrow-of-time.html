Entropy and the Arrow of Time

Nothing in Newton's laws or Maxwell's equations cares which way time runs — reverse every velocity and they work exactly as well backward. Entropy is the one physical quantity that doesn't play along.

← Lesson 11 Unit 5, Lesson 13 → Contents Glossary →

Definition — Microstates, Macrostates, and Boltzmann Entropy A macrostate is what you can measure at a large scale (e.g. "5 gas particles somewhere in this box"); a microstate is one exact, fully-detailed configuration consistent with that macrostate (exactly which particle is where, moving how). Many different microstates usually correspond to the same macrostate — the count is written Ω. Boltzmann's formula: S = k_B ln Ω (k_B ≈ 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K) defines entropy directly from that count, without ever mentioning heat or temperature.
Method — Why Entropy Tends to Increase A system left alone doesn't move toward higher-Ω macrostates because some force pushes it there — it moves there because higher-Ω macrostates are simply more probable. If every microstate is equally likely, a macrostate with 1000× more microstates than another is 1000× more likely to be the one you observe next. "Entropy increases" isn't a separate law bolted onto mechanics; it's what overwhelming statistical likelihood looks like at the scale of ~10²³ particles.
Why This Is the Only Law That Picks a Time Direction Run a video of two billiard balls colliding backward, and it still obeys Newton's laws perfectly — nothing about the microscopic physics forbids it. Run a video of a shattered glass reassembling itself backward, and something feels obviously wrong, even though each individual atomic collision involved is, in isolation, just as time-symmetric as the billiard balls. The difference is entropy: a shattered glass has colossally more possible microstates than an intact one, so reassembly — while not literally forbidden by any single physical law — is so statistically improbable that it's never once been observed. The "arrow of time" is a statistical arrow, not a mechanical one.
Worked Example — Counting Microstates for 4 Particles in a Box 4 distinguishable gas particles are each independently in the left or right half of a box (2⁴ = 16 total microstates, all equally likely). Compare two macrostates.
Macrostate "all 4 particles in the left half": only 1 microstate matches (Ω = 1).
Macrostate "2 particles left, 2 particles right": Ω = C(4,2) = 6 microstates match — six times more likely to be observed than the all-left macrostate, purely from counting.
With more particles this effect explodes: for ~10²³ particles, the even-split macrostate outnumbers the all-on-one-side macrostate by a factor no calculator can even display — which is the real reason gas never spontaneously crowds into one corner of a room.

1. In Boltzmann's S = k_B ln Ω, what does Ω represent?

Ω counts microstates — the exact, fully-detailed configurations — that all look identical at the macrostate level.

2. Fill in the blank: for 6 distinguishable particles independently placed in the left or right half of a box, the number of microstates matching the macrostate "3 particles left, 3 particles right" is Ω = C(6,3) = ___.

C(6,3) = 6!/(3!3!) = 720/(6×6) = 20 — far more than the single microstate matching "all 6 particles on one side."

3. Statistically speaking, is it absolutely impossible for the entropy of an isolated system to ever decrease, even briefly?

The second law is a statistical near-certainty for large numbers of particles, not an absolute mechanical prohibition — a spontaneous entropy decrease is possible in principle, just astronomically unlikely for any macroscopic system.

4. Why is entropy singled out as "the" thermodynamic quantity that defines an arrow of time, when the underlying microscopic laws (Newton's laws, electromagnetism) are all time-symmetric?

Each individual microscopic interaction is time-reversible, but the number of microstates strongly favors "spread out and mixed" over "concentrated and ordered," so macroscopic entropy overwhelmingly increases going forward in time even though no single law demands it.
