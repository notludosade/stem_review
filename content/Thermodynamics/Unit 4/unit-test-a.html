1. Which pair correctly matches each name to its statement of the second law?

Clausius is the refrigerator-flavored statement (no free "uphill" heat flow); Kelvin is the engine-flavored statement (no 100%-efficient engine) — the two are logically equivalent.

2. A gas absorbs Q = 850 J of heat during a reversible isothermal process at T = 170 K. What is ΔS?

ΔS = Q/T = 850/170 = 5.0 J/K.

3. Why is entropy described as giving physics an "arrow of time," when the microscopic laws underneath it are time-symmetric?

Each microscopic interaction is reversible, but the sheer statistical dominance of high-Ω macrostates makes macroscopic entropy increase overwhelmingly likely going forward in time.

4. A process that can be run exactly backward, retracing every intermediate state with no lasting trace anywhere, is called:

Reversibility is the idealized, dissipation-free, quasi-static limit — real processes only approach it.

5. 1.2 kg of water (c ≈ 4186 J/(kg·K)) is heated from 280 K to 320 K. Using ΔS = mc ln(T₂/T₁), what is ΔS?

ΔS = mc ln(T₂/T₁) = (1.2)(4186) ln(320/280) = 5023.2 × 0.1335 ≈ 671 J/K.

6. A proposed device claims to transfer Q_c = 200 J from a cold reservoir at T_c = 250 K to a hot reservoir at T_h = 400 K, with zero work input and no other effect. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: if this worked as claimed, the combined entropy change of the two reservoirs (ΔS_cold + ΔS_hot) would be ___ J/K.

(b) Since this total entropy change is negative, this device:

(a) ΔS_cold = −Q_c/T_c = −200/250 = −0.8 J/K; ΔS_hot = +Q_c/T_h = 200/400 = 0.5 J/K; total = −0.8 + 0.5 = −0.3 J/K. (b) A negative total entropy change for an isolated pair of reservoirs is exactly what the second law (ΔS_total ≥ 0) forbids — this restates the Clausius statement numerically.

7. A gas absorbs Q = 560 J of heat during a reversible isothermal process at T = 140 K. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: ΔS = ___ J/K.

(b) If this same Q = 560 J were instead absorbed at a much higher temperature, ΔS would be:

(a) ΔS = Q/T = 560/140 = 4 J/K. (b) The same heat spreads its "disordering effect" less at high T than at low T, so ΔS shrinks as T rises.

8. 0.8 kg of water is heated from 300 K to 340 K (c ≈ 4186 J/(kg·K)). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: ΔS = mc ln(T₂/T₁) ≈ ___ J/K.

(b) If both T₁ and T₂ were doubled instead (600 K and 680 K, the same ratio T₂/T₁), ΔS would:

(a) ΔS = (0.8)(4186) ln(340/300) = 3348.8 × 0.1252 ≈ 419.1 J/K. (b) ln(T₂/T₁) only cares about the ratio, so scaling both temperatures by the same factor leaves ΔS unchanged.

9. 8 distinguishable particles are each independently placed in the left or right half of a box. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the number of microstates matching "5 particles left, 3 right" is Ω = C(8,5) = ___.

(b) Compared to the macrostate "all 8 particles on one side" (Ω = 1), the 5-3 split is:

(a) C(8,5) = 8!/(5!3!) = 56. (b) If every microstate is equally likely, the probability of observing a macrostate is directly proportional to its Ω — 56 microstates vs. 1 means 56 times more likely.

10. A macrostate has Ω = 1000 microstates (k_B ≈ 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: using S = k_B ln Ω, S ≈ ___ J/K (scientific notation, 3 sig figs, e.g. 9.53e-23).

(b) If Ω were instead much larger, the Boltzmann entropy S = k_B ln Ω would:

(a) S = k_B ln Ω = (1.38×10⁻²³)(ln 1000) = (1.38×10⁻²³)(6.908) ≈ 9.53×10⁻²³ J/K. (b) ln is a strictly increasing function, so more available microstates always means higher entropy.
