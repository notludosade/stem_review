1. A gas releases Q = −120 J of heat (heat leaves the gas) while W = −50 J (work is done on the gas). What is ΔU?

ΔU = Q − W = −120 − (−50) = −120 + 50 = −70 J.

2. How much heat is needed to raise 1.5 kg of water from 20°C to 45°C? (c_water ≈ 4186 J/(kg·K))

Q = mcΔT = (1.5)(4186)(25) = 156,975 J ≈ 157 kJ.

3. In an isolated calorimetry mixture, if all else is equal, the substance with the greater mass will experience:

Since Q = mcΔT, the same magnitude of heat exchanged spread across a larger mass produces a proportionally smaller ΔT.

4. n = 3 mol of an ideal gas is at P = 150,000 Pa and T = 350 K. What is its volume?

V = nRT/P = (3)(8.314)(350)/(150,000) = 8729.7/150,000 ≈ 0.0582 m³.

5. A process in which a gas's temperature stays exactly constant throughout is called:

"Isothermal" literally means constant temperature — isobaric is constant pressure, isochoric is constant volume.

6. A gas undergoes a process with Q = −90 J and W = −150 J. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: ΔU for this process is ___ J.

(b) A second process reaches that same ΔU with Q = 0 instead. What must W be for this second process?

(a) ΔU = Q − W = −90 − (−150) = 60 J. (b) With Q = 0 and ΔU still 60 J: W = Q − ΔU = 0 − 60 = −60 J.

7. 0.25 kg of water at 100°C is fully vaporized into steam (L_v ≈ 2.26×10⁶ J/kg). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the heat required is ___ kJ (whole number).

(b) Compared to melting that same 0.25 kg of ice (L_f ≈ 3.34×10⁵ J/kg), vaporizing it requires:

(a) Q = mL_v = (0.25)(2.26×10⁶) = 565,000 J = 565 kJ. (b) Melting requires Q = mL_f = (0.25)(3.34×10⁵) = 83,500 J ≈ 83.5 kJ — vaporization takes roughly 6.8 times more heat.

8. 0.25 kg of water at 85°C is mixed with 0.6 kg of water at 10°C in an insulated container. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the equilibrium temperature is T_f ≈ ___ °C.

(b) Which sample experiences the larger temperature change?

(a) 0.25(85 − T_f) = 0.6(T_f − 10) → 21.25 − 0.25T_f = 0.6T_f − 6 → 27.25 = 0.85T_f → T_f ≈ 32.06°C. (b) The hot sample swings from 85 to ≈32.06 (ΔT ≈ 52.9), while the cold sample swings from 10 to ≈32.06 (ΔT ≈ 22.1) — the smaller mass changes more.

9. An ideal gas has P = 2×10⁵ Pa, V = 0.02 m³, and n = 1.2 mol. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: its temperature is T ≈ ___ K.

(b) If n were instead doubled while P and V stayed fixed, T would:

(a) T = PV/(nR) = (2×10⁵)(0.02)/[(1.2)(8.314)] = 4000/9.977 ≈ 400.9 K. (b) T is inversely proportional to n at fixed P and V, so doubling n halves T.

10. An ideal gas with n = 1 mol has P = 1.5×10⁵ Pa held fixed while its volume is 0.02 m³. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: at this instant, its temperature is T ≈ ___ K.

(b) Since P is held fixed as V changes, this process traces which shape on a PV diagram?

(a) T = PV/(nR) = (1.5×10⁵)(0.02)/[(1)(8.314)] = 3000/8.314 ≈ 360.8 K. (b) Constant pressure with changing volume is exactly the definition of isobaric — a horizontal line on a PV diagram.
