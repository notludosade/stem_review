1. What does a port number identify?

A port identifies the program; an IP address identifies the device — both are needed together.

2. Fill in the blank: by convention, a browser connects to port ___ by default for HTTPS traffic.

Port 443 is the well-known port for HTTPS; port 80 is the well-known port for plain HTTP.

3. A laptop has a browser, a video call, and an email client all sending traffic over the same IP address at once. What lets replies reach the correct program?

Demultiplexing by destination port is exactly what lets many programs share one IP address without traffic getting mixed up.

4. What does TCP's sequence number enable?

Sequence numbers are the mechanism behind TCP's ordering and loss-detection guarantees.

5. Fill in the blank: when a TCP sender doesn't receive an acknowledgment within an expected time, it assumes the segment was lost and performs a ___, sending it again.

Retransmission is TCP's response to a missing acknowledgment, part of its reliability guarantee.

6. The TCP three-way handshake establishes a connection. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the first message sent, by the client?

(b) Why is the second message called SYN-ACK instead of two separate messages?

Combining the server's SYN and its ACK of the client's SYN into one message is what makes the handshake three messages instead of four.

7. What does "connectionless" mean about UDP?

UDP skips connection setup and state tracking entirely, sending each datagram independently.

8. Fill in the blank: a single, independently sent chunk of UDP data, with no tracked relationship to any other chunk, is called a ___.

Each datagram stands alone — UDP never links them together the way TCP's sequence numbers do.

9. Two applications: (1) downloading a critical software update, and (2) a live video call. Answer both parts:

(a) Which protocol fits the software update better, and why?

(b) Which protocol fits the live video call better, and why?

Correctness-critical transfers favor TCP; real-time, loss-tolerant traffic favors UDP's lower overhead and lack of retransmission delay.

10. Fill in the blank: the protocol with lower overhead and lower latency, because it skips the handshake, sequence numbers, and retransmission entirely, is ___.

UDP's lack of connection setup and reliability machinery is exactly what gives it lower overhead than TCP.
