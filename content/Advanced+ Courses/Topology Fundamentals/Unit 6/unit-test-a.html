1. X is Hausdorff exactly when:

Disjoint open separation of any two distinct points is exactly the Hausdorff condition.

2. What role does the Hausdorff property play in the uniqueness-of-limits proof?

The disjoint neighborhoods around a and b, both eventually containing the sequence's tail, produce a direct contradiction unless a = b.

3. Fill in the blank: X is T1 exactly when every singleton {x} is ___ in X.

Closed singletons is the equivalent characterization of T1 used throughout this unit.

4. Which implication in the separation axiom hierarchy is correct?

The hierarchy runs strictly one direction: normal ⟹ regular ⟹ Hausdorff ⟹ T1, none of which reverse in general.

5. Fill in the blank: every metrizable space is guaranteed to be ___, using the same d(x,F) − d(x,G) construction proved for ℝⁿ.

Normal is the property every metrizable space inherits, since the separating-function construction only ever needs a metric to work.

6. Consider the proof that ℝⁿ is Hausdorff, for distinct x, y with r = |x−y|/2. Answer both parts:

(a) What would happen if a point z were in both B(x,r) and B(y,r)?

(b) Fill in the blank: this contradiction shows the two balls must be ___, completing the Hausdorff verification.

(a) The triangle inequality directly rules out a shared point. (b) Disjointness of the two balls is exactly what the contradiction establishes.

7. Consider the cofinite topology on an infinite set X (open sets are ∅ and sets with finite complement). Answer both parts:

(a) Is this topology T1?

(b) Fill in the blank: any two nonempty open sets must intersect, since their complements are both ___, so a union of two finite sets can never be all of an infinite X.

(a) Cofinite complements make singletons closed, satisfying T1. (b) Finite complements can never union up to cover an infinite set, forcing any two nonempty opens to intersect — blocking Hausdorff.

8. Consider the function h(x) = d(x,F) − d(x,G) used to prove ℝⁿ is normal, for disjoint closed F, G. Answer both parts:

(a) What is true of h on F?

(b) Fill in the blank: U = h⁻¹((−∞,0)) and V = h⁻¹((0,∞)) are open because they are ___ of open sets under the continuous function h.

(a) F and G being disjoint and closed forces h to be strictly negative on F. (b) Preimages of open intervals under the continuous function h are exactly what makes U and V open.

9. Consider why the indiscrete topology on a set with two or more points fails to be metrizable. Answer both parts:

(a) What property does every metrizable space have that the indiscrete topology lacks?

(b) Fill in the blank: the argument uses the ___ of "metrizable ⟹ Hausdorff" to conclude the indiscrete topology cannot be metrizable.

(a) Hausdorff is exactly the property every metric space automatically has, and the indiscrete topology lacks. (b) The contrapositive of the metrizable-implies-Hausdorff theorem is what rules out metrizability here.

10. Consider the statement of the Urysohn Metrization Theorem. Answer both parts:

(a) What three hypotheses does the theorem require?

(b) Fill in the blank: this course states Urysohn's theorem without a ___, since its standard proof needs machinery (an embedding into an infinite-dimensional cube) beyond this course's scope.

(a) Regular, Hausdorff, and countable basis are exactly the theorem's three hypotheses. (b) The theorem is stated but not proved here, given its depth.
