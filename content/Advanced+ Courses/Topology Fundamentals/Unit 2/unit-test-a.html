1. According to the open-set definition, f : X → Y is continuous when:

The definition is built on preimages of open sets specifically, since images of opens need not stay open.

2. In the proof that ε-δ continuity implies topological continuity for f : ℝ → ℝ, what does δ end up witnessing?

The δ that ε-δ continuity produces becomes exactly the radius of the ε-ball demonstrating that f⁻¹(V) satisfies the open-set condition at x.

3. Fill in the blank: continuity is defined using ___ rather than images, because f⁻¹ always commutes cleanly with unions and intersections, for any function f whatsoever.

Preimages are the well-behaved direction — images of open sets under continuous functions need not be open.

4. Which condition, if dropped from the definition of homeomorphism, is exactly what fails for f(t) = (cos t, sin t) from [0, 2π) to S¹?

f itself is a perfectly good continuous bijection — it's f⁻¹'s discontinuity at (1,0) that blocks this from being a homeomorphism.

5. Fill in the blank: since (−1, 1) ≅ ℝ via a genuine homeomorphism despite one being bounded and the other not, ___ is shown not to be a topological property.

Boundedness is a metric notion, not preserved by homeomorphism, exactly as this example demonstrates.

6. Consider Y = (0, 5] with the subspace topology from (ℝ, τstd). Answer both parts:

(a) Is (3, 5] open in Y?

(b) Fill in the blank: openness in Y is tested by writing the set as the ___ of Y with some open subset of ℝ, not by testing it directly against ℝ's own ε-ball condition.

(a) (3,6) ∩ (0,5] = (3,5], satisfying the subspace definition, even though (3,5] is not open in ℝ. (b) Intersection with an ambient open set is the entire subspace test.

7. Consider proving τY satisfies (T2), arbitrary unions stay open. Answer both parts:

(a) Which set-algebra identity makes this proof work?

(b) Fill in the blank: since ⋃Uα ∈ τ by axiom (T2) applied to ___ itself, the identity in part (a) shows the union is also in τY.

(a) Distributing the union over the intersection with Y is exactly the identity used. (b) The ambient space X's own (T2) axiom is what the proof leans on.

8. Suppose f : X → ℝ is continuous, and f only ever takes values in [0, 10]. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the codomain-restriction theorem let you conclude?

(b) Fill in the blank: the theorem requires the smaller codomain Z to contain the entire ___ of f, f(X), or the restriction wouldn't even be well-defined.

(a) Shrinking the codomain to a superset of the image preserves continuity, by the theorem. (b) Z must contain f(X), the image, or f wouldn't map into Z at all.

9. Which of these is an example of a property that is NOT a topological property (i.e. not preserved by every homeomorphism)?

Boundedness depends on an ambient metric, not on open sets alone, so it is not guaranteed to transfer across a homeomorphism — exactly what (−1,1) ≅ ℝ demonstrates.

10. Let X carry the discrete topology and Y be any topological space. Answer both parts about a function f : X → Y:

(a) Is f automatically continuous?

(b) Fill in the blank: this holds because every subset of a discrete space is ___, so f⁻¹(V) automatically satisfies the continuity condition for any V.

(a) Every function out of a discrete space is continuous. (b) Every subset of X being open is exactly why the preimage condition is automatic.
