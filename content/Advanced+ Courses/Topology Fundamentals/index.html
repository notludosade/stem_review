Unit 1 — Topological Spaces
Lesson 1

Open Sets and Topological Spaces

Generalizing "open" from ε-balls to a short list of closure axioms — the definition that lets everything Real Analysis A proved about ℝ get proved again, with no notion of distance anywhere in sight.

 Lesson 2

Bases for a Topology

A small generating set of "building-block" open sets — so a topology can be described by a handful of shapes instead of listing every open set by hand.

 Lesson 3

Closed Sets, Closure, Interior, and Boundary

The rest of Real Analysis A's Unit 3 vocabulary — now defined for any topological space, with no sequences and no distance required.

Unit 2 — Continuity and Homeomorphism
Lesson 4

Continuity via Open Sets

Real Analysis A's ε-δ definition, rewritten with no ε, no δ, and no distance — then proved to say exactly the same thing on ℝ.

 Lesson 5

Homeomorphisms

What it means for two spaces to be "the same shape" topologically — and a proof that a bounded interval and the entire real line qualify, despite looking nothing alike.

 Lesson 6

The Subspace Topology

How any subset of a topological space inherits a topology of its own — quietly already at work every time Real Analysis A discussed a function's restricted domain.

Unit 3 — Connectedness
Lesson 7

Connectedness

"No gaps to jump across," rebuilt with open sets alone — and the separation-based proof technique every connectedness argument in this course will reuse.

 Lesson 8

Path-Connectedness

A stronger, more visual cousin of connectedness — "you can walk from any point to any other without leaving the space" — and a famous space where the two properties come apart.

 Lesson 9

The Intermediate Value Theorem, Revisited

Three purely topological facts, already in hand, combine into the exact theorem Real Analysis A needed a dedicated lesson and a supremum argument to prove.

Unit 4 — Compactness
Lesson 10

Compactness via Open Covers

Real Analysis A's sequential compactness needed sequences, which need countability. This definition needs neither — and it's the one that actually survives outside metric spaces.

 Lesson 11

The Heine-Borel Theorem

Compact subsets of ℝⁿ are exactly the closed, bounded ones — reproved by bisection from the open-cover definition, and extended past ℝ to every dimension at once.

 Lesson 12

Compactness, Continuity, and the Extreme Value Theorem

Continuous functions preserve compactness — and once that's known, the Extreme Value Theorem drops out in three lines, no maximizing sequence required.

Unit 5 — Product and Quotient Spaces
Lesson 13

The Product Topology

Building a topology on X × Y out of the topologies on X and Y separately — and confirming it reproduces ℝ² exactly as expected.

 Lesson 14

The Quotient Topology

Gluing points of a space together, and topologizing the result — the machinery Lesson 15 uses to build a circle, a torus, and a Möbius strip out of nothing but a square.

 Lesson 15

Building Spaces — Circles, Tori, and Möbius Strips

Three familiar shapes, all constructed from nothing but a square (or an interval) and an instruction for which edges to glue.
