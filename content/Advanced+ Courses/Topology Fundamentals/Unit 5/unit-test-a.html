1. The product topology on X × Y is generated by which basis?

Rectangles U × V, built from opens in each factor, form the basis for the product topology.

2. Why is π_X⁻¹(U) = U × Y automatically open in the product topology, for U open in X?

Since Y is always open in its own topology, U × Y always matches the basis definition, making projections automatically continuous.

3. Fill in the blank: in the quotient topology, V ⊆ Y is declared open exactly when its ___ under q is open in X.

The quotient topology's openness test is defined entirely through the preimage q⁻¹(V).

4. Why does [0,1]/(0~1) ≅ S¹ succeed as a genuine homeomorphism, unlike Lesson 5's [0,2π) → S¹ map?

Compactness of the quotient domain is exactly the missing ingredient Lesson 5's non-compact [0,2π) lacked.

5. Fill in the blank: the Möbius strip is built by identifying (0,y) ~ (1, 1−y) — a ___ in the gluing direction — distinguishing it from the cylinder.

The flip is the single distinguishing feature of the Möbius strip's construction compared to the cylinder.

6. Consider proving ℬ = {U × V} satisfies basis axiom (B2). Answer both parts:

(a) What set-algebra identity does the proof use?

(b) Fill in the blank: U₁ ∩ U₂ ∈ τ_X and V₁ ∩ V₂ ∈ τ_Y follow from axiom (___) applied to each factor space.

(a) Distributing the intersection across the two rectangles is the key identity. (b) (T3), finite intersections of open sets stay open, applied to each factor.

7. Consider verifying the quotient topology satisfies axiom (T2). Answer both parts:

(a) What identity does the proof rely on?

(b) Fill in the blank: since each q⁻¹(V_α) is ___ in X by hypothesis, their union is too (axiom T2 for X), showing ⋃V_α belongs to the quotient topology.

(a) Preimages commute with unions for any function. (b) Open is the hypothesis on each q⁻¹(V_α), and (T2) for X carries it through to the union.

8. Consider the difference between the cylinder and torus constructions from a square. Answer both parts:

(a) How many pairs of edges does the torus construction identify, compared to the cylinder?

(b) Fill in the blank: forming a torus can be seen as first building a cylinder, then gluing together its two circular ___.

(a) Two pairs of edges for the torus versus one pair for the cylinder. (b) The cylinder's two boundary rims are exactly what get glued together to close it into a torus.

9. Consider applying the compact-to-Hausdorff theorem to a continuous bijection g : A → B. Answer both parts:

(a) What two hypotheses does the theorem require?

(b) Fill in the blank: the theorem's conclusion is that g's ___, g⁻¹, is automatically continuous, making g a genuine homeomorphism.

(a) Compact domain and Hausdorff codomain are the two required hypotheses. (b) The inverse function is what's guaranteed continuous, upgrading the bijection to a homeomorphism.

10. Consider the qualitative difference between a Möbius strip and a cylinder. Answer both parts:

(a) How many boundary curves does a Möbius strip have, compared to a cylinder's two?

(b) Fill in the blank: the Möbius strip also has only ___ side, unlike the cylinder's distinguishable inside and outside.

(a) The flip merges what would be two separate boundary edges into a single continuous curve. (b) One side — the Möbius strip's defining one-sidedness, informally described in this lesson.
