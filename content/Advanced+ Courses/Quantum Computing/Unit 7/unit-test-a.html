1. How does the classical query complexity for finding one marked item among N unstructured items scale?

With no exploitable structure, classical search must check items roughly one at a time, giving linear scaling with N.

2. What does the Grover oracle Of do to the marked item |x₀⟩?

O_f|x⟩ = (−1)^f(x)|x⟩, and f(x₀) = 1, so the marked item's amplitude is multiplied by −1.

3. Right after applying Of, the average amplitude across all items is 0.15, and the marked item's amplitude is −0.05. What is the marked item's amplitude after applying the diffusion operator D?

2⟨c⟩ − c_x = 2(0.15) − (−0.05) = 0.3 + 0.05 = 0.35.

4. Fill in the blank: √400 = ___, illustrating roughly how many Grover iterations are needed for a 400-item search space.

√400 = 20 — Grover's algorithm needs roughly this many iterations (up to a constant factor), compared to up to 400 classically.

5. What does the BBBT lower-bound theorem establish?

BBBT proves Ω(√N) is a hard floor for any quantum algorithm on unstructured search — Grover's algorithm already meets it.

6. Factoring N is reduced to finding what quantity, for a random a coprime to N?

Once the order r is known, efficient classical arithmetic recovers N's factors — the order-finding step is the one classically hard piece.

7. How does the Quantum Fourier Transform help find the period of aˣ mod N?

The QFT generalizes the same interference principle used throughout this course, specifically tuned to reveal periodic structure.

8. Fill in the blank: Shor's algorithm runs in ___ time relative to the number of digits of N, unlike the sub-exponential scaling of the best classical factoring algorithms.

Polynomial-time scaling in log N is exactly the exponential advantage that makes Shor's algorithm practically consequential.

9. What assumption does RSA's security depend on, that Shor's algorithm directly threatens?

RSA's public key includes a large composite N, and its security rests entirely on factoring N being classically infeasible — exactly what Shor's algorithm undermines.

10. Which statement accurately summarizes what this unit's three algorithms demonstrate about quantum speedups in general?

Every speedup studied comes from exploiting specific structure via interference — quantum computers are specialized tools, not general accelerators.
