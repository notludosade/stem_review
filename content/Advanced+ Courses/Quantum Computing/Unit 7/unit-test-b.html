1. For a search space of N = 1,000,000 unstructured items, roughly how many queries does classical search need in the worst case?

Without structure to exploit, the classical worst case is proportional to N itself — up to a million queries here.

2. What does the Grover oracle Of do to a non-marked item |x⟩ (where f(x) = 0)?

O_f|x⟩ = (−1)^f(x)|x⟩, and f(x) = 0 for every non-marked item, giving a factor of (−1)⁰ = 1 — no change at all.

3. Right after applying Of, the average amplitude across all items is 0.2, and the marked item's amplitude is −0.1. What is the marked item's amplitude after applying the diffusion operator D?

2⟨c⟩ − c_x = 2(0.2) − (−0.1) = 0.4 + 0.1 = 0.5.

4. Fill in the blank: √900 = ___, illustrating roughly how many Grover iterations are needed for a 900-item search space.

√900 = 30 — Grover's algorithm needs roughly this many iterations, compared to up to 900 classically.

5. Suppose a researcher claims to have found a quantum algorithm solving unstructured search in O(log N) queries. What does the BBBT theorem say about this claim?

Since BBBT establishes Ω(√N) as a hard lower bound for any quantum algorithm on this problem, and O(log N) is asymptotically smaller than Ω(√N), such a claim would contradict a proven theorem.

6. Once the order r of a modulo N has been found, what efficient classical step recovers N's actual prime factors?

Once r is known, ordinary efficient classical arithmetic (greatest common divisor computations) recovers N's factors with good probability — no further quantum step is needed.

7. What is the Quantum Fourier Transform's relationship to Deutsch-Jozsa's repeated Hadamard gates?

Both rely on the same underlying idea — interference concentrating amplitude on the answer — with the QFT generalizing it specifically toward detecting periodicity.

8. Fill in the blank: for a 2048-bit number, Shor's algorithm's runtime scales ___ in the bit-length of N, not sub-exponentially like the best classical methods.

Polynomial scaling in log N is exactly what makes Shor's algorithm exponentially faster than any known classical factoring method for large N.

9. Besides RSA, which other cryptographic approach is threatened by a related Shor's-algorithm technique?

A related quantum algorithm using the same period-finding and QFT machinery efficiently solves the discrete logarithm problem underlying Diffie-Hellman and elliptic-curve cryptography.

10. This unit compares a quantum computer to a specialized coprocessor like a GPU. What point does that analogy make?

The analogy emphasizes specialization: real, powerful advantages exist, but only within a narrow class of structured problems — not as a general-purpose speedup.
