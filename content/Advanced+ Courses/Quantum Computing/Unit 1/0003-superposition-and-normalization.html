Superposition and Normalization

Not every vector in ℂ² is a valid qubit state — exactly one constraint singles out the physical ones, and it's the same constraint that will let Lesson 11 turn amplitudes into probabilities.

← Lesson 2 Lesson 4 → Contents Glossary →

Lesson 2 wrote a general qubit state as |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ for any α, β ∈ ℂ. That was slightly too generous. Physically, only some of these vectors correspond to states a qubit can actually be in — this lesson pins down exactly which ones, and names the phenomenon of a genuine combination of |0⟩ and |1⟩.

1. Superposition

Definition — Superposition A qubit state |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ is a superposition of |0⟩ and |1⟩ whenever both α ≠ 0 and β ≠ 0. If β = 0, the qubit is simply in state |0⟩; if α = 0, it's simply in state |1⟩ — no superposition at all, just one of the two classical-looking basis states.

"Superposition" is not a special exotic state reserved for advanced setups — it's the generic case. Almost every vector in ℂ² is a superposition of |0⟩ and |1⟩; the two basis states themselves are the rare exception.

2. The normalization condition

Rule — Normalization A vector α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ represents a valid qubit state if and only if |α|² + |β|² = 1. Equivalently, ⟨ψ|ψ⟩ = 1 — the state vector must have unit length under the inner product from Lesson 2.

This isn't an arbitrary bookkeeping rule. Lesson 11's Born rule will identify |α|² as the probability of measuring outcome 0, and |β|² as the probability of measuring outcome 1. Probabilities of mutually exclusive, exhaustive outcomes have to sum to 1 — so normalization is really just "these two probabilities add up correctly," stated one lesson before probability is formally introduced. Every valid qubit state, in other words, is a unit vector in ℂ².

Example — Checking Normalization Is |ψ⟩ = (3/5)|0⟩ + (4/5)|1⟩ a valid state? Compute |3/5|² + |4/5|² = 9/25 + 16/25 = 25/25 = 1. Yes. Is |φ⟩ = (1/2)|0⟩ + (1/2)|1⟩ valid? |1/2|² + |1/2|² = 1/4 + 1/4 = 1/2 ≠ 1. No — this vector needs to be rescaled by a factor of √2 to become valid, giving (1/√2)|0⟩ + (1/√2)|1⟩.

3. Two states worth naming now

Two particular equal-weight superpositions come up constantly enough in later units that they get their own names and symbols:

|+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩) / √2  |−⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩) / √2

Both are properly normalized: (1/√2)² + (1/√2)² = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1 for both. Both give a 50/50 split between outcomes 0 and 1 under the Born rule, since |1/√2|² = 1/2 either way — yet |+⟩ and |−⟩ are different states. Lesson 5 explains exactly what that difference is (it's not visible in the |0⟩/|1⟩ probabilities at all), and Lesson 8's Hadamard gate is the tool that produces both of them directly from |0⟩ and |1⟩.

Why normalization doesn't pin down a unique state Notice the normalization condition |α|² + |β|² = 1 is one real equation in what is really four real unknowns (the real and imaginary parts of α and β) — so it carves out a 3-dimensional surface of valid states inside ℂ² ≅ ℝ⁴, not a single point. There are infinitely many valid qubit states, not just |0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, and |−⟩. Lesson 4's Bloch sphere gives a way to see that entire 3-dimensional surface of states at once (after one further simplification Lesson 5 justifies).

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Which condition must α and β satisfy for α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ to be a valid qubit state?

The normalization condition is |α|² + |β|² = 1 — the sum of the squared magnitudes of the amplitudes must equal 1, matching the eventual requirement that the two measurement probabilities sum to 1.

2. Is |χ⟩ = (√3/2)|0⟩ + (1/2)|1⟩ a valid, normalized qubit state?

Squaring and adding gives (√3/2)² + (1/2)² = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1, so this state passes the normalization test. Amplitudes are never required to be equal or rational — only the sum of their squared magnitudes matters.

3. Which state is |−⟩?

|−⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2 — the equal-weight superposition with a relative minus sign between the two amplitudes. |+⟩ is the version with a plus sign instead.

4. Fill in the blank: the normalization condition requires |α|² + |β|² = ___.

The squared magnitudes of the two amplitudes must sum to exactly 1, matching the eventual sum of two measurement probabilities.

5. Fill in the blank: if α = 3/5 and β is a positive real number, normalization forces β = ___.

(3/5)² + β² = 1 gives β² = 1 − 9/25 = 16/25, so β = 4/5 (taking the positive root).
