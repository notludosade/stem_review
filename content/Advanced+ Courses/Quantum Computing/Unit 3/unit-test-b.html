1. For |ψ⟩ = (√3/2)|0⟩ + (1/2)|1⟩, what is P(1), the probability of measuring outcome 1?

P(1) = |1/2|² = 1/4 — square the amplitude's magnitude.

2. When measuring |ψ⟩ in an orthonormal basis {|e₀⟩, |e₁⟩} instead of the computational basis, which rule still correctly gives P(i)?

The Born rule P(i) = |⟨eᵢ|ψ⟩|² is fully general — it applies to any orthonormal basis, not just {|0⟩, |1⟩}.

3. A qubit in state (5/13)|0⟩ + (12/13)|1⟩ is measured and the outcome is 0. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the qubit's state immediately afterward?

(b) Fill in the blank: measuring the same qubit again immediately (same basis) gives outcome 0 with probability ___.

(a) The state collapses exactly to |0⟩. (b) Now exactly in state |0⟩, a second measurement returns 0 with certainty.

4. A qubit is measured and gives outcome 0. A classmate suggests measuring that same qubit five more times (same basis) to learn more about its original amplitudes. What actually happens?

A collapsed qubit stays collapsed — repeated same-basis measurement of one qubit gives no additional information about the original superposition.

5. Fill in the blank: measuring directly in the X basis is equivalent to first applying H and then measuring in the ___ basis.

H maps |+⟩ and |−⟩ to |0⟩ and |1⟩, converting an X-basis measurement into an ordinary computational (Z) basis measurement.

6. A qubit in state |0⟩ is measured in the X basis. What is P(−)?

⟨−|0⟩ = 1/√2, so P(−) = |1/√2|² = 1/2 — |0⟩ splits evenly when measured in the X basis, the mirror of |+⟩ splitting evenly in the Z basis.

7. Which of these is NOT required for an operator to represent a measurable observable?

Observables must be Hermitian, not unitary — these are different (if overlapping) conditions. Real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues both follow automatically from being Hermitian.

8. For |ψ⟩ = (√3/2)|0⟩ + (1/2)|1⟩, answer both parts:

(a) What is ⟨Z⟩?

(b) Fill in the blank: since ⟨Z⟩ is positive, measuring this state is more likely to give outcome ___.

(a) ⟨Z⟩ = |√3/2|² − |1/2|² = 3/4 − 1/4 = 1/2. (b) A positive ⟨Z⟩ means outcome 0 (eigenvalue +1) carries more weight — consistent with P(1) = 1/4 from Question 1.

9. The no-cloning proof shows that assuming a single U clones two states |ψ⟩ and |φ⟩ forces ⟨ψ|φ⟩ = ⟨ψ|φ⟩². What does this equation imply about ⟨ψ|φ⟩?

x = x² has only the solutions x = 0 and x = 1 — so ⟨ψ|φ⟩ is forced to one of exactly those two values, ruling out cloning for any other pair of states.

10. Fill in the blank: two states can be cloned by the same fixed device only if they are identical (up to global phase) or ___ to each other.

Orthogonal states (⟨ψ|φ⟩ = 0) are the other case the no-cloning proof leaves open — exactly why classical bit values, which are orthogonal, can be freely copied.
