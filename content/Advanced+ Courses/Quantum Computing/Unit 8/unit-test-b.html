1. A qubit left idle for too long gradually loses its superposition due to stray electromagnetic fields in the lab. What is this process called?

Decoherence is exactly this loss of quantum information from unwanted, uncontrolled environmental interaction over time.

2. Which coherence time measures how quickly a qubit prepared in |1⟩ decays toward |0⟩ due to energy loss?

T1 tracks energy relaxation specifically — how long before a qubit decays toward its lower-energy state.

3. A circuit with 500 sequential gates, each individually correct 99.8% of the time, is run on real hardware. What is the most accurate expectation?

Errors compound across sequential gates — a long circuit built from imperfect gates can have a substantially worse overall success rate than any single gate's fidelity alone suggests.

4. Answer both parts about quantum error correction's core challenge:

(a) Why can't error correction just directly measure each data qubit to check whether it flipped?

(b) Fill in the blank: making several truly independent copies of an unknown qubit, as a classical repetition code would, is forbidden by the ___ theorem.

(a) Direct measurement causes collapse, exactly what syndrome measurement is designed to avoid. (b) The no-cloning theorem is the specific obstacle ruling out independent copies.

5. In the 3-qubit bit-flip code, a syndrome measurement returns (1, 1). Which physical qubit needs correcting?

The syndrome table gives (0,0) for no error, (1,0) for qubit 1, (1,1) for qubit 2, and (0,1) for qubit 3.

6. According to the fault-tolerance threshold theorem, what determines whether arbitrarily long, reliable quantum computation is eventually possible?

The threshold theorem's condition is specifically about the physical error rate per gate falling below a fixed threshold — below it, sufficient encoding overhead can suppress logical error rates arbitrarily.

7. Superconducting qubits require which of the following to function?

Superconducting circuits built from Josephson junctions require extreme, continuous cooling to millikelvin temperatures to exhibit usable quantum behavior.

8. What does randomized benchmarking specifically measure?

Randomized benchmarking is a technique for isolating average gate error rates specifically, complementing composite benchmarks like Quantum Volume.

9. Fill in the blank: the physicist who coined the term NISQ to describe the current era of quantum hardware is John ___.

John Preskill introduced NISQ (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum) as the standard term for today's hardware era.

10. Which statement best summarizes this course's closing assessment of where quantum computing stands today?

The course's closing position holds both truths at once: rigorous, already-useful theory, alongside hardware that is real and improving but still short of large-scale fault tolerance.
