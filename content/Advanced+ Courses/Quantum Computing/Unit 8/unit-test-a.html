1. What is decoherence?

Decoherence is specifically about unwanted, uncontrolled environmental interaction degrading a qubit's quantum information over time.

2. Which coherence time measures how long relative phase information survives?

T2 specifically tracks how long relative phase survives — the fragile quantity every interference-based algorithm depends on — while T1 tracks energy relaxation.

3. Why do gate errors become a bigger problem as circuit depth increases?

Even gates that are individually accurate most of the time compound their errors across a long circuit, making the overall probability of a fully correct run drop sharply with depth.

4. Answer both parts about quantum error correction's core challenge:

(a) Why can't quantum error correction simply make independent copies of a qubit, unlike a classical repetition code?

(b) Fill in the blank: directly measuring the data qubits to check for errors would cause ___, destroying the encoded superposition.

(a) No-cloning rules out independent copies of an unknown state. (b) Direct measurement causes collapse, which is exactly what syndrome measurement via ancillas is designed to avoid.

5. In the 3-qubit bit-flip code, a syndrome measurement returns (0, 1). Which physical qubit needs correcting?

The syndrome table gives (0,0) for no error, (1,0) for qubit 1, (1,1) for qubit 2, and (0,1) for qubit 3.

6. Fill in the blank: the theorem stating that arbitrarily long, reliable quantum computation becomes possible once physical error rates drop below a fixed value is called the ___ theorem.

The fault-tolerance threshold theorem is the proven basis for believing large-scale, reliable quantum computation is eventually achievable.

7. Trapped-ion qubits are primarily manipulated using which tool?

Trapped-ion qubits are held by electromagnetic fields and controlled with lasers — microwave pulses in a dilution refrigerator describe superconducting qubits, and optical tweezers describe neutral-atom qubits.

8. Fill in the blank: IBM's composite benchmark combining qubit count, connectivity, and error rates into a single number is called ___.

Quantum Volume measures the largest random circuit a processor can run and verify correctly, rewarding genuine overall capability rather than qubit count alone.

9. What does NISQ stand for?

NISQ (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum), coined by John Preskill, describes the current era's hardware: meaningful but modest qubit counts, still too noisy for large fault-tolerant algorithms.

10. What happened after Google's 2019 quantum supremacy claim with its Sycamore processor?

The claim received substantial follow-up scrutiny, including improved classical approaches — exactly the normal, healthy process of testing a bold scientific claim.
