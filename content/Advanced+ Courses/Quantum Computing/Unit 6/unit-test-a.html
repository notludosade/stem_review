1. What does the oracle Uf do to |x⟩|y⟩?

The oracle's defining action is U_f|x⟩|y⟩ = |x⟩|y⊕f(x)⟩ — a reversible way to compute f without disturbing the input register.

2. What is the result of applying Uf to |+⟩ ⊗ |0⟩?

One query to U_f, applied to a superposed input, evaluates f at both 0 and 1 simultaneously.

3. Answer both parts about phase kickback:

(a) What state must the target register be prepared in for phase kickback to work?

(b) Fill in the blank: for f(x) = 0, Uf|x⟩|−⟩ = (___)|x⟩|−⟩.

(a) |−⟩ is the specific target state that produces a phase factor instead of altering the target register. (b) (−1)^f(x) with f(x) = 0 gives (−1)⁰ = 1 — the state is left completely unchanged.

4. Why isn't quantum parallelism alone (without phase kickback and interference) enough for a speedup?

Without further processing before measurement, collapse throws away every branch except one, exactly matching a classical random single query in usefulness.

5. How many oracle queries does any classical deterministic algorithm need to determine whether a single-bit function f is constant or balanced, with certainty?

Knowing only one of f(0) or f(1) never determines constant-versus-balanced with certainty — both must be checked classically.

6. In Deutsch's algorithm, measuring the first qubit and obtaining outcome 0 indicates that f is:

The trace shows outcome 0 occurs with certainty exactly when f is constant, and outcome 1 occurs with certainty exactly when f is balanced.

7. Fill in the blank: the Deutsch-Jozsa promise requires f to be either constant or ___ — output 0 on exactly half of all inputs, output 1 on the other half.

Balanced is the technical name for this exact-half-and-half output pattern that the algorithm is promised to distinguish from constant.

8. When f is balanced, what is the probability of measuring the all-zero string at the end of the Deutsch-Jozsa circuit?

The +1 and −1 contributions from a balanced f's inputs cancel exactly, leaving zero amplitude, and hence zero probability, on the all-zero outcome — regardless of n.

9. What does "query complexity" measure?

Query complexity specifically counts oracle calls, giving a clean, implementation-independent way to compare classical and quantum algorithms.

10. How does the Deutsch-Jozsa query-complexity separation compare, in terms of certainty, to the P ≠ NP conjecture?

Both the quantum and classical query-complexity bounds for Deutsch-Jozsa are rigorously proven — unlike P vs NP, no unverified assumptions are involved.
