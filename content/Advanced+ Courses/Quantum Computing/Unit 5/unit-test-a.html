1. What is CNOT|11⟩ (control = 1, target = 1)?

With control = 1, CNOT flips the target: 1 becomes 0, giving |10⟩.

2. What does CNOT give when applied to [(3/5)|0⟩ + (4/5)|1⟩] ⊗ |0⟩?

CNOT flips the target only on the |1⟩ branch of the control's superposition: (3/5)CNOT|00⟩ + (4/5)CNOT|10⟩ = (3/5)|00⟩ + (4/5)|11⟩, an entangled state.

3. Tracing |11⟩ through H (on qubit 1) then CNOT, what state results?

H|1⟩⊗|1⟩ = (|01⟩−|11⟩)/√2, then CNOT flips the second term's target: (|01⟩−|10⟩)/√2 = |Ψ⁻⟩.

4. Answer both parts about CZ:

(a) What is CZ|10⟩?

(b) Fill in the blank: CZ|11⟩ = (___)|11⟩.

(a) CZ only touches |11⟩, so |10⟩ is left completely unchanged. (b) CZ multiplies |11⟩ specifically by −1.

5. What is SWAP|10⟩?

SWAP exchanges the two qubits' values, turning |10⟩ into |01⟩.

6. Fill in the blank: SWAP can be built from exactly ___ CNOT gates, alternating which qubit is the control.

CNOT(1→2), then CNOT(2→1), then CNOT(1→2) — three alternating CNOTs reproduce SWAP exactly.

7. What is Toffoli|1, 1, 0⟩ (both controls 1, target 0)?

Both controls are 1, so the target flips from 0 to 1 — Toffoli|1,1,0⟩ = |1,1,1⟩, exactly the AND of the two controls written into the target.

8. Trace |00⟩ through: H applied to qubit 2, then CNOT with qubit 2 as control and qubit 1 as target. What is the final state?

H on qubit 2 gives (|00⟩+|01⟩)/√2. CNOT with qubit 2 as control leaves |00⟩ alone (control 0) and flips qubit 1 in |01⟩ to give |11⟩ (control 1) — result (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2 = |Φ⁺⟩.

9. What does it mean for a set of quantum gates to be universal?

Universality means arbitrarily close approximation of any unitary with a finite circuit — a necessary standard given that quantum gates form a continuous family.

10. Fill in the blank: the standard three-gate universal set for quantum computing is {H, ___, CNOT}.

T, combined with H, generates enough single-qubit rotations to densely approximate any single-qubit gate, while CNOT supplies the necessary entangling power.
