Reading and Building Quantum Circuits

One longer circuit, mixing single- and multi-qubit gates, traced completely from |00⟩ to a final measurement — everything this unit has built, in one worked example.

← Lesson 23 Lesson 25 → Contents Glossary →

Lesson 2.10 established the order-reversal rule for single-qubit circuits. Multi-qubit circuits follow the exact same rule, just with more wires to track at once — and two more notational pieces worth having names for: the measurement symbol, and the classical wire.

1. Two new notation pieces

Convention — Measurement and Classical Wires A meter symbol on a wire marks a measurement at that point in the circuit. After a measurement, the classical outcome is sometimes carried forward on a classical wire, conventionally drawn as a double line, to control a later gate (applying it only if the classical bit equals 1, for instance) — this pattern is exactly how quantum teleportation, which Lesson 22 gestured at, uses a measurement result to decide a correction gate.

2. A full worked trace

Trace — Step by Step Start: |00⟩. Apply X to qubit 2: |0⟩ ⊗ X|0⟩ = |01⟩. Apply H to qubit 1: (H|0⟩) ⊗ |1⟩ = |+⟩ ⊗ |1⟩ = (|01⟩ + |11⟩)/√2. Apply CNOT (qubit 1 control, qubit 2 target): CNOT|01⟩ = |01⟩ (control 0, unchanged), CNOT|11⟩ = |10⟩ (control 1, target flips) — giving (|01⟩ + |10⟩)/√2 = |Ψ⁺⟩. Apply Z to qubit 1: Z leaves the |0⟩ term of |01⟩ untouched, but multiplies the |1⟩ term of |10⟩ by −1 — giving (|01⟩ − |10⟩)/√2 = |Ψ⁻⟩. The final state, right before measurement, is the Bell state |Ψ⁻⟩.

Measuring both qubits now gives perfectly anti-correlated, individually random outcomes — exactly Lesson 19's entanglement signature, arrived at here through a circuit mixing single-qubit gates (X, H, Z) with the two-qubit CNOT, traced using the same left-to-right, then-reversed-into-a-matrix-product rule as any single-qubit circuit.

Why tracing basis-state-by-basis-state beats writing out the full matrix A two-qubit circuit corresponds to an 4×4 matrix product, and a five-qubit circuit to a 32×32 one — multiplying these out directly gets unwieldy fast. Tracing a superposition term by term through each gate, as this example did, scales far better by hand: apply each gate to each basis term currently present, using the single- or two-qubit rule directly, and only worry about the handful of terms that are actually there rather than a full matrix. This is the practical skill worth building fluency in before Unit 6's circuits add even more gates.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. In the traced circuit, what is the state after applying X to qubit 2 and then H to qubit 1 (before CNOT)?

X on qubit 2 first gives |01⟩; H on qubit 1 then gives |+⟩ ⊗ |1⟩ = (|01⟩+|11⟩)/√2.

2. Continuing the trace, what is the state right after CNOT (before the final Z)?

CNOT leaves |01⟩ alone (control 0) and flips |11⟩'s target to give |10⟩ (control 1), producing (|01⟩+|10⟩)/√2 = |Ψ⁺⟩.

3. What is the final state of the full circuit, after the closing Z gate on qubit 1?

Z leaves |01⟩'s qubit-1-is-0 term alone but multiplies |10⟩'s qubit-1-is-1 term by −1, giving (|01⟩−|10⟩)/√2 = |Ψ⁻⟩.

4. Fill in the blank: the symbol marking a measurement at a specific point in a circuit diagram is called a ___ symbol.

A meter symbol marks where in the circuit a qubit is measured, converting a quantum wire's contents into a classical outcome.

5. Fill in the blank: a double line in a circuit diagram represents a ___ wire, carrying a measurement outcome forward to possibly control a later gate.

A classical wire (double line) is the standard way to show a measured, classical bit feeding forward into the rest of the circuit — exactly the mechanism quantum teleportation relies on.
