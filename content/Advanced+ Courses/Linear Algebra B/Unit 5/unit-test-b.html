1. Which of the following is guaranteed by Cauchy-Schwarz for any u,v?

The full statement includes both the inequality and its exact equality condition — linear dependence, not any relationship about sign or equal norms.

2. Fill in the blank: the inner product axiom ⟨u,v⟩=⟨v,u⟩ is called ___.

Symmetry (conjugate symmetry over ℂ) is exactly this axiom.

3. Suppose {u₁,u₂,u₃} are nonzero and pairwise orthogonal in a 5-dimensional space V. What can you conclude?

Orthogonality of nonzero vectors guarantees independence, but 3 vectors can never span a 5-dimensional space — nothing here concerns norm or eigenvectors.

4. For v₁=(0,1,1), v₂=(1,0,1), Gram-Schmidt gives u₁=(0,1,1) and u₂ = (1, −1/2, ___).

⟨v₂,u₁⟩=1, ⟨u₁,u₁⟩=2, so u₂ = (1,0,1) − (1/2)(0,1,1) = (1, −1/2, 1/2).

5. Which statement about W⊥ is always true?

0 is orthogonal to everything, so it's always in W⊥ — the other claims are false in general (e.g. W⊆W⊥ would force W∩W⊥={0} to fail unless W={0}).

6. For u=(1,2,2), v=(2,−1,2) in ℝ³. Answer both parts:

(a) What is ⟨u,v⟩?

(b) ‖u‖=‖v‖=3. Cauchy-Schwarz predicts |⟨u,v⟩| ≤ ___.

(a) ⟨u,v⟩ = 1(2)+2(−1)+2(2) = 2−2+4 = 4. (b) ‖u‖‖v‖ = 3×3 = 9, and indeed 4 ≤ 9.

7. For v₁=(3,0,0), v₂=(1,0,1). Answer both parts:

(a) What is u₁?

(b) u₂ = (0,0,___).

(a) u₁ = v₁ = (3,0,0) always. (b) ⟨v₂,u₁⟩=3, ⟨u₁,u₁⟩=9, so u₂ = (1,0,1) − (1/3)(3,0,0) = (0,0,1).

8. Why is W+W⊥ = V, rather than possibly a smaller subspace? Answer both parts:

(a) What is the key reason?

(b) Fill in the blank: if dim(W)=3 and dim(V)=7, then dim(W⊥) = ___.

(a) The extended-and-Gram-Schmidt-processed basis is, by construction, a full basis of V, split exactly between W and W⊥. (b) dim(W⊥) = dim(V) − dim(W) = 7 − 3 = 4.

9. For W=span{(0,1,0)} and v=(3,4,5). Answer both parts:

(a) What is projW(v)?

(b) v − projW(v) = (3, 0, ___).

(a) projW(v) = (⟨v,e₂⟩/⟨e₂,e₂⟩)e₂ = 4(0,1,0) = (0,4,0). (b) (3,4,5)−(0,4,0) = (3,0,5), orthogonal to (0,1,0).

10. In the Triangle Inequality's proof. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is the Cauchy-Schwarz bound on ⟨u,v⟩ essential to the proof, rather than something that could just be assumed?

(b) Fill in the blank: taking square roots of both sides of ‖u+v‖² ≤ (‖u‖+‖v‖)² is valid because both sides are ___.

(a) Cauchy-Schwarz is exactly the theorem guaranteeing the needed bound on the cross term. (b) Square roots preserve inequalities only when both sides are already nonnegative, which norms squared always are.
