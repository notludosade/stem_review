# Advanced Algorithms Resources

## Knowledge

- [Book: _Introduction to Algorithms_ (4th ed.) — Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest & Stein (CLRS)](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262046305/introduction-to-algorithms/)
  The standard reference for every topic in this course — recurrences, greedy proofs, graph algorithms, flow, NP-completeness. Use for: the primary "go to the source" citation in nearly every lesson.
- [Book: _Algorithm Design_ — Jon Kleinberg & Éva Tardos](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/algorithm-design/P200000003142)
  Gentler exposition than CLRS with excellent greedy/exchange-argument and NP-completeness chapters. Use for: Units 3 (Greedy & MST) and 8 (Complexity & Intractability).
- [MIT OCW 6.046J: Design and Analysis of Algorithms](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-046j-design-and-analysis-of-algorithms-spring-2015/)
  Full lecture notes and video for the exact university course this lesson sequence mirrors. Use for: pacing and topic ordering, and as a video supplement per lesson.
- [Stanford CS161/CS261 lecture notes (via Stanford CS Theory group public course pages)](https://cs161.stanford.edu/)
  Clear treatment of amortized analysis (potential method) and network flow. Use for: Unit 1 (amortized analysis) and Unit 4 (flow).
- [USACO Guide](https://usaco.guide/)
  Competitive-programming-oriented but rigorous coverage of segment trees, Fenwick trees, and advanced graph algorithms with runnable code. Use for: Unit 6 (Advanced Data Structures) implementation detail.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/algorithms](https://reddit.com/r/algorithms)
  Active community for algorithm design questions and proof review. Use for: sanity-checking a correctness proof or asking why an approach doesn't meet a stated bound.
- [Codeforces](https://codeforces.com/)
  The highest-signal competitive-programming community and problem archive; editorials frequently include full proofs. Use for: applying a technique from this course against a fresh, uncited problem — the wisdom step this course's lessons can't fully substitute for.

## Gaps

- No single high-quality free resource covers computational geometry or suffix arrays at the depth this course would need if it went further — flagged in the capstone as an open area rather than under-cited here.
