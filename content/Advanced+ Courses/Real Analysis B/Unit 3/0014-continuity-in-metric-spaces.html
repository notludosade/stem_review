Continuity in Metric Spaces

The ε–δ definition of continuity generalizes to any two metric spaces by simply replacing |·| with d. What's surprising is how far it can be pushed: continuity turns out to be expressible with no numbers at all — only open sets.

← Lesson 13 Lesson 15 → Contents Glossary →

Definition — Continuity Between Metric Spaces Let (X, dX) and (Y, dY) be metric spaces. A function f: X → Y is continuous at x₀ ∈ X if for every ε > 0 there is δ > 0 such that dX(x, x₀) < δ implies dY(f(x), f(x₀)) < ε. f is continuous if it is continuous at every point of X.
Why this is exactly Real Analysis A's definition, with d in place of |·| Replace dX(x,x₀) with |x−x₀| and dY(f(x),f(x₀)) with |f(x)−f(x₀)|, and this is precisely Real Analysis A's ε–δ definition of continuity (Unit 4, Lesson 20). Nothing about ℝ's algebra was ever needed — only that "close" is measured by a metric on each side.
Theorem — Sequential Characterization of Continuity f is continuous at x₀ if and only if for every sequence (xₙ) in X with xₙ → x₀, it follows that f(xₙ) → f(x₀) in Y.
Why no new proof is needed here This is the metric-space restatement of Real Analysis A's sequential characterization (Unit 4, Lesson 20) — the proof there used only the triangle inequality and the ε–δ definition, neither of which referenced ℝ's algebra, so it carries over unchanged with dX and dY replacing |·|.

The next theorem goes further than anything possible in Real Analysis A, because it eliminates ε and δ — and the metric itself — from the definition entirely.

Theorem — Topological Characterization of Continuity f: X → Y is continuous (at every point of X) if and only if for every open set V ⊆ Y, the preimage f⁻¹(V) is open in X.
Proof (⇒) Suppose f is continuous everywhere, and let V ⊆ Y be open. Let x₀ ∈ f⁻¹(V), so f(x₀) ∈ V. Since V is open, some ball B(f(x₀), ε) ⊆ V. By continuity at x₀, there is δ > 0 such that dX(x,x₀) < δ implies dY(f(x),f(x₀)) < ε — that is, f(B(x₀,δ)) ⊆ B(f(x₀),ε) ⊆ V, so B(x₀,δ) ⊆ f⁻¹(V). Since x₀ ∈ f⁻¹(V) was arbitrary, f⁻¹(V) is open.

(⇐) Suppose f⁻¹(V) is open for every open V ⊆ Y. Fix x₀ ∈ X and ε > 0. The ball B(f(x₀), ε) is open in Y (Lesson 12), so f⁻¹(B(f(x₀), ε)) is open in X and contains x₀ (since f(x₀) ∈ B(f(x₀), ε)). Being open and containing x₀, it contains some ball B(x₀, δ). So dX(x,x₀) < δ implies x ∈ f⁻¹(B(f(x₀),ε)), i.e. dY(f(x), f(x₀)) < ε. This is exactly continuity at x₀, and x₀ was arbitrary. ∎
Why this theorem is the payoff of the whole unit so far Continuity was originally defined entirely in terms of distances getting small. This theorem shows it can be restated using only the concept of an open set — no distances, no ε, no δ. Lessons 11–13 built balls, open sets, and convergence directly from a metric d; this theorem reveals that continuity itself doesn't actually need d once open sets are in hand, only the collection of which sets count as open. (Making that observation completely precise — studying "open sets" with no metric underneath them at all — is the subject of general topology, a step beyond this course's scope.)
Example: continuity in the discrete metric is automatic Let X have the discrete metric (Lesson 11) and let (Y, dY) be any metric space. Every subset of X is open (Lesson 12), so in particular f⁻¹(V) is open in X for every V ⊆ Y, trivially — regardless of what f actually is. By the Topological Characterization, every function out of a discrete metric space is continuous, no matter how wild.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. What does the Topological Characterization say continuity is equivalent to?

The characterization is about preimages of open sets, not images — and it holds in exactly one direction (preimage, not image).

2. Why does every function out of a discrete metric space turn out to be continuous?

Since every subset of X is open when X carries the discrete metric, the preimage condition in the Topological Characterization is satisfied vacuously.

3. Why does the sequential characterization from Real Analysis A carry over to metric spaces with no new proof?

Just as with open balls in Lesson 12, the proof only ever used the metric axioms, so it transfers verbatim with d in place of absolute value.

4. Fill in the blank: f is continuous if and only if the preimage of every ___ set is ___ (same word both blanks).

Both blanks are "open" — the Topological Characterization states continuity purely in terms of open sets.

5. Fill in the blank: the theorem stating f is continuous at x₀ iff f(xₙ) → f(x₀) for every sequence xₙ → x₀ is called the ___ characterization of continuity.

This is the sequential characterization, generalized directly from Real Analysis A.
