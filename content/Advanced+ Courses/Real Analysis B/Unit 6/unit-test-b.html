1. Let φ = 2χ_{[1,3]} + 7χ_{(3,5]} be in canonical form. What is ∫_{[1,5]} φ dm?

∫φdm = Σᵢaᵢm(Eᵢ∩E) = 2·m([1,3]) + 7·m((3,5]) = 2·2 + 7·2 = 4 + 14 = 18.

2. Let f be Dirichlet's function, restricted to [0,1]. Answer both parts:

(a) What is ∫₀¹ f dm?

(b) Fill in the blank: in canonical form f = 1·χ_ℚ + 0·χ_{[0,1]\ℚ}, and since m(ℚ∩[0,1]) = ___, the integral is exactly 0.

(a) ∫₀¹fdm = 1·m(ℚ∩[0,1]) + 0·m([0,1]\ℚ) = 1·0 + 0·1 = 0. (b) m(ℚ∩[0,1]) = 0 exactly because ℚ is countable — this single fact is what makes f trivially integrable, unlike in the Riemann framework.

3. Let f(x) = x − 1 on [0,2]. Answer both parts:

(a) On which subinterval is f⁻ = max(−f,0) nonzero?

(b) Fill in the blank: since f is bounded and [0,2] has finite measure, f is automatically Lebesgue ___ there.

(a) f(x) = x−1 is negative exactly on [0,1), where −f = 1−x ≥ 0, so f⁻(x) = 1−x there and 0 on [1,2]. (b) A measurable function bounded on a finite-measure set is automatically Lebesgue integrable, with no further conditions needed.

4. Which lemma shows that a bounded measurable function on a finite-measure set is always Lebesgue integrable?

Bounding |f| by the simple function M·χ_E and applying monotonicity of the simple integral gives ∫|f|dm ≤ M·m(E), finite exactly when m(E) is finite — which is exactly what makes f Lebesgue integrable.

5. In the proof of MCT, why is c chosen strictly less than 1 (rather than c = 1) when defining Eₙ = {fₙ ≥ cφ}?

Taking c = 1 would only guarantee fₙ(x) ≥ φ(x) in the limit, not after any finite n — strict inequality is what lets the covering claim go through, and c is sent to 1 only afterward.

6. Let fₙ = (2 − 1/n)·χ_{[0,3]}. This sequence increases, with fₙ → 2·χ_{[0,3]} pointwise. Answer both parts:

(a) What is ∫fₙ dm?

(b) Fill in the blank: by MCT, lim_n ∫fₙ dm = ∫(2χ_{[0,3]})dm = 2·m([0,3]) = ___.

(a) ∫fₙdm = (2−1/n)·m([0,3]) = (2−1/n)·3 = 6 − 3/n. (b) As n → ∞, 6−3/n → 6, matching 2·m([0,3]) = 2·3 = 6 exactly, as MCT guarantees.

7. Let fₙ = 2n·χ_{(0,1/n)}. Answer both parts:

(a) What is ∫fₙ dm, for every n?

(b) Fill in the blank: fₙ → 0 pointwise, yet ∫fₙdm stays fixed at 2, showing limits and integrals cannot be swapped here without extra hypotheses — precisely because no integrable function can ___ every fₙ at once, since sup_nfₙ(x) grows unboundedly near 0.

(a) ∫fₙdm = 2n·m((0,1/n)) = 2n·(1/n) = 2 for every n. (b) A function dominating this whole family would need to bound peaks of height 2n squeezed into a shrinking interval near 0 — no integrable function can do that, exactly the hypothesis DCT requires.

8. In the moving-bump family (fₙ = n·χ_{(0,1/n)}, or scaled variants), why is Fatou's Lemma's inequality strict rather than an equality?

The bump's area escapes into a shrinking interval near 0 rather than disappearing entirely, so the integral of the limit (0) is strictly less than the limit of the integrals (the fixed area) — exactly the gap Fatou's Lemma leaves open.

9. Fill in the blank: the capstone highlights Lesson 26's theorem that pointwise limits of measurable functions stay measurable. The Riemann-era failure this directly repairs is that pointwise limits of Riemann integrable functions need not preserve ___.

The fₙ example from Lesson 22 showed Riemann integrability itself could vanish under a pointwise limit; measurability, by contrast, is always preserved, no matter what.

10. Answer both parts, reflecting on the whole course's dependency chain:

(a) The capstone notes that Lesson 30's moving-bump construction achieves something Lesson 4 (Unit 1) could only describe. What is it?

(b) Fill in the blank: the capstone traces two projects converging on the same destination — generalizing convergence (Units 1–4) and rebuilding the ___ (Units 5–6).

(a) Unit 1 Lesson 4 could only call the moving bump "beyond this course's scope"; with Lebesgue measure available, it becomes fₙ = n·χ_{(0,1/n)}, a two-line computation with a precise explanation of the failure. (b) Units 1–4 generalized the machinery around the Riemann integral; Units 5–6 questioned the integral itself and rebuilt it from measure.
