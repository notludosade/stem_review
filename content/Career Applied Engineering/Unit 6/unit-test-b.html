1. A recent engineering graduate wants to know what their FE exam pass actually entitles them to do professionally. What is the accurate answer?

The FE is an early knowledge check; the full PE license requires the later exam plus qualifying supervised experience.

2. A licensing board explains that the PE exam cannot be taken until an engineer has completed several years of qualifying work under a licensed PE. Why does the exam require that prerequisite?

Real-world judgment can't be verified any earlier than after years of supervised practice, which is exactly why the experience requirement exists.

3. An engineer is deciding between two roles: one where they'd become the organization's go-to expert on a single failure mode, and one where they'd need working competence across several subsystems at once. Which path does each role represent?

Deep, narrow expertise is the specialist path; working competence across many areas is the generalist path.

4. A hiring manager assumes that whoever is the strongest individual engineer on the team will automatically also be the best fit for an open management role. What does the lesson say about this assumption?

Deep focus and independent problem-solving, which drive individual excellence, aren't the same as communication, delegation, and developing other people.

5. Fill in the blank: because a five-year plan is only a forecast based on today's information, an unexpected opportunity or a role that turns out to be a poor fit should lead to revising it — treating it as a ___ document rather than a fixed commitment.

A five-year plan should be revisited and revised as new information comes in, exactly like the project schedules covered earlier in the course.

6. A newly graduated engineer asks why they can't just take one comprehensive exam and become fully licensed immediately. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the actual reason licensure is split into two exams years apart?

(b) Fill in the blank: the years between the FE and PE exams exist specifically to let real-world ___ develop under supervision before it's tested.

(a) The gap between the two exams exists specifically to let real-world judgment develop under supervision. (b) That judgment is exactly what the experience-gated PE exam is built to verify.

7. An internal R&D engineer at a private manufacturing company never submits plans for public approval and has never pursued a PE license. Answer both parts:

(a) Does this mean the engineer's work is somehow less legitimate?

(b) Fill in the blank: since PE licensure is ___-specific rather than universal, an engineer's career can be entirely legitimate without ever requiring one.

(a) The legal requirement only applies to specific regulated work, not every engineering role. (b) This makes the license role-specific rather than a universal marker of legitimacy.

8. A generalist engineer, after a decade of broad systems work, decides to develop deep expertise in one specific area for the first time. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the lesson say about this kind of shift?

(b) Fill in the blank: none of these three paths is a ___, irreversible choice — many engineers move between them more than once over a career.

(a) Movement between paths, including later specialization, is common and expected. (b) None of the three paths is a locked-in, permanent commitment.

9. An engineer is choosing between two early-career job offers: one with heavy PE supervision and mentorship, one without any licensed PE oversight at all. Answer both parts:

(a) Why might this choice matter years before the PE exam is ever taken?

(b) Fill in the blank: choosing a role that provides qualifying supervised ___ early on avoids discovering, years later, that the wrong kind was accumulated.

(a) Since qualifying experience takes years to accumulate under a licensed PE, the choice of an early role has lasting consequences. (b) Confirming a role provides the right kind of supervised experience needs to happen early.

10. A student reviewing their five-year plan wonders which specific year they should schedule "work on ethical judgment." Answer both parts:

(a) What is the accurate response to this question?

(b) Fill in the blank: ethical judgment functions as a constant ___ across every year of a career, rather than a phase that gets checked off once.

(a) Ethical judgment doesn't get its own year because it applies at every stage simultaneously. (b) It's a constant backdrop, not a phase of its own.
