Kirchhoff's Rules and Combination Circuits

Two bookkeeping rules — charge in equals charge out, and energy gained equals energy lost — solve any circuit, no matter how tangled.

← Lesson 4 Lesson 6 → Contents Glossary →

Kirchhoff's Junction Rule At any junction (node) in a circuit, the total current flowing in equals the total current flowing out: ΣIin = ΣIout. This is simply charge conservation applied to a branch point.
Kirchhoff's Loop Rule Around any closed loop in a circuit, the sum of all voltage changes (EMF gains and IR drops) equals zero: Σ(voltage changes around loop) = 0. This is simply energy conservation applied to a full circuit path.
Why Kirchhoff's rules are just conservation laws in disguise Nothing new is being introduced here: the junction rule is charge conservation (whatever charge arrives at a point per second must leave per second, exactly the reasoning behind why series current is constant, Lesson 3), and the loop rule is energy conservation (a charge that returns to its starting point after a full loop must have net zero energy change, since it's back where it started). These two familiar conservation principles are powerful enough to solve any circuit, however complicated the mix of series and parallel branches.
Combination Circuits Most real circuits are a mix of series and parallel sections. Strategy: identify and collapse purely parallel or purely series sub-groups into single equivalent resistances step by step, working from the "innermost" combination outward, until the whole network reduces to one equivalent resistance.
Example: reducing a combination circuit A 10 Ω resistor is in series with a parallel combination of 6 Ω and 3 Ω. First, reduce the parallel pair: 1/Rp = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2, so Rp = 2 Ω. Then add this in series with the 10 Ω resistor: Req = 10 + 2 = 12 Ω.
Example: applying the junction rule At a junction, two currents flow in (3 A and 5 A) and two flow out, one of which is known to be 4 A. Find the unknown outgoing current. ΣIin = ΣIout ⟹ 3 + 5 = 4 + Iunknown ⟹ 8 = 4 + Iunknown ⟹ Iunknown = 4 A.

1. Kirchhoff's junction rule is a direct statement of:

The junction rule says current in equals current out at any node, which is exactly charge conservation applied at a branch point.

2. Kirchhoff's loop rule (that voltage changes around any closed loop sum to zero) follows from which reasoning?

Energy conservation guarantees a charge returning to its starting point has undergone zero net potential change, which is the loop rule.

3. A 4 Ω resistor is in series with a parallel pair of 8 Ω and 8 Ω resistors. What is the total equivalent resistance?

The parallel pair reduces to 4 Ω (1/8+1/8=1/4), then adding the series 4 Ω resistor gives 4+4=8 Ω.

4. Fill in the blank: the junction rule and loop rule, used together to solve any circuit network, are collectively known as ___.

Kirchhoff's rules are charge conservation and energy conservation, respectively, applied to circuit analysis.
