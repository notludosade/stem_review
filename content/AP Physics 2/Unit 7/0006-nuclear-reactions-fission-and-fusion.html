Nuclear Reactions: Fission and Fusion

Splitting or joining nuclei releases energy on a scale no chemical reaction can match — the closing idea of the course, and the same E = mc² that ties mass and energy together at the deepest level.

← Lesson 5 Contents Glossary →

Mass-Energy Equivalence E = mc² — mass and energy are interchangeable. In nuclear reactions, the total mass of the products is measurably less than the total mass of the reactants; this "missing" mass defect (Δm) is released as energy, E = Δmc².
Nuclear Fission Fission splits a heavy, unstable nucleus (like uranium-235) into two lighter nuclei plus extra neutrons, releasing a large amount of energy. Released neutrons can trigger fission in nearby nuclei, producing a self-sustaining chain reaction — the basis of nuclear power plants and fission weapons.
Nuclear Fusion Fusion joins two light nuclei (like hydrogen isotopes) into a heavier one, also releasing energy. Fusion powers the Sun and other stars, fusing hydrogen into helium under extreme temperature and pressure.
Why both splitting heavy nuclei and joining light nuclei release energy Nuclear binding energy per nucleon peaks around iron (mass number ≈ 56) and is lower for both very light nuclei (like hydrogen) and very heavy ones (like uranium). Moving toward that peak — either by splitting a heavy nucleus into medium-sized pieces (fission) or by joining light nuclei into a somewhat heavier one (fusion) — increases the total binding energy, and that increase in binding energy is released as the mass defect's equivalent energy (E = Δmc²). This single principle explains why fission works for heavy elements and fusion for light ones, but never the reverse.
Example: energy from a mass defect A fission reaction has a mass defect of 3.2×10⁻²⁸ kg. E = Δmc² = (3.2×10⁻²⁸)(3×10⁸)² = (3.2×10⁻²⁸)(9×10¹⁶) ≈ 2.88×10⁻¹¹ J — an enormous amount of energy from a vanishingly small mass, since c² is such a large multiplier.
Example: identifying fission vs. fusion A reaction combines two hydrogen-2 (deuterium) nuclei into one helium-4 nucleus. Since this joins light nuclei into a heavier one, this is fusion — the same basic reaction that powers the Sun.

1. Why does splitting a heavy nucleus (fission) release energy, while splitting a light nucleus would not?

Moving toward peak binding energy per nucleon (near iron) releases energy — true for heavy nuclei splitting apart, but not for light nuclei, which instead release energy by fusing together.

2. In a nuclear reaction, if the total mass of the products is less than the total mass of the reactants, what happens to the missing mass?

Mass-energy equivalence means the mass defect corresponds directly to released energy via E = Δmc².

3. A reaction has a mass defect of 2×10⁻²⁸ kg. How much energy is released (c = 3×10⁸ m/s)?

E = Δmc² = (2×10⁻²⁸)(9×10¹⁶) = 1.8×10⁻¹¹ J.

4. Fill in the blank: the process of joining two light nuclei into a heavier one, releasing energy and powering the Sun, is called ___.

Fusion releases energy by moving light nuclei toward the peak of binding energy per nucleon near iron.
