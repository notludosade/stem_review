# Mission: AP Physics 2

## Why
The second half of algebra-based physics: heat and thermodynamics, electricity and magnetism, optics, waves, and modern physics — following the College Board's AP Physics 2: Algebra-Based course framework (2025-26 revision, 7 units, numbered 9-15 in the official framework but restarting at Unit 1 here per this site's convention). Assumes AP Physics 1's mechanics is already solid; builds the rest of the introductory physics picture on top of it.

## Success looks like
- Can apply the ideal gas law and the first and second laws of thermodynamics to gases, heat engines, and everyday heat-transfer situations
- Can compute electric forces, fields, and potentials from point charges, and analyze conductors and capacitors
- Can analyze series, parallel, and combination resistor circuits using Ohm's law and Kirchhoff's rules
- Can describe magnetic forces on moving charges and currents, and apply Faraday's and Lenz's laws to induction
- Can trace rays through mirrors and lenses to locate and characterize images, and explain total internal reflection
- Can describe mechanical and sound waves, standing waves, the Doppler effect, and wave interference (including double-slit and thin-film effects)
- Can explain the photoelectric effect, wave-particle duality, atomic spectra, and basic nuclear reactions

## Constraints
- Algebra/trigonometry-based, not calculus-based — matches the real AP Physics 2 course
- Assumes AP Physics 1 (all 8 units) and Precalculus; does not assume AP Calculus BC
- Follows the College Board's 2025-26 unit structure and ordering: Thermodynamics, Electric Force/Field/Potential, Electric Circuits, Magnetism and Electromagnetic Induction, Geometric Optics, Waves/Sound/Physical Optics, Modern Physics — the same order real AP Physics 2 students see, so cross-checking against official materials shows the same shape
- Diagrams are static, labeled SVG figures (ray diagrams, circuit schematics, field-line diagrams, wave diagrams) in the style already established across the site's other physics and math courses

## Out of scope
- Calculus-based E&M (AP Physics C: Electricity and Magnetism) — no derivatives, integrals, or differential equations, no Gauss's/Ampere's law in integral form
- Fluids and rotational mechanics — already covered in AP Physics 1
- Lab work and hands-on experimental design — this course covers the conceptual and quantitative content, not lab technique
