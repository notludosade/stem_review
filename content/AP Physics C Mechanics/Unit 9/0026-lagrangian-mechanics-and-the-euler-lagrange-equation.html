Lagrangian Mechanics and the Euler–Lagrange Equation

Newton's laws track forces vector by vector. Lagrangian mechanics packages the same classical physics into kinetic energy, potential energy, and one equation that works in any convenient generalized coordinate.

← Lesson 25 Lesson 27 → Contents Glossary →

The Lagrangian For the conservative systems in this lesson, define L(q, q̇, t) = T − U: kinetic energy minus potential energy, written in generalized coordinates q and generalized velocities q̇. L is not the total mechanical energy—that is T + U. Its minus sign is what lets one equation reproduce Newton's laws.
The equation of motion For each generalized coordinate q, calculate d/dt(∂L/∂q̇) − ∂L/∂q = 0. This is the Euler–Lagrange equation. Constraints already absorbed into the choice of q do not need separate force equations, which is why the method becomes powerful for pendulums, linkages, and coupled systems.
Check: a particle in one dimension For L = ½mẋ² − U(x), ∂L/∂ẋ = mẋ, so d/dt(∂L/∂ẋ) = mẍ. Also ∂L/∂x = −dU/dx. The Euler–Lagrange equation gives mẍ + dU/dx = 0, or mẍ = −dU/dx = F. Newton's second law returns exactly.

A pendulum in one line of coordinates

For a bob of mass m on a string of length ℓ, choose q = θ. Its speed is ℓθ̇, so T = ½mℓ²θ̇². Taking the lowest point as U = 0 gives U = mgℓ(1 − cos θ). Therefore:

Pendulum derivation L = ½mℓ²θ̇² − mgℓ(1 − cos θ)
∂L/∂θ̇ = mℓ²θ̇  →  d/dt(∂L/∂θ̇) = mℓ²θ̈
∂L/∂θ = −mgℓ sin θ
Therefore mℓ²θ̈ + mgℓ sin θ = 0, or θ̈ + (g/ℓ)sin θ = 0.

1. For a standard conservative mechanical system, the Lagrangian is:

The Lagrangian is kinetic minus potential energy. Total mechanical energy is T + U, a different quantity.

2. Applying the Euler–Lagrange equation to L = ½mẋ² − U(x) produces:

This is Newton's second law because F = −dU/dx for a conservative force.

3. Why is θ a particularly useful coordinate for an ideal pendulum?

Using x and y would require enforcing x² + y² = ℓ² separately; θ builds that constraint into the coordinates.

4. Fill in the blank: the exact pendulum equation is θ̈ + ___ sin θ = 0.

Dividing mℓ²θ̈ + mgℓ sin θ = 0 by mℓ² gives θ̈ + (g/ℓ)sin θ = 0.
