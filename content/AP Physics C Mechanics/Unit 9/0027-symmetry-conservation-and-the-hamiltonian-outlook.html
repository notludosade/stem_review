Symmetry, Conservation, and the Hamiltonian Outlook

Conservation laws are not a disconnected list. In Lagrangian mechanics, each continuous symmetry leaves a coordinate absent from the physics—and makes its conjugate momentum constant.

← Lesson 26 Unit 9 Test → Contents Glossary →

When a coordinate is absent Define the generalized momentum conjugate to q as pq = ∂L/∂q̇. The Euler–Lagrange equation says dpq/dt = ∂L/∂q. If L does not explicitly depend on q, then ∂L/∂q = 0 and dpq/dt = 0. The coordinate is called cyclic, and its generalized momentum is conserved.
Translation symmetry → linear momentum A free particle has L = ½mẋ². The coordinate x is absent: shifting the entire experiment left or right changes nothing. Therefore px = ∂L/∂ẋ = mẋ is conserved. Linear momentum conservation is the mathematical trace of translation symmetry.
Rotation symmetry → angular momentum For motion in a central potential U(r), L = ½m(ṙ² + r²θ̇²) − U(r). The angle θ is absent because rotating the entire orbit changes no physics. Its conjugate momentum pθ = mr²θ̇ is therefore conserved—the angular momentum result used to derive Kepler's second law in Unit 7.
Time symmetry → energy When the rules of a system do not explicitly change with time, an associated energy quantity is conserved. For the standard conservative systems in this unit, that quantity is the familiar T + U. This is the deeper reason energy conservation applies: the same experiment performed tomorrow obeys the same laws as today.

The Hamiltonian outlook

The Hamiltonian reorganizes mechanics around coordinates qi and generalized momenta pi:

From L to H H = Σpiq̇i − L. For a particle with L = ½mẋ² − U(x), p = mẋ, so H = pẋ − L = ½mẋ² + U = T + U. This equality holds for many standard conservative systems, but it is not a universal identity for every possible Lagrangian.
Try It — Match Symmetry to Conservation

Unchanged operation: move the whole isolated experiment by the same distance.

Conserved quantity: linear momentum.

Unchanged operation: rotate the whole isolated experiment by the same angle.

Conserved quantity: angular momentum.

Unchanged operation: shift when the isolated experiment begins while keeping its conditions the same.

Conserved quantity: energy.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. If L has no explicit dependence on q, what follows?

Euler–Lagrange gives dp_q/dt = ∂L/∂q = 0, so the conjugate momentum remains constant.

2. Rotational symmetry is associated with conservation of:

When rotating the system changes no physics, the angular coordinate is cyclic and its conjugate angular momentum is conserved.

3. For L = ½mẋ² − U(x), the Hamiltonian is:

With p = mẋ, H = pẋ − L = mẋ² − (½mẋ² − U) = ½mẋ² + U.

4. Fill in the blank: a coordinate absent from the Lagrangian is called a ___ coordinate.

A cyclic coordinate produces a conserved conjugate momentum directly through the Euler–Lagrange equation.
