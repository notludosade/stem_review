Classical Mechanics: Frames and Generalized Coordinates

The first eight units were already classical mechanics. This extension makes that framework explicit, marks its limits, and describes constrained systems with only the independent coordinates they actually need.

What “classical” means Classical mechanics describes macroscopic motion when quantum effects are negligible and speeds are much smaller than the speed of light. Newton's laws, energy, momentum, rotation, oscillations, and gravitation from Units 1–8 all belong to it. Relativity and quantum mechanics do not make those results useless; they identify the regimes where classical approximations stop being accurate.
Reference frames matter An inertial reference frame is one in which a force-free object moves at constant velocity. Newton's laws take their familiar form there. An accelerating or rotating frame is non-inertial, so an observer using it must introduce apparent forces—such as centrifugal or Coriolis forces—to account for the frame's own acceleration.

Use the coordinates the system wants

A particle free to move in a plane needs two independent Cartesian coordinates, x and y. A pendulum bob also moves in a plane, but its fixed string enforces x² + y² = ℓ². The constraint removes one independent choice: the single angle θ specifies the bob's entire configuration through x = ℓ sin θ and y = −ℓ cos θ.

Generalized coordinate A generalized coordinate q is any independent variable that helps specify a system's configuration. It can be a distance, angle, or another convenient quantity. The number of independent generalized coordinates is the number of degrees of freedom. Choosing θ for a pendulum automatically satisfies the string constraint and avoids solving separately for the tension.

1. Which statement best describes Units 1–8 of this course?

Newtonian motion, energy, momentum, rotation, oscillations, and gravitation are the core subjects of classical mechanics.

2. What distinguishes an inertial reference frame?

An inertial frame is defined by Newton's first law, not by being at rest; any frame moving at constant velocity relative to an inertial frame is also inertial.

3. A planar pendulum bob is attached to a rigid support by a fixed-length string. How many degrees of freedom does it have?

The constraint x² + y² = ℓ² leaves one independent coordinate, conveniently the angle θ.

4. Fill in the blank: the pendulum angle θ is a ___ because it independently specifies the system's configuration.

A generalized coordinate need not be Cartesian; θ is the natural independent coordinate for a fixed-length pendulum.
