A Full Worked Problem: Rolling Without Slipping

Why does a solid ball always beat a hoop down the same ramp? Physics 1 could only show you the race. This lesson proves it two ways — energy and force — and gets the same answer both times.

← Lesson 22 Lesson 24 → Contents Glossary →

Setup A uniform object of mass M, radius R, and moment of inertia I = cMR² (c depends on shape: solid sphere c = 2/5, solid cylinder c = 1/2, thin hoop c = 1) rolls without slipping down an incline of angle θ from rest. Rolling without slipping means the contact point is instantaneously at rest, linking v = Rω at every instant.
Method 1: Energy Conservation Total kinetic energy is translational plus rotational: KE = ½Mv² + ½Iω² = ½Mv² + ½(cMR²)(v/R)² = ½Mv²(1 + c). Setting this equal to the gravitational potential energy lost, Mgh = ½Mv²(1 + c), and solving: v = √(2gh / (1 + c)). Smaller c (mass concentrated near the axis, like a solid sphere) gives a larger final speed.
Method 2: Force and Torque, Together Newton's second law along the incline: Mg sinθ − f = Ma (f is static friction, providing the torque). Torque about the center: fR = Iα = (cMR²)(a/R), so f = cMa. Substituting: Mg sinθ − cMa = Ma → a = g sinθ / (1 + c). Both methods agree — energy conservation gives the same physics as solving the coupled force and torque differential equations, exactly Lesson 22's point.
Why c alone decides the winner Neither M nor R appears in the final acceleration or speed formulas — only c does. A solid sphere (c = 2/5) always beats a solid cylinder (c = 1/2), which always beats a hoop (c = 1), regardless of their actual masses or sizes, because less of a low-c object's mass sits far from the axis, leaving more of gravity's energy available for translation rather than rotation.

1. Rolling without slipping requires which constraint?

Rolling without slipping means the contact point has zero velocity, which requires the translational speed v to exactly equal Rω.

2. Released from the same height on the same incline, which reaches the bottom first: a solid sphere (c = 2/5) or a hoop (c = 1)?

Smaller c means a larger acceleration (and higher final speed), and neither mass nor radius affects the outcome — only the shape factor c does.

3. A solid cylinder (c = 1/2) rolls from rest down a 20° incline. What is its acceleration (g = 9.8 m/s², sin 20° ≈ 0.342)?

a = g sinθ/(1+c) = 9.8(0.342)/1.5 = 3.352/1.5 ≈ 2.23 m/s².

4. Fill in the blank (as a formula in R and ω): the rolling-without-slipping constraint linking translational speed to angular velocity is v = ___.

v = Rω is the constraint used in both the energy and force derivations to eliminate ω in favor of v (or vice versa).
