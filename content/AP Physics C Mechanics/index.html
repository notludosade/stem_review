Unit 1: Kinematics with Calculus
Lesson 1

Position, Velocity, and Acceleration as Derivatives

AP Physics 1 gave you four kinematic equations to memorize. This course gives you one idea instead: velocity and acceleration are derivatives of position, taken once and twice.

 Lesson 2

Recovering Position and Velocity via Integration

Running the derivative chain backward — integrating acceleration to recover velocity, and velocity to recover position.

 Lesson 3

Motion in Two Dimensions with Vectors

Physics 1's "x-story and y-story, told independently" gets a formal calculus backbone: differentiate a vector by differentiating each component separately.

Unit 2: Newton's Laws with Calculus
Lesson 4

F = ma as a Differential Equation

Newton's second law looks like algebra until you remember a is d²x/dt². Then it's an equation for an unknown function.

 Lesson 5

Solving Simple Equations of Motion

Constant force reproduces Physics 1's straight-line v(t). Drag force needs separation of variables — and gives terminal velocity.

 Lesson 6

Systems of Connected Objects

A string that doesn't stretch is a calculus statement in disguise: differentiate the constraint, and the objects' accelerations lock together.

Unit 3: Work, Energy & Power
Lesson 7

Work as an Integral of Force over Distance

W = Fd cos θ only works when force stays constant. A changing force turns work into a definite integral.

 Lesson 8

The Work-Energy Theorem, Derived

Physics 1 handed you W = ΔKE as a given fact. It falls directly out of F = ma and a single u-substitution.

 Lesson 9

Conservative Forces and Potential Energy Functions

mgh and ½kx² were given as facts in Physics 1. They're antiderivatives — and the derivative that undoes them recovers the force itself.

Unit 4: Momentum & Collisions
Lesson 10

Impulse as an Integral of Force over Time

J = FΔt assumed constant force. A force that changes with time needs the same integral treatment work got in Unit 3.

 Lesson 11

Conservation of Momentum

Physics 1 stated momentum conservation as a fact. It follows directly from Newton's third law and an integral that cancels itself out.

 Lesson 12

Center of Mass and Variable-Mass Systems

Center of mass becomes an integral for continuous objects. A rocket burning fuel needs the product rule to explain why it accelerates at all.

Unit 5: Rotation & Angular Momentum
Lesson 13

Torque and Moment of Inertia via Integration

Physics 1 handed you I = ⅓ML² and told you to trust it. This lesson builds it from I = ∫r² dm.

 Lesson 14

Rotational Kinematics and Dynamics

Every idea from Units 1 and 2 has a rotational twin — θ(t), ω(t), α(t) are the same derivative chain, and τ = Iα is the same differential equation as F = ma.

 Lesson 15

Angular Momentum and Its Conservation

The figure skater who spins faster pulling her arms in isn't a special rule — it's the product rule applied to L = Iω.

Unit 6: Oscillations
Lesson 16

Simple Harmonic Motion as a Differential Equation

Physics 1 gave you x(t) = A cos(ωt) as a fact. Here it's the solution to a differential equation — checked by taking two derivatives.

 Lesson 17

Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion

E = ½kA² isn't a separate fact to memorize — substitute x(t) into KE + U and a single trig identity does the rest.

 Lesson 18

Damped and Driven Oscillators

Real oscillators lose energy to friction and can regain it dramatically if pushed at just the right rhythm — three regimes, kept qualitative.

Unit 7: Gravitation
Lesson 19

Newton's Law of Gravitation Revisited with Calculus

Physics 1 used F = GMm/r² as a magnitude-only formula. As a function of r, it becomes a conservative force with its own potential energy.

 Lesson 20

Orbital Mechanics and Kepler's Laws

Equal areas in equal times isn't a separate empirical rule — it's conservation of angular momentum, applied to a force that always points at the center.

 Lesson 21

Gravitational Potential Energy and Escape Velocity

U = mgh only works near Earth's surface. The general potential energy comes from an improper integral of F(r) out to infinity.

Lesson 22

Choosing Between Energy and Force Methods

Seven units built two complete toolkits. Neither is "more correct" — the skill left to build is knowing which one to reach for first.

 Lesson 23

A Full Worked Problem: Rolling Without Slipping

Why does a solid ball always beat a hoop down the same ramp? This lesson proves it two ways — energy and force — and gets the same answer both times.

 Lesson 24

From Algebra-Based to Calculus-Based Mechanics — What Changed

Not one physical law changed across this course. What changed is that every formula Physics 1 asked you to trust, this course showed you how to build. Course capstone.
