1. What does "axis-aligned" mean about an AABB?

Never rotating is exactly what keeps an AABB overlap test cheap.

2. Fill in the blank: updating a rigid body's velocity and position each small timestep by accumulating forces is called numerical ___.

Integration accumulates force effects across discrete timesteps.

3. Why do most teams use an existing physics engine instead of writing their own from scratch?

Difficulty and maturity of existing solutions drive teams to reuse rather than reinvent.

4. Fill in the blank: the stage that runs the expensive, exact collision test only on pairs broad phase couldn't rule out is ___.

Narrow phase runs the precise, expensive check only on surviving candidate pairs.

5. In semi-implicit Euler integration, what gets updated first each step?

Velocity updates first, feeding directly into the position update that follows.

6. A scene has 500 objects. Answer both parts:

(a) Checking every pair with exact mesh collision would require roughly...

(b) Fill in the blank: cheap AABB checks that quickly rule out most pairs before any exact test runs is called ___.

Broad phase narrows the workload dramatically before any expensive check runs.

7. A physics simulation uses a much larger timestep than usual. Answer both parts:

(a) This makes the constant-acceleration assumption within one step...

(b) At an extreme enough size, a fast-moving object could...

Larger timesteps increase per-step error and can reproduce the same tunneling problem from Unit 2.

8. A crate on a ramp needs friction, and a rope needs its joints to stay connected while swinging. Answer both parts:

(a) Both problems are examples of...

(b) By contrast, "how much damage a collision deals" is typically decided by...

Physics engines own the numerically hard simulation; game-specific meaning stays in game code.

9. Two circular objects have overlapping AABBs, but the circles themselves don't actually touch. Answer both parts:

(a) This happens because an AABB is...

(b) This is exactly why...

AABB overlap is a necessary but not sufficient condition for a true collision.

10. Answer both parts about a physics engine as a subsystem:

(a) Fill in the blank: resolving overlap and updating velocities so bodies push apart realistically is called ___.

(b) A rule like "these two bodies must stay connected at this joint" is called a...

Collision response resolves contacts; constraints maintain persistent relationships between bodies.
