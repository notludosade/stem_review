1. Fill in the blank: the umbrella term for any data capable of identifying a specific individual, whether alone or combined with other data, is ___.

PII is the umbrella term for any data capable of identifying a specific person, alone or in combination.

2. Which of these is an indirect identifier (quasi-identifier) rather than a direct identifier?

Birth date doesn't identify anyone on its own, but combined with a few other indirect identifiers like ZIP code and gender, it can narrow a population down to one person.

3. Showing only the last four digits of a customer's account number is an example of which anonymization technique?

Masking replaces part of a value — like showing only the last four digits of an account number — while leaving the rest hidden.

4. According to this unit, can bias only be introduced at the point data is first collected?

Bias can enter at multiple stages of a pipeline, not just collection — including cleaning judgment calls, analysis choices, and data that accurately records a biased past.

5. Fill in the blank: the EU regulation whose "right to be forgotten" lets individuals request that an organization delete their personal data is abbreviated ___.

GDPR is the EU's General Data Protection Regulation.

6. A university releases a "de-identified" student research dataset with names removed, but keeps exact birth date, ZIP code, and gender for each student. Answer both parts:

(a) Why can this still allow students to be re-identified?

(b) What fix would reduce this re-identification risk?

Removing direct identifiers alone is often not enough — indirect identifiers left at full precision can still be cross-referenced against other records to re-identify individuals, which is exactly why generalization reduces that risk.

7. A bank trains a loan-approval model on decades of historical lending data that accurately reflects a period of discriminatory lending practices. Answer both parts:

(a) What does this dataset represent, even though every record in it is accurate?

(b) What happens if a model is trained directly on this data without any correction?

Historical bias means the data can be a completely accurate record of a biased past — and any analysis or model built directly on it will reproduce that same pattern unless it's explicitly corrected for.

8. A fitness app trains an exercise-recommendation model mostly on usage data from people who kept the app installed for months, systematically underrepresenting people who tried it briefly and uninstalled. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the root cause here, first introduced back in Lesson 2?

(b) According to this unit, what is the broader real-world consequence beyond just an imprecise estimate?

The same root cause from Lesson 2 (a non-random subset standing in for the whole) resurfaces here with a higher-stakes consequence — not just an imprecise number, but a decision that can structurally disadvantage whoever the sample underrepresented.

9. This unit introduced several regulatory frameworks by name. Answer both parts:

(a) Which regulation specifically governs health information in the United States, including a de-identification standard?

(b) Which regulation grants California residents the right to know what personal data is collected about them and to opt out of its sale?

HIPAA governs health information specifically, including the de-identification standard from Lesson 27; CCPA grants California residents specific data-sale opt-out rights.

10. This unit distinguished "regulation" from "data governance." Answer both parts:

(a) What is "data governance"?

(b) What does regulation set, according to this unit?

Regulation is the external legal requirement; governance is how an organization actually operationalizes meeting that requirement internally.
