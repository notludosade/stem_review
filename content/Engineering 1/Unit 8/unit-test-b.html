Capstone — the Tacoma Narrows Bridge case study and a full design brief walked end to end. 10 points across every lesson in this unit — 5 questions worth 1 point, 5 worth 1 point split across two parts. Score 90% or higher to clear this unit.

1. Which statement accurately describes the cause of the original Tacoma Narrows Bridge's 1940 collapse?

No earthquake, overload, or material defect was involved — an ordinary 40 mph wind the bridge was expected to easily withstand tore it apart.

2. Fill in the blank: the bridge's own twisting motion altered how it shed wind, which altered the force acting on it, which fed back into more twisting — the same self-reinforcing pattern Unit 5 calls positive ___.

This is exactly the positive feedback loop from Unit 5's systems thinking — each cycle reinforced the next, rather than damping out.

3. The alternating aerodynamic force on the bridge deck happened to match a natural twisting mode of the structure. What was the consequence?

The force matched a natural twisting mode of the structure, so each cycle amplified rather than damped the oscillation, eventually exceeding what the steel connections could survive.

4. Fill in the blank: the investigation into the collapse directly led to routine ___-tunnel testing of scale models becoming a standard, required part of long-span bridge design.

This is a direct, traceable example of Lesson 20's principle: a documented failure becomes a permanent, binding requirement for every future project of that type.

5. The Tacoma Narrows case study is described as anchoring the whole Engineering 1 course. What makes that true?

Units and estimation, forces and equilibrium, materials and failure modes, systems thinking, and codes and ethics all appear in this one, real, well-documented failure.

6. The footbridge brief specifies pedestrians and occasional maintenance carts, a fixed budget, a 6-meter span, and exposure to moderate wind and occasional flooding. Answer both parts:

(a) According to Unit 1's reasoning, what role do these specific requirements play in the design problem?

(b) Fill in the blank: a Fermi-style ___ of the design load, done early, can immediately rule out unsuitable materials or spans before any detailed calculation is performed.

A precise problem definition and its constraints are the substance of the design problem, and an early rough estimate cheaply rules out unsuitable options.

7. For this 6-meter span, the truss design is expected to achieve the required strength using less total material than a solid beam. Answer both parts:

(a) What structural principle explains why a truss can do this?

(b) Fill in the blank: once the truss geometry and material are set, Unit 3's stress calculation, σ = F / ___, confirms each member is sized adequately for the estimated load.

Triangulated members carry load efficiently through pure tension and compression, often achieving the needed strength with less material than a solid beam.

8. Steel, treated timber, and aluminum are compared for the footbridge deck. Answer both parts:

(a) In the "eliminate, then rank" method, which factor most directly eliminates timber from consideration here?

(b) Fill in the blank: steel and aluminum, the materials that survive elimination, are then ranked on strength-to-weight, cost, and ___.

Timber is disqualified by a hard requirement (flood-driven rot risk against the budget), leaving steel and aluminum to be ranked against the remaining criteria.

9. The footbridge's calculated stress from the estimated pedestrian-and-cart load is deliberately not treated as the final design target. Answer both parts:

(a) What additional step does the team take before finalizing the design, and why?

(b) Fill in the blank: local building ___ for pedestrian bridges are consulted directly, since they encode lessons from decades of prior public-structure failures.

A factor of safety absorbs real-world uncertainty, and consulting local codes brings in decades of collective, hard-won lessons the team shouldn't have to relearn firsthand.

10. Despite the footbridge's short span making wind-driven oscillation unlikely, the team still checks for it, and later they hold firm on a safety-related code requirement when the park's budget officer pushes back. Answer both parts:

(a) Which lesson most directly explains why the team checks for dynamic effects even though the specific risk is low for this span?

(b) Fill in the blank: when the budget officer pushes to skip a safety-related requirement, Unit 7's engineering ___ principle says public safety still comes first.

The Tacoma Narrows habit of checking for missed dynamic effects and the public-first ethics principle both apply directly, even to a modest, low-risk footbridge.
