AI & Data Capstone

An open-ended build, not a quiz — this project exists to see whether you've actually proved yourself on Computer Programming 1, Computer Programming 2, Data Handling: CB, and AI Developer.

← AI & Data Pathway All Projects →

The Brief

Take a real dataset — one you collect yourself or pull from anywhere public — from raw file to a small, honestly-scoped AI or ML feature: a classifier, a simple recommender, a script that calls an LLM API over your data, whatever fits the data you have. Keep the scope small. The bar is whether you handled the data and the model honestly, not whether the model is impressive.

Deliverables

  • A dataset you cleaned yourself, with at least one documented cleaning decision and why you made it
  • At least one honest visualization of the data, plus one sentence on how it could be drawn to mislead
  • A small AI/ML feature built on the data — classical ML, a basic neural net, or a pretrained model/API call
  • Code organized the way Computer Programming 1/2 taught — functions, at least one class, a deliberately chosen data structure
  • A "known limitations" section: at least one way your model or system could fail or mislead someone, and who it would affect
  • A one-paragraph data-privacy or governance note: what would need to change if this dataset held real people's information

Reflection

Answer in your own words. There's no grading here — just save your answers below.

1. Data Handling: CB's cleaning unit — what's one row or value you decided to fix, drop, or flag during cleaning, and what would your analysis have concluded if you'd left it alone?

2. Describe one chart from your project. What's one way you could have drawn the same data to tell a more flattering, less honest story — and why didn't you, per Data Handling: CB's visualization & storytelling unit?

3. Which model or approach did you choose in AI Developer's classical ML or neural network units, and what alternative did you rule out? What made the one you picked the right trade-off for this dataset's size and shape?

4. AI Developer's responsible-AI unit: if your feature were wrong 10% of the time in production, who would notice first, and who would be hurt worst? Did that change anything about how you built it?

5. Where in this project did a data structure or function boundary from Computer Programming 1/2 save you from a mess — or where do you wish you'd applied one you skipped?

6. What's the smallest, most honest claim you can make about what your model or feature actually does — and how is that different from the claim you were tempted to make in your write-up?
