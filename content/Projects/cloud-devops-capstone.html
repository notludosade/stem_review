Cloud & DevOps Capstone

An open-ended design, not a quiz — this project exists to see whether you've actually proved yourself on Computer Programming 1, Cloud Computing A, and Cloud Computing B / DevOps.

← Cloud & DevOps Pathway All Projects →

The Brief

Pick a small application idea — yours from an earlier capstone, or a new one scoped just for this — and design the cloud architecture and CI/CD pipeline that would actually ship and run it in production. You're not writing the application itself; you're writing the infrastructure and process around it, the way a team's first DevOps hire would be asked to.

Deliverables

  • An architecture diagram or written description covering compute, storage, and networking choices, each justified against the app's actual traffic and data shape
  • A CI pipeline stage list: what runs on every commit, in what order, and what blocks a merge
  • A named deployment strategy (blue-green, canary, or rolling) with a real reason it fits this app, plus a rollback plan for when a deploy goes wrong
  • An infrastructure-as-code sketch (even pseudocode) for at least one resource, and one config-drift scenario it prevents
  • An observability plan: what gets logged, one metric that would page someone, and one that's just a dashboard number
  • A cost and vendor lock-in note: one place you deliberately accepted lock-in, and why the managed service was worth it anyway

Reflection

Answer in your own words. There's no grading here — just save your answers below.

1. Cloud Computing A's shared responsibility model splits security into "of the cloud" and "in the cloud." Where in your design does that split actually matter, and what did you do on your side of the line?

2. Which storage model (object, block, or file) did you pick for your app's main data, and what would have broken if you'd picked one of the other two instead?

3. Walk through what happens, step by step, from the moment a developer pushes a commit to the moment it's serving real traffic in your pipeline. Where's the slowest stage, and is that acceptable?

4. Why did you choose the deployment strategy you picked over the other two? What's the failure mode it's specifically protecting against?

5. Name one thing your observability plan would catch before a user complains, and one thing it would only explain after the fact. Is that split acceptable for this app?

6. Looking back across all three courses, what's the one DevOps habit you expect to keep using even outside a course — and which one did you have to force yourself to actually do here?
