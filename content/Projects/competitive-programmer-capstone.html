Competitive Programmer Capstone

An open-ended problem set, not a quiz — this project exists to see whether you've actually proved yourself on Computer Programming 1, Computer Programming 2, Computer Programming 2+, and Advanced Algorithms.

← Competitive Programmer Pathway All Projects →

The Brief

This site can't grade code, so instead of a fixed judge problem, source your own: solve a small set of unseen, contest-style problems under a self-imposed time box, then write each one up the way you'd talk through it out loud in a technical interview — including the approach you tried first and abandoned.

Deliverables

  • Pick 3 problems, one each shaped like: (a) two pointers / sliding window / binary search, (b) a graph or tree, (c) dynamic programming — pulled from a judge (LeetCode, Codeforces, etc.) or invented by you at similar difficulty
  • For each: your working solution, its time and space complexity in Big-O, and one sentence on why that complexity is what it is
  • For at least one problem: the brute-force approach you started with, and specifically why it was too slow — a concrete input size where it fails
  • For at least one problem: an exchange argument, amortized-analysis argument, or correctness argument from Advanced Algorithms justifying why your approach is actually correct, not just "it passed the examples"
  • A short interview-style write-up for one problem: the exact way you'd talk through it out loud, including a wrong turn you'd admit to

Reflection

Answer in your own words. There's no grading here — just save your answers below.

1. For your DP or graph problem, what was the tell — the specific phrase or constraint in the problem — that made you reach for that pattern instead of something else, drawing on Computer Programming 2+'s pattern units?

2. Where did Big-O actually save you — where did you catch yourself about to write something too slow, purely from reasoning about the input size before running it?

3. For the problem where you used an exchange argument, amortized analysis, or an invariant from Advanced Algorithms, explain it in plain language. What would you have missed if you'd only checked "does it pass the sample cases"?

4. Was there a moment where a basic Computer Programming 1/2 habit — naming, small functions, a specific data structure — either saved you time, or where skipping it cost you time under the clock?

5. If you'd had 5 more minutes on your hardest problem, what's the next thing you would have tried, and why do you believe that would have worked?

6. Which of these three problems would you be least comfortable seeing again in a live interview, and specifically what about it is still shaky?
