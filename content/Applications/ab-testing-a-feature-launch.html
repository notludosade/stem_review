A/B Testing a Feature Launch

Your redesigned checkout converts 12% of visitors instead of the old page’s 10%. The dashboard says “+20%,” but a decision still depends on whether that lift is a real signal or ordinary sampling noise.

← Applications STEM+ Home →

The Concept, From Data Handling: CB An A/B test randomly assigns comparable visitors to a control and a treatment. The observed conversion-rate difference is useful only relative to its standard error: larger samples reduce sampling noise, making a real difference easier to distinguish from chance.

The same lift can support different decisions

With 1,000 visitors in each group, 100 control conversions and 120 treatment conversions produce the advertised 10% and 12% rates. But the two-proportion z-score is only about 1.43, below the usual two-sided 5% cutoff of 1.96. With 10,000 visitors per group and the same rates, the z-score rises to about 4.52. The effect did not become larger; the estimate became more precise.

Statistical significance is not business significance A huge sample can make a tiny lift statistically convincing while the engineering cost still exceeds its value. A launch decision needs both: evidence that the effect is probably real and an estimate showing that the effect is large enough to matter.

Check your understanding

1. Why can 10% versus 12% be inconclusive with 1,000 visitors per group but convincing with 10,000?

More observations narrow uncertainty; they do not change the 2-percentage-point effect being estimated.

2. What should happen before treating an observed lift as causal?

Random assignment prevents pre-existing group differences from being confused with the treatment’s effect.
