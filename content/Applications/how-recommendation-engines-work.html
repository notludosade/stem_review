How Recommendation Engines Actually Work

“Because you watched…” starts as a linear-algebra problem: represent users and items as vectors, then find directions that align.

← Applications STEM+ Home →

The Concept, From Linear Algebra AA user profile and an item can be encoded as vectors of feature weights. Their cosine similarity, (u·v)/(|u||v|), is near 1 when their directions align. Industrial systems learn far larger latent vectors from an interaction matrix rather than hand-labeling every feature.

Recommendation is ranking, not mind reading

Suppose the three coordinates mean science fiction, comedy, and adventure. The vectors [5, 1, 4] and [5, 0, 4] point in nearly the same direction, so their cosine score is high. Scale does not control cosine similarity: [10, 2, 8] expresses the same preference direction. Matrix factorization generalizes this idea by approximating a sparse user–item matrix as the product of two smaller matrices.

A high score is not a neutral factThe training data records what the old system exposed and what people chose from those options. Optimizing clicks can amplify popularity, narrow exposure, and mistake repeated exposure for genuine preference. Evaluation therefore needs diversity, calibration, novelty, and long-term outcomes—not accuracy alone.

Check your understanding

1. Why is cosine similarity unchanged if every coordinate in one nonzero vector is doubled?

Cosine similarity measures angle; positive rescaling changes magnitude but not direction.

2. Why can historical clicks create a feedback loop?

Observed behavior is partly a consequence of earlier recommendations, so it is not an unbiased sample of all possible choices.
