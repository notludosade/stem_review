Linear Inequalities and Their Graphs

Solving an inequality works almost exactly like solving an equation — with one sign-flip rule that trips up nearly everyone at least once.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

An equation like x = 5 has exactly one solution. An inequality like x > 5 has infinitely many — every number bigger than 5. Solving one uses the same addition/subtraction and multiplication/division moves from Lesson 1, plus one extra rule for a single specific case.

The one new rule

Rule You may add or subtract anything from both sides of an inequality freely, and multiply or divide by a positive number freely — the direction of the inequality stays the same. But multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number flips the inequality sign. This is the single rule this whole lesson exists to teach: forget it, and every other step can be perfect while the final answer is still backwards.
Why the flip actually happens Take the true statement 2 < 5. Multiply both sides by −1: you'd get −2 < −5 if the sign didn't flip — but −2 is bigger than −5 on the number line, not smaller. Multiplying by a negative number reverses order because it reflects every number across zero, which swaps which one is further right. Flipping the inequality sign is what keeps the statement true after that reflection.

Solving and graphing

Solve and graph: −2x + 3 ≤ 11 Step 1 — Subtract 3: −2x ≤ 8.
Step 2 — Divide by −2 (negative — flip the sign): x ≥ −4.
Graph — A closed (filled) circle at −4, because ≥ includes −4 itself, with an arrow shading everything to the right.

Reading the graph An open circle (hollow) means the boundary value is not included — used for < and >. A closed circle (filled) means it is included — used for ≤ and ≥. The shaded arrow always points toward the numbers that make the inequality true.

Compound inequalities

Definition A compound inequality like −3 < x ≤ 5 says both conditions hold at once — x is greater than −3 and x is at most 5. Its graph is a segment between the two boundaries, with an open or closed circle on each end chosen independently.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Solve: 3x − 4 > 8. What is the solution?

Add 4: 3x > 12. Divide by 3 (positive, no flip): x > 4.

2. Solve: −5x ≥ 20. What is the solution?

Dividing both sides by −5 flips the inequality: x ≤ −4.

3. Which circle style belongs on the graph of x ≤ 6?

≤ includes 6 itself (closed circle) and means "6 or less," which shades toward smaller numbers — to the left.

4. Fill in the blank: multiplying or dividing both sides of an inequality by a negative number ___ the direction of the inequality sign.

A negative multiplier reflects both sides across zero, reversing their order — the sign has to flip to keep the statement true.

5. Fill in the blank: the graph of x > 3 uses a(n) ___ circle at 3, since 3 itself is not part of the solution.

Strict inequalities (< or >) exclude the boundary value itself, shown with an open (hollow) circle.
