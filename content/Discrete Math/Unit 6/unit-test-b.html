1. Does 8 divide 96?

96 = 8 × 12 exactly, with no remainder, so 8 | 96.

2. Applying the Division Algorithm to a = 73, b = 9, what are q and r?

73 = 9(8) + 1, and r = 1 satisfies 0 ≤ r < 9.

3. Which statement about the number 1 is correct?

The definition of prime requires p > 1, so 1 is excluded by design — it's neither prime nor composite, specifically to preserve unique factorization.

4. Fill in the blank: the prime factorization of 72 is 2³ · 3^___.

72 = 8 × 9 = 2³ × 3², so the exponent on 3 is 2.

5. What is 34 mod 9?

34 = 9(3) + 7, so 34 mod 9 = 7.

6. Suppose 7 | a and 7 | b. Answer both parts:

(a) Which of these must also be true?

(b) Fill in the blank: this holds because a = 7k and b = 7m for integers k, m, so a − b = 7(k − ___).

(a) If a and b are both multiples of 7, so is their difference. (b) a − b = 7k − 7m = 7(k − m), so 7 | (a−b).

7. Applying the Division Algorithm to a = −45, b = 6. Answer both parts:

(a) What are q and r?

(b) Fill in the blank: since b = 6 here, r must satisfy 0 ≤ r < ___.

(a) −45 = 6(−8) + 3, and r = 3 is the unique remainder satisfying 0 ≤ r < 6. (b) The Division Algorithm requires 0 ≤ r < b, and here b = 6.

8. Consider prime factorization. Answer both parts:

(a) What would break if 1 were allowed to count as prime?

(b) Fill in the blank: the prime factorization of 150 is 2 · 3 · ___.

(a) Allowing 1 as a prime factor would let any factorization gain extra "different" versions by inserting more 1s, destroying uniqueness. (b) 150 = 2 × 3 × 25 = 2 × 3 × 5².

9. Consider 53 and 29 modulo 6. Answer both parts:

(a) Is 53 ≡ 29 (mod 6)?

(b) Fill in the blank: both 53 and 29 leave remainder ___ when divided by 6.

(a) 6 | 24, confirming the congruence. (b) 53 = 6(8) + 5 and 29 = 6(4) + 5 — both leave remainder 5.

10. Consider arithmetic modulo 9. Answer both parts:

(a) What is (5 + 7) mod 9?

(b) Fill in the blank: (−4) mod 9 = ___.

(a) 5 + 7 = 12, and 12 mod 9 = 3. (b) −4 = 9(−1) + 5, so (−4) mod 9 = 5, since the remainder must stay in [0, 9).
