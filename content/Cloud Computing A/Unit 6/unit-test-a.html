1. What is elasticity in cloud infrastructure?

Elasticity is specifically about automatic, demand-driven capacity adjustment.

2. Fill in the blank: a defined pool of identical servers with a minimum size, maximum size, and a scaling policy is called an ___.

An auto scaling group is the mechanism that implements elasticity automatically.

3. Why does an auto scaling group use horizontal scaling (adding more servers) rather than vertical scaling (making one server bigger)?

Horizontal scaling avoids vertical scaling's instance-size ceiling and restart requirement, and works naturally with a load balancer.

4. How does a cloud bill differ from a one-time on-prem hardware purchase?

Itemized, recurring, usage-based billing is the defining trait of a cloud bill.

5. What is "egress," and how is it typically priced?

Egress (outbound data) is typically metered and billed, while ingress (inbound) is usually free.

6. A service unexpectedly generates a huge data transfer bill because it constantly sends results between two different regions. Answer both parts:

(a) Which bill line does this show up on?

(b) What does this scenario illustrate about reading a cloud bill?

Cross-region transfer shows up as egress, and itemized billing is exactly what makes it possible to trace an unexpected cost back to its architectural cause.

7. Fill in the blank: a physically separate data center facility within a region, with independent power and cooling, is called an ___.

Availability zones provide physical independence within a broader region.

8. What is the key difference between redundancy and failover?

Redundancy is having the extra copies; failover is the automatic mechanism that switches to them.

9. An application's servers are deployed across three availability zones behind a load balancer. If one entire AZ loses power, what happens?

Health checks and load balancing (Unit 4) combine with multi-AZ redundancy to survive a whole zone failing, with no manual intervention.

10. Fill in the blank: the realistic goal of resilient design is eliminating any ___ — a single component whose failure alone could take down the entire system — rather than trying to prevent every possible failure.

Eliminating single points of failure, not preventing all failure, is the realistic and achievable goal.
