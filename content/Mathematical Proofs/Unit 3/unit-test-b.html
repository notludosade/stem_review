1. What is the contrapositive of "If m is a multiple of 6, then m is even"?

Negate and swap both parts: ¬(m even) → ¬(m is a multiple of 6), i.e., m is odd → m is not a multiple of 6.

2. To prove "if n is a multiple of 9, then n is a multiple of 3" by contrapositive, what should you prove instead?

Negate and swap: ¬(n is a multiple of 3) → ¬(n is a multiple of 9), i.e., n is not a multiple of 3 → n is not a multiple of 9.

3. Fill in the blank: proof by ___ assumes the claim is false and derives a statement together with its own negation.

Contradiction — assuming the claim false and deriving an impossibility is what forces the claim to be true.

4. A contradiction proof of "if x is odd, then x² is odd" assumes which two statements simultaneously?

Contradiction proofs of P → Q assume P and ¬Q together — here, "x is odd" (P) and "x² is even" (¬Q).

5. Fill in the blank: the proof that √2 is irrational relies on p/q being written in ___ at the very start.

Lowest terms is the assumption violated once both p and q are shown even, producing the contradiction.

6. Consider proving "if n² is even, then n is even." Answer both parts:

(a) A contrapositive proof of this claim would directly prove which statement?

(b) Fill in the blank: a contradiction proof of the same claim would instead begin by assuming n² is even and n is ___, simultaneously.

(a) Contrapositive negates and swaps: n odd → n² odd. (b) Contradiction assumes the hypothesis (n² even) and the negated conclusion (n odd) together.

7. Continuing the √2 proof once p is shown even (p = 2k) and q² = 2k² is derived. Answer both parts:

(a) What is concluded about q at this step?

(b) Fill in the blank: since both p and q turn out even, they share a common factor of ___, contradicting lowest terms.

(a) By Lesson 7's theorem, q² even forces q even. (b) A shared factor of 2 directly contradicts the lowest-terms assumption.

8. Suppose someone claims the complete list of primes is {2, 3}. Answer both parts:

(a) What role does the constructed number N play in Euclid's proof?

(b) Fill in the blank: for the assumed list {2, 3}, N = 2 × 3 + 1 = ___.

(a) N's prime factor must be new, contradicting the assumed complete list. (b) 2 × 3 + 1 = 7, itself prime and absent from {2, 3}.

9. Consider the claim: "if 5n + 3 is even, then n is odd." Answer both parts:

(a) What is the contrapositive?

(b) Fill in the blank: assuming n = 2k (even), 5n + 3 = 10k + 3 = 2(___) + 1.

(a) Contrapositive negates and swaps: n even → 5n+3 odd. (b) 10k + 3 = 2(5k + 1) + 1, confirming 5n + 3 is odd whenever n is even.

10. Consider proving: "if n² is divisible by 5, then n is divisible by 5." Answer both parts:

(a) To prove this by contradiction, which two statements would you assume simultaneously?

(b) Fill in the blank: if n is not divisible by 5, checking every possible remainder of n mod 5 shows n² can never leave remainder ___ when divided by 5.

(a) Contradiction assumes the hypothesis and negated conclusion together. (b) None of remainders 1, 2, 3, 4 squared and reduced mod 5 ever give 0 — exactly the needed contradiction.
