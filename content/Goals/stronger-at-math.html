Become Stronger at Math

Not about outrunning a class schedule — about actually understanding why the math works, not just how to compute it. Logic and structure first, then the proof-writing skill that ties it together, then the linear algebra nearly everything past high school assumes.

The Route

If needed

Algebra/Geometry Fundamentals Review

If it's been a while since the fundamentals — linear and quadratic equations, factoring, exponents, similarity. Skip it if you're already solid.

 1 of 3

Discrete Math

Logic, sets, combinatorics, graph theory, and number theory — the toolkit behind rigorous proofs and most of theoretical computer science.

 2 of 3

Mathematical Proofs

Direct proof, contradiction, induction, and contrapositive — learning to write mathematics that convinces someone, not just reach a right answer.

 3 of 3

Linear Algebra A

Vectors, matrices, linear transformations, eigenvalues, and eigenvectors — the computational core behind graphics, statistics, and machine learning.

If You Want to Keep Going

Real Analysis is where this all gets proven properly — including the calculus you may already know how to compute. It's part of the Mathematics Pathway, which ends in a real capstone project.
