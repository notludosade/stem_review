1. A spacecraft in deep space, far from any planet, coasts with its engines off. According to Newton's first law, its velocity will:

With no net force acting (no air, no friction, engines off), Newton's first law says the spacecraft keeps moving at constant velocity indefinitely — inertia doesn't run out, it just persists until a net force intervenes.

2. A tennis ball hits a racket and bounces back. Which pair of forces is a genuine Newton's third-law pair?

A true third-law pair acts on two different objects, is the same type of force, and occurs simultaneously — the racket pushing the ball and the ball pushing back on the racket fits exactly. The other options either act on the same object or aren't forces at all (inertia isn't a force).

3. A 50 N box sits on a horizontal floor. A person then pushes straight down on the box with an additional 10 N, without moving it. The normal force from the floor is now:

The box remains in vertical equilibrium, so the normal force must balance every downward force acting on it: N = weight + additional push = 50 + 10 = 60 N. The normal force isn't a fixed value — it adjusts to whatever is needed.

4. A car travels around a circular track at constant speed. If the car's speed doubles while the radius stays the same, its centripetal acceleration:

Centripetal acceleration is a_c = v²/r. Since it depends on speed squared, doubling v multiplies a_c by 2² = 4, for the same radius.

5. Fill in the blank: If the distance between two masses is tripled, the gravitational force between them decreases to ___ of its original value (enter as a fraction like 1/9, or as a decimal rounded to two places).

Gravitational force follows an inverse-square law, F ∝ 1/r². Tripling r divides the force by 3² = 9, leaving one-ninth of the original value.

6. A 5 kg sled is pulled forward with 30 N of force while friction of 10 N acts backward. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the net force on the sled?

(b) Fill in the blank: the sled's acceleration is ___ m/s².

(a) Net force = 30 − 10 = 20 N, forward. (b) a = ΣF/m = 20/5 = 4 m/s².

7. A 20 kg crate sits on a floor with μs = 0.4 and μk = 0.25. Using g = 9.8 m/s², answer both parts:

(a) If a horizontal push of 90 N is applied, does the crate begin to slide?

(b) Fill in the blank: once the crate is sliding, the kinetic friction force acting on it is ___ N.

(a) Normal force N = mg = 20(9.8) = 196 N, so maximum static friction is μ_sN = 0.4(196) = 78.4 N. Since 90 N > 78.4 N, the push overcomes static friction and the crate slides. (b) Once sliding, kinetic friction is μ_kN = 0.25(196) = 49 N.

8. A 5 kg block rests on a frictionless incline at 37°, held in place by a rope parallel to the incline surface. Using g = 9.8 m/s², sin 37° ≈ 0.6, and cos 37° ≈ 0.8, answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the tension in the rope is ___ N.

(b) What is the normal force on the block from the incline surface?

(a) Along the slope: T = mg sin θ = 5(9.8)(0.6) = 29.4 N. (b) Perpendicular to the slope: N = mg cos θ = 5(9.8)(0.8) = 39.2 N.

9. A 0.2 kg ball moves in a horizontal circle of radius 0.5 m at a constant speed of 2.5 m/s, held by a string. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the ball's centripetal acceleration?

(b) What is the tension in the string providing this centripetal force?

(a) a_c = v²/r = 2.5²/0.5 = 6.25/0.5 = 12.5 m/s². (b) The string supplies the entire centripetal force: T = ma_c = 0.2(12.5) = 2.5 N.

10. Two masses, 4 × 10⁵ kg and 3 × 10⁴ kg, are separated by 2 m. Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the gravitational force between them is approximately ___ N.

(b) If the distance between them were instead doubled to 4 m, the new gravitational force would be approximately:

(a) F = Gm₁m₂/r² = (6.67×10⁻¹¹)(4×10⁵)(3×10⁴)/2² ≈ 0.2 N. (b) Doubling r from 2 m to 4 m divides the force by 2² = 4, giving 0.2/4 = 0.05 N.
