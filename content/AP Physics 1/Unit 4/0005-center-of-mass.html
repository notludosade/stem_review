Center of Mass

Every system of particles, no matter how complicated, has one special point that moves as if all the mass and all the external force were concentrated there.

← Lesson 4 Unit 5, Lesson 1 → Contents Glossary →

Center of Mass The center of mass of a system of particles is the mass-weighted average position: xcm = (m1x1 + m2x2 + ... ) / (m1 + m2 + ...), with the same formula applied separately for ycm. For a uniform, symmetric object, the center of mass is simply its geometric center.
Why the center of mass is physically special Newton's second law, ΣFext = mtotalacm, holds for the center of mass of any system, regardless of how complicated its internal motion is (tumbling, exploding, colliding internally). This is why a thrown wrench tumbling wildly still has its center of mass trace out a simple parabola (Unit 1, Lesson 6), even while every other point on it spins in a complex path — only external forces affect the center of mass's motion; all internal forces (Newton's third law pairs between the wrench's own atoms) cancel out of the sum entirely.
Example: two masses on a line A 2 kg mass sits at x = 0 m and a 6 kg mass sits at x = 4 m. xcm = [(2)(0) + (6)(4)] / (2+6) = 24/8 = 3 m — closer to the heavier mass, as expected from a mass-weighted average.
Example: an explosion in flight A firework shell in projectile motion explodes mid-air into two equal-mass fragments. Even after the explosion, the system's center of mass continues along the exact same parabolic path the unexploded shell would have followed — the explosion is entirely internal force, contributing nothing to the net external force (only gravity does, both before and after).
Connecting back to momentum conservation Total momentum ptotal = mtotalvcm — the system's total momentum is exactly the same as if all its mass moved at the center of mass's velocity. This is why Lesson 2's conservation of momentum (Σp constant for an isolated system) is equivalent to saying the center of mass moves at constant velocity whenever no external force acts — collisions rearrange momentum between individual objects, but never change the system's overall center-of-mass motion.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. A tumbling wrench is thrown through the air. What path does its center of mass follow?

Only external forces (here, gravity) affect the center of mass's motion; internal forces from tumbling cancel out entirely, leaving simple parabolic projectile motion.

2. A 1 kg mass is at x = 2 m and a 3 kg mass is at x = 6 m. What is the center of mass?

x_cm = [(1)(2) + (3)(6)] / (1+3) = [2+18]/4 = 20/4 = 5 m.

3. A firework shell explodes mid-flight into several fragments, with only gravity acting externally before and after. What happens to the system's center of mass at the instant of explosion?

The explosion is purely internal force; the only external force throughout is gravity, so the center of mass is unaffected by the explosion itself.

4. Fill in the blank: the mass-weighted average position of a system, which moves according to Newton's second law using only external forces, is called the ___.

The center of mass obeys ΣF_ext = m_total·a_cm regardless of how complex the system's internal motion is.
