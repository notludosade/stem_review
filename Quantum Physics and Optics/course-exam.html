1. Light with frequency 4×10¹⁴ Hz travels through vacuum. What is its wavelength?

λ = c/f = (3×10⁸)/(4×10¹⁴) = 7.5×10⁻⁷ m = 750 nm.

2. Huygens' principle constructs a new wavefront as the envelope tangent to secondary wavelets. Applied at a boundary between two media, this construction predicts:

The same wavelet-envelope construction, applied at a boundary, derives both reflection and Snell's law without any separate assumptions.

3. Fill in the blank: light in air (n = 1.00) strikes glass (n = 1.50) at θ₁ = 30°. Using sinθ₂ = (n₁/n₂) sinθ₁, θ₂ ≈ ___ ° (round to nearest whole number).

sinθ₂ = (1.00/1.50)(0.50) = 0.333, so θ₂ = sin⁻¹(0.333) ≈ 19.5°.

4. Slits separated by d = 0.01 mm produce a first-order (m = 1) bright fringe at θ = 3°. What is the wavelength?

λ = d sinθ/m = (1×10⁻⁵)(sin3°)/1 ≈ (1×10⁻⁵)(0.0523) ≈ 5.23×10⁻⁷ m ≈ 524 nm.

5. A soap film in air is viewed in reflected light. Why does light reflecting off the top surface pick up a 180° phase shift while light reflecting off the bottom surface does not?

The asymmetric phase-shift rule (only reflecting off a higher-index medium adds a shift) is what gives the +1/2 term in the constructive-interference condition for a film in air.

6. Fill in the blank: compared to a double slit, a diffraction grating with many more slits produces bright fringes that are much ___ (narrower/wider).

With many slits, only a very narrow range of angles keeps every wavelet in phase, producing much sharper, narrower bright fringes.

7. Below a metal's threshold frequency, increasing the light's intensity:

No amount of intensity below the threshold frequency ejects electrons — a common misconception is that brighter light alone should eventually work, but it does not.

8. A metal's work function is 2.0×10⁻¹⁹ J. Light of frequency 5×10¹⁴ Hz strikes it (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s). What is the maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons?

hf = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(5×10¹⁴) ≈ 3.31×10⁻¹⁹ J. KE_max = hf − φ = 3.31×10⁻¹⁹ − 2.0×10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 1.31×10⁻¹⁹ J.

9. Fill in the blank: an X-ray photon Compton-scatters at θ = 90°. Using Δλ = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(1 − cosθ), Δλ ≈ ___ ×10⁻¹² m.

cos90° = 0, so Δλ = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(1 − 0) = 2.43×10⁻¹² m.

10. An electron (m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg) moves at 2×10⁶ m/s. What is its approximate de Broglie wavelength (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s)?

λ = h/(mv) = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/((9.11×10⁻³¹)(2×10⁶)) ≈ 3.64×10⁻¹⁰ m.

11. When electrons are fired one at a time through a double slit, with a which-path detector turned off, the pattern that builds up over many electrons is:

Despite each electron landing as a discrete point, the accumulated pattern over many electrons is a genuine interference pattern — wave-particle duality applies to matter, not just light.

12. Fill in the blank: Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2 is known as the Heisenberg ___ principle.

The Heisenberg uncertainty principle sets a lower bound on the combined precision of position and momentum, reflecting a genuine property of the wavefunction, not just a measurement limitation.

13. A classical (planetary) model of the atom predicts that an orbiting, accelerating electron should:

Classical electromagnetism requires an accelerating charge to radiate continuously, predicting near-instant atomic collapse — directly contradicted by the existence of stable atoms with discrete line spectra.

14. An electron in hydrogen drops from n = 2 (E = −3.4 eV) to n = 1 (E = −13.6 eV). What is the emitted photon's energy?

E_photon = E_high − E_low = −3.4 − (−13.6) = 10.2 eV.

15. Fill in the blank: the Bohr model correctly predicts hydrogen's spectral lines, but fails for atoms with more than one electron because it cannot account for electron-electron ___.

The Bohr model has no way to represent electron-electron repulsion, which is exactly why it fails for multi-electron atoms like neutral helium.

16. An electron is confined to a 1 nm box. Using Eₙ = n²h²/(8mL²), what is its ground-state (n = 1) energy (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s, m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg)?

E₁ = (1)²(6.626×10⁻³⁴)²/(8×9.11×10⁻³¹×(1×10⁻⁹)²) ≈ 6.02×10⁻²⁰ J.

17. According to the Born interpretation, what does |Ψ(x)|² represent?

|Ψ(x)|² dx is a probability, not a definite position or momentum — the core claim of the Born interpretation.

18. Fill in the blank: this course presents the Schrödinger equation qualitatively and solves it only for simple cases, such as the particle in a ___.

The particle-in-a-box problem is the one case this course solves fully and explicitly, rather than treating the equation in full generality.

19. If a tunneling barrier's width is doubled while its height stays the same, the transmission probability T:

Since T ~ e^(−2κL), doubling the barrier width L drops T by a large exponential factor, not a simple linear amount.

20. Fill in the blank: a scanning tunneling microscope achieves atomic-resolution imaging by measuring a tunneling current that changes exponentially with tip-to-surface ___.

Tunneling current's exponential sensitivity to distance is exactly what gives an STM its atomic-scale resolution.

21. What is the common pattern across this course's arc, from wave optics through the Schrödinger equation?

The course's throughline is a genuine chain of borrowed resolutions — each domain's leftover puzzle motivated, and was resolved by, the next domain's new idea.
