Unit 1: Wave Optics Fundamentals

Amplitude, wavelength, period, frequency

Amplitude is a wave's maximum displacement from equilibrium; wavelength λ is the distance between repeating points; period T is the time for one cycle; frequency f = 1/T is cycles per second.

Wave speed, v = fλ

Relates a wave's speed to its frequency and wavelength. For light in vacuum, v = c is fixed, so frequency and wavelength are not independent.

Huygens' principle

Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets; the new wavefront is the envelope tangent to all of them.

Law of reflection

The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal — derivable from Huygens' wavelets since reflected wavelets never change speed.

Index of refraction, n = c/v

The ratio of light's speed in vacuum to its speed in a given medium, always ≥ 1.

Snell's law, n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂

Relates the angles of incidence and refraction to the refractive indices of the two media, derivable from a wavefront's speed mismatch as it crosses a boundary.

Unit 2: Interference & Diffraction

Principle of superposition

Overlapping waves add algebraically at every point — the basis of all interference phenomena.

Double-slit equation, d sinθ = mλ (bright), d sinθ = (m + 1/2)λ (dark)

Predicts the angles of bright and dark fringes in Young's double-slit experiment, proving light behaves as a wave.

Thin-film interference

Interference between light reflecting off the top and bottom surfaces of a thin film. A reflection off a higher-index medium adds a 180° phase shift; off a lower-index medium, it does not.

Single-slit diffraction, a sinθ = mλ (dark)

For a single slit, this condition locates dark fringes (the reverse of its meaning for a double slit or grating), flanking a bright central maximum.

Diffraction grating

A surface with many closely spaced slits, using the same d sinθ = mλ relation as a double slit but producing much sharper, brighter fringes due to many-slit interference.

Unit 3: The Photoelectric Effect & Photons

Photoelectric effect

Light striking certain metals ejects electrons, but only above a metal-specific threshold frequency f₀, regardless of intensity — brighter light below f₀ never ejects electrons.

Threshold frequency, work function φ

The work function is the minimum energy needed to free an electron from a metal's surface; the threshold frequency is f₀ = φ/h.

Photon, E = hf

A discrete packet of light energy, with h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s (Planck's constant).

Photoelectric equation, KE_max = hf − φ

Einstein's equation for the maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons, resolving every fact of the photoelectric puzzle via one-photon-to-one-electron interactions.

Photon momentum, p = h/λ = E/c

Photons carry real, particle-like momentum alongside their energy.

Compton effect, Δλ = (h/(m_ec))(1 − cosθ)

The measurable wavelength shift of a photon scattering off a free electron, proving photons carry conserved momentum like ordinary particles in a collision.

Unit 4: Wave-Particle Duality

De Broglie wavelength, λ = h/(mv)

Every moving mass has an associated wavelength, extending wave-particle duality from light to matter — observable for particles as light as electrons, unmeasurably small for everyday objects.

Electron double-slit experiment

Firing electrons one at a time through a double slit produces individually discrete landing points that, in aggregate, build up an interference pattern — proof that matter, not just light, shows wave-particle duality.

Which-path measurement

Detecting which slit a particle passes through destroys the interference pattern, replacing it with a simple two-band pattern.

Wave-particle duality

The principle that both light and matter display wave-like behavior (interference, diffraction) in some experiments and particle-like behavior (discrete detection, momentum) in others — both simultaneously valid, and true of both light and matter.

Heisenberg uncertainty principle, Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2

Position and momentum cannot be simultaneously well-defined below this combined limit — a genuine property of the wavefunction, not merely a measurement limitation.

Unit 5: The Bohr Model & Atomic Spectra

Atomic spectrum

The discrete set of wavelengths an element emits or absorbs, corresponding to transitions between quantized energy levels — a unique fingerprint per element.

Bohr's postulates

An electron occupies only specific non-radiating "stationary state" orbits, with quantized angular momentum L = nħ; photons are emitted or absorbed only when an electron jumps between these levels.

Bohr energy levels, Eₙ = −13.6 eV/n²

The quantized energy levels of the hydrogen atom, correctly predicting hydrogen's spectral lines and reproducing the empirical Rydberg constant from first principles.

Bohr model's limitations

The model fails for atoms with more than one electron (it cannot account for electron-electron repulsion), does not derive why angular momentum should be quantized, and pictures the electron in a definite orbit — a classical-trajectory picture the uncertainty principle rules out in general.

Unit 6: The Schrödinger Equation

Schrödinger equation (qualitative)

iħ ∂Ψ/∂t = −(ħ²/2m)∂²Ψ/∂x² + V(x)Ψ, the equation governing how a quantum wavefunction evolves — presented qualitatively in this course, and solved fully only for simple cases like the particle in a box.

Wavefunction, Ψ(x,t)

A complex-valued function with no direct physical meaning by itself; only |Ψ|² connects to anything measurable.

Particle in a box, Eₙ = n²h²/(8mL²)

A particle confined between two impenetrable walls has quantized energy levels that fall directly out of requiring Ψ to vanish at both walls — a genuine derivation, unlike Bohr's postulated quantization.

Born interpretation, |Ψ(x)|²

|Ψ(x)|² dx gives the probability of finding the particle between x and x + dx upon measurement; ∫|Ψ|² dx = 1 over all space (normalization).

Unit 7: Quantum Mechanics Applications & Synthesis

Quantum tunneling, T ~ e^(−2κL)

A quantum particle has a nonzero probability of passing through a barrier it classically shouldn't have enough energy to cross; transmission probability drops off exponentially with barrier width and height.

Scanning tunneling microscope (STM)

Exploits tunneling current's exponential sensitivity to tip-to-surface distance for atomic-resolution imaging.

Quantized energy bands (semiconductors)

An extension of the particle-in-a-box's discrete-levels idea to solids, determining whether a material conducts, insulates, or acts as a tunable semiconductor.

Quantum technology survey

Lasers and LEDs rely on quantized atomic or semiconductor energy-level transitions; MRI relies on quantized nuclear spin states; electron microscopes rely on electrons' short de Broglie wavelength.
