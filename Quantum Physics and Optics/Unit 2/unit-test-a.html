1. Slits separated by d = 0.015 mm produce a first-order (m = 1) bright fringe at θ = 2°. What is the wavelength?

λ = d sinθ/m = (1.5×10⁻⁵)(sin2°)/1 ≈ (1.5×10⁻⁵)(0.0349) ≈ 5.24×10⁻⁷ m ≈ 524 nm.

2. A soap film in air (n = 1.33) is viewed in reflected light. Why does the constructive-interference condition include a +1/2 term (2nt = (m+1/2)λ) instead of the simpler 2nt = mλ?

The asymmetric phase-shift rule (shift only when reflecting off a higher-index medium) is exactly what introduces the +1/2 term for a film in air.

3. A single slit of width 0.025 mm is illuminated with λ = 500 nm light. What angle gives the first dark fringe?

sinθ = λ/a = (500×10⁻⁹)/(2.5×10⁻⁵) = 0.02 → θ ≈ 1.15°.

4. Fill in the blank: a surface with many thousands of closely spaced slits, used to produce sharp spectral lines, is called a diffraction ___.

A diffraction grating scales the double-slit d sinθ = mλ condition up to many slits, producing much sharper bright fringes.

5. Compared to a plain double slit with the same slit spacing d, a diffraction grating with many more slits produces bright fringes that are:

With N slits, small angular deviations cause most wavelets to interfere destructively, sharply narrowing the bright maxima compared to just two slits.

6. Slits separated by d = 0.03 mm are illuminated with λ = 600 nm light. Answer both parts:

(a) What angle gives the second-order (m = 2) bright fringe?

(b) Fill in the blank: at a point on the screen where the path difference between the two slits is exactly 1.5λ, the interference is ___ (constructive or destructive).

(a) sinθ = mλ/d = 2(600×10⁻⁹)/(3×10⁻⁵) = 0.04 → θ ≈ 2.29°. (b) A path difference of a half-integer number of wavelengths (1.5λ) gives destructive interference — a dark fringe.

7. A soap film (n = 1.33) in air is viewed in reflected light. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the minimum nonzero thickness giving constructive reflection of λ = 480 nm light is t = λ/(4n) ≈ ___ nm (round to nearest whole number).

(b) As the film continues to thin well below this thickness (much less than λ/4), the film eventually appears:

(a) t = 480/(4×1.33) ≈ 90 nm. (b) As thickness approaches zero, the path difference approaches zero while the phase shift remains, producing destructive interference and a dark appearance — the basis of Newton's rings' central dark spot.

8. A single slit of width 0.04 mm is illuminated with λ = 450 nm light. Answer both parts:

(a) The a sinθ = mλ condition for a single slit locates:

(b) Fill in the blank: the first dark fringe occurs at sinθ = λ/a ≈ ___ (round to 4 decimal places).

(a) For a single slit, this condition gives destructive interference (dark fringes) — the reverse of its meaning for a double slit or grating. (b) sinθ = (450×10⁻⁹)/(4×10⁻⁵) ≈ 0.01125 ≈ 0.0113.

9. A diffraction grating has 400 lines per millimeter. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the slit spacing d = 1/400 mm = ___ nm.

(b) For λ = 500 nm light, the first-order (m = 1) maximum occurs at approximately:

(a) d = 1/400 mm = 0.0025 mm = 2500 nm. (b) sinθ = λ/d = 500/2500 = 0.2 → θ ≈ 11.5°.

10. Two coherent light sources of wavelength 550 nm produce a point on a screen where the path difference is 1650 nm. Answer both parts:

(a) Is this point a bright fringe or a dark fringe?

(b) Fill in the blank: this bright fringe is the order m = ___.

(a) 1650 nm = 3 × 550 nm = 3λ, a whole-number multiple, so this is constructive interference — a bright fringe. (b) Since the path difference equals 3λ, this is the third-order (m = 3) bright fringe.
