Thin-Film Interference

A soap bubble's swirling colors and an anti-reflective camera lens coating both come from the same interference effect — light bouncing off two very close surfaces instead of two separated slits.

← Lesson 4 Lesson 6 → Contents Glossary →

Interference in a Thin Film Light reflects off both the top and bottom surfaces of a thin film of thickness t. The two reflected waves interfere, with an extra path length of about 2t traveled by the wave that reflects off the bottom surface, measured in the film's own wavelength λfilm = λ/nfilm.
The Phase-Shift Rule A wave reflecting off a higher-index medium picks up an extra 180° (half-wavelength) phase shift; reflecting off a lower-index medium adds no shift. For a film in air (like a soap bubble), only the top-surface reflection gets this shift, so constructive interference in reflection happens at 2nt = (m + 1/2)λ, not the simpler 2nt = mλ you might expect.
Why the color you see depends on thickness — and shifts as the film drains Only certain wavelengths satisfy the constructive-interference condition for a given thickness t; the rest interfere destructively and mostly cancel in reflection. As a soap film's thickness changes (draining under gravity, or viewed at a different angle, which changes the effective path length), the wavelength that constructively interferes shifts too — which is exactly why a soap bubble's colors swirl and shift instead of staying fixed.
Example: minimum thickness for a given color A soap film has n = 1.33. Find the minimum nonzero thickness giving constructive reflection of λ = 500 nm green light. 2nt = (0 + 1/2)λ → t = λ/(4n) = 500/(4×1.33) ≈ 94 nm.

1. Why does light reflecting off the top surface of a soap film (air to soap) pick up a 180° phase shift, while light reflecting off the bottom surface (soap to air) does not?

The rule is asymmetric: only reflecting off a higher-index medium adds the extra half-wavelength shift, which is why the constructive-interference condition for a film in air has the +1/2 term.

2. A soap film (n = 1.33) is being viewed in reflected light. What is the minimum nonzero thickness that reflects λ = 600 nm most strongly?

t = λ/(4n) = 600/(4×1.33) ≈ 113 nm.

3. Anti-reflective camera lens coatings are engineered so that reflected light of a target wavelength interferes:

A coating's thickness is chosen so the reflected wavelength destructively interferes, minimizing reflected light and maximizing light transmitted into the lens.

4. Fill in the blank: a soap bubble's colors shift and swirl mainly because its ___ is constantly changing as it drains and moves.

Which wavelength constructively interferes depends directly on the film's thickness, so a changing thickness shifts the visible color.
