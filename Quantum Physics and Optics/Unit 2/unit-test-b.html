1. Slits separated by d = 0.02 mm produce a first-order (m = 1) bright fringe at θ = 1.5°. What is the wavelength?

λ = d sinθ/m = (2×10⁻⁵)(sin1.5°)/1 ≈ (2×10⁻⁵)(0.0262) ≈ 5.24×10⁻⁷ m ≈ 524 nm.

2. A thin oil film (n = 1.45) floats on water (n = 1.33), with air (n = 1.00) above the oil. Light reflects off both the air-oil and oil-water boundaries. At the air-oil boundary, the reflection:

Reflecting off a higher-index medium (air → oil, since n_oil > n_air) always adds a 180° phase shift, regardless of what lies beneath.

3. A single slit of width 0.02 mm is illuminated with λ = 600 nm light. What angle gives the first dark fringe?

sinθ = λ/a = (600×10⁻⁹)/(2×10⁻⁵) = 0.03 → θ ≈ 1.72°.

4. Fill in the blank: compared to a double slit with the same spacing d, a grating with many more slits produces bright fringes that are much ___ (a one-word description of their width).

With many slits interfering, only a very narrow angular range keeps every wavelet in phase — the bright maxima become sharp and narrow.

5. A single-slit diffraction pattern has a bright central maximum flanked by much dimmer side bands. Compared to the central maximum, these side bands are:

The single-slit diffraction envelope has a strong central maximum with much weaker side lobes, unlike the roughly equal-height fringes of a double slit.

6. Slits separated by d = 0.025 mm are illuminated with λ = 500 nm light. Answer both parts:

(a) What angle gives the second-order (m = 2) bright fringe?

(b) Fill in the blank: at a point on the screen where the path difference between the two slits is exactly 2λ, the interference is ___ (constructive or destructive).

(a) sinθ = mλ/d = 2(500×10⁻⁹)/(2.5×10⁻⁵) = 0.04 → θ ≈ 2.29°. (b) A path difference of a whole-number multiple of λ (2λ) gives constructive interference — a bright fringe.

7. A soap film (n = 1.33) in air is viewed in reflected light. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the minimum nonzero thickness giving constructive reflection of λ = 560 nm light is t = λ/(4n) ≈ ___ nm (round to nearest whole number).

(b) Anti-reflective coatings on camera lenses are engineered so reflected light interferes:

(a) t = 560/(4×1.33) ≈ 105 nm. (b) A coating's thickness is chosen so reflected light destructively interferes, minimizing reflection and maximizing transmitted light.

8. A single slit of width 0.03 mm is illuminated with λ = 450 nm light. Answer both parts:

(a) Unlike the double-slit bright-fringe condition, a sinθ = mλ for a single slit locates:

(b) Fill in the blank: the first dark fringe occurs at sinθ = λ/a ≈ ___ (round to 4 decimal places).

(a) For a single slit, this condition gives destructive interference (dark fringes). (b) sinθ = (450×10⁻⁹)/(3×10⁻⁵) = 0.015.

9. A diffraction grating has 600 lines per millimeter. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the slit spacing d = 1/600 mm ≈ ___ nm (round to nearest whole number).

(b) For λ = 500 nm light, the first-order (m = 1) maximum occurs at approximately:

(a) d = 1/600 mm ≈ 0.001667 mm ≈ 1667 nm. (b) sinθ = λ/d = 500/1667 ≈ 0.30 → θ ≈ 17.5°.

10. Two coherent light sources of wavelength 500 nm produce a point on a screen where the path difference is 1250 nm. Answer both parts:

(a) Is this point a bright fringe or a dark fringe?

(b) Fill in the blank: this dark fringe corresponds to m = ___ in the dark-fringe formula d sinθ = (m + 1/2)λ.

(a) 1250 nm = 2.5 × 500 nm = 2.5λ, a half-integer multiple, so this is destructive interference — a dark fringe. (b) (m + 1/2)λ = 2.5λ means m = 2.
