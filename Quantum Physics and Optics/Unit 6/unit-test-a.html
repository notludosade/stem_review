1. True or false: this course derives and solves the Schrödinger equation exactly for every possible potential energy function V(x).

The Schrödinger equation is presented qualitatively here; only simple, specific cases are worked through in full.

2. An electron is confined to a 0.5 nm box. Using Eₙ = n²h²/(8mL²), what is its ground-state (n = 1) energy (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s, m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg)?

E₁ = (1)²(6.626×10⁻³⁴)²/(8×9.11×10⁻³¹×(0.5×10⁻⁹)²) ≈ 2.41×10⁻¹⁹ J — four times the 1 nm box's energy, since E ∝ 1/L².

3. What does |Ψ(x)|² represent, according to the Born interpretation?

|Ψ(x)|² dx is a probability, not a definite position — the core claim of the Born interpretation.

4. Fill in the blank: the complex-valued function Ψ(x,t) governed by the Schrödinger equation is called the ___.

Ψ is the wavefunction; only |Ψ|² connects directly to a measurable probability.

5. Where does the quantization of energy in the particle-in-a-box problem come from?

Unlike Bohr's model, this quantization is a genuine mathematical consequence of the boundary conditions, not an extra postulate.

6. An electron is confined to a 1 nm box. Answer both parts:

(a) Compared to the ground state (n = 1), the n = 2 energy level is:

(b) Fill in the blank: the wavefunction Ψₙ(x) has exactly ___ interior nodes (points of zero probability, excluding the walls) for n = 3.

(a) Since Eₙ ∝ n², E₂ = 4E₁. (b) Ψₙ(x) has n − 1 interior nodes; for n = 3, that's 2 interior nodes.

7. For the particle-in-a-box ground state (n = 1), |Ψ₁(x)|² peaks at the box's center and is zero at both walls. Answer both parts:

(a) Where is a measurement most likely to find the particle?

(b) Fill in the blank: the requirement that ∫|Ψ|² dx = 1 over all space is called ___.

(a) |Ψ₁(x)|² is largest at the center, so measurements most often find the particle there. (b) Normalization ensures total probability across all space sums to exactly 1.

8. Compare the classical wave equation to the Schrödinger equation. Answer both parts:

(a) What role does the imaginary unit i play in the Schrödinger equation?

(b) The free-particle solution Ψ(x,t) = Ae^(i(kx−ωt)) connects directly back to which earlier idea in this course?

(a) Ψ's complex nature is essential, unlike i's role as a mere convenience in classical AC analysis. (b) Plugging de Broglie's relations into this solution connects the Schrödinger equation back to Unit 4's matter-wave hypothesis.

9. Using Eₙ = n²h²/(8mL²) for an electron in a 1 nm box (E₁ ≈ 0.376 eV). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: E₃ = 9 × E₁ ≈ ___ eV (round to 2 decimal places).

(b) If the box width L were doubled instead, the ground-state energy E₁ would become:

(a) E₃ = 9 × 0.376 ≈ 3.38 eV. (b) Since Eₙ ∝ 1/L², doubling L divides Eₙ by 4.

10. Two electrons are prepared in exactly identical quantum states, and their positions are each measured once. Answer both parts:

(a) Quantum mechanics predicts the two measurement results will be:

(b) Fill in the blank: only the ___ distribution, not a single trajectory, is what quantum mechanics actually predicts.

(a)-(b) Even for identically prepared systems, individual outcomes vary — only their statistical distribution, across many trials, matches |Ψ|².
