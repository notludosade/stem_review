Probability Densities and What the Wavefunction Means

Ψ itself isn't directly measurable — it's |Ψ|² that tells you something you can actually check against experiment: the probability of finding the particle at a given location.

← Lesson 17 Unit 7, Lesson 19 → Contents Glossary →

The Born Interpretation |Ψ(x)|² dx gives the probability of finding the particle between x and x + dx, if its position is measured. Because the particle must be found somewhere, the total probability must be 1: ∫|Ψ|² dx = 1 over all space (normalization). Ψ itself can be complex or negative; |Ψ|² is always real and non-negative.
Why this is genuinely probabilistic, not "we just don't know yet" Connecting back to Lesson 11's electron double-slit and Lesson 12's uncertainty principle: quantum mechanics does not predict where any single particle will be found upon measurement — only the probability distribution of where it's likely to be found across many identical trials. This is a fundamentally different kind of theory from classical mechanics, which (in principle) predicts one exact trajectory. Two electrons prepared identically can land at different spots — not because of hidden information we're missing, but because the theory itself is probabilistic at its core.
Example: where is the particle most likely to be found? For the particle-in-a-box ground state (n = 1, Lesson 17), |Ψ₁(x)|² peaks at the box's center (x = L/2) and is exactly zero at both walls — a measurement is most likely to find the particle near the middle, and never exactly at an edge.

1. What physical meaning does |Ψ(x)|² have, according to the Born interpretation?

|Ψ(x)|² dx is a probability, not a definite position — this is the Born interpretation's core claim.

2. Why must ∫|Ψ|² dx = 1 over all space?

Total probability across all possible locations must sum (integrate) to exactly 1, since the particle is somewhere.

3. Two particles are prepared in exactly identical quantum states and their positions are each measured once. Quantum mechanics predicts that the two measurement results will be:

Even for identically prepared systems, individual measurement outcomes vary — only their statistical distribution, across many trials, matches |Ψ|².

4. Fill in the blank: the requirement that ∫|Ψ|² dx = 1 over all space is called ___.

Normalization ensures the total probability of finding the particle somewhere is exactly 1.
