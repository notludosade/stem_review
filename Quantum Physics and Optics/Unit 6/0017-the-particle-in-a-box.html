The Particle in a Box

One genuinely solvable case, worked all the way through: trap a particle between two impenetrable walls, and the Schrödinger equation forces its energy into a clean, quantized ladder.

← Lesson 16 Lesson 18 → Contents Glossary →

The Infinite Square Well A particle is confined between two impenetrable walls: V = 0 for 0 < x < L, and V = ∞ outside. Since the particle can never be found outside the box, Ψ = 0 at both walls. Solving the Schrödinger equation with this boundary condition forces standing-wave solutions: Ψₙ(x) = √(2/L) sin(nπx/L), with quantized energy Eₙ = n²h²/(8mL²), n = 1, 2, 3, ….
Why quantization falls out automatically here — no postulate required Unlike Bohr's model (Unit 5), where quantization had to be assumed by hand, here it emerges directly and unavoidably from requiring Ψ to be a smooth, single-valued function that equals zero at both walls — exactly the same mathematical requirement that forces a guitar string fixed at both ends to vibrate only at specific harmonic frequencies. This is a genuine derivation from the Schrödinger equation, not an extra assumption bolted on.
Example: ground-state energy of a confined electron An electron confined to a 1 nm box: E₁ = (1)²(6.626×10⁻³⁴)²/(8×9.11×10⁻³¹×(1×10⁻⁹)²) ≈ 6.02×10⁻²⁰ J ≈ 0.376 eV.

1. Why must Ψ equal zero at both walls of an infinite square well?

Since V = \u221e outside the box, the particle has zero probability of ever being found there, which forces Ψ = 0 exactly at the walls.

2. Where does the quantization of energy in the particle-in-a-box problem come from?

Unlike Bohr's model, this quantization is a genuine mathematical consequence of the boundary conditions, not an extra postulate.

3. An electron is confined to a 2 nm box (double the earlier 1 nm example). Compared to the 1 nm case, its ground-state energy E₁ is approximately:

Since Eₙ = n²h²/(8mL²), doubling L divides Eₙ by 2² = 4 — a wider box means more widely-spread wavefunctions and lower confinement energy.

4. Fill in the blank: the integer n = 1, 2, 3, … that labels each allowed energy level and standing-wave shape in the particle-in-a-box problem is called the ___.

n is the quantum number; each value labels one allowed standing-wave solution and its corresponding energy.
