1. A particle with energy E less than a barrier's height V₀ (E < V₀) approaches the barrier. Classically, what happens?

Classically, a particle without enough energy to clear a barrier is always reflected — quantum tunneling (a nonzero transmission probability) is what breaks this rule.

2. If a tunneling barrier's width is doubled while its height stays the same, the transmission probability T:

Since T ~ e^(−2κL), doubling the width L drops T by a large exponential factor.

3. A scanning tunneling microscope (STM) achieves atomic-resolution imaging by measuring how a tunneling current changes with:

Tunneling current's exponential sensitivity to barrier width means tiny distance changes produce large, measurable current changes.

4. Fill in the blank: the phenomenon where a quantum particle has a nonzero probability of passing through a barrier it classically shouldn't have enough energy to cross is called quantum ___.

Quantum tunneling is this nonzero transmission probability through a classically forbidden barrier.

5. Which of these correctly describes the overall arc of this course?

The course's throughline is a genuine chain: each domain's leftover puzzle motivated, and was resolved by, the next domain's new idea.

6. A barrier has E < V₀, so classical physics forbids transmission. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the particle's wavefunction do inside the barrier, according to the Schrödinger equation?

(b) Fill in the blank: because the wavefunction is nonzero throughout the barrier, it reconnects to a smaller-amplitude traveling wave beyond it, giving a nonzero transmission ___.

(a) The wavefunction decays exponentially but never abruptly vanishes inside the barrier. (b) This nonzero tail beyond the barrier is exactly the nonzero transmission probability of tunneling.

7. Several modern technologies rely directly on quantum mechanical effects covered in this course. Answer both parts:

(a) A laser's coherent light output relies most directly on:

(b) Fill in the blank: an electron microscope achieves much higher resolution than a light microscope because electrons have a much shorter de Broglie ___ than visible light.

(a) Lasers rely on the same quantized-energy-level transitions from the Bohr model (Unit 5), driven by stimulated emission. (b) Shorter wavelength directly enables finer resolution, per Unit 4's de Broglie relation.

8. Is the treatment of quantum mechanics in this course the complete, final theory of the atom? Answer both parts:

(a) What does full multi-electron atomic theory require, beyond what this course covers?

(b) Fill in the blank: this course is best described as a foundation, not a ___ structure.

(a)-(b) This course presents genuine, fully solvable simple cases, not the complete machinery needed for real multi-electron atoms — a deliberate, honest limitation, not an oversight.

9. A tunneling barrier's height is increased while its width stays fixed. Answer both parts:

(a) What happens to κ = √(2m(V₀ − E))/ħ as V₀ increases?

(b) Fill in the blank: since T ~ e^(−2κL), a larger κ makes the transmission probability ___ (larger/smaller).

(a) Increasing V₀ increases (V₀ − E), and therefore κ. (b) A larger κ makes the exponent more negative, sharply decreasing T.

10. MRI machines and LEDs both rely on quantum effects, though different ones. Answer both parts:

(a) MRI relies most directly on:

(b) Fill in the blank: an LED emits a photon of a specific color because an electron crosses a semiconductor's quantized ___ gap.

(a) MRI detects quantized nuclear spin transitions, a further example of quantization beyond atomic energy levels. (b) LEDs emit photons of energy set by the semiconductor's band gap, the same E = hf idea from Unit 3.
