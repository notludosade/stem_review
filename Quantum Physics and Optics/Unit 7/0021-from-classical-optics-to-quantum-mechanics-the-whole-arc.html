From Classical Optics to Quantum Mechanics — The Whole Arc

Twenty lessons ago, this course started with light as a simple wave bouncing off a mirror. It ends with a wavefunction whose only physical meaning is a probability. Trace the whole chain, one link at a time.

← Lesson 20 Contents Glossary →

The Wave-Optics Chain (Units 1-2) Huygens' wavelets explained reflection and refraction from pure geometry. Applied to two slits, the same wave logic predicted — and Young confirmed — interference. Scaled to one slit and then thousands, it predicted diffraction and sharp grating lines. By the end of Unit 2, light's wave nature looked settled.
The Photon Chain (Unit 3) The photoelectric effect broke the wave-only picture: threshold frequency, not intensity, controlled emission. Einstein's photon (E = hf) fixed it — but only by treating light as discrete particle-like packets. Compton scattering then showed those photons carry real momentum (p = h/λ), settling the question by direct collision physics.
The Duality Chain (Unit 4) De Broglie flipped the photon logic around: if light has particle properties, maybe matter has wave properties (λ = h/(mv)). The electron double-slit experiment confirmed it directly. The Heisenberg uncertainty principle then supplied the mathematical price tag every wave — quantum or classical — pays for being localized.
The Atomic-Structure Chain (Unit 5) Atomic spectra's sharp lines were unexplainable classically. Bohr's postulated quantized orbits fixed hydrogen's spectrum almost perfectly — a real success — but failed for multi-electron atoms and never explained why quantization should hold in the first place.
The Full Quantum-Mechanical Chain (Units 6-7) The Schrödinger equation finally supplied the "why": quantization falls directly out of requiring a wavefunction to satisfy boundary conditions, worked through completely for the particle in a box. The Born rule (|Ψ|²) gave the wavefunction actual physical meaning as probability. Tunneling followed as a direct, testable consequence — and today powers real devices, closing the loop from abstract equation back to concrete technology.
Why each unit's puzzle only got solved by importing an idea from the next Waves explained optics but not the photoelectric effect. Particles explained the photoelectric effect but not the double slit. Quantization explained atomic spectra, but only as an assumption — until the Schrödinger equation derived it from first principles. Notice the pattern: this course is a genuine chain of borrowed resolutions, not a flat list of unrelated topics. And it isn't the final word, either — multi-electron atoms need the full three-dimensional Schrödinger equation plus electron spin and the Pauli exclusion principle, topics for a course beyond this one. Just as Lesson 15 was careful not to overstate the Bohr model, this course itself is a foundation, not a completed structure.
