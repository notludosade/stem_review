1. A classical model of the atom predicts that, as the electron spirals inward, the light it radiates should form:

As the orbital frequency continuously changes while spiraling in, classical theory predicts continuously varying radiated frequency — a smear, contradicted by the sharp lines actually observed.

2. An electron in hydrogen drops from n = 5 (E = −0.54 eV) to n = 2 (E = −3.4 eV). What is the emitted photon's energy?

E_photon = E_high − E_low = −0.54 − (−3.4) = 2.86 eV.

3. Which of the following best describes the status of the Bohr model today?

The Bohr model's hydrogen predictions are a genuine success, but it is superseded by full quantum mechanics, which correctly extends to every atom.

4. Fill in the blank: for hydrogen, the quantized energy of level n is given by Eₙ = −13.6 eV divided by n ___ (what power of n?).

Eₙ = −13.6 eV/n² — energy depends on the inverse square of the quantum number n.

5. What did Bohr have to simply assume, by postulate, rather than derive from a deeper principle?

The "no-radiation, stationary-state" rule was Bohr's central postulate — not derived from anything deeper, which is exactly the model's key limitation.

6. Using Eₙ = −13.6 eV/n² for hydrogen. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the energy of the n = 5 level is E₅ = ___ eV (round to 2 decimal places).

(b) An electron absorbs a photon and jumps from n = 2 to n = 5. The photon's energy is:

(a) E₅ = −13.6/25 = −0.544 ≈ −0.54 eV. (b) E_photon = E₅ − E₂ = −0.54 − (−3.4) = 2.86 eV.

7. Emission and absorption spectra are both measured for the same element. Answer both parts:

(a) How do the wavelengths of an element's emission and absorption lines compare?

(b) Fill in the blank: this is because both come from the same underlying quantized energy-level ___.

(a)-(b) Both emission and absorption reflect the same set of transitions between quantized energy levels, so they occur at identical wavelengths.

8. Bohr's model postulates angular momentum quantization, L = nħ. Answer both parts:

(a) Is this postulate derived from any deeper physical principle in the Bohr model itself?

(b) Which later development finally supplied a genuine derivation of energy-level quantization, rather than an assumption?

(a) L = nħ is postulated, not derived, in the Bohr model. (b) The Schrödinger equation, solved for the particle in a box (Unit 6), derives quantization directly from boundary conditions.

9. Using Eₙ = −13.6 eV/n² for hydrogen. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the ground-state energy (n = 1) of hydrogen is E₁ = ___ eV.

(b) Compared to n = 1, the n = 2 energy level (E₂ = −3.4 eV) is:

(a) E₁ = −13.6/1² = −13.6 eV, the most tightly bound (lowest-energy) state. (b) E₂ = −3.4 eV is higher (less negative) than E₁, meaning n = 2 is a less tightly bound, higher-energy state.

10. Compare Bohr's model to the full quantum-mechanical treatment (Unit 6). Answer both parts:

(a) Does the full quantum treatment abandon the idea of quantized energy levels entirely?

(b) Fill in the blank: unlike Bohr's definite orbits, full quantum mechanics describes the electron using a ___, giving only probabilities rather than an exact path.

(a) Quantization survives, but as a derived consequence rather than a postulate. (b) The wavefunction replaces the definite-orbit picture, consistent with the uncertainty principle.
