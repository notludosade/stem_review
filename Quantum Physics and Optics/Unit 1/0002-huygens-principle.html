Huygens' Principle

Every point on a wavefront can be treated as its own tiny source of wavelets — a simple picture, over two centuries old, that predicts straight-line propagation, reflection, refraction, and diffraction from one geometric rule.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

Huygens' Principle Every point on a wavefront can be treated as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, spreading outward at the wave's speed. The new wavefront, a short time later, is the envelope — the surface tangent to all of those wavelets.
Why one geometric rule explains so much Huygens proposed this in 1678, over a century before Maxwell's equations existed, using no equations for electric or magnetic fields at all — purely geometry. Applied to a flat wavefront in open space, the envelope construction predicts straight-line propagation. Applied at a boundary between two media, it predicts reflection and refraction (Lesson 3). Applied at the edge of an obstacle or narrow opening, it predicts diffraction (Unit 2) — light bending into regions a strict ray picture says it shouldn't reach.
Example: a plane wave stays a plane wave Picture a flat wavefront moving right, with wavelet sources spaced evenly along it. After time Δt, each wavelet is a circle of radius vΔt. The tangent line touching all those circles is itself flat and parallel to the original wavefront, shifted right by vΔt — exactly what a straight-traveling wave should do. The construction doesn't assume straight-line travel; it produces it.

1. According to Huygens' principle, a new wavefront is constructed as:

The envelope — the tangent surface touching every wavelet — is the new wavefront, not any single point or average.

2. Which of these does Huygens' principle predict, beyond simple straight-line propagation?

Applying the same wavelet-envelope construction at a boundary or an obstacle predicts reflection, refraction, and diffraction — all without assuming light is made of particles.

3. Fill in the blank: each point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary ___, according to Huygens.

These secondary wavelets are the building blocks of the envelope construction.

4. A flat (plane) wavefront moving through open space is used to construct the next wavefront via Huygens' principle. What shape is the result?

The tangent line touching every wavelet from a flat wavefront is itself flat — the construction reproduces ordinary straight-line propagation.
