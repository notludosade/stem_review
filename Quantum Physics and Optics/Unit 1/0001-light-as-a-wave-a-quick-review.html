Light as a Wave — A Quick Review

This course picks up right where AP Physics C: Electricity and Magnetism left off — light as an electromagnetic wave. Before the leap into quantum mechanics later in this course, that wave picture needs to be second nature.

Lesson 2 → Contents Glossary →

Wave Vocabulary A wave's amplitude is its maximum displacement from equilibrium. Its wavelength λ is the distance between two consecutive points in exactly the same phase (crest to crest). Its period T is the time for one full cycle, and its frequency f = 1/T is the number of cycles per second. Wave speed relates them: v = fλ.
Light as an Electromagnetic Wave Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave — oscillating electric and magnetic fields, perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel — that needs no medium and travels at c = 3×10⁸ m/s in vacuum. Since v = fλ is fixed at v = c for light in vacuum, frequency and wavelength are not independent: pick one and the other is determined.
Why frequency, not wavelength, is what actually matters going forward A given color of light has a fixed frequency no matter what medium it travels through — frequency is set by the source. Wavelength, by contrast, changes when light enters a new medium and slows down (Lesson 3 revisits this). Starting in Unit 3, energy will turn out to depend on frequency directly (E = hf) — one more reason frequency, not wavelength, is the more fundamental quantity to track going forward.
Example: wavelength from frequency Light with frequency f = 5×10¹⁴ Hz travels through vacuum. λ = c/f = (3×10⁸)/(5×10¹⁴) = 6×10⁻⁷ m = 600 nm — in the visible orange range.

1. Light with wavelength 500 nm travels through vacuum. If a different beam of light had double the frequency of this one, and both travel at c, what would its wavelength be?

Since v = fλ and v = c is fixed for light in vacuum, f and λ are inversely proportional — doubling f halves λ.

2. In a wave, the number of complete cycles passing a fixed point per second is called its:

Frequency f is cycles per second; period T = 1/f is the time for one cycle — the two are reciprocals, not the same quantity.

3. A radio wave has frequency 1×10⁸ Hz and travels at c = 3×10⁸ m/s. What is its wavelength?

λ = c/f = (3×10⁸)/(1×10⁸) = 3 m.

4. Fill in the blank: the distance between two consecutive points on a wave that are in exactly the same phase, such as crest to crest, is called its ___.

This distance is the wavelength, λ.
