1. An electron (m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg) moves at 3×10⁶ m/s. What is its approximate de Broglie wavelength (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s)?

λ = h/(mv) = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/((9.11×10⁻³¹)(3×10⁶)) ≈ 2.42×10⁻¹⁰ m.

2. When electrons are fired one at a time through a double slit, the pattern that builds up after many electrons is:

Despite each electron landing as a discrete point, the accumulated pattern over many electrons is a genuine interference pattern.

3. An electron is confined to Δx = 2×10⁻¹⁰ m. Using Δp ≥ ħ/(2Δx) with ħ ≈ 1.055×10⁻³⁴ J·s, what is the minimum possible Δp?

Δp ≥ ħ/(2Δx) = (1.055×10⁻³⁴)/(2×2×10⁻¹⁰) ≈ 2.64×10⁻²⁵ kg·m/s.

4. Fill in the blank: the wavelength λ = h/(mv) associated with any moving mass is called its de Broglie ___.

This is the de Broglie wavelength, extending wave-particle duality from light to matter.

5. If a which-path detector is added at the slits to determine which slit each electron passes through, what happens to the interference pattern?

Measuring which-path information destroys the interference pattern — a well-verified and genuinely strange feature of quantum measurement.

6. A proton (m = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg) and an electron (m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg) travel at the same speed. Answer both parts:

(a) Which particle has the longer de Broglie wavelength?

(b) Fill in the blank: this is because de Broglie wavelength is inversely proportional to ___.

(a)-(b) Since λ = h/(mv), a smaller mass (the electron) gives a larger wavelength at the same speed — mass and wavelength are inversely related.

7. Consider the historical order of discoveries: light was long thought purely a wave, then shown (Unit 3) to also have particle properties. Answer both parts:

(a) De Broglie's 1924 hypothesis proposed the mirror-image idea for matter — that particles like electrons might also have:

(b) Fill in the blank: wave-particle duality is a property of ___ (light only / matter only / both light and matter).

(a) De Broglie extended p = h/λ, established for photons, to propose matter waves for all masses. (b) Duality is universal — it applies to light and matter alike, not to light alone.

8. Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2 is the Heisenberg uncertainty principle. Answer both parts:

(a) If a particle's position is confined more tightly (Δx shrinks), what must happen to Δp?

(b) This uncertainty is best understood as reflecting:

(a) Since the product has a lower bound, shrinking one factor forces the other to grow. (b) The uncertainty reflects the wavefunction's genuine mathematical properties, not merely instrument limitations.

9. A 0.20 kg ball moves at 15 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: its de Broglie wavelength λ = h/(mv) ≈ ___ ×10⁻³⁴ m (round to 2 decimal places).

(b) This wavelength is best described as:

(a) λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/((0.20)(15)) ≈ 2.21×10⁻³⁴ m. (b) This is vastly smaller than any measurable scale — the formula applies, but the effect is undetectable for macroscopic masses.

10. In the electron double-slit experiment, individual electrons are fired one at a time, with no other electron nearby to interact with. Answer both parts:

(a) Does each individual electron still land as a single, discrete point on the detector?

(b) Fill in the blank: even though each electron lands individually, the pattern built up from many electrons is an ___ pattern.

(a) Each electron registers as one discrete point, confirming particle-like detection. (b) The aggregate pattern over many such points is nonetheless a genuine interference pattern — both particle-like detection and wave-like statistics, at once.
