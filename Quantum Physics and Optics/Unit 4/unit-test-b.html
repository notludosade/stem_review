1. An electron (m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg) moves at 1.5×10⁶ m/s. What is its approximate de Broglie wavelength (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s)?

λ = h/(mv) = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/((9.11×10⁻³¹)(1.5×10⁶)) ≈ 4.85×10⁻¹⁰ m.

2. The electron double-slit experiment is significant mainly because it shows:

This experiment extends wave-particle duality to matter, mirroring what Unit 3 established for light.

3. A particle is confined to Δx = 5×10⁻¹¹ m. Using Δp ≥ ħ/(2Δx) with ħ ≈ 1.055×10⁻³⁴ J·s, what is the minimum possible Δp?

Δp ≥ ħ/(2Δx) = (1.055×10⁻³⁴)/(2×5×10⁻¹¹) ≈ 1.055×10⁻²⁴ kg·m/s.

4. Fill in the blank: Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2 is known as the Heisenberg ___ principle.

The Heisenberg uncertainty principle sets a lower bound on the combined precision of position and momentum.

5. Which statement correctly describes wave-particle duality, based on this unit?

Both light (Unit 3) and matter (this unit) display both wave-like and particle-like behavior — duality is universal, not a light-only or matter-only quirk.

6. A helium nucleus (m ≈ 6.64×10⁻²⁷ kg) and an electron (m ≈ 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg) travel at the same speed. Answer both parts:

(a) Which particle has the shorter de Broglie wavelength?

(b) Fill in the blank: at the same speed, the more massive particle has the ___ (shorter/longer) de Broglie wavelength.

(a)-(b) Since λ = h/(mv), a larger mass gives a smaller wavelength at fixed speed — the massive helium nucleus has the shorter wavelength.

7. De Broglie's 1924 hypothesis reversed the logic that had already been established for photons. Answer both parts:

(a) Which earlier relation did de Broglie generalize to propose matter waves?

(b) Fill in the blank: de Broglie's hypothesis was first confirmed experimentally by observing ___ diffracting off a crystal, matching the predicted wavelength.

(a) Solving photon momentum p = h/λ for λ and applying it to any mass is exactly de Broglie's move. (b) The Davisson-Germer experiment (1927) confirmed electron diffraction matching de Broglie's predicted wavelength.

8. Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2 sets a fundamental limit. Answer both parts:

(a) A sharply localized (narrow) quantum wave packet necessarily contains:

(b) This broad-range-of-wavelengths property is:

(a) Building a narrow spatial packet requires superposing many wavelengths, hence many momenta via de Broglie. (b) This is a general wave property (seen even in classical pulses); quantum mechanics inherits it and gives it physical meaning via Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2.

9. A 0.50 kg object moves at 8 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: its de Broglie wavelength λ = h/(mv) ≈ ___ ×10⁻³⁴ m (round to 2 decimal places).

(b) This wavelength is best described as:

(a) λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/((0.50)(8)) ≈ 1.66×10⁻³⁴ m. (b) This is vastly below any measurable physical scale.

10. A which-path detector is added to an electron double-slit setup, then later removed. Answer both parts:

(a) With the detector active, what pattern is observed?

(b) Fill in the blank: once the detector is removed, the ___ pattern returns.

(a) Measuring which-path information collapses the interference pattern into simple two-band behavior. (b) Removing the detector restores the full interference pattern, since which-path information is no longer being recorded.
