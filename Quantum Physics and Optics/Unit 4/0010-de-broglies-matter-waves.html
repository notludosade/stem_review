De Broglie's Matter Waves

If light, long assumed a pure wave, secretly behaves like particles, de Broglie asked the mirror-image question in 1924: what if matter, long assumed pure particles, secretly behaves like waves too?

← Unit 3, Lesson 9 Lesson 11 → Contents Glossary →

The de Broglie Wavelength Every moving mass has an associated wavelength: λ = h/p = h/(mv). This is the same relation as photon momentum (p = h/λ, Lesson 9), simply solved for λ and applied to any object with momentum, not just light.
Why this idea is symmetric with — not separate from — Unit 3 Unit 3 showed light, normally thought of as a wave, also has particle properties (E = hf, p = h/λ). De Broglie's move was to take that same equation and run it in reverse: if p = h/λ holds for photons, why not propose it holds for everything with momentum? The catch is Planck's constant h is astonishingly small, so λ is only large enough to notice for extremely small masses like electrons — for anything macroscopic, the wavelength is far too small ever to observe.
Example: electron vs. baseball An electron (m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg) moving at 1×10⁶ m/s: λ = h/(mv) = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/((9.11×10⁻³¹)(1×10⁶)) ≈ 7.27×10⁻¹⁰ m — comparable to atomic spacing, easily observable. A 0.145 kg baseball at 40 m/s: λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/((0.145)(40)) ≈ 1.14×10⁻³⁴ m — unmeasurably smaller than any physical scale, which is why baseballs never visibly diffract.

1. De Broglie's hypothesis proposes that λ = h/(mv) applies to:

De Broglie proposed this relation for all matter, extending wave-particle duality beyond light — though the wavelength is only observable for very small masses.

2. Why is a baseball's de Broglie wavelength never observed in everyday life, even though the formula technically applies to it?

The formula applies universally, but a baseball's huge mass (relative to an electron) makes λ = h/(mv) fantastically, unmeasurably small — not zero, just practically undetectable.

3. An electron moves at 2×10⁶ m/s. What is its approximate de Broglie wavelength?

λ = h/(mv) = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/((9.11×10⁻³¹)(2×10⁶)) ≈ 3.64×10⁻¹⁰ m.

4. Fill in the blank: the wavelength λ = h/(mv) associated with any moving mass is called its ___.

This is the de Broglie wavelength, extending wave-particle duality to matter.
