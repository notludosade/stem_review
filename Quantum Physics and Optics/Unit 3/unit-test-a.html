1. Below a metal's threshold frequency, increasing the light's intensity:

No amount of intensity below the threshold frequency ejects electrons — each photon individually carries too little energy, regardless of how many arrive.

2. A metal's work function is 2.5×10⁻¹⁹ J. Light of frequency 7×10¹⁴ Hz strikes it (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s). What is the maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons?

hf = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(7×10¹⁴) ≈ 4.64×10⁻¹⁹ J. KE_max = hf − φ = 4.64×10⁻¹⁹ − 2.5×10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 2.14×10⁻¹⁹ J.

3. An X-ray photon Compton-scatters off a free electron at θ = 180° (straight back). Using Δλ = (h/(m_ec))(1 − cosθ) with h/(m_ec) ≈ 2.43×10⁻¹² m, what is Δλ?

Δλ = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(1 − cos180°) = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(1 − (−1)) = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(2) ≈ 4.86×10⁻¹² m.

4. Fill in the blank: the minimum light frequency below which a metal ejects no photoelectrons at all is called its ___ frequency.

Every metal has its own threshold frequency f₀ = φ/h, set by its work function.

5. What does Compton scattering demonstrate about photons that the photoelectric effect alone does not?

The photoelectric effect established quantized photon energy; Compton scattering additionally showed photons carry momentum, conserved exactly as in an ordinary particle collision.

6. A metal's work function is 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ J. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the threshold frequency f₀ = φ/h ≈ ___ ×10¹⁴ Hz (round to 1 decimal place; h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s).

(b) Light of frequency 3×10¹⁴ Hz (below this threshold) strikes the metal. What happens?

(a) f₀ = φ/h = (3.2×10⁻¹⁹)/(6.626×10⁻³⁴) ≈ 4.83×10¹⁴ Hz. (b) Below the threshold frequency, no photon carries enough energy to eject an electron, no matter how many photons (intensity) arrive.

7. A photoelectric experiment shines light above a metal's threshold frequency onto its surface. Answer both parts:

(a) Increasing the light's intensity, while keeping frequency fixed above threshold, increases:

(b) Increasing the light's frequency instead, while keeping intensity fixed, increases:

(a) Intensity controls photon arrival rate, hence electrons ejected per second. (b) Frequency controls energy per photon, hence each ejected electron's maximum kinetic energy — these are two independent knobs.

8. An X-ray photon Compton-scatters at θ = 60°. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: Δλ = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(1 − cos60°) ≈ ___ ×10⁻¹² m (round to 2 decimal places).

(b) Compared to scattering at θ = 60°, scattering at θ = 120° would produce a wavelength shift that is:

(a) Δλ = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(1 − 0.5) = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(0.5) ≈ 1.22×10⁻¹² m. (b) cos120° = −0.5, so (1 − cosθ) = 1.5, larger than at 60° (where it's 0.5) — the shift grows as scattering angle increases toward 180°.

9. Light of wavelength 400 nm strikes a metal with work function 3.0×10⁻¹⁹ J (use hc ≈ 1240 eV·nm, or in SI: E = hc/λ). Answer both parts:

(a) The photon energy E = hc/λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(3×10⁸)/(400×10⁻⁹) is approximately:

(b) Fill in the blank: the maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons is KE_max ≈ ___ ×10⁻¹⁹ J (round to 2 decimal places).

(a) E = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(3×10⁸)/(4×10⁻⁷) ≈ 4.97×10⁻¹⁹ J. (b) KE_max = 4.97×10⁻¹⁹ − 3.0×10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 1.97×10⁻¹⁹ J.

10. A photon has wavelength 500 nm. Answer both parts:

(a) Its momentum p = h/λ is approximately:

(b) Fill in the blank: this momentum relation, p = h/λ, is the same equation later flipped around (Unit 4) to propose that matter, not just light, has a wave___.

(a) p = h/λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/(5×10⁻⁷) ≈ 1.325×10⁻²⁷ kg·m/s. (b) Solving p = h/λ for λ gives de Broglie's matter-wave wavelength, λ = h/p.
