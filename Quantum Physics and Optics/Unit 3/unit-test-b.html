1. Above a metal's threshold frequency, dim (low-intensity) light ejects electrons:

Emission is instantaneous above threshold, even at very low intensity — each photon-electron interaction is a single, immediate event.

2. A metal's work function is 3.5×10⁻¹⁹ J. Light of frequency 8×10¹⁴ Hz strikes it (h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s). What is the maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons?

hf = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(8×10¹⁴) ≈ 5.30×10⁻¹⁹ J. KE_max = hf − φ = 5.30×10⁻¹⁹ − 3.5×10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 1.80×10⁻¹⁹ J.

3. An X-ray photon Compton-scatters off a free electron at θ = 0° (no deflection at all). Using Δλ = (h/(m_ec))(1 − cosθ), what is Δλ?

At θ = 0°, cosθ = 1, so (1 − cosθ) = 0 — no scattering means no wavelength shift, as expected.

4. Fill in the blank: a metal's minimum energy needed to free an electron from its surface is called its work ___.

The work function φ sets the threshold frequency f₀ = φ/h.

5. A purely classical (non-particle) wave picture of light scattering off an electron predicts the scattered light's frequency should:

Classically, a wave sloshing a charge back and forth should re-radiate at the same frequency — the measured shift to lower frequency is what only a particle-collision picture explains.

6. A metal's work function is 4.0×10⁻¹⁹ J. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the threshold frequency f₀ = φ/h ≈ ___ ×10¹⁴ Hz (round to 1 decimal place; h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s).

(b) Light of frequency 9×10¹⁴ Hz (above this threshold) strikes the metal, then its intensity is doubled. What happens to the maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons?

(a) f₀ = φ/h = (4.0×10⁻¹⁹)/(6.626×10⁻³⁴) ≈ 6.04×10¹⁴ Hz. (b) KE_max depends only on frequency, not intensity; doubling intensity only doubles the rate of ejected electrons.

7. A photoelectric experiment uses light with frequency well above a metal's threshold. Answer both parts:

(a) Is there any measurable time delay between the light striking the metal and the first electron being ejected?

(b) What does this instantaneous emission suggest about how energy is transferred from light to an electron?

(a) Emission is instantaneous above threshold. (b) This rules out gradual classical accumulation and supports discrete, one-photon-to-one-electron energy transfer.

8. An X-ray photon Compton-scatters at θ = 90°. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: Δλ = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(1 − cos90°) = ___ ×10⁻¹² m (round to 2 decimal places).

(b) In this collision, compared to before scattering, the photon after scattering has:

(a) cos90° = 0, so Δλ = (2.43×10⁻¹²)(1) = 2.43×10⁻¹² m. (b) The photon loses energy and momentum to the recoiling electron, so its wavelength increases (energy decreases) after scattering.

9. Light of wavelength 350 nm strikes a metal with work function 4.0×10⁻¹⁹ J. Answer both parts:

(a) The photon energy E = hc/λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(3×10⁸)/(350×10⁻⁹) is approximately:

(b) Fill in the blank: the maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons is KE_max ≈ ___ ×10⁻¹⁹ J (round to 2 decimal places).

(a) E = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(3×10⁸)/(3.5×10⁻⁷) ≈ 5.68×10⁻¹⁹ J. (b) KE_max = 5.68×10⁻¹⁹ − 4.0×10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 1.68×10⁻¹⁹ J.

10. A photon has wavelength 200 nm. Answer both parts:

(a) Its momentum p = h/λ is approximately:

(b) Fill in the blank: photon momentum p = h/λ = E/___ relates momentum directly to energy and the speed of light.

(a) p = h/λ = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)/(2×10⁻⁷) ≈ 3.31×10⁻²⁷ kg·m/s. (b) p = E/c is the equivalent form of photon momentum, connecting momentum to energy via the speed of light.
