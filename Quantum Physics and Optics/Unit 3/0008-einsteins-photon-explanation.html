Einstein's Photon Explanation

Einstein's fix wasn't a tweak to wave theory — it was to say light itself arrives in discrete particle-like chunks, energy E = hf, each interacting with exactly one electron at a time.

← Lesson 7 Lesson 9 → Contents Glossary →

Photons and the Photoelectric Equation A photon is a discrete packet of light energy, E = hf, where h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s is Planck's constant. A metal's work function φ is the minimum energy needed to free an electron from its surface. Einstein's photoelectric equation: KEmax = hf − φ, with threshold frequency f₀ = φ/h.
Why treating light as particles resolves every fact from Lesson 7 Each photon interacts with exactly one electron, transferring its full energy hf in a single event — not gradually, the way a continuous wave would. Below f₀, a single photon's energy is less than φ, so it can never free an electron, regardless of how many additional (still individually too-weak) photons arrive per second — that's why brighter light below threshold does nothing. Above f₀, one photon does the job instantly, with no accumulation delay. More intensity means more photons per second, hence more ejected electrons per second — but each photon still carries the same energy hf, so each electron's maximum kinetic energy depends only on frequency, exactly as observed.
Example: maximum kinetic energy A metal has work function φ = 2.0×10⁻¹⁹ J. Light of frequency 6×10¹⁴ Hz strikes it. hf = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(6×10¹⁴) ≈ 3.98×10⁻¹⁹ J. KEmax = hf − φ = 3.98×10⁻¹⁹ − 2.0×10⁻¹⁹ = 1.98×10⁻¹⁹ J.

1. In Einstein's photon picture, why does a single dim photon above the threshold frequency eject an electron instantly, with no delay?

A single photon-electron interaction transfers hf all at once — there is no accumulation step, so no delay, regardless of intensity.

2. A metal's work function is 3.0×10⁻¹⁹ J, struck by light delivering 5.0×10⁻¹⁹ J per photon. What is the maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons?

KE_max = hf − φ = 5.0×10⁻¹⁹ − 3.0×10⁻¹⁹ = 2.0×10⁻¹⁹ J.

3. Raising the frequency of light shining on a metal, while keeping it above threshold, increases:

Higher frequency means more energy per photon (E = hf), which raises KE_max = hf − φ directly; intensity, not frequency, controls electron count.

4. Fill in the blank: the minimum energy needed to free an electron from a particular metal's surface is called its ___.

The work function φ sets the threshold frequency f₀ = φ/h below which no photoelectrons are ejected.
