"Data job" covers several genuinely different roles, distinguished mainly by which parts of this course's material they lean on most heavily day to day.

Common data roles

Where each role sits relative to this course Data Analyst — spends most of their time in Units 2-6: spreadsheets and SQL (querying and joining tables), descriptive statistics, and turning findings into charts and stories for stakeholders. This is the role this course maps to most directly. Data Engineer — builds and maintains the pipelines and databases (Unit 3's relational model, at production scale) that get raw data into a queryable, analysis-ready state (Unit 5) in the first place, usually with much heavier programming involvement than an analyst role. Data Scientist — takes the statistical foundation from Unit 4 (correlation, regression) further into predictive modeling and machine learning — the territory this course's MISSION.md explicitly deferred to AI Developer. Business Intelligence (BI) Analyst — focuses heavily on Unit 6's dashboards and recurring reporting, often using dedicated BI tools (Tableau, Power BI) built on the same SQL and chart-choice fundamentals from this course.
Why the boundaries between these roles are blurrier in practice than the list above suggests At a smaller organization, one person might do all four jobs; at a larger one, they might be four separate teams with a formal handoff between them. Job titles also aren't standardized across companies — one company's "Data Analyst" does what another calls a "BI Analyst." The useful takeaway isn't memorizing exact titles, it's recognizing which skills from this course (SQL, statistics, visualization, cleaning) a given job posting is actually asking for, regardless of what it's called — since that's what determines whether the role is a good fit for what you've learned.
Reading a job posting A posting titled "Data Analyst" that lists "SQL, Excel, dashboard creation, stakeholder communication" as requirements maps closely to Units 2, 3, and 6 of this course. A posting titled the same but listing "Python, statistical modeling, A/B testing, machine learning" is really describing a Data Scientist role, regardless of its title — and would need skills this course explicitly scoped out to AI Developer.
