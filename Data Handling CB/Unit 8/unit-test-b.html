1. Which data role is most focused on recurring dashboards and reporting, often using dedicated tools like Tableau or Power BI?

BI Analysts specialize in dashboard tooling built on the same SQL and chart-choice fundamentals covered in this course.

2. Which data role does this course's curriculum map to most directly?

The Data Analyst role's core day-to-day work overlaps most directly with this course's units.

3. A job posting titled "BI Analyst" lists SQL, Excel, dashboard creation, and stakeholder communication as its requirements. According to this course's mapping, which units does this posting's actual work most resemble?

Job titles aren't standardized across companies — the actual listed skills, not the title, are the reliable signal of what a role really is.

4. What is a reviewer evaluating a data portfolio piece actually checking for, beyond whether the final chart looks nice?

The visible reasoning across every stage of the pipeline is what distinguishes a strong portfolio piece from a merely polished chart.

5. Fill in the blank: an interview format that presents a scenario like "how would you investigate why signups dropped 20% last month?" and asks the candidate to reason through it live is called a ___.

This is the standard term for the format used across data roles to evaluate reasoning process rather than one "correct" number.

6. Two candidates submit portfolio pieces built from the same public dataset: one shows only a clean final chart, the other shows the messy raw data and every cleaning decision made along the way. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is the second candidate's piece considered stronger?

(b) What specific artifact, first argued for back in Lesson 22, should document those cleaning decisions?

Showing the raw data and the specific judgment calls made to clean it, recorded in a cleaning log, is what lets a reviewer trust the conclusion rather than just admire the picture.

7. A candidate is deciding what dataset to build a portfolio project around. Answer both parts:

(a) Which of these is a reasonable source of raw material for the project, per this lesson?

(b) Why does choosing a question the candidate personally cares about tend to help the final result?

Public datasets from sources like Kaggle or data.gov work well as raw material, as long as the project still goes through the full pipeline — and genuine motivation for the question supplies context for free.

8. A case-study prompt asks: "Weekly active users dropped 15% last week — what would you look into?" Answer both parts:

(a) What should a strong candidate check first, before assuming a product-side explanation?

(b) Why is this careful, ruling-out-confounders approach a stronger signal than naming one specific root cause immediately?

What's being tested is whether the candidate's instinct is to reach for a plausible driver immediately or to first rule out measurement issues and confounders — the discipline this course built across several units.

9. The capstone walks a churn-analysis dataset through every unit of this course, in order. Answer both parts:

(a) Why does the capstone apply Unit 7's ethics considerations throughout the whole pipeline, rather than only at the very end?

(b) Which unit's tools put honest error bars on the churn-rate estimate itself?

Because the dataset involves real customers, Unit 7 applies throughout, not just at the end; Unit 4's confidence intervals are what put honest error bars on the churn-rate estimate itself.

10. The capstone closes with an argument about a single discipline running through all eight units. Answer both parts:

(a) Which lesson's argument is the clearest direct example of this discipline?

(b) According to this argument, what single discipline underlies every unit in this course?

Deliberate, disclosed judgment calls — not any single technique — is the thread tying every unit together, and the cleaning log is the clearest concrete example of it.
