1. A small company has nine employees earning $40,000 each and one executive earning $1,040,000. The mean salary is $140,000. Why is the median a more representative "typical" salary here?

The mean is correctly computed but sensitive to outliers; the median, defined by position rather than magnitude, resists exactly this kind of distortion.

2. Fill in the blank: the measure of spread computed as Q3 minus Q1, which — like the median — resists distortion by outliers unlike the plain range or standard deviation, is the ___.

The interquartile range only looks at the middle 50% of the data, unlike the range, which is fully determined by the two most extreme values.

3. What defines a normal distribution?

Symmetry and bell-shaped clustering around the mean are the defining features of a normal distribution.

4. Fill in the blank: home prices in a city cluster around $250,000, but a few multi-million-dollar mansions stretch the tail of the distribution toward high values. This shape is described as ___.

Right-skewed (positive skew) data has a long tail stretching toward high values, exactly as a few very expensive homes would produce.

5. A study finds a correlation coefficient of -0.85 between hours of television watched per week and exam score. What does this indicate?

A coefficient near -1 signals a strong tendency for one variable to rise as the other falls — it says nothing on its own about which variable, if either, drives the other.

6. A study finds that towns with more ice cream parlors also have more sunburn cases. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the most likely explanation, other than one directly causing the other?

(b) What does this correlation, by itself, fail to establish?

Warm weather is the classic confounder here — it drives both quantities upward together, which is exactly why a correlation alone can't establish that either one causes the other.

7. A survey of 800 shoppers finds 45% prefer online shopping, with a margin of error of ±4% at 95% confidence. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the ±4% actually quantify?

(b) What range does this imply for the true population value?

Margin of error is the plus-or-minus half-width around a sample statistic due to random sampling variability — here, 45% ± 4% spans roughly 41% to 49%.

8. A pollster reports a very narrow 95% confidence interval around a survey result. Answer both parts:

(a) What does "95% confidence interval" actually mean?

(b) Does this narrow interval rule out sampling bias in how respondents were recruited?

A narrow confidence interval only rules out one kind of error — random noise. It says nothing about systematic sampling bias from the collection method itself.

9. A regression fits hours studied (x) to predicted exam score (y): score = 5x + 50. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the predicted score for a student who studies 6 hours?

(b) What does the slope value of 5 mean?

5(6) + 50 = 80. The slope (5) is the predicted change in y per one-unit increase in x; the intercept (50) is the predicted y when x is 0.

10. The regression from question 9 has an R-squared of 0.64. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: this means ___% of the variation in exam score is explained by hours studied.

(b) What criterion does simple linear regression use to choose the single best-fit line?

R-squared reports the share of variation in y explained by the line; least squares is the fitting method that determines the line itself.
