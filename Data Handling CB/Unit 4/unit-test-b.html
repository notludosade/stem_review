1. A neighborhood has nine houses valued at $300,000 each and one mansion valued at $2,700,000. The mean home value is $540,000. Why is the median a more representative "typical" home value here?

The mean is correctly computed but sensitive to outliers; the median, defined by position rather than magnitude, resists exactly this kind of distortion.

2. Fill in the blank: the measure of spread that ignores the most extreme 25% of values on each end, computed as Q3 minus Q1, is the ___.

The interquartile range only looks at the middle 50% of the data, unlike the range, which is fully determined by the two most extreme values.

3. Which of these best describes a normal distribution?

Symmetry and bell-shaped clustering around the mean are the defining features of a normal distribution.

4. Fill in the blank: retirement ages in a public-sector pension system cluster near a mandatory retirement age of 65, with a longer tail stretching toward much lower ages for early retirees. This shape is described as ___.

Left-skewed (negative skew) data has most values clustered near an upper limit, with the longer tail stretching toward lower values.

5. A study finds a correlation coefficient of +0.78 between weekly exercise hours and self-reported energy level. What does this indicate?

A coefficient near +1 signals a strong tendency for both variables to move in the same direction — it says nothing on its own about which variable, if either, drives the other.

6. A study finds that cities with more firefighters dispatched to a fire also tend to have more property damage from that fire. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the most likely explanation, other than firefighters directly causing damage?

(b) What does this correlation, by itself, fail to establish?

Fire severity is the classic confounder here — bigger fires get more firefighters dispatched and cause more damage, so neither number is causing the other.

7. A survey of 1,200 residents finds 62% support a local measure, with a margin of error of ±3% at 95% confidence. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the ±3% actually quantify?

(b) What range does this imply for the true population value?

Margin of error is the plus-or-minus half-width around a sample statistic due to random sampling variability — here, 62% ± 3% spans roughly 59% to 65%.

8. A researcher reports a very narrow 95% confidence interval around a survey result recruited entirely through one social media platform. Answer both parts:

(a) What does "95% confidence interval" actually mean?

(b) Does this narrow interval rule out sampling bias from recruiting only on one platform?

A narrow confidence interval only rules out one kind of error — random noise. It says nothing about systematic sampling bias from the collection method itself.

9. A regression fits hours of sleep (x) to predicted productivity score (y): productivity = 8x + 20. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the predicted productivity score for someone who sleeps 5 hours?

(b) What does the slope value of 8 mean?

8(5) + 20 = 60. The slope (8) is the predicted change in y per one-unit increase in x; the intercept (20) is the predicted y when x is 0.

10. The regression from question 9 has an R-squared of 0.49. Answer both parts:

(a) What criterion does simple linear regression use to choose the single best-fit line?

(b) Fill in the blank: an R-squared of 0.49 means ___% of the variation in productivity score is explained by hours of sleep.

R-squared reports the share of variation in y explained by the line; least squares is the fitting method that determines the line itself.
