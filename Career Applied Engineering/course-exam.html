1. A candidate's draft bullet reads "Owned the sensor calibration workstream." A mentor suggests naming the specific defect caught and its estimated impact instead. What underlying principle is the mentor applying?

A specific defect and its estimated impact are verifiable evidence a hiring manager can actually weigh; "owned a workstream" only restates that a role existed.

2. Fill in the blank: whether it's a resume bullet, a portfolio write-up, or a STAR story, a hiring process keeps asking for the same thing — a claim backed by specific ___.

Evidence is the constant thread across every format — a claim without it reads the same way anywhere: unverifiable.

3. Across both a whiteboard design problem and a behavioral question, what is the one underlying thing an interviewer is usually trying to evaluate?

Both a design prompt and a behavioral question are structured to surface reasoning and judgment on display, not to check a single fixed correct output.

4. Fill in the blank: whether the stage is a design problem or a behavioral question, what's actually being scored is the candidate's visible ___, not just a final outcome.

An interviewer can't evaluate a process they never see — narrating is what turns internal reasoning into evaluable evidence.

5. Writing honest documentation, surfacing a trade-off's real risks to a stakeholder, and responding non-defensively to feedback all share which underlying professional value?

Documentation, honest trade-off framing, and non-defensive feedback all come down to making true information available to people who need it.

6. Fill in the blank: in both documentation and stakeholder communication, withholding true information eventually costs the same thing — the ___ that other people place in the engineer's word.

Trust is the resource that erodes in both cases — a team's confidence in undocumented code, and a stakeholder's confidence in what they're told.

7. A client demands the original scope, delivered a month earlier than planned, at the original budget — with nothing else changing. What does the iron triangle predict?

Tightening all three linked constraints at once is a structural impossibility, not a matter of team effort.

8. Fill in the blank: a project manager is asked to compress the schedule by three weeks with no change to scope or budget. If this is agreed to without any other visible change, ___ is the most likely constraint absorbing the cost invisibly.

Quality is the fourth variable that tends to give silently under this kind of pressure.

9. A team is building a compliance report generator whose exact regulatory field requirements were finalized by law before the project began. Which process fits, and why?

When requirements are genuinely fixed and known in advance, waterfall's full up-front planning avoids the rework a change mid-project would cause.

10. Fill in the blank: a firm runs waterfall for a bridge's physical design but agile for its traffic-sensor firmware, within the same overall project. Combining two processes like this within a single project is called a ___ approach.

Matching each piece of work to how knowable its requirements are matters more than applying one label to the whole project — this mixed approach is called hybrid.

11. A recent graduate assumes that passing the FE exam should be functionally equivalent to a full PE license, since both are administered by the same body. What is the actual relationship?

The FE is an early knowledge check; the PE, gated by years of supervised experience, is what actually confers the full license.

12. Fill in the blank: a hiring manager wants to let a new hire skip the supervised-experience period before the PE exam. The multi-year gap exists specifically to let real-world ___ develop under supervision before the PE exam tests for it.

Judgment specifically cannot be verified any earlier than after years of supervised practice.
