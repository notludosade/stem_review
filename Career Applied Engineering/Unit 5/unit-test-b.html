Unit 5 Test — Version B

1. A team is finalizing structural drawings for a building whose code-mandated requirements were locked in months ago and cannot realistically change. Which process fits this situation best?

When requirements are genuinely stable and known in advance, waterfall's up-front planning avoids costly rework compared to changing course mid-project.

2. A startup is prototyping a new feature where the team genuinely doesn't know what users will want until they try it. Which process fits, and why?

When real requirements are uncertain up front, agile's short cycles let a team learn from actual feedback instead of building the wrong thing carefully and on schedule.

3. An invitation instead reads: "Decide which of two vendor proposals to accept — bring your cost comparison." How does this differ from a vague "Weekly Sync" invite, based on the lesson?

Stating the decision or output needed by the end is the single highest-leverage fix for meetings that would otherwise drift.

4. An engineer realizes partway through a vendor evaluation that they have a financial stake in one of the companies being considered, but says nothing because the stake doesn't end up affecting their vote. Did they meet the professional standard?

The actual violation is staying silently involved without disclosing a conflict, regardless of whether it actually changed the outcome.

5. Fill in the blank: because pressure to cut a corner usually shows up as ordinary ___ pressure or a teammate's optimism rather than an explicit demand, it's harder to recognize as an ethical question in the moment.

This kind of pressure doesn't feel like an ethical question — it feels like an ordinary scheduling conversation, which is exactly what makes it dangerous.

6. An engineering firm is running one large project with both a fixed physical structure and accompanying custom firmware. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the lesson say about picking a single process label for the whole project?

(b) Fill in the blank: using waterfall for the fixed structure and agile for the firmware within the same project is an example of a ___ approach.

(a) The underlying question — how much can genuinely be known in advance for a given piece of work — matters more than applying one label uniformly. (b) Mixing both approaches within one project is a hybrid approach.

7. A meeting was held with a clear purpose and only the necessary attendees, but no one wrote down what was decided. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the likely consequence, based on the lesson?

(b) Fill in the blank: a brief ___ record of the decision and who owns each resulting action is the third thing every meeting needs, alongside purpose and the right people.

(a) Without a written record, a meeting's outcome exists only in memory, which fades and diverges within days. (b) A written record is the third essential ingredient, alongside purpose and the right people.

8. A team member's status update leads with three paragraphs of completed work and buries a serious blocker in the final line. Answer both parts:

(a) What's wrong with this structure, based on the lesson?

(b) Fill in the blank: a well-structured update leads with what's ___, since that's the one category readers can actually act on.

(a) Since most readers skim, burying the one actionable item delays exactly the help a status update should enable. (b) Leading with what's blocked respects how the update will actually be read.

9. An engineer reports a safety concern directly to a regulator on the very first day they notice it, without raising it internally at all. Answer both parts:

(a) What does the lesson say about this sequence?

(b) Fill in the blank: a direct supervisor, an ethics hotline, or the engineer of record are all examples of ___ channels that should typically be tried before reporting externally.

(a) External reporting as a first response, before internal channels get a real chance, is usually the wrong sequence. (b) These are all internal channels that should be tried first.

10. A concerned engineer mentions a possible safety issue verbally in a hallway conversation but never writes it down. Answer both parts:

(a) Why is this a weaker response than documenting the concern in writing?

(b) Fill in the blank: recording a concern in ___, even briefly, is most valuable exactly when it feels inconvenient, since it protects both the person raising it and anyone who acts on it later.

(a) A written record is the only version of the concern that survives a later dispute. (b) Writing the concern down, even briefly, is what actually protects everyone involved.
