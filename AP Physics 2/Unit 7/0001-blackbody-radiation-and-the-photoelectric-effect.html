Blackbody Radiation and the Photoelectric Effect

Two puzzles physicists couldn't explain with wave theory around 1900 both had the same surprising resolution: energy comes in discrete chunks, not a continuous stream.

← Unit 6, Lesson 6 Lesson 2 → Contents Glossary →

The Blackbody Radiation Problem Classical wave theory predicted that a hot object should radiate infinite energy at short wavelengths (the "ultraviolet catastrophe") — but real objects don't. Max Planck resolved this by proposing that energy is emitted only in discrete packets, E = hf, where h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s is Planck's constant.
The Photoelectric Effect Shining light on certain metals ejects electrons — but only if the light's frequency exceeds a metal-specific threshold frequency f0, regardless of intensity. Einstein explained this using Planck's idea: KEmax = hf − φ, where φ = hf0 is the metal's work function (minimum energy needed to free an electron).
Why brighter light alone can't eject electrons below the threshold frequency Classical wave theory predicted that enough intensity (brightness) should eventually shake an electron loose, regardless of frequency, since more intense waves carry more total energy. But experiments showed dim light above f₀ ejects electrons instantly, while intense light below f₀ ejects none at all — because each individual light packet (photon) carries energy hf, and it takes one single photon-electron interaction to eject one electron. If a single photon's energy hf is less than the work function φ, no amount of additional (but still individually too-weak) photons can add up — each interaction is one photon transferring its full energy to one electron, not a gradual accumulation.
Example: photon energy from frequency Find the energy of a photon with frequency 5×10¹⁴ Hz. E = hf = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(5×10¹⁴) ≈ 3.31×10⁻¹⁹ J.
Example: maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons A metal has work function φ = 2.0×10⁻¹⁹ J. Light of frequency 6×10¹⁴ Hz strikes it. E = hf = (6.626×10⁻³⁴)(6×10¹⁴) ≈ 3.98×10⁻¹⁹ J. KEmax = hf − φ = 3.98×10⁻¹⁹ − 2.0×10⁻¹⁹ = 1.98×10⁻¹⁹ J.

1. In the photoelectric effect, increasing the intensity (brightness) of light below the threshold frequency:

Each photon interacts individually with one electron; below the threshold frequency, no single photon carries enough energy, regardless of how many photons (intensity) arrive.

2. Planck resolved the blackbody radiation problem by proposing that:

Quantized energy packets (E = hf) avoided the classical prediction of infinite radiated energy at short wavelengths.

3. A metal's work function is 3.0×10⁻¹⁹ J, and it's struck by light delivering 5.0×10⁻¹⁹ J per photon. What is the maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons?

KEmax = hf − φ = 5.0×10⁻¹⁹ − 3.0×10⁻¹⁹ = 2.0×10⁻¹⁹ J.

4. Fill in the blank: the minimum energy needed to free an electron from a particular metal's surface is called its ___.

The work function φ = hf0 sets the threshold frequency below which no photoelectrons are ejected.
