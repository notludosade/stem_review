1. A light ray strikes a flat mirror at an angle of 35° from the normal. At what angle from the normal does it reflect?

θᵢ = θᵣ — the law of reflection holds regardless of the specific incidence angle, so the reflected ray also makes 35° with the normal.

2. You stand 2.5 m in front of a plane mirror. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: your image appears ___ m behind the mirror.

(b) Compared to you, this image is:

(a) A plane mirror places the image as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, so 2.5 m. (b) Plane mirror images are always virtual (rays only appear to diverge from behind the mirror), upright, and equal in size to the object.

3. Fill in the blank: Light travels at 2.0×10⁸ m/s inside a certain material (c = 3.0×10⁸ m/s). This material's index of refraction is ___.

n = c/v = (3.0×10⁸)/(2.0×10⁸) = 1.5.

4. Light traveling through air (n = 1.00) strikes a slab of plastic (n = 1.4) at an incidence angle of 45°. Answer both parts:

(a) Which equation correctly sets up solving for the refraction angle?

(b) Fill in the blank: since the plastic is denser (has a higher index of refraction) than air, the refracted ray bends ___ the normal.

(a) n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ with n₁ = 1.00 (air, the incident medium) and n₂ = 1.4 (plastic, the medium of refraction). (b) Entering a higher-n (denser) medium always bends the ray toward the normal.

5. Light travels from a medium with n₁ = 1.6 toward one with n₂ = 1.2. What is the critical angle for total internal reflection?

θ_c = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁) = sin⁻¹(1.2/1.6) = sin⁻¹(0.75) ≈ 48.6°.

6. Light inside a fiber-optic core (n = 1.5) strikes the core-cladding boundary (cladding n = 1.3) at 70° from the normal. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the critical angle for this boundary, rounded to the nearest degree, is ___°.

(b) Since the light strikes the boundary at 70°, which is greater than the critical angle, what happens?

(a) θ_c = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁) = sin⁻¹(1.3/1.5) = sin⁻¹(0.867) ≈ 60°. (b) Since 70° > 60°, the incidence angle exceeds the critical angle, so total internal reflection occurs — all the light reflects, none refracts out.

7. Which type of mirror can never form a real image, regardless of where the object is placed?

A convex mirror's outward curvature spreads reflected rays apart no matter where the object sits, so it can only ever produce virtual images. A concave mirror, by contrast, can form real images when the object sits far enough from the focal point.

8. An object sits 24 cm from a concave mirror with a focal length of 8 cm. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the image distance?

(b) Fill in the blank: the magnification is ___ (include the sign).

(a) 1/d₀ + 1/dᵢ = 1/f → 1/24 + 1/dᵢ = 1/8 → 1/dᵢ = 1/8 − 1/24 = 2/24 = 1/12 → dᵢ = 12 cm. (b) m = −dᵢ/d₀ = −12/24 = −0.5 — a reduced, inverted image (the negative sign means inverted).

9. An object sits 50 cm from a converging lens with a focal length of 20 cm. What is the image distance?

1/d₀ + 1/dᵢ = 1/f → 1/dᵢ = 1/20 − 1/50 = 5/100 − 2/100 = 3/100 → dᵢ ≈ 33.3 cm.

10. An object sits 20 cm from a diverging lens with f = −12 cm. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the image distance is ___ cm.

(b) This image is best described as:

(a) 1/20 + 1/dᵢ = 1/(−12) → 1/dᵢ = −1/12 − 1/20 = −8/60 = −2/15 → dᵢ = −7.5 cm (negative → virtual, same side as the object). (b) Diverging lenses always produce virtual, upright, shrunken images, regardless of object distance.
