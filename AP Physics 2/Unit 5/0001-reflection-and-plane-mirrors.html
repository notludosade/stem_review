Reflection and Plane Mirrors

Unit 4 was about invisible fields bending charges and currents. Unit 5 turns to something you can see directly: light bouncing off surfaces in perfectly predictable ways.

← Unit 4, Lesson 6 Lesson 2 → Contents Glossary →

The Law of Reflection θi = θr — the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal (an imaginary line perpendicular to the surface at the point of contact), not from the surface itself.
Why angles are measured from the normal, not the surface Measuring from the surface would make the law depend on how you happen to define "along" a curved or irregular surface, but the normal is always well-defined at any single point, on any surface, flat or curved — this is exactly what lets the same law of reflection extend cleanly from flat mirrors to the curved mirrors of Lesson 4.
Plane Mirror Images A flat (plane) mirror produces an image that is virtual (light rays only appear to come from behind the mirror; they don't actually converge there), upright, and the same size as the object, located as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it.
Why the image is virtual, not real A real image forms where light rays actually cross and converge — it can be projected onto a screen. A plane mirror's reflected rays diverge outward from the mirror exactly as if they'd come from a point behind it, but no light actually passes through that point; your eye/brain traces the diverging rays backward in straight lines and perceives an image there, even though nothing physical is present. This is why you can't project a plane mirror's image onto a screen placed behind the mirror.
Example: locating a plane mirror image You stand 1.5 m in front of a plane mirror. Your image appears 1.5 m behind the mirror — a total of 3 m from you to your image, upright and the same height as you.

1. The law of reflection states that the angle of incidence and angle of reflection, both measured from the normal, are:

θi = θr is the law of reflection, holding for any reflecting surface, flat or curved.

2. Why is a plane mirror's image called "virtual" rather than "real"?

A real image requires actual convergence of light rays; a plane mirror's rays only seem to trace back to a point behind the mirror.

3. You stand 2 m in front of a plane mirror. How far is your image from you?

The image sits 2 m behind the mirror, so it's 2 m (to the mirror) + 2 m (behind it) = 4 m from you.

4. Fill in the blank: angles of incidence and reflection are measured from an imaginary line called the ___, perpendicular to the surface.

The normal, not the surface itself, is the reference line for both angles.
