Thin Lenses

Lenses use refraction instead of reflection to form images — but the same equation from Lesson 4 describes them almost unchanged. The eye, the camera, and the microscope all come down to this.

← Lesson 4 Unit 6, Lesson 1 → Contents Glossary →

Converging and Diverging Lenses A converging (convex) lens is thicker at the center and bends parallel rays inward to a real focus. A diverging (concave) lens is thinner at the center and spreads parallel rays outward, always producing virtual images.
The Thin Lens Equation 1/do + 1/di = 1/f — identical in form to the mirror equation (Lesson 4). m = −di/do, same magnification formula as well. Converging lenses have positive f; diverging lenses have negative f.
Why the same equation works for both mirrors and lenses Both devices produce an image by bending light rays from an object so they converge (or appear to diverge from) a single point — a mirror does this through reflection, a lens through refraction at two curved surfaces, but the underlying geometry of ray convergence relative to a focal point is identical either way. This is why physics courses can teach one equation, 1/d₀ + 1/dᵢ = 1/f, and apply it to both curved mirrors and thin lenses with only the sign conventions differing.
Example: image from a converging lens An object sits 40 cm from a converging lens with f = 20 cm. 1/40 + 1/dᵢ = 1/20 → 1/dᵢ = 1/20 − 1/40 = 1/40 → dᵢ = 40 cm. m = −40/40 = −1 — real, inverted, same size.
Example: image from a diverging lens An object sits 25 cm from a diverging lens with f = −15 cm. 1/25 + 1/dᵢ = 1/(−15) → 1/dᵢ = −1/15 − 1/25 = −5/75 − 3/75 = −8/75 → dᵢ ≈ −9.4 cm (negative → virtual). m = −(−9.4)/25 ≈ 0.375 — upright and shrunken, as diverging lenses always produce.
Unit 5 in review This unit built geometric optics from a single rule — the law of reflection (Lesson 1) — up through Snell's law for refraction (Lesson 2), the special case of total internal reflection (Lesson 3), and finally image formation with curved mirrors and lenses using one shared equation (Lessons 4–5). Unit 6 shifts from ray optics to wave behavior — treating light (and sound) as waves that interfere, diffract, and resonate, phenomena that ray tracing alone can't explain.

1. Why does the same equation, 1/d₀ + 1/dᵢ = 1/f, describe both curved mirrors and thin lenses?

Reflection (mirrors) and refraction (lenses) both produce images through ray convergence relative to a focal point, so the same geometric relationship applies to each.

2. A diverging lens will always produce an image that is:

A diverging lens spreads rays apart regardless of object position, always producing a virtual, upright, shrunken image — never a real one.

3. An object sits 30 cm from a converging lens with f = 10 cm. Find the image distance.

1/30 + 1/di = 1/10 → 1/di = 1/10 − 1/30 = 3/30 − 1/30 = 2/30 = 1/15 → di = 15 cm.

4. Fill in the blank: a thinner-at-the-center lens that always spreads rays outward and forms only virtual images is called a ___ lens.

A diverging lens has negative focal length and, like a convex mirror, can never form a real image.
