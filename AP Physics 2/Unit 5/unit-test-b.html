1. Fill in the blank: A light ray strikes a flat mirror at 52° from the normal. The reflected ray leaves at ___° from the normal.

θᵢ = θᵣ — the angle of incidence always equals the angle of reflection, both measured from the normal.

2. You stand 4 m from a plane mirror. Answer both parts:

(a) How far is your image from the mirror?

(b) Fill in the blank: the total distance from you to your image is ___ m.

(a) A plane mirror places the image as far behind the mirror as the object is in front — 4 m. (b) The total distance from you to the image is 4 m (to the mirror) + 4 m (behind it) = 8 m.

3. Light slows to 1.5×10⁸ m/s inside a certain material (c = 3.0×10⁸ m/s). What is this material's index of refraction?

n = c/v = (3.0×10⁸)/(1.5×10⁸) = 2.0.

4. Light in air (n = 1.00) strikes a pool of water (n = 1.33) at a 40° angle of incidence. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the refraction angle?

(b) Fill in the blank: since water is denser (higher index) than air, the refracted ray bends ___ the normal, compared to the incident ray.

(a) sin θ₂ = (1.00 sin 40°)/1.33 = 0.643/1.33 ≈ 0.483 → θ₂ ≈ 28.9°. (b) Entering the denser medium (water) bends the ray toward the normal, matching the smaller refraction angle found in part (a).

5. Light travels from a medium with n₁ = 1.4 toward one with n₂ = 1.0. What is the critical angle for total internal reflection?

θ_c = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁) = sin⁻¹(1.0/1.4) = sin⁻¹(0.714) ≈ 45.6°.

6. Light inside a fiber-optic core (n = 1.55) strikes the core-cladding boundary (cladding n = 1.45). Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the critical angle for this boundary, rounded to the nearest degree, is ___°.

(b) Light strikes this boundary at 55° from the normal, which is less than the critical angle. What happens?

(a) θ_c = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁) = sin⁻¹(1.45/1.55) = sin⁻¹(0.935) ≈ 69°. (b) Since 55° < 69°, the incidence angle is below the critical angle, so ordinary partial reflection and refraction occur — total internal reflection requires exceeding the critical angle.

7. A concave mirror forms a virtual (rather than real) image when the object is placed:

Inside the focal length, reflected rays diverge before reaching the eye, producing a virtual, upright, magnified image — the shaving-mirror case. Beyond the focal length, rays instead converge to form a real image.

8. An object sits 45 cm from a concave mirror with a focal length of 15 cm. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the image distance is ___ cm.

(b) The magnification of this image is:

(a) 1/d₀ + 1/dᵢ = 1/f → 1/45 + 1/dᵢ = 1/15 → 1/dᵢ = 1/15 − 1/45 = 2/45 → dᵢ = 22.5 cm. (b) m = −dᵢ/d₀ = −22.5/45 = −0.5 — the negative sign means inverted, and |m| < 1 means reduced.

9. An object sits 15 cm from a converging lens with a focal length of 10 cm. What is the image distance?

1/d₀ + 1/dᵢ = 1/f → 1/dᵢ = 1/10 − 1/15 = 3/30 − 2/30 = 1/30 → dᵢ = 30 cm.

10. An object sits 18 cm from a diverging lens with f = −9 cm. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the image distance?

(b) Fill in the blank: since diverging lenses always produce upright, reduced, ___ images, this image forms on the same side of the lens as the object.

(a) 1/18 + 1/dᵢ = 1/(−9) → 1/dᵢ = −1/9 − 1/18 = −3/18 = −1/6 → dᵢ = −6 cm. (b) A negative image distance indicates a virtual image — diverging lenses always produce virtual, upright, reduced images, regardless of object position.
