Total Internal Reflection and Fiber Optics

Push Snell's law far enough and refraction simply stops happening — light gets trapped inside a medium entirely, which is exactly how a fiber optic cable moves data at the speed of light.

← Lesson 2 Lesson 4 → Contents Glossary →

Critical Angle When light travels from a denser (higher-n) medium toward a less dense one, there exists a critical angle θc = sin⁻¹(n2/n1) beyond which no light refracts out at all — it's all reflected back into the original medium.
Why a critical angle exists only going from denser to less dense Going from higher n₁ to lower n₂, Snell's law gives sin θ₂ = (n₁/n₂) sin θ₁, and since n₁/n₂ > 1, sin θ₂ grows faster than sin θ₁ — at some incidence angle θc, sin θ₂ reaches exactly 1 (θ₂ = 90°, refracting parallel to the surface). Push θ₁ past θc and the equation would require sin θ₂ > 1, which is impossible — so refraction simply stops occurring, and all the light is reflected instead. Going the other direction (less dense to denser), sin θ₂ only ever shrinks, so this situation can never arise.
Total Internal Reflection Beyond θc, 100% of incident light reflects back into the denser medium — no energy is lost to refraction at all, unlike ordinary partial reflection at smaller angles. This is called total internal reflection (TIR).
Fiber Optics An optical fiber's core has a higher index of refraction than its outer cladding. Light entering at a shallow enough angle always strikes the core-cladding boundary beyond the critical angle, undergoing total internal reflection over and over — bouncing down the fiber's length with essentially no loss, even around gentle bends.
Example: finding a critical angle Light travels from glass (n₁ = 1.5) toward air (n₂ = 1.00). θc = sin⁻¹(n₂/n₁) = sin⁻¹(1.00/1.5) = sin⁻¹(0.667) ≈ 41.8°. Any incidence angle beyond 41.8° inside the glass results in total internal reflection.
Example: checking whether TIR occurs Light inside a fiber core (n = 1.6) hits the core-cladding boundary (cladding n = 1.4) at 65°. θc = sin⁻¹(1.4/1.6) = sin⁻¹(0.875) ≈ 61°. Since 65° > 61°, total internal reflection occurs — the light stays trapped in the core.

1. A critical angle for total internal reflection can only exist when light travels:

Only going from higher n to lower n can sin θ2 reach 1 at some finite incidence angle, defining a critical angle.

2. Beyond the critical angle, what fraction of incident light is reflected back into the denser medium?

Total internal reflection means exactly that — all the light reflects, with zero transmitted through refraction.

3. Light travels from a medium with n₁ = 2.0 toward one with n₂ = 1.0. What is the critical angle?

θc = sin⁻¹(n2/n1) = sin⁻¹(1.0/2.0) = sin⁻¹(0.5) = 30°.

4. Fill in the blank: when light beyond the critical angle is entirely reflected with none refracted out, this is called ___.

Total internal reflection is the principle that keeps light trapped inside a fiber optic core.
