Curved Mirrors

Curve the reflecting surface and the same law of reflection from Lesson 1 now produces real, magnified, or shrunken images — the basis of telescopes, headlights, and makeup mirrors alike.

← Lesson 3 Lesson 5 → Contents Glossary →

Concave and Convex Mirrors A concave mirror curves inward (like the inside of a bowl) and can converge reflected rays to a real focus. A convex mirror curves outward and always diverges reflected rays, producing only virtual images.
The Mirror Equation 1/do + 1/di = 1/f, where do is object distance, di is image distance, and f is the focal length (f = R/2, where R is the mirror's radius of curvature). Magnification: m = −di/do, where negative m means an inverted image and |m| > 1 means enlarged.
Why concave mirrors can form both real and virtual images, but convex mirrors cannot A concave mirror's curvature actively converges parallel rays toward a real focal point in front of the mirror — placing an object far from that focus produces rays that actually cross and converge again, forming a real, invertible image; but placing the object very close (inside the focal length) prevents convergence before the rays reach your eye, and the reflected rays only appear to diverge from a point behind the mirror, giving a virtual image (the shaving/makeup mirror case). A convex mirror's curvature only ever spreads rays apart no matter where the object sits, so it can never converge them into a real image — only ever virtual, upright, and shrunken, which is exactly why convex mirrors are used for wide-view surveillance and car side mirrors.
Example: real image from a concave mirror An object sits 20 cm from a concave mirror with f = 10 cm. 1/d₀ + 1/dᵢ = 1/f → 1/20 + 1/dᵢ = 1/10 → 1/dᵢ = 1/10 − 1/20 = 1/20 → dᵢ = 20 cm. m = −dᵢ/d₀ = −20/20 = −1 — same size, inverted, real image.
Example: virtual image from a convex mirror An object sits 15 cm from a convex mirror with f = −10 cm (convex mirrors have negative focal length by convention). 1/15 + 1/dᵢ = 1/(−10) → 1/dᵢ = −1/10 − 1/15 = −3/30 − 2/30 = −5/30 = −1/6 → dᵢ = −6 cm (negative means virtual, behind the mirror). m = −(−6)/15 = 0.4 — upright and shrunken, as expected for a convex mirror.

Check your recall

No peeking back up — answer from memory first, then check.

1. Why can a convex mirror never form a real image, regardless of object placement?

A convex mirror's outward curvature spreads reflected rays apart no matter where the object is, so only virtual images can form.

2. A concave mirror forms a virtual image instead of a real one when:

Inside the focal length, reflected rays don't converge before reaching the eye, producing a virtual (magnified, upright) image — the shaving-mirror case.

3. An object sits 30 cm from a concave mirror with f = 15 cm. Find the image distance.

1/30 + 1/di = 1/15 → 1/di = 1/15 − 1/30 = 2/30 − 1/30 = 1/30 → di = 30 cm.

4. Fill in the blank: a mirror that curves outward and always produces virtual, upright, shrunken images is called ___.

Convex mirrors always diverge reflected rays, so no real image is ever possible.
