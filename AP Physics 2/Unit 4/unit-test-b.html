1. Fill in the blank: A charge of +3×10⁻⁶ C moves at 4×10⁴ m/s perpendicular to a 0.5 T magnetic field. The magnitude of the magnetic force on it, in newtons, is ___.

F = qvB sin θ = (3×10⁻⁶)(4×10⁴)(0.5)(sin 90°) = 0.06 N.

2. A 0.6 m wire carries 5 A of current perpendicular to a 0.3 T magnetic field. What is the force on the wire?

F = BIL sin θ = (0.3)(5)(0.6)(sin 90°) = 0.9 N.

3. Two long, parallel wires carry current in opposite directions. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the wires will ___ each other.

(b) If instead one of the currents is reversed so both now flow in the same direction, the force between the wires becomes:

(a) Opposite-direction currents repel. (b) Reversing one current so both flow the same direction switches the interaction to attraction.

4. Find the magnetic field strength 0.08 m from a long, straight wire carrying 4 A of current.

B = μ₀I/(2πr) = (4π×10⁻⁷)(4)/(2π(0.08)) = (2×10⁻⁷)(4)/(0.08) = 1×10⁻⁵ T.

5. A solenoid has 2500 turns per meter and carries 1.5 A of current. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the magnetic field strength inside the solenoid?

(b) If the number of turns per meter were doubled while the current stayed the same, the field inside the solenoid would:

(a) B = μ₀nI = (4π×10⁻⁷)(2500)(1.5) ≈ 4.7×10⁻³ T. (b) Since B = μ₀nI is directly proportional to n, doubling n doubles B when I is unchanged.

6. A circular loop of area 0.06 m² is tilted so its normal makes a 60° angle with a 0.5 T magnetic field. What is the magnetic flux through the loop?

Φ_B = BA cos θ = (0.5)(0.06)(cos 60°) = (0.5)(0.06)(0.5) = 0.015 Wb.

7. A single-loop coil's magnetic flux changes from 0.02 Wb to 0.05 Wb over 0.15 s. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the magnitude of the induced EMF?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the number of loops were instead 2 (with everything else unchanged), the induced EMF would become ___ V.

(a) ε = N(ΔΦ_B/Δt) = (1)(0.05 − 0.02)/0.15 = 0.2 V. (b) Doubling N to 2 doubles the induced EMF to 0.4 V, since EMF is directly proportional to the number of loops.

8. A loop sits near a solenoid whose current is steadily increasing, increasing the flux through the loop out of the page. Answer both parts:

(a) By Lenz's law, the field created by the induced current inside the loop must point:

(b) If the solenoid's current instead steadily decreases (so the out-of-page flux shrinks), the induced current's direction will:

(a) Opposing an increasing outward flux requires an induced field pointing into the page. (b) Opposing a decrease means maintaining the original (out-of-page) direction, which reverses the current's direction relative to the increasing-flux case.

9. A generator's loop rotates steadily within a magnetic field. Answer both parts:

(a) As the loop rotates through one full turn, the induced EMF:

(b) Fill in the blank: household electricity is alternating current (AC) precisely because it is generated by loops that continuously ___ within a magnetic field at power plants.

(a) Continuous rotation cycles the flux through the loop, producing a sinusoidal (alternating) EMF via Faraday's law. (b) Power plant generators work by continuously rotating loops within a magnetic field, which is exactly why the electricity they produce alternates.

10. A transformer's primary coil has 800 turns and is connected to 240 V. Its secondary coil has 100 turns. What is the secondary voltage, and is this a step-up or step-down transformer?

V_s = V_p(N_s/N_p) = 240(100/800) = 30 V. Since the secondary has fewer turns and a lower voltage than the primary, this is a step-down transformer.
