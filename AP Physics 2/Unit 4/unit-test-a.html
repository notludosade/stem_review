1. A charge of +4×10⁻⁶ C moves at 5×10⁴ m/s perpendicular to a 0.6 T magnetic field. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force on it?

F = qvB sin θ = (4×10⁻⁶)(5×10⁴)(0.6)(sin 90°) = 0.12 N.

2. A 0.8 m wire carries 3 A of current perpendicular to a 0.4 T magnetic field. What is the force on the wire?

F = BIL sin θ = (0.4)(3)(0.8)(sin 90°) = 0.96 N.

3. Two long, parallel wires each carry current. Answer both parts:

(a) If the currents in the two wires flow in the same direction, the wires will:

(b) Fill in the blank: if the currents instead flow in opposite directions, the wires will ___ each other.

(a) Parallel currents flowing in the same direction attract. (b) Parallel currents flowing in opposite directions repel — the reverse of the same-direction case.

4. Find the magnetic field strength 0.05 m from a long, straight wire carrying 6 A of current.

B = μ₀I/(2πr) = (4π×10⁻⁷)(6)/(2π(0.05)) = (2×10⁻⁷)(6)/(0.05) = 2.4×10⁻⁵ T.

5. A solenoid has 1000 turns per meter and carries 4 A of current. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the magnetic field strength inside the solenoid?

(b) Compared to a single long, straight wire carrying the same current, measured at a similar distance, the solenoid's field is much stronger because:

(a) B = μ₀nI = (4π×10⁻⁷)(1000)(4) ≈ 5.0×10⁻³ T. (b) Each loop's field reinforces the others inside a solenoid, unlike a single wire whose field spreads out along one lone circle at a given distance.

6. Fill in the blank: A circular loop of area 0.08 m² sits with its normal exactly along a 0.25 T magnetic field (θ = 0°). The magnetic flux through the loop, in webers, is ___.

Φ_B = BA cos θ = (0.25)(0.08)(1) = 0.02 Wb.

7. A single-loop coil's magnetic flux changes from 0.03 Wb to 0.09 Wb over 0.2 s. Answer both parts:

(a) What is the magnitude of the induced EMF?

(b) Fill in the blank: this EMF exists only because the flux is ___ over time — a perfectly steady flux, no matter how large, induces no EMF at all.

(a) ε = N(ΔΦ_B/Δt) = (1)(0.09 − 0.03)/0.2 = 0.3 V. (b) Faraday's law depends on the rate of change of flux, not flux itself — a constant flux induces zero EMF.

8. A bar magnet's north pole is pushed toward a loop of wire, increasing the flux through the loop into the page. Answer both parts:

(a) By Lenz's law, the field created by the induced current must point:

(b) If the magnet is instead pulled away from the loop (decreasing the into-the-page flux), the induced current's direction will:

(a) Lenz's law requires the induced field to oppose the increasing flux, so it must point opposite the original field — out of the page. (b) Opposing a decrease instead requires maintaining the original into-the-page direction, which reverses the induced current's direction compared to the approaching-magnet case.

9. A generator's rotating loop induces an alternating (rather than constant) EMF because:

Continuous rotation cycles the angle θ in Φ_B = BA cos θ, so flux rises and falls repeatedly, and Faraday's law then produces a sinusoidally oscillating EMF.

10. A transformer's primary coil has 250 turns and is connected to 60 V. Its secondary coil has 1000 turns. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the secondary coil's voltage, in volts, is ___.

(b) This transformer is best described as a:

(a) V_s = V_p(N_s/N_p) = 60(1000/250) = 240 V. (b) More turns in the secondary than the primary steps voltage up, matching the higher computed secondary voltage.
