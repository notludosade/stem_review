Force on Current-Carrying Wires

A wire carrying current is just a coordinated stream of moving charges — so it feels a magnetic force too, scaled up to a whole conductor.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

Force on a Current-Carrying Wire F = BIL sin θ, where B is the magnetic field, I is current, L is the length of wire in the field, and θ is the angle between the wire (current direction) and the field. Direction again follows the right-hand rule, using the current direction in place of individual velocity.
Why F = BIL sin θ is just F = qvB sin θ scaled up A current is literally a large number of moving charges (Lesson 1, Unit 3), each individually feeling F = qvB sin θ. Summing this force over every charge carrier in a wire segment, using the definition of current as charge per unit time, reorganizes cleanly into F = BIL sin θ — this is not a new law of physics, just the single-moving-charge force applied collectively to the coordinated flow of charges that makes up a current.
Application: Force Between Parallel Current-Carrying Wires Two current-carrying wires exert magnetic forces on each other (each wire creates a field the other wire's current then feels, Lesson 3). Parallel currents in the same direction attract; parallel currents in opposite directions repel.
Example: force on a wire segment A 0.5 m wire carries 4 A of current perpendicular to a 0.2 T magnetic field. F = BIL sin θ = (0.2)(4)(0.5)(sin 90°) = 0.4 N.
Example: a wire at an angle to the field A 1 m wire carries 6 A of current at a 30° angle to a 0.3 T field. F = BIL sin θ = (0.3)(6)(1)(sin 30°) = (1.8)(0.5) = 0.9 N — smaller than if the wire were perpendicular to the field, since sin 30° < sin 90°.

1. The formula for force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field is derived from:

F = BIL sin θ comes directly from scaling up F = qvB sin θ over all the charge carriers making up the current.

2. Two long, parallel wires carry current in the same direction. What happens between them?

Parallel currents flowing the same direction attract; opposite directions repel.

3. A 0.4 m wire carries 5 A of current perpendicular to a 0.25 T field. What is the force on the wire?

F = BIL sin θ = (0.25)(5)(0.4)(1) = 0.5 N.

4. Fill in the blank: two parallel wires carrying current in the same direction will ___ each other.

Same-direction parallel currents attract; opposite-direction parallel currents repel.
