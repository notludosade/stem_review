Magnetic Fields and Force on Moving Charges

A stationary charge never feels a magnetic force — only a moving one does, and the force always points sideways to the motion.

← Unit 3, Lesson 6 Lesson 2 → Contents Glossary →

Magnetic Force on a Moving Charge F = qvB sin θ, where q is the charge, v is its speed, B is the magnetic field strength (unit: tesla, T), and θ is the angle between the velocity and field vectors. The direction of F is given by the right-hand rule: point fingers along v, curl toward B, thumb points along F (for a positive charge; reverse for negative).
Why magnetic force is always perpendicular to velocity The formula's structure (a cross-product relationship, captured here by the right-hand rule) guarantees F is always perpendicular to both v and B — this means magnetic force can never speed up or slow down a charge, only redirect it, since a force perpendicular to velocity does zero work (recall from AP Physics 1 that work requires a force component along the direction of motion). This is exactly why magnetic fields are used to steer charged particles in circular paths (as in mass spectrometers or particle accelerators) without ever changing their speed.
Why a stationary charge feels no magnetic force at all F = qvB sin θ contains v explicitly — with v = 0, the force is exactly zero no matter how strong the field B is. This is a fundamental difference from both gravity and the electric force, which act on masses and charges regardless of whether they're moving.
Example: force on a moving charge A charge of +2×10⁻⁶ C moves at 3×10⁴ m/s perpendicular to a 0.5 T magnetic field. F = qvB sin θ = (2×10⁻⁶)(3×10⁴)(0.5)(sin 90°) = (2×10⁻⁶)(3×10⁴)(0.5)(1) = 0.03 N.
Example: charge moving parallel to the field A charge moves exactly parallel to a magnetic field line (θ = 0°). F = qvB sin(0°) = qvB(0) = 0 N — no magnetic force at all, regardless of speed or field strength, since motion parallel to the field produces no sideways deflection.

1. A charged particle moving through a magnetic field experiences a force that:

Since the magnetic force is always perpendicular to velocity, it does zero work and can only change direction, never speed.

2. A charge sits at rest in a very strong magnetic field. What magnetic force does it experience?

With v = 0, the magnetic force formula gives exactly zero regardless of field strength, unlike gravity or electric force.

3. A charge of +5×10⁻⁶ C moves at 2×10⁴ m/s perpendicular to a 0.3 T field. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force?

F = qvB sin θ = (5×10⁻⁶)(2×10⁴)(0.3)(1) = 0.03 N.

4. Fill in the blank: the mnemonic used to determine the direction of magnetic force on a moving positive charge is the ___.

Point fingers along velocity, curl toward the field, and the thumb gives the force direction for a positive charge; reverse for negative charges.
