1. A wave has a period of 0.02 s and a wavelength of 6 m. What is its speed?

f = 1/T = 1/0.02 = 50 Hz. v = fλ = (50)(6) = 300 m/s. Dividing λ by f instead of multiplying gives 0.12; adding f and λ gives 56; dividing f by λ gives 8.33 — none of those is the wave speed relationship.

2. In a longitudinal wave, the particles of the medium oscillate:

Longitudinal waves (like sound) oscillate along their direction of travel, as alternating compressions and rarefactions; transverse waves (like light or a wave on a string) oscillate perpendicular to it.

3. A string of length 0.6 m has wave speed 180 m/s. Answer both parts:

(a) What is its third harmonic frequency?

(b) Fill in the blank: a standing wave's fixed points of zero displacement are called ___.

(a) f₁ = v/(2L) = 180/(2×0.6) = 150 Hz. The third harmonic is 3f₁ = 3(150) = 450 Hz. (b) Nodes are the fixed zero-displacement points; antinodes are the points of maximum displacement.

4. Which statement correctly contrasts sound and light waves?

Sound is particles of a medium bumping into their neighbors — with no medium (vacuum), there's nothing to bump. Light is an electromagnetic wave needing no material substance at all, which is why sunlight reaches Earth through space but sound cannot.

5. Answer both parts about the Doppler effect:

(a) A listener driving toward a stationary factory whistle hears a pitch that is:

(b) A source emits 450 Hz while receding from a stationary listener at 17 m/s (speed of sound v = 340 m/s). What frequency does the listener hear?

(a) Motion that decreases the distance between source and listener always raises the observed frequency, regardless of whether it's the source or the listener that's moving. (b) f_obs = f_source · v/(v + v_source) = 450 · 340/(340+17) = 450 · 340/357 ≈ 429 Hz — lower than the emitted 450 Hz, as expected for a receding source.

6. Fill in the blank: you can typically hear around a corner but not see around one, because sound's wavelength is comparable to everyday opening sizes, while light's wavelength is far ___ than those openings.

Diffraction is most pronounced when opening size is comparable to wavelength. Visible light's wavelength (~500 nm) is far smaller than a doorway, so it barely bends; sound's wavelength (cm to m) is comparable, so it diffracts noticeably.

7. Answer both parts about interference:

(a) Two identical in-phase waves meet crest-to-crest at a point. By the principle of superposition, the resulting displacement there is:

(b) Fill in the blank: two speakers emit identical in-phase waves of wavelength 2.4 m. At a point where the path difference is exactly 2.4 m, this produces ___ interference (constructive or destructive).

(a) In-phase superposition adds displacements directly, doubling the amplitude — constructive interference. (b) 2.4 m = 1 × 2.4 m, a whole number of wavelengths, so the waves arrive in phase — constructive interference (a bright/loud spot).

8. Answer both parts about the double-slit experiment:

(a) Bright fringes (constructive interference) occur where the path difference between the two slits equals:

(b) Fill in the blank: slits separated by d = 0.03 mm produce a second-order (m = 2) bright fringe at θ = 2.5°. The wavelength is λ ≈ ___ nm (nearest 10).

(a) d sin θ = mλ (whole-number path difference) gives bright fringes; d sin θ = (m + 1/2)λ gives dark fringes. (b) λ = d sin θ/m = (3×10⁻⁵)(sin 2.5°)/2 ≈ (3×10⁻⁵)(0.0436)/2 ≈ 6.54×10⁻⁷ m ≈ 650 nm.

9. An oil slick on a wet road shows swirling rainbow bands, but a thick block of the same oil would not. What's the best explanation?

The reflected-wave path difference is 2t. In a thin film this changes gently enough with thickness that distinct colors survive; in a thick sample it changes so rapidly across visible wavelengths that the colors average out to gray.

(a) Real spectrometers use diffraction gratings rather than simple double slits mainly because gratings:

(b) A diffraction grating has 400 lines per millimeter. What is its slit spacing d?

(a) With thousands of lines, only very precise angles keep light from every single line in phase; slightly off-angle light cancels almost completely, producing much sharper, brighter fringes for precise wavelength measurement. (b) d = 1 mm/400 = 0.0025 mm = 2.5×10⁻⁶ m.
