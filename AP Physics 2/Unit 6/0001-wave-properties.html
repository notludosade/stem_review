Wave Properties

Unit 5 treated light as rays traveling in straight lines. That model breaks down at small scales — to explain interference and diffraction, light needs to be understood as a wave.

Wave Vocabulary Amplitude (A) — maximum displacement from equilibrium. Wavelength (λ) — distance between two identical points on consecutive cycles. Period (T) — time for one full cycle. Frequency (f = 1/T) — cycles per second. Wave speed: v = fλ.
Transverse vs. Longitudinal Waves In a transverse wave (like light, or a wave on a string), the medium oscillates perpendicular to the direction of travel. In a longitudinal wave (like sound), the medium oscillates parallel to (along) the direction of travel, as compressions and rarefactions.
Why v = fλ holds for any wave In one period T, a wave advances exactly one wavelength λ (by definition of both quantities). Speed is distance over time, so v = λ/T — and since f = 1/T, this is identical to v = fλ. This relationship isn't a separate law; it falls directly out of what "wavelength" and "period" already mean, which is why it applies equally to sound, light, and water waves alike.
Example: finding wave speed A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 2 m. v = fλ = (5)(2) = 10 m/s.
Example: finding wavelength from speed and period Sound travels at 340 m/s with a period of 0.002 s. f = 1/T = 1/0.002 = 500 Hz. v = fλ → λ = v/f = 340/500 = 0.68 m.

1. In a transverse wave, the medium oscillates:

Transverse waves (like light or a wave on a string) oscillate perpendicular to their direction of travel; longitudinal waves (like sound) oscillate parallel to it.

2. Why does v = fλ hold true for essentially any wave, without needing separate proof for each type?

v = fλ is a direct consequence of the definitions of wavelength, period, and frequency — not an independent physical law.

3. A wave has a period of 0.5 s and a wavelength of 4 m. What is its speed?

f = 1/T = 1/0.5 = 2 Hz. v = fλ = (2)(4) = 8 m/s.

4. Fill in the blank: a wave (like sound) in which the medium oscillates parallel to the direction of travel is called ___.

Longitudinal waves oscillate along their direction of travel, as alternating compressions and rarefactions.
