1. Two point charges, q₁ = +5×10⁻⁶ C and q₂ = +2×10⁻⁶ C, are separated by 0.4 m. What is the magnitude of the force between them, and is it attractive or repulsive?

F = k|q₁q₂|/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(5×10⁻⁶)(2×10⁻⁶)/(0.4)² = 0.0899/0.16 ≈ 0.56 N. Since both charges are positive, the force is repulsive.

2. Two fixed point charges are 3 m apart, producing a force F between them. Answer both parts:

(a) If the charges are moved to 1 m apart, the new force is:

(b) Fill in the blank: if instead the distance is tripled (to 9 m), the force becomes ___ of its original value.

(a) The distance shrinks by a factor of 3, and force scales as 1/r², so the force increases by 3² = 9. (b) Tripling r scales the force by (1/3)² = 1/9 of its original value.

3. What is the electric field at a distance of 0.2 m from a point charge Q = +6×10⁻⁶ C?

E = kQ/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(6×10⁻⁶)/(0.2)² = 53,940/0.04 = 1,348,500 N/C.

4. A uniform electric field of 2.5×10⁵ N/C points to the left. A charge of +8×10⁻⁶ C sits in this field. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the magnitude of the force on the charge is ___ N.

(b) In which direction does the force point?

(a) F = qE = (8×10⁻⁶)(2.5×10⁵) = 2.0 N. (b) Since the charge is positive, the force on it points in the same direction as the field — to the left.

5. Two charges, +4×10⁻⁶ C and −4×10⁻⁶ C, sit 0.8 m apart, forming a dipole. At the point exactly midway between them, the two individual fields:

Both fields point away from the positive charge and toward the negative charge, so at the midpoint they point the same direction and add — the opposite of what happens between two equal like charges, where the fields would cancel instead.

6. In a field-line diagram, several lines are drawn very close together in one region, and widely spaced in another region of the same diagram. Answer both parts:

(a) What does this spacing indicate?

(b) Fill in the blank: field lines point in the direction a small positive ___ would be pushed if placed at that location.

(a) Denser field lines represent a stronger field at that location; sparser lines indicate a weaker field. (b) Field-line direction is defined by the force a small positive test charge would feel, radiating outward from positive sources and inward toward negative ones.

7. Two charges, +3×10⁻⁶ C and +7×10⁻⁶ C, are separated by 0.35 m. What is their electric potential energy, and what does its sign indicate?

U = kq₁q₂/r = (8.99×10⁹)(3×10⁻⁶)(7×10⁻⁶)/0.35 = 0.1888/0.35 ≈ +0.54 J. The positive sign reflects the repulsive (like-charge) configuration — energy would be released if the charges were let go.

8. A point charge Q = +1.2×10⁻⁵ C creates an electric potential in the surrounding space. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the electric potential at a distance of 0.6 m from Q is ___ V.

(b) How much work is required to move a +3×10⁻⁶ C charge from this point (0.6 m away, at 179,800 V) to a closer point at 400,000 V?

(a) V = kQ/r = (8.99×10⁹)(1.2×10⁻⁵)/0.6 = 107,880/0.6 = 179,800 V. (b) W = qΔV = (3×10⁻⁶)(400,000 − 179,800) = (3×10⁻⁶)(220,200) ≈ +0.66 J — positive, since moving a positive charge toward higher potential requires external work against the field.

9. A charged conducting shell sits in electrostatic equilibrium with −6×10⁻⁶ C of excess charge. Answer both parts:

(a) Where does this excess charge reside, and what is the electric field inside the shell?

(b) Fill in the blank: two points on this same conducting surface differ in potential by ___ V, since the entire surface sits at one constant potential.

(a) In electrostatic equilibrium, all excess charge (positive or negative) resides on a conductor's outer surface, and the interior field is exactly zero. (b) Since the whole surface is one equipotential, any two points on it differ by ΔV = 0 V.

10. Fill in the blank: the property of a capacitor that fixes the ratio of stored charge to voltage, determined solely by its physical geometry, is called its ___.

Capacitance C = Q/V is a fixed property of a capacitor's construction (such as plate area and separation) — charge and voltage always scale together, keeping the ratio constant.
