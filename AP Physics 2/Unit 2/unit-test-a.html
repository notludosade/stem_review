1. Two point charges, q₁ = +3×10⁻⁶ C and q₂ = −4×10⁻⁶ C, are separated by 0.6 m. What is the magnitude of the force between them, and is it attractive or repulsive?

F = k|q₁q₂|/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(3×10⁻⁶)(4×10⁻⁶)/(0.6)² = 0.10788/0.36 ≈ 0.30 N. Since the charges have opposite signs, the force is attractive.

2. Two fixed point charges are 2 m apart, producing a force F between them. Answer both parts:

(a) If the distance is reduced to 0.5 m, the new force is:

(b) Fill in the blank: if the distance between the two charges is doubled instead, the force becomes ___ of its original value.

(a) The distance shrinks by a factor of 2/0.5 = 4, and force scales as 1/r², so the force increases by 4² = 16. (b) Doubling r scales the force by (1/2)² = 1/4 of its original value.

3. What is the electric field at a distance of 0.5 m from a point charge Q = +8×10⁻⁶ C?

E = kQ/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(8×10⁻⁶)/(0.5)² = 71,920/0.25 = 287,680 N/C.

4. A uniform electric field of 4×10⁵ N/C points to the right. A charge of −5×10⁻⁶ C is placed in this field. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the magnitude of the force on the charge is ___ N.

(b) In which direction does the force point?

(a) F = |q|E = (5×10⁻⁶)(4×10⁵) = 2.0 N. (b) Since the charge is negative, the force on it points opposite to the field direction — to the left.

5. Two charges, +6×10⁻⁶ C and +6×10⁻⁶ C, sit 0.6 m apart. What is the net electric field at the point exactly midway between them?

Each positive charge's field points away from itself, so at the midpoint the two contributions point in opposite directions and cancel exactly, giving a net field of 0 N/C. (Electric field depends on charge, not mass, so mass is irrelevant here.)

6. Consider two point charges, one +Q and one −Q, placed near each other, with the resulting field-line pattern drawn around them. Answer both parts:

(a) In this diagram, the field lines:

(b) Fill in the blank: field lines can never cross, because the net field at any point must be a single, well-defined ___ — having two directions at one point would be a contradiction.

(a) Field lines run from positive charges to negative charges and curve smoothly without intersecting. (b) A vector has one specific direction at each point in space; two crossing lines would require two different directions at the same location, which is impossible for a single well-defined field.

7. Two charges, −2×10⁻⁶ C and +5×10⁻⁶ C, are separated by 0.25 m. What is their electric potential energy, and what does its sign indicate?

U = kq₁q₂/r = (8.99×10⁹)(−2×10⁻⁶)(5×10⁻⁶)/0.25 = −0.0899/0.25 ≈ −0.36 J. The negative sign reflects the attractive (unlike-charge) configuration, meaning external energy would be required to separate the charges.

8. A point charge Q = +9×10⁻⁶ C creates an electric potential in the surrounding space. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the electric potential at a distance of 0.3 m from Q is ___ V.

(b) How much work is required to move a +4×10⁻⁶ C charge from this point (0.3 m away, at 269,700 V) to a farther point at 100,000 V?

(a) V = kQ/r = (8.99×10⁹)(9×10⁻⁶)/0.3 = 80,910/0.3 = 269,700 V. (b) W = qΔV = (4×10⁻⁶)(100,000 − 269,700) = (4×10⁻⁶)(−169,700) ≈ −0.679 J — negative, meaning the field does positive work moving the positive charge toward lower potential, so no external work input is needed.

9. A charged metal sphere sits in electrostatic equilibrium with 8×10⁻⁶ C of excess charge. Answer both parts:

(a) Where does this excess charge reside, and what is the electric field inside the sphere?

(b) Fill in the blank: moving a charge along the sphere's surface (an equipotential) requires ___ J of work, regardless of the path or distance traveled.

(a) In electrostatic equilibrium, all excess charge resides on a conductor's outer surface, and the interior field is exactly zero — otherwise free charges would still be accelerating, contradicting equilibrium. (b) The entire surface sits at one constant potential, so ΔV = 0 along it, meaning W = qΔV = 0 regardless of path.

10. Fill in the blank: the fixed ratio of stored charge to voltage across a capacitor, determined only by its geometry (such as plate area and separation), is called its ___.

Capacitance C = Q/V stays constant because charge and voltage scale together proportionally for a given capacitor's construction, exactly like a spring's stiffness constant k = F/x stays fixed regardless of how far it's stretched.
