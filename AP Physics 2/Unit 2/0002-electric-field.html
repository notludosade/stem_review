The Electric Field

Instead of tracking force between two specific charges, define a field that fills space — then any charge dropped in feels a force immediately.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

Electric Field E = F/q, where E is the electric field (N/C) at a point, F is the force a small positive "test charge" q would feel if placed there. Rearranged, F = qE gives the force on any charge placed in a known field. For a point charge Q, the field it creates is E = kQ/r², pointing away from Q if positive, toward Q if negative.
Why define a field instead of just using Coulomb's law directly Coulomb's law needs two charges to give a force. The field concept splits this into two steps: one charge creates a field that exists at every point in space regardless of whether anything else is there, and any second charge placed at a point simply reads off the force from the field already present. This mirrors AP Physics 1's gravitational field idea (g = 9.8 N/kg near Earth, independent of what object experiences it) and becomes essential once field patterns from multiple sources need to be combined (Lesson 3).
Electric Field Lines Field lines point in the direction a positive test charge would be pushed: they radiate outward from positive charges and inward toward negative charges. Density of field lines represents field strength — lines drawn closer together indicate a stronger field.
Example: field from a point charge Find the electric field 0.3 m from a point charge Q = +5×10⁻⁶ C. E = kQ/r² = (8.99×10⁹)(5×10⁻⁶)/(0.3)² = 44,950/0.09 ≈ 499,444 N/C, pointing away from the charge.
Example: force on a charge in a known field A charge of −2×10⁻⁶ C sits in a uniform field of 3×10⁵ N/C pointing right. F = qE = (2×10⁻⁶)(3×10⁵) = 0.6 N — but since the charge is negative, the force points opposite the field, i.e., to the left.

1. The electric field at a point is defined as:

E = F/q defines field strength independent of any specific test charge's magnitude.

2. Why is it useful to define an electric field rather than always applying Coulomb's law directly between two charges?

The field concept separates "what a source charge creates" from "what force a second charge feels," which becomes essential when combining multiple sources.

3. A charge of +3×10⁻⁶ C is placed in a field of 2×10⁵ N/C. What force does it feel?

F = qE = (3×10⁻⁶)(2×10⁵) = 0.6 N.

4. Fill in the blank: the visual representation showing field direction and relative strength via line direction and spacing is called ___.

Field lines point in the direction a positive test charge would be pushed, with closer spacing indicating stronger field regions.
