Electric Potential Energy

Pushing two like charges together stores energy in their configuration, exactly the way lifting a mass stores gravitational potential energy.

← Lesson 3 Lesson 5 → Contents Glossary →

Electric Potential Energy U = kq₁q₂/r, the potential energy stored in the configuration of two point charges separated by distance r. Unlike Coulomb's law's force (which uses |q₁q₂|), potential energy keeps the sign: U is positive for like charges (repulsive configuration, energy released if let go) and negative for unlike charges (attractive configuration, energy required to pull apart).
Why this exactly parallels gravitational potential energy In AP Physics 1, lifting a mass against gravity stores energy that converts back to kinetic energy when released (U = mgh, or more generally U = −GMm/r). Electric potential energy works the same way: work done by an external agent to move charges against the electric force is stored as potential energy, recoverable as kinetic energy if the charges are released and allowed to move under their mutual force. Pushing two positive charges together stores energy exactly like compressing a spring or lifting a mass — release either charge and it accelerates away, converting stored U back into kinetic energy.
Conservation of Energy for Charge Systems As with all closed systems, total mechanical energy (KE + U) is conserved as charges move under only their mutual electric force: KE_i + U_i = KE_f + U_f.
Example: potential energy between two charges Two charges, +3×10⁻⁶ C and +5×10⁻⁶ C, are separated by 0.2 m. U = kq₁q₂/r = (8.99×10⁹)(3×10⁻⁶)(5×10⁻⁶)/0.2 = 0.13485/0.2 ≈ 0.674 J — positive, since both charges are positive (a repulsive, energy-storing configuration).
Example: using energy conservation to find speed A charge of +2×10⁻⁶ C (mass 1×10⁻³ kg) is released from rest at a point where U = 0.5 J relative to a fixed charge, and moves to a point where U = 0.1 J. Energy conservation: KE_i + U_i = KE_f + U_f ⟹ 0 + 0.5 = KE_f + 0.1 ⟹ KE_f = 0.4 J. Then (1/2)mv² = 0.4 ⟹ v² = 0.8/0.001 = 800 ⟹ v ≈ 28.3 m/s.

1. Electric potential energy between two charges is positive when:

Unlike Coulomb's law for force, U = kq₁q₂/r keeps the sign of the product q₁q₂ — positive for like charges, negative for unlike charges.

2. Releasing two like charges that were held close together and letting them fly apart is most directly analogous to which AP Physics 1 scenario?

Both scenarios store potential energy in a configuration that converts to kinetic energy once released, following the same energy-conservation logic.

3. Two charges, +2×10⁻⁶ C and +6×10⁻⁶ C, are separated by 0.3 m. What is their electric potential energy?

U = kq₁q₂/r = (8.99×10⁹)(2×10⁻⁶)(6×10⁻⁶)/0.3 = 0.10788/0.3 ≈ 0.360 J.

4. Fill in the blank: the energy stored in the configuration of two charges due to their mutual electric interaction is called ___.

Electric potential energy follows U = kq₁q₂/r and converts to kinetic energy as charges move under their mutual force.
