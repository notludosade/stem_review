EMF and Simple Circuits

A battery doesn't supply current directly — it supplies the "push" that drives current around a closed loop, and it always has some cost of doing business.

← Lesson 1 Lesson 3 → Contents Glossary →

Electromotive Force (EMF) EMF (ε) is the potential difference a source (like a battery) provides to drive current around a circuit, measured in volts despite not literally being a force. A real battery has some internal resistance r, so the actual terminal voltage is V = ε − Ir, slightly less than the ideal EMF once current is flowing.
Why terminal voltage drops below EMF once current flows Internal resistance behaves exactly like an extra resistor built into the battery itself — some of the EMF is "used up" driving current through this internal resistance before the rest ever reaches the external circuit (V = ε − Ir mirrors Ohm's law's V = IR applied to the internal resistance's own voltage drop). This is why a battery's voltage sags under heavy load (large I) but reads closer to its rated EMF when barely any current is drawn.
A Simple Circuit A basic complete circuit needs: a source of EMF (battery), a closed conducting path, and a resistance (the "load," such as a light bulb) to convert electric energy to another form. Current only flows when the loop is fully closed.
Example: current in a simple circuit A battery with EMF 12 V (negligible internal resistance) is connected to a single 4 Ω resistor. Using Ohm's law across the whole loop: I = ε/R = 12/4 = 3 A.
Example: accounting for internal resistance A battery with ε = 9 V and internal resistance r = 0.5 Ω drives 2 A through an external resistor. What is the terminal voltage? V = ε − Ir = 9 − (2)(0.5) = 9 − 1 = 8 V — the external circuit only "sees" 8 V, even though the battery's rated EMF is 9 V.

1. A battery's terminal voltage is generally less than its EMF once current is flowing because:

Internal resistance acts like a built-in resistor, so V = ε − Ir is always less than ε whenever current I flows.

2. What three things does the simplest complete circuit require?

A complete loop needs an EMF source, a closed path for current, and a load resistance that converts electrical energy to another form.

3. A battery with EMF 6 V and internal resistance 0.2 Ω drives 3 A of current. What is its terminal voltage?

V = ε − Ir = 6 − (3)(0.2) = 6 − 0.6 = 5.4 V.

4. Fill in the blank: the potential difference a source provides to drive current around a circuit, before any internal resistance losses, is called ___.

EMF is measured in volts despite the name, and terminal voltage is always EMF minus the internal-resistance voltage drop.
