Series Circuits

One path, one current — but voltage splits up as it drops across each resistor in turn.

← Lesson 2 Lesson 4 → Contents Glossary →

Series Circuits In a series circuit, components are connected end-to-end along a single path, so the same current flows through every component. Equivalent resistance: Req = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ... — resistances simply add.
Why current is identical everywhere in a series circuit Charge conservation (Lesson 1) requires that whatever charge flows into a section of wire per second must flow back out per second — there's nowhere else for it to go with only one path available. Since there's only a single loop with no branching, the current must be exactly the same at every single point along that loop, whether measured before, between, or after any resistor.
Why series resistances simply add Since the same current I flows through every resistor, each one drops its own voltage V₁ = IR₁, V₂ = IR₂, etc., and these drops must sum to the total EMF (energy conservation around the loop). Factoring out the common I: V_total = I(R₁ + R₂ + ...), meaning the single equivalent resistance that would produce the same total voltage drop for the same current is just the sum of the individual resistances.
Example: equivalent resistance in series Three resistors, 5 Ω, 10 Ω, and 15 Ω, are connected in series. Req = 5 + 10 + 15 = 30 Ω.
Example: finding current and individual voltage drops A 12 V battery drives the series combination above (Req = 30 Ω). I = V/Req = 12/30 = 0.4 A, the same current through all three resistors. Voltage drops: V₁ = (0.4)(5) = 2 V, V₂ = (0.4)(10) = 4 V, V₃ = (0.4)(15) = 6 V. Check: 2 + 4 + 6 = 12 V, matching the battery's EMF exactly.

1. In a series circuit, the current through each resistor is:

Charge conservation with only one available path forces the same current everywhere in a series circuit.

2. Why do resistances in series simply add to give the equivalent resistance?

With current I common to all series resistors, factoring I out of the summed voltage drops gives R_eq = R1 + R2 + ... directly.

3. Two resistors, 8 Ω and 12 Ω, are in series with a 20 V battery. What is the current in the circuit?

R_eq = 8 + 12 = 20 Ω, so I = V/R_eq = 20/20 = 1 A.

4. Fill in the blank: a circuit arrangement where components share a single path, so the same current flows through each, is called a ___ circuit.

Series resistances add directly, while individual voltage drops split proportionally to each resistance.
