1. Beakers of liquid X and Y are each placed in contact with the same metal block Z, at different times, until each reaches thermal equilibrium with Z. What does the zeroth law of thermodynamics guarantee about X and Y?

The zeroth law states that two objects each in thermal equilibrium with a third object are in thermal equilibrium with each other — X and Y never need to touch for this conclusion to hold, which is exactly what justifies comparing two objects with a single thermometer.

2. A rigid cylinder holds 2.5 mol of an ideal gas at 400 K in a volume of 0.10 m³. What is the gas's pressure?

P = nRT/V = (2.5)(8.31)(400)/0.10 = 8,310/0.10 = 83,100 Pa.

3. How much heat must be removed to cool 3 kg of water from 90°C to 60°C?

Q = mcΔT = (3)(4186)(30) = 376,740 J — the same formula applies whether the water is heating up or, as here, cooling down; this is the amount of heat that must flow out.

4. Fill in the blank: the tendency of an isolated system's ___ to never decrease over time is what gives the universe an "arrow of time."

Unlike Newton's laws or energy conservation, which look equally valid played forward or backward in time, entropy's steady increase (or, at best, constancy for an idealized reversible process) gives time a definite direction.

5. A heat engine absorbs 7,500 J of heat from its hot reservoir and exhausts 4,500 J to its cold reservoir each cycle. What is its efficiency?

W = Q_H − Q_C = 7500 − 4500 = 3000 J, so e = W/Q_H = 3000/7500 = 0.40, or 40%. The 60% figure is the fraction of heat exhausted as waste (4500/7500), not the efficiency.

6. A liquid nitrogen bath sits at −196°C. Answer both parts:

(a) What is this temperature in Kelvin?

(b) Fill in the blank: a patient's body temperature of 310 K corresponds to ___ °C.

(a) T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15 = −196 + 273.15 = 77.15 K — still far above 0 K, since absolute zero is much colder than any everyday cryogenic temperature. (b) T(°C) = T(K) − 273.15 = 310 − 273.15 = 36.85°C, close to normal human body temperature.

7. Samples of hydrogen gas and carbon dioxide gas are both at 260 K, even though CO₂ molecules are roughly 22 times more massive than H₂ molecules. Answer both parts:

(a) How do their average molecular kinetic energies compare?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the Kelvin temperature of a gas is cut in half, its average molecular kinetic energy becomes ___ of its original value.

(a) KE_avg = (3/2)k_BT depends only on T, not on molecular mass, so the two gases have identical average kinetic energy despite the huge mass difference. (b) Since KE_avg is directly proportional to T, halving T exactly halves KE_avg.

8. A 0.6 kg block of metal (c = 450 J/(kg·°C)) at 200°C is dropped into 1.2 kg of water (c = 4186 J/(kg·°C)) at 15°C, and no heat is lost to the surroundings. Answer both parts:

(a) Which equation correctly sets up the energy balance to find the final equilibrium temperature T_f?

(b) Fill in the blank: the final equilibrium temperature will end up much closer to ___'s starting temperature.

(a) Heat lost by the hotter metal equals heat gained by the cooler water: mc(T_initial − T_f) for the object cooling equals mc(T_f − T_initial) for the object warming. (b) Water's much larger specific heat means the same heat exchange produces only a small temperature change in the water, pulling T_f close to water's original 15°C (working it out gives T_f ≈ 24.4°C).

9. A 1.5 kg block of ice at 0°C is completely melted into water at 0°C, using L_f = 334,000 J/kg for water. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: how much heat is required, in joules? Q = ___ J.

(b) Once the ice has fully melted and heat continues to be added, the water's temperature will:

(a) Q = mL_f = (1.5)(334,000) = 501,000 J. (b) Once melting is complete, there are no more bonds left to break, so added heat resumes increasing average molecular kinetic energy, and temperature rises again.

10. A gas releases 250 J of heat to its surroundings while 600 J of work is done on the gas, compressing it. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the gas's internal energy changes by ΔU = ___ J.

(b) If this same compression happened adiabatically instead (with the same 600 J of work done on the gas), the internal energy would change by:

(a) Heat leaving the system means Q = −250 J, and work done on the gas means the work done by the gas is W = −600 J, so ΔU = Q − W = (−250) − (−600) = 350 J. (b) Adiabatic means Q = 0, so ΔU = −W = −(−600) = +600 J — the internal energy rises entirely from the compression work, with no heat loss to offset it.
