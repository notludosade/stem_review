1. Thermometer C is placed in contact with patient A until they reach thermal equilibrium, then removed and placed in contact with a bath of water B until it reaches thermal equilibrium with B as well. What can be concluded about A and B?

This is exactly the zeroth law of thermodynamics: two objects each in equilibrium with a third object are in equilibrium with each other, even though A and B never touched. This transitive property is what makes a single thermometer meaningful for comparing two objects.

2. A rigid tank holds 4 mol of an ideal gas at 350 K in a volume of 0.20 m³. What is the gas's pressure?

P = nRT/V = (4)(8.31)(350)/0.20 = 11,634/0.20 = 58,170 Pa.

3. How much heat is required to raise the temperature of 5 kg of water from 25°C to 45°C?

Q = mcΔT = (5)(4186)(20) = 418,600 J.

4. Fill in the blank: according to the second law of thermodynamics, the total ___ of an isolated system never decreases over time.

Entropy stays constant only for an idealized reversible process and increases for any real process, but it never decreases in an isolated system — this steady increase is often described as the "arrow of time."

5. A heat engine absorbs 6,000 J of heat from its hot reservoir and performs 1,800 J of useful work each cycle. What is its efficiency?

e = W/Q_H = 1800/6000 = 0.30, or 30%. The remaining 4,200 J (70% of the input heat) is exhausted to the cold reservoir as waste heat — that waste fraction is not the efficiency itself, a common mix-up.

6. A cold pack reaches a temperature of −18°C. Answer both parts:

(a) What is this temperature in Kelvin?

(b) Fill in the blank: a comfortable indoor temperature of 295 K is equal to ___ °C.

(a) T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15 = −18 + 273.15 = 255.15 K. (b) T(°C) = T(K) − 273.15 = 295 − 273.15 = 21.85°C.

7. Samples of neon gas and argon gas are both at 320 K, even though argon atoms are roughly twice as massive as neon atoms. Answer both parts:

(a) How do their average molecular kinetic energies compare?

(b) Fill in the blank: if the Kelvin temperature of a gas is doubled, its average molecular kinetic energy becomes ___ times as large.

(a) KE_avg = (3/2)k_BT depends only on T, not on molecular mass, so both gases have identical average kinetic energy despite the mass difference. (b) Since KE_avg is directly proportional to T, doubling T exactly doubles KE_avg — the relationship is linear, not squared.

8. A 0.4 kg block of metal (c = 900 J/(kg·°C)) at 150°C is dropped into 0.8 kg of water (c = 4186 J/(kg·°C)) at 20°C, and no heat is lost to the surroundings. Answer both parts:

(a) Which equation correctly sets up the energy balance to find the final equilibrium temperature T_f?

(b) Fill in the blank: the final equilibrium temperature will end up much closer to ___'s starting temperature.

(a) Heat lost by the hotter metal equals heat gained by the cooler water: mc(T_initial − T_f) for the object cooling down equals mc(T_f − T_initial) for the object warming up. (b) Because water's specific heat is so much larger, the same heat exchange produces a much smaller temperature change in the water, pulling T_f close to water's original 20°C (working it out gives T_f ≈ 32.6°C).

9. A 0.8 kg block of ice at 0°C is completely melted into water at 0°C, using L_f = 334,000 J/kg for water. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: how much heat is required, in joules? Q = ___ J.

(b) While this ice is melting, its temperature:

(a) Q = mL_f = (0.8)(334,000) = 267,200 J. (b) During melting, added heat goes into breaking intermolecular bonds rather than increasing average molecular kinetic energy, so temperature holds flat at 0°C until melting finishes.

10. A gas absorbs 900 J of heat while expanding and doing 350 J of work on a piston. Answer both parts:

(a) Fill in the blank: the gas's internal energy changes by ΔU = ___ J.

(b) If instead this same gas underwent this process adiabatically (with the same 350 J of work done by the gas), its internal energy would change by:

(a) ΔU = Q − W = 900 − 350 = 550 J. (b) Adiabatic means Q = 0, so ΔU = Q − W = 0 − 350 = −350 J — the gas's internal energy decreases since it does work with no heat input to replace that energy. This differs from an isothermal process (ΔU = 0) and an isochoric process (W = 0).
