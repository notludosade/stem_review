1. A set of vectors {v1, …, vk} is linearly dependent when:

Dependence is the existence of a nontrivial combination equal to 0 — not a statement about every vector being zero, set size, or spanning.

2. Fill in the blank: any single nonzero vector, by itself, is always linearly ___.

cv = 0 with v ≠ 0 forces c = 0 — the trivial solution is the only one, so a lone nonzero vector is always independent.

3. For v1 = (1, 5) and v2 = (2, 3), answer both parts:

(a) What is ad − bc for this pair?

(b) Fill in the blank: so {v1, v2} is linearly ___.

(a) (1)(3) − (5)(2) = 3 − 10 = −7. (b) A nonzero result means neither vector is a multiple of the other — independent.

4. Answer both parts:

(a) Every basis of a given finite-dimensional vector space V has:

(b) Fill in the blank: the dimension of ℝ⁶ is ___.

(a) That shared count is exactly what "dimension" means. (b) The standard basis of ℝ⁶ has 6 vectors.

5. What is the standard basis for ℝ³?

Three vectors, each with a single 1 in a different coordinate, matching ℝ³'s dimension of 3. Including the zero vector (last option) would make the set automatically dependent.

6. For v1 = (1, 2) and v2 = (3, −1), answer both parts:

(a) Is {v1, v2} a basis for ℝ²?

(b) Fill in the blank: a set with exactly dim(V) vectors is a basis for V exactly when it's linearly ___ (spanning then comes for free).

(a) det = (1)(−1) − (2)(3) = −7 ≠ 0. (b) When the count already matches the dimension, checking independence alone is enough — spanning is automatic.

7. Fill in the blank: the two defining properties of a linear transformation, T(u+v) = T(u)+T(v) and T(cv) = cT(v), are together called ___.

T(u+v) = T(u)+T(v) is additivity; T(cv) = cT(v) is homogeneity (scalar compatibility) — together, linearity.

8. For A = [[2, −1], [0, 3]] and v = (1, 4), answer both parts:

(a) What is T(v) = Av?

(b) Fill in the blank: T(e1), the image of e1, equals column 1 of A = ___.

(a) Av = (2·1 + −1·4, 0·1 + 3·4) = (2 − 4, 12) = (−2, 12). (b) Column 1 of A is (2, 0) — always the image of e1.

9. A linear transformation collapses the unit square down to a line segment (zero area). What must be true of the transformed area everywhere else in the plane?

Linearity scales area uniformly across the whole plane, so a zero area-scaling factor (a singular matrix) flattens every region, not just the unit square.

10. For A = [[−1, 0], [0, 1]], answer both parts:

(a) Geometrically, what does T(v) = Av do to the plane?

(b) Fill in the blank: T((6, 2)) = ___.

(a) This matrix negates x and keeps y the same: (x, y) ↦ (−x, y), a reflection across the y-axis. (b) T((6,2)) = (−6, 2).
