Nonlinear Systems and Partial Fractions

Two loosely related but genuinely useful skills: solving systems where a curve replaces a line, and unwinding a combined fraction back into its simpler pieces.

← Lesson 33 Lesson 35 → Contents Glossary →

1. Nonlinear systems

A nonlinear system has at least one equation that isn't linear — a parabola and a line, a circle and a line, two curves. The same two tools from Lessons 32–33 still apply: substitution (solve one equation for a variable, plug into the other) and elimination. The difference is that a curve can cross a line zero, one, or two times, so a nonlinear system can have zero, one, or two solutions — never a whole line's worth, the way two identical linear equations would.

Worked Example — Parabola and Line y = x² − 4x + 3 and y = x − 1. Substituting: x² − 4x + 3 = x − 1 → x² − 5x + 4 = 0 → (x − 1)(x − 4) = 0 → x = 1 or x = 4. Then y = x − 1 gives y = 0 and y = 3. Two solutions: (1, 0) and (4, 3) — the two points where the parabola and the line actually cross.

3. Partial fractions

Adding two rational expressions with different denominators means finding a common denominator and combining into one fraction. Partial fraction decomposition reverses that: starting from one combined fraction, it recovers the simpler fractions that were added to produce it. For a denominator that factors into distinct linear pieces, each piece gets its own constant numerator.

Method — Distinct Linear Factors To decompose a fraction with denominator (x − a)(x − b): write it as A/(x − a) + B/(x − b), clear denominators to get an equation in x, then substitute x = a and x = b one at a time — each substitution zeroes out one unknown and solves for the other directly.
Worked Example Decompose (x + 7) / [(x − 3)(x + 2)]. Write x + 7 = A(x + 2) + B(x − 3). Let x = 3: 10 = 5A → A = 2. Let x = −2: 5 = −5B → B = −1. So the decomposition is 2/(x − 3) − 1/(x + 2). Recombining confirms it: [2(x+2) − (x−3)] / [(x−3)(x+2)] = (x + 7) / [(x−3)(x+2)] ✓.

Check your recall

1. A system with one line and one parabola can have at most how many solutions?

A line can cross a parabola at most twice — geometrically, a straight line intersecting a curve that only bends one way runs out of new crossings after two points.

2. For a system pairing one linear and one nonlinear equation, the preferred method is usually ___.

Solving the linear equation for one variable and substituting it into the nonlinear equation is usually the most direct route, since the linear equation is easy to isolate a variable from.

3. Decomposing (x+7)/[(x−3)(x+2)] gives:

Substituting x=3 into x+7=A(x+2)+B(x−3) gives A=2, and substituting x=−2 gives B=−1 — matching 2/(x−3) − 1/(x+2).
