Series and Their Notations

A sequence lists terms one at a time. A series adds them all up — and for an infinite geometric sequence, that sum can still land on a single finite number.

← Lesson 38 Lesson 40 → Contents Glossary →

Definition — Series A series is the sum of the terms of a sequence. Summation (sigma) notation, Σ, writes a series compactly: Σ (from k=1 to n) of aₖ means "add up aₖ for every k from 1 to n."

1. Arithmetic series

Formula — Arithmetic Series The sum of the first n terms of an arithmetic sequence is Sₙ = n(a₁ + aₙ) / 2 — n copies of the average of the first and last term.
Worked Example For a₁ = 5, d = 4 (Lesson 38), a₁₀ = 41. S₁₀ = 10(5 + 41)/2 = 10(46)/2 = 230.

2. Geometric series

Formula — Finite Geometric Series The sum of the first n terms of a geometric sequence (r ≠ 1) is Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ) / (1 − r).
Worked Example For a₁ = 3, r = 2 (Lesson 38), S₇ = 3(1 − 2⁷)/(1 − 2) = 3(1 − 128)/(−1) = 3(−127)/(−1) = 381 — matching 3+6+12+24+48+96+192 added directly.

3. Infinite geometric series

Formula — Infinite Geometric Series If |r| < 1, the terms shrink toward 0 and the sum settles on a finite value: S = a₁ / (1 − r). If |r| ≥ 1, the terms don't shrink, the partial sums grow without bound, and the series diverges — it has no sum.
Worked Example a₁ = 8, r = 1/2: the sequence runs 8, 4, 2, 1, 0.5, …. S = 8 / (1 − 0.5) = 8 / 0.5 = 16, even though infinitely many positive terms are being added.

Check your recall

1. A series is:

A sequence lists terms one after another; a series is what you get by adding all of them together, whether finitely or infinitely many.

2. An infinite geometric series has a finite sum only when |r| is less than ___.

When |r| < 1, each term shrinks toward zero fast enough that the running total settles on a finite value; otherwise the partial sums grow without bound.

3. The sum of the first 7 terms of 3, 6, 12, 24, … is:

S₇ = 3(1 − 2⁷)/(1 − 2) = 3(−127)/(−1) = 381, matching the direct sum 3+6+12+24+48+96+192.
