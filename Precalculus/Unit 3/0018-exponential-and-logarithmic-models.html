Exponential and Logarithmic Models

Same one formula, whether it's bacteria multiplying or carbon-14 fading away — only the sign of one constant changes.

← Lesson 17 Lesson 19 → Contents Glossary →

Lesson 14's f(x) = a·bˣ is the general exponential shape. Real growth and decay problems almost always get written in a slightly different form — one built around e — because it makes the constant k directly readable as a continuous rate.

1. The continuous growth/decay model

Model A(t) = A₀ekt, where A₀ = A(0) is the starting amount and k is the continuous rate. k > 0 means growth; k < 0 means decay.

Set k below and watch the same formula sweep from decay through no-change to growth.

Curve shown as A(t)/A₀, so it always starts at 1 regardless of the actual starting amount.

2. Half-life and doubling time

Rules Setting A(t) = A₀/2 and solving for t gives the half-life: t = −ln(2)/k (k < 0). Setting A(t) = 2A₀ gives the doubling time: t = ln(2)/k (k > 0). Neither formula depends on A₀ — the time to halve or double is the same no matter the starting amount.

The readout above already computes whichever of these applies to the current k. Carbon-14 dating runs the same idea in reverse: its half-life is known (5,730 years), which pins down k, and then any remaining percentage can be converted straight into an age.

Try It — Carbon-14 Dating
50

—

k is fixed by the half-life: k = −ln(2)/5730. Age = ln(percent/100) / k.

Check your recall

1. In A(t) = A₀ekt, a negative k describes:

e raised to a negative-times-increasing-t power shrinks toward zero as t grows — that's decay, not growth, a constant, or a straight line.

2. The half-life formula, solved from A₀/2 = A₀ekt, is:

Dividing both sides by A₀ and taking ln gives ln(1/2) = kt, and since ln(1/2) = −ln(2), t = −ln(2)/k — the same magnitude as ln(2)/|k|, since k is negative for decay.

3. Carbon-14's half-life, used to fix k for radiocarbon dating, is ___ years.

5,730 years is carbon-14's measured half-life — the fixed constant that lets a measured remaining percentage be converted into an age.
