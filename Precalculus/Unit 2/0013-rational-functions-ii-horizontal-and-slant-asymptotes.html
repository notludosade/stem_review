Rational Functions II: Horizontal & Slant Asymptotes

Lesson 12 looked at the excluded values. This lesson looks at the far edges instead.

← Lesson 12 Lesson 14 → Contents Glossary →

As x heads toward +∞ or −∞, a rational function's graph often settles toward a line — just like the vertical asymptotes from Lesson 12, but describing what happens at the far edges instead of at an excluded value. Which kind of line shows up, if any, comes down to one comparison: the degree of the numerator versus the degree of the denominator.

1. Horizontal asymptotes: compare the degrees

Rule For f(x) = p(x) / q(x): if deg(p) < deg(q), the horizontal asymptote is y = 0. If deg(p) = deg(q), it's y = (leading coefficient of p) / (leading coefficient of q). If deg(p) > deg(q), there is no horizontal asymptote — the far edges of the graph never level off.

All three cases below share the same denominator, x² + 1, so the only thing changing is the numerator's degree. Switch between them and watch the dashed guide line move — or disappear.

2. When the numerator wins by exactly one degree: slant asymptotes

Case C above — f(x) = x³ / (x² + 1) — has no horizontal asymptote. But it doesn't grow shapelessly either: its numerator's degree beats the denominator's by exactly one, and whenever that happens, long division reveals a straight line the graph hugs instead.

Worked example Divide x³ by (x² + 1): x³ = (x² + 1)·x + (−x). Quotient x, remainder −x. So f(x) = x − x / (x² + 1) — and as |x| grows, that remainder term shrinks toward 0, leaving f(x) closer and closer to the line y = x.
Rule Slant (oblique) asymptote: when deg(p) is exactly one more than deg(q), divide p(x) by q(x); the quotient (ignoring the remainder) is the equation of the slant asymptote. The graph approaches this line as x → ±∞, the same way it approached y = 0 or a constant in the horizontal cases above.

Drag along the curve below and compare it to the line y = x directly — the gap between them is exactly the remainder term, −x / (x² + 1), shrinking as x moves outward.

When the degrees match, the far-edge behavior is governed by the leading terms only — their ratio is the horizontal asymptote, y = 0 only applies when the numerator's degree is smaller.

2. A slant asymptote appears only when:

Exactly one degree of difference leaves a linear quotient after division — a bigger gap leaves a higher-degree quotient, which is a curve, not a line.
