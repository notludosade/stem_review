Right Triangle Trigonometry

The same six functions, seen from inside a triangle instead of on a circle — with real side lengths attached.

← Lesson 22 Lesson 24 → Contents Glossary →

Shrink the unit circle picture down to just one triangle: the radius becomes the hypotenuse, the horizontal drop becomes one leg, the vertical drop becomes the other. Nothing about sine, cosine, or any of the other four functions actually changes — only the vocabulary does.

1. SOH-CAH-TOA

Definitions For an acute angle θ in a right triangle, name the side across from θ the opposite side, the side next to θ (that isn't the hypotenuse) the adjacent side, and the longest side the hypotenuse. Then: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent.

These match Lesson 21's unit-circle definitions exactly when the hypotenuse has length 1: opposite becomes the y-coordinate (sin θ), adjacent becomes the x-coordinate (cos θ). A triangle with any other hypotenuse length just scales both legs by that same factor, leaving every ratio — and so every trig function's value — unchanged.

Definitions — reciprocals csc θ = hypotenuse/opposite, sec θ = hypotenuse/adjacent, cot θ = adjacent/opposite — the same reciprocal relationships from Lesson 22, just written in terms of sides instead of ratios of sin and cos.
Worked Example A right triangle has legs 3 and 4, hypotenuse 5. For the angle θ opposite the side of length 3: sin θ = 3/5, cos θ = 4/5, tan θ = 3/4. Reciprocals follow immediately: csc θ = 5/3, sec θ = 5/4, cot θ = 4/3.

Drag the point to change the triangle's angle. Hypotenuse is fixed at 5 units — watch the legs and every ratio update together.

2. Cofunctions and applied problems

A triangle's three angles sum to 180°. With one angle fixed at 90°, the other two acute angles always sum to 90° — they're complementary. That forces a direct relationship between a function and its "co-" partner.

Formula — Cofunction identities sin θ = cos(90° − θ), and the same pairing holds for tan/cot and sec/csc. Whatever θ's opposite side is, that same side is the adjacent side for the other acute angle (90° − θ) — so its sine and that angle's cosine are computing the identical ratio.
Worked Example — Angle of elevation A 10-foot ladder leans against a wall, making a 60° angle with the ground. The height it reaches is the side opposite that 60° angle, with the ladder itself as the hypotenuse: height = 10 · sin(60°) = 10 · (√3/2) ≈ 8.7 feet. The angle between the ground and the ladder is called the angle of elevation.

Check your recall

1. A right triangle has opposite side 5, adjacent side 12, hypotenuse 13. sin θ equals:

sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse = 5/13. 5/12 is tan θ, 12/5 is cot θ, and 13/5 is csc θ — each distractor names a different one of the six ratios built from the same two side lengths.

2. Using the cofunction identity, cos(35°) equals:

cos θ = sin(90° − θ), and 90° − 35° = 55°. Keeping the same angle, or subtracting from the wrong total (120° or 50°), gives the three distractors.

3. A 10 ft ladder leans against a wall at 60° from the ground. Its height up the wall is 10·sin(60°) ≈ ___ ft (nearest tenth).

sin(60°) = √3/2 ≈ 0.866, and 10 × 0.866 ≈ 8.7 feet.
