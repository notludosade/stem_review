The Unit Circle — Sine and Cosine

Two new functions, defined not by a formula but by a single point's coordinates.

← Lesson 20 Lesson 22 → Contents Glossary →

Take the circle from Lesson 20 and fix its radius at exactly 1 — the unit circle. For any angle θ in standard position, its terminal side crosses the unit circle at exactly one point. That point's coordinates are the two new functions this lesson introduces.

1. Defining sine and cosine

Definition For an angle θ in standard position, let (x, y) be the point where its terminal side meets the unit circle. Then cos θ = x and sin θ = y. Nothing more — sine and cosine are just the coordinates of that one point, read off as functions of θ.

Because every point on the unit circle satisfies x² + y² = 1, substituting x = cos θ and y = sin θ gives the single most-used identity in trigonometry:

Formula Pythagorean Identity: cos²θ + sin²θ = 1, true for every angle θ — a direct restatement of "this point sits on a circle of radius 1."

Since x and y on the unit circle never leave [−1, 1], and θ can be any real number of radians (positive, negative, or wrapping around multiple times), the domain and range are set immediately:

Domain & Range Domain of sin θ and cos θ: all real numbers. Range of both: [−1, 1].

2. Special angles and reference angles

A handful of angles have coordinates worth memorizing outright — the rest of the unit circle follows from reference angles (Lesson 20) plus a sign pattern per quadrant.

Special Angles (first quadrant) 30°: (cos, sin) = (√3/2, 1/2). 45°: (√2/2, √2/2). 60°: (1/2, √3/2). These come from the 30-60-90 and 45-45-90 right triangles, scaled to hypotenuse 1.
Formula Quadrant signs: QI: cos +, sin +. QII: cos −, sin +. QIII: cos −, sin −. QIV: cos +, sin −. To evaluate any angle: find its reference angle's values, then apply the sign for its quadrant.
Worked Example Find sin(150°). 150° is in QII, with reference angle 180° − 150° = 30°. sin is positive in QII, so sin(150°) = +sin(30°) = 1/2. cos(150°) = −cos(30°) = −√3/2, negative since QII has cos negative.

Check your recall

1. cos(270°) equals:

270° is a quadrantal angle landing at the unit circle's bottom point, (0, −1) — so cos(270°), the x-coordinate, is 0, even though sin(270°) is −1.

2. sin(315°) equals:

315° is in QIV, reference angle 360° − 315° = 45°. QIV has sin negative, so sin(315°) = −sin(45°) = −√2/2. The other three distractors flip the sign, swap in 60°'s value, or both.

3. For any angle θ, cos²θ + sin²θ = ___.

The Pythagorean Identity — a direct restatement that (cos θ, sin θ) always sits on the unit circle x² + y² = 1.
