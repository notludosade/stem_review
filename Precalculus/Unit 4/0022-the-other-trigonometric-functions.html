The Other Trigonometric Functions

Four more functions, and not one new definition — every one of them is built entirely out of sine and cosine.

← Lesson 21 Lesson 23 → Contents Glossary →

Sine and cosine are the whole foundation. The four remaining trigonometric functions are just ratios and reciprocals built from those two — no new picture on the unit circle is required, only new arithmetic on coordinates you already know how to find.

1. Tangent, cotangent, secant, cosecant

Definitions tan θ = sin θ / cos θ. cot θ = cos θ / sin θ (equivalently, 1/tan θ). sec θ = 1/cos θ. csc θ = 1/sin θ.

Because these are ratios and reciprocals of sin θ and cos θ, they inherit a restriction sine and cosine never had: they're undefined wherever their denominator hits zero.

Domain tan θ and sec θ are undefined wherever cos θ = 0 — at θ = 90°, 270°, and every angle coterminal with them. cot θ and csc θ are undefined wherever sin θ = 0 — at θ = 0°, 180°, 360°, and coterminal angles.

2. Signs, reference angles, and even/odd behavior

Every one of the six trig functions gets evaluated at any angle the same way: find the reference angle's value, then fix the sign for the quadrant.

Formula — "All Students Take Calculus" QI: all six positive. QII: only sin and csc positive. QIII: only tan and cot positive. QIV: only cos and sec positive. A reciprocal function always matches its partner's sign — sec matches cos, csc matches sin, cot matches tan.
Formula — Even and odd cos θ and sec θ are even: cos(−θ) = cos θ. The other four are odd: sin(−θ) = −sin θ, and likewise for tan, cot, csc. Try dragging the widget above to a negative angle and its mirror-image positive angle to see this directly.
Worked Example Find tan(210°). Reference angle: 210° − 180° = 30°. 210° is in QIII, where tangent is positive. So tan(210°) = +tan(30°) = √3/3 (equivalently 1/√3), using tan(30°) = sin(30°)/cos(30°) = (1/2)/(√3/2).

Check your recall

1. sec θ is defined as:

Secant is the reciprocal of cosine specifically. 1/sin θ is cosecant, sin θ/cos θ is tangent, and cos θ/sin θ is cotangent — each distractor names one of the other three ratio/reciprocal functions instead.

2. tan θ is positive but sin θ is negative in which quadrant?

QIII has both sin and cos negative, so tan = sin/cos divides a negative by a negative and comes out positive — while sin itself stays negative. No other quadrant has that particular combination.

3. sec(180°) equals ___.

cos(180°) = −1, and sec θ = 1/cos θ, so sec(180°) = 1/(−1) = −1.
