Graphs of Sine and Cosine

Unroll the unit circle along a line, and the two coordinates you've been reading off it become two waves.

← Lesson 23 Lesson 25 → Contents Glossary →

Every value you've computed with sin θ and cos θ so far has been a single coordinate at a single angle. Plot θ on a horizontal axis and the coordinate on the vertical axis instead, and the unit circle's endless loop turns into a wave.

1. From circle to wave

Key features Both sin x and cos x are periodic with period 2π — the wave repeats exactly every 2π, since adding 2π to any angle lands back on the same unit-circle point. Domain: all reals. Range: [−1, 1], since a unit-circle coordinate never leaves that interval. sin x is odd (symmetric about the origin); cos x is even (symmetric about the y-axis) — matching Lesson 22's even/odd facts.

2. Amplitude, period, midline, and phase shift

Scaling and shifting the parent sine or cosine graph produces every sinusoidal function — any wave with that same basic shape, however tall, wide, or shifted.

Formula For y = A sin(B(x − C)) + D (or the same with cos): amplitude = |A| (peak height above the midline), period = 2π/|B| (length of one full cycle), phase shift = C (horizontal shift), midline: y = D (vertical shift, the wave's new center line).
Worked Example y = 3 sin(2x): amplitude = 3 (peaks at y = 3, troughs at y = −3), period = 2π/2 = π (twice as fast a cycle as plain sin x).
y = 2 cos(x − π/3) + 1: amplitude = 2, period = 2π, phase shift = π/3 to the right, midline y = 1 (the wave oscillates between y = −1 and y = 3).

Check your recall

1. The period of both sin x and cos x (the parent functions) is:

A full trip around the unit circle is 2π radians, and the wave repeats exactly then — no shorter or longer interval works for the un-scaled parent functions.

2. cos x is symmetric about the:

cos is even (cos(−x) = cos x), which means its graph mirrors across the y-axis — sin x is the one symmetric about the origin, not cos x.

3. The amplitude of y = 3 sin(2x) − 1 is ___.

Amplitude is |A|, the coefficient multiplying the sine term — here 3. The −1 only sets the midline; it doesn't change how tall the wave is.
