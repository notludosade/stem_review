Double-Angle, Half-Angle, and Reduction Formulas

Set β = α in Lesson 27's sum formulas, and they collapse into a shortcut for doubling an angle — which then unlocks formulas for halving one too.

← Lesson 27 Lesson 29 → Contents Glossary →

Lesson 27's sum formulas hold for any two angles α and β — including the special case where they're the same angle. That single substitution, β = α, is the entire origin of everything in this lesson.

1. Double-angle formulas

Formula — Double-angle Substituting β = α into sin(α+β) gives sin 2θ = 2 sinθ cosθ. Substituting into cos(α+β) gives cos 2θ = cos²θ − sin²θ, which the Pythagorean identity lets you rewrite two more ways: = 1 − 2sin²θ = 2cos²θ − 1. Tangent's version: tan 2θ = 2tanθ / (1 − tan²θ).

Three equivalent forms for cos 2θ sounds redundant, but each is the useful one in a different situation — whichever one leaves you with a single function to solve for.

2. Reduction and half-angle formulas

Formula — Reduction Solving the last two cos 2θ forms above for sin²θ and cos²θ instead of cos 2θ gives sin²θ = (1 − cos 2θ)/2 and cos²θ = (1 + cos 2θ)/2 — a way to trade a squared function for a plain cosine at double the angle. This is the exact algebra a Calculus integral will lean on to integrate sin²x and cos²x.
Formula — Half-angle Replacing θ with θ/2 in the reduction formulas (and rearranging) gives sin(θ/2) = ±√((1 − cosθ)/2) and cos(θ/2) = ±√((1 + cosθ)/2). The ± is real — it isn't resolved by algebra, only by knowing which quadrant θ/2 actually lands in.
Worked Example Find sin(15°) exactly, using 15° = 30°/2. sin(15°) = √((1 − cos 30°)/2) = √((1 − √3/2)/2) = √(2 − √3)/2. 15°/2's terminal side is in Quadrant I, where sine is positive, so the + root is the one to keep.

Check your recall

1. cos 2θ can be written as:

cos(α+β) with β = α gives cosθcosθ − sinθsinθ = cos²θ − sin²θ. The last two options both simplify to the Pythagorean identity's 1, not cos 2θ; the first distractor has the sign flipped.

2. sin(2θ) = 2 sinθ ___.

sin(α+β) with β = α gives sinθcosθ + cosθsinθ = 2 sinθ cosθ — the missing factor is cosθ.

3. cos²θ equals:

cos 2θ = 2cos²θ − 1 rearranges to cos²θ = (1 + cos2θ)/2. Swapping the sign gives sin²θ's reduction formula instead; swapping cos2θ for sin2θ doesn't correspond to either identity.
