Modeling with Trigonometric Functions

Anything that rises, falls, and repeats on a fixed cycle — tides, temperatures, the phase of the moon, a pendulum — can be captured by one sinusoidal formula.

← Lesson 30 Contents Glossary →

Everything in this unit has been algebra — rewriting one trig expression as another. This closing lesson turns that algebra outward, onto real periodic data, using four numbers to describe an entire repeating pattern.

1. The general sinusoidal model

Formula — General sinusoidal model y = A sin(B(x − C)) + D, where |A| is the amplitude (distance from the midline to a peak), D is the midline (the average value), 2π/B is the period (length of one full cycle), and C is the phase shift (how far the whole cycle is nudged sideways).
Concept — Sine vs. cosine as a starting choice Sine and cosine are the exact same shape, just offset — so either can model any periodic pattern; the difference is only which one starts more simply. A cosine model begins its cycle at a maximum or minimum; a sine model begins at the midline. Picking whichever matches your data's starting point removes the need for a phase shift entirely.
Worked Example New York City's average monthly temperature swings from a January low of about 30°F to a July high of about 80°F. Midline D = (80+30)/2 = 55°F. Amplitude A = (80−30)/2 = 25°F. Period is 12 months, so B = 2π/12. Since the minimum lands at month 1, a cosine model starting at a minimum fits directly: T(t) = 55 − 25 cos(2π/12 · (t − 1)).

Check your recall

1. In y = A sin(B(x − C)) + D, the amplitude is:

A scales the curve's height above and below the midline — that's the amplitude. B controls the period, C shifts the cycle sideways, and D sets the midline itself.

2. The midline of a sinusoidal model equals the ___ of the maximum and minimum values.

D = (max + min)/2 — the midline is exactly the average of the highest and lowest values the model reaches.

3. Sine begins a cycle at the midline; cosine begins at:

Cosine's graph starts a cycle at its highest or lowest point, depending on the sign of A — never at the midline, which is where sine starts instead.
