# Mission: Precalculus

## Why
Currently enrolled in a Precalculus course and want to *actually* understand it — not just clear tests — while also building a foundation solid enough that Calculus, which follows next, doesn't require re-learning precalc fundamentals along the way.

## Success looks like
- Can explain and apply the core precalculus function families (polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric) from first principles, not formula recall
- Reads and constructs graphs fluently: transformations, domain/range, asymptotes, symmetry, end behavior
- Solves trigonometric equations and works with identities, with real command of the unit circle (not memorized-only)
- Retains this well enough that Calculus can build directly on it without a remedial detour

## Constraints
- Fast, steady pace — move briskly through the standard sequence, but depth over speed when the two trade off
- Algebra (Algebra 1/2: equations, factoring, exponent rules) is already solid — don't re-teach it from scratch; only backfill a gap if one actually surfaces
- No assigned textbook/course to sync with — sequence is designed by the teacher (me) against a standard precalculus curriculum
- Also enrolled in a live school course in parallel — lessons should track a typical precalc ordering so they reinforce classroom material rather than run ahead/behind in a confusing way

## Out of scope
- Calculus proper (limits, derivatives, integrals) — this mission stops at the precalc/calc boundary, though intuition previews are fair game when they motivate *why* a precalc topic matters
- Standardized test strategy / test-taking tricks — not the driver here
